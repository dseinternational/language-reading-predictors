# Methods

See [README.md](README.md) for the project introduction and [the model catalogue](docs/models/README.md) for registered analyses.

## Overview

We use two processes for exploring predictors of language and reading outcomes:

1. **Gradient boosting algorithms** to identify the most influential predictors for each
   outcome.
2. **Bayesian statistical models** to estimate interpretable quantities with quantified uncertainty — treatment effects, dose-response curves, mediation — answering specific questions.

## 1. Gradient-boosting models

**Purpose:** rank predictors by how much they help held-out (cross-validated) prediction, and read the direction and consistency of each effect.

**Fit and cross-validate.** LightGBM with an MAE objective, `GroupKFold` by `subject_id`. One module per problem; level and gain targets are separate models. Because hyperparameters are tuned on the same grouped folds (see **Tune**), the fit pipeline's cross-validation metrics — including the pooled out-of-fold R² — are **post-selection internal cross-validation** numbers, not independent generalisation estimates; `metrics.json` records this as `cv_post_selection: true`.

**Tune.** `scripts/tune_model.py` runs an Optuna TPE study under `GroupKFold`. Each training fold uses an inner `GroupShuffleSplit` for early stopping, keeping the outer validation fold out of that decision. The mean best iteration sets `n_estimators`. Hyperparameters are still selected on the grouped validation folds, so later cross-validation on those folds is post-selection. Results go to `output/tuning/{model_id}/`; applying them requires a manual, reviewable registry edit.

**Rank predictors.** The gradient-boosting layer no longer performs hard feature selection. Each model fits the full default predictor set for its target family and treats the ordered, clustered ranking as the deliverable. Per-fit diagnostics — Spearman correlation, a distance-correlation dendrogram, a mutual-information heatmap, and importance pairing — guard against over-reading a single predictor that is collinear with another. Gain-model defaults exclude intervention-period process measures (`attend`, `tascore`, `tachang`) so the ranking is about baseline/state predictors of progress rather than variables observed during the gain interval.

**Interpret.** Read the SHAP beeswarm (`output/models/{model_id}/shap_summary.png`) alongside permutation importance. Importance ranks predictive contributions; the beeswarm shows their direction and consistency. For each leading predictor, check whether low (blue) and high (red) values separate across zero, whether the pattern is monotonic, and whether tails or similarly ranked predictors point in another direction. State direction explicitly. Predictive importance for a `_GAIN` or `_NEXT` variable does not establish causation.

## 2. Bayesian statistical models

**Undergraduate route.** The [model-methods chapter](docs/report/chapters/methods-models.qmd) follows `lrp-rli-itt-001` from its question and DAG through data, equations, priors, sampling, marginal effects and checks. It includes synthetic examples and a misinterpretations checklist. The [glossary](docs/report/glossary.qmd) assumes no prior Bayesian training.

**Specification contract.** Every registered family declares immutable typed settings. Before creating a fit context or reading data, `resolve_<family>_run_plan()` rejects unknown keys, mixed typed/legacy declarations and contradictory choices, then fills defaults. Check settings-only constraints here, before the factory runs or output is reset.

The family uses this plan for loader arguments, adjustment terms, factory arguments and diagnostic variables. ITT also uses it for analysis-set audits, predictive calibration, influence refits and metadata. Each fit saves `resolved_run_plan` in `config.json` and writes `model_recipe.md`, an introductory account of the question, rows, likelihood, treatment contrast, non-causal terms and checks. See [the architecture guide](AGENTS.md#statistical-models-statistical_models) for module ownership.

**Replication gates and publication transaction.** Every fit writes to a hidden same-filesystem staging directory. The final report stage promotes that complete directory to `output/statistical_models/models/{model_id}-{config}/`; a failed refit leaves the previous successful publication intact rather than deleting it or mixing old and partial artefacts. Each published directory includes `environment-lock.json`, a deterministic snapshot of the resolved `uv.lock` digest, every installed Python distribution version and direct-install source metadata, referenced by filename and SHA-256 digest from `config.json`. CI runs a fixed-seed real PyMC/nutpie posterior-sampling smoke test, an incremental mypy gate over the typed specification/reporting boundaries, and a registry-derived documentation-count check in addition to the deterministic unit tests.

**Execution and release.** Family code chooses the rows, equation and summaries. Models import their owning `pipelines/` entry point directly; the CLI imports only selected models. `stages.py` supplies shared execution, and `subfits.py` samples secondary and sensitivity fits and records their convergence, fitted rows and settings in `subfit_provenance.csv`.

`release/` checks inputs, sampling, required artefacts and treatment-effect robustness in that order. It writes `release_decision.json` before generating findings. `definitions.KINDS` and the [model catalogue](docs/models/README.md) list all families; the summaries below describe eight principal designs.

- **ITT** — the randomised-phase assigned-arm contrast (τ) on one outcome (the uniform DAG-faithful **LRPITT01–11** suite; the own baseline and linear age are _precision_ terms only, no cross-baselines enter, and covariate adjustment is not what creates exchangeability). These fits preserve assigned arm but use the available analysis data rather than every randomised child, so they are **available-case modified ITT analyses**, not a complete ITT analysis of all 57 children. Randomisation balances the original assigned arms in expectation, but a causal interpretation after restricting to archived and observed cases requires the selection and missing-outcome assumptions stated below. Heavily-floored outcomes (phonetic spelling, nonword reading) use a **post-hoc, arm-blind, data-adaptive floor rule** (≥ 40 % of post-scores at zero at t2): the exploratory headline is the off-floor transition risk difference `Pr(post > 0 | observed at floor at t1)`, fit on the baseline-floored at-risk children only. Baseline floor status is pre-randomisation, so this remains a randomised subgroup contrast among children whose floor status and post-score are observed, but its causal reading still requires the same available-case assumption; because the rule and its 40 % threshold were chosen after examining this trial's outcome distribution, it is an exploratory reanalysis estimand rather than a prospectively pre-specified trial primary. A graded Beta-Binomial over all available children and a graded contrast among the off-floor children (an untruncated proxy for the conditional-above-floor mean) are flagged, detection-limited secondaries read only beside per-arm mover counts. Phoneme blending (`B`) uses the ordinary logit mean link in the working model, which permits fitted means below the one-third guessing level of its three-alternative items; any headline `B` interpretation must therefore be accompanied by the mechanically motivated link sensitivity `mu = 1/3 + (2/3) * inverse_logit(eta)` and remains qualified until that companion has passed the same computational and reporting gates. **The requirement is per family, not per study**, because a companion fitted under one likelihood does not validate a `B` headline fitted under another: the pairing is enforced separately for ITT (LRPITT08 + LRPITT108), level factors (LRPLF06 + LRPLF106, #584 decision 2) and waitlist-crossover (LRPDID03 + LRPDID103, #576 finding 2), whose longitudinal random-intercept likelihood lets t1 and t3 data inform the t2 posterior. In each pair neither fit releases without its current, converged, provenance-matched twin. **The requirement is not confined to the families that happen to have a pair built**: it covers every registered fit whose outcome is `B`, in every family, as set out below (#608). The coefficient priors are shared on the latent scale, but the inverse link maps them to different prior distributions for the items-scale effect, so reports show both prior pushforwards rather than attributing every posterior difference to the observed data alone.
- **Joint** — the registered joint specifications place several outcome models in one PyMC graph, but residual correlation is off and the outcome-specific priors and likelihoods factorise. They therefore reproduce separate marginal posteriors rather than learning within-child residual covariance. Current reports lead with per-draw contrasts between probability-scale average marginal effects, which puts tests with different denominators on the common proportion-correct scale; `tau_i - tau_j` conditional-logit contrasts are supplementary. Neither interval preserves within-child outcome covariance under the factorised model, so cross-outcome conclusions remain exploratory sensitivity results pending a paired child-level randomisation-inference/permutation analysis, bootstrap, sandwich, or defensible dependence-model analysis. In the taught-versus-not-taught models, a positive contrast establishes only that the taught effect is larger; describing transfer as limited additionally requires the marginal not-taught effect to be small against a substantively defined negligible-effect threshold.
- **Mechanism** — a dose-response curve `f_mech` of one measure on another (e.g. letter-sound → word reading), conditioned on a DAG-derived adjustment set and a per-child random intercept.
- **Mediation** — a direct/indirect decomposition through a mediator by integrating the posterior g-formula. The current implementation sums finite count supports exactly and uses checked numerical quadrature for normal mediators. The derived-effect MCSE measures posterior sampling precision; it does not assess inner integration error. The software computes the NDE/NIE _functionals_, but under this study's DAG those are **not identified natural effects**, and no amount of extra data repairs that. Two obstacles stack: latent general ability confounds every mediator → outcome path, and intervention dose (`IS`) is a **treatment-induced (exposure-induced) mediator–outcome confounder** — a descendant of the randomised group pointing into both mediator and outcome — so natural effects fail to be identified even under randomisation, and are _not_ rescued by measuring and adjusting dose (VanderWeele, Vansteelandt & Robins 2014, DOI [10.1097/EDE.0000000000000034](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000000034)). Report every mediation quantity as a **model-based decomposition under stated assumptions**, never as a causal route. The **interventional** companions (MED-078 for $L\to W$, MED-186 for $L\to N$ and MED-187 for $L\to B$; `decompose(..., interventional=True)`) invoke no cross-world quantity and so escape the recanting-witness obstacle in the target estimand, but they still assume no unmeasured mediator–outcome confounding (Hejazi, Rudolph, van der Laan & Díaz 2022, assumption A5, DOI [10.1093/biostatistics/kxac002](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxac002)) — weaker-assumption targets, not identified effects in this study.
- **Waitlist-crossover arm-by-wave models** — a longitudinal sensitivity analysis alongside the available-case modified ITT estimates (the **LRPDID01–15** family plus its registered companions): the binary-treatment models jointly model the bounded t1, t2 and t3 levels with separate immediate-minus-waitlist gaps at each wave. `arm_gap_t1` checks pre-randomisation balance. `tau_t2` is the randomised contrast of _assignment to immediate treatment versus no treatment yet_; it is the covariate-adjusted t2 arm-gap **level**, not the differenced `tau_t2 - arm_gap_t1`, so the baseline adjustment it carries is soft and prior-weighted — a realised t1 imbalance is allocated between the tightly regularised `arm_gap_t1` and the shared child intercepts, and LRPDID104 is the registered sensitivity over that allocation (2026-08-24 sign-off, `notes/202608241100-did-t2-estimand-signoff.md`). `arm_gap_t3` is **also identified by the original randomisation** — assignment does not stop being random after crossover — but it contrasts a _different exposure_: the early-start treatment history against the delayed-start one, both arms having been treated by t3. It is therefore not a treated-versus-untreated effect, and latent ability is not what limits it; what is unavailable is the mechanism, because duration, carryover, maturation, ceiling effects and different taught blocks are inseparable within it. `delta_crossover = tau_t2 - arm_gap_t3` is the change between those two randomised regime contrasts and is never evidence of an identified catch-up process. A child random intercept partially pools stable between-child differences; it does not make every child their own fixed-effect control, and the within-child posterior-predictive check (`did_within_child_ppc.csv`) tests the restrictive covariance that assumption implies. The models do not condition on each period's start outcome because t2 is already treatment-affected for the immediate arm when it becomes the P2 baseline. Phoneme blending (`B`) takes the same mandatory response-link pairing the ITT and level families use: **LRPDID03** (ordinary logit) and **LRPDID103** (three-choice guessing floor) are a registered pair and neither releases without the other. Dose variants retain the two transition rows, adjust for randomised arm, the arm-by-period cell structure, pre-randomisation t1 outcome and t1 age, and estimate observational treated-centred session-dose associations; because `treated = (immediate arm) OR (period 2)` saturates the four-cell design, `theta_treated` at the mean treated dose is a crossover _cell_ contrast rather than an isolated treatment-presence effect. Every dose fit publishes one named focal estimand — the natural-scale treated-row dose marginal — which its prior pushforward, prior-sensitivity sweep and release gate all check. LRPDID07 resolves the session association by period and LRPDID07base is its pooled-dose LOO comparator.
- **Gain factors** — a DAG-focused ANCOVA of a period's post-score on its own pre-score (the **LRPGF01–11** family, one model per outcome; LRPGF01–08 each have a treated-only companion), with a per-child random intercept that accounts for repeated observations and partially pools stable residual between-child heterogeneity. It does not control omitted latent general ability that is correlated with the included predictors. Only the randomised on-intervention term is causal; every other covariate (own baseline, age, cognitive ability, upstream DAG skills, focal interactions) is reported as an explicit _adjusted association_, never as "X drives Y". SES is excluded — not a DAG node, and statistically redundant. Phonetic spelling inherits the ITT floor rule (an off-floor `Pr(post > 0)` Bernoulli, the treatment marginal an off-floor risk difference).
- **Level factors** — the companion _levels_ view (the **LRPLF01–11** family): the score at each timepoint, with group-by-time and ability-by-time as per-timepoint coefficient vectors. Only the t2 group contrast is the randomised treated-versus-untreated effect; the t3/t4 changes are also identified by the original randomisation — early-start-versus-delayed-start treatment-schedule contrasts, both arms having been taught — and are reported as randomised schedule contrasts with no mechanistic reading, while ability and adjuster terms remain latent-ability-confounded adjusted associations. In both factor families "factors" carries its plain-English sense — the observed covariates _associated with_ gains or levels — not the factor-analysis sense: these are regression models with no latent variables (the latent measurement model is the separate correlated-domain-factor model, LRP-RLI-MM-001).
- **Aligned** — an onset-aligned per-protocol single gain (the **LRPAL01–08** family, plus a dose sensitivity variant): both arms are aligned by intervention onset (immediate t1 → t3, wait-list t2 → t4) into one cross-sectional ANCOVA per child. This trades randomisation for a like-for-like dose comparison, so the cohort contrast is **not** the ITT effect — it is confounded by age-at-onset and cohort/timing, and every coefficient is reported as an association. Cumulative sessions (dose) are a collider and enter only the sensitivity variant.

**Likelihood and priors.** A Beta-Binomial likelihood is used as a **working model** for each bounded total score via a logit linear predictor. It respects the score bounds and permits extra-binomial variation, but it should not be read as a literal claim that every test item is an exchangeable Bernoulli trial: heterogeneous item difficulty, stopping rules and floor or ceiling compression can violate that interpretation. Posterior-predictive checks must therefore assess the full score distribution, including zero and ceiling mass, by arm and relevant baseline strata; material mismatch triggers a likelihood sensitivity analysis. Priors come from shared constructors in `priors.py` (so the factories cannot drift apart); smooth nonlinear terms use Hilbert-space Gaussian-process approximations (`hsgp.py`).

**The phoneme-blending guessing floor binds every `B` fit, in every family (#608).** The requirement above follows from the _link_, not from the estimand: the ordinary inverse-logit score mean permits expected scores anywhere in (0, 1), while ten three-alternative forced-choice items put a child answering at random at about 3.3 out of 10. A model that can predict below-chance expected performance is misspecified whether the quantity it publishes is a randomised contrast or an adjusted association, so the scope is **every registered model with `outcome_symbol="B"`**, and the release gate is keyed on that symbol rather than on the model's family — a new `B` model in any family fails closed until it is paired or carries a recorded, dated exemption.

Two measurements settled this rather than an argument from first principles. First, the defect is realised in **every** stored `B` fit, not merely permitted: posterior mass below one third ranges from 4.9 % (LRPAL06) to 13.7 % (LRPLF06), and every fit has rows where the model is 80–100 % certain of below-chance performance. No fit would be exempted by a threshold rule. Second, the link changes the finding rather than rescaling it. At reporting tier the ITT pair moves from 0.99 items (89 % CI 0.22 to 1.74, evidence **strong**, P(practically negligible) 0.51) under the ordinary link to 0.49 items (89 % CI −0.14 to 1.09, evidence **suggestive**, P(practically negligible) 0.91) under the floor link — the estimate halves, the interval crosses zero and the practical verdict inverts — while the floor link fits marginally _better_ by PSIS-LOO (Δelpd +1.09, SE 2.68). The constraint costs nothing in fit and is mechanically motivated, so the ordinary-link number is the one requiring justification.

The distinction between a causal contrast and an observational slope does not rescue the exemption, because the published quantities are natural-scale on both sides: a dose fit's focal estimand is the natural-scale treated-row dose marginal, which depends on the link exactly as an items-scale treatment effect does.

**Blending-pair evidence.** The [signed-off amendment to #608](notes/202608252100-blending-pair-binding-608-decision-2.md) keeps the content-addressed archive ITT-only. Other families use stored-artefact checks. Every pair must have matching resolved plans apart from the score-mean link and its pairing bookkeeping, and each stored plan must match its current specification. Record and show differences in source commit, dirty flag and environment lock; these need not match. Both evidence tiers enforce pairing.

**Prior-setting protocol.** Priors are **weakly informative and scale-calibrated**, not "uninformative": on a bounded-count logit model a very broad coefficient prior implies implausibly large probability- and item-scale effects — a `Normal(0, 0.5)` on τ is a plausible ~4 readable items on the 32-item letter-sound test but a ~35-item 95 % swing on the 170-item vocabulary tests. Each prior is **role-specific** — the causal τ, the _precision_ covariates (own baseline, age), the adjusted _associations_ (`gamma_cross`, the mechanism and mediator slopes), the _nuisance_ terms (intercept, the dispersion `kappa`, random-effect scales) and the _GP_ amplitudes / lengthscales — and its role is documented per parameter in every report's `priors_table.csv` (a guard test refuses any prior the table cannot describe). Calling a term a precision covariate describes its causal role, not immunity from modelling choices: in a finite Bayesian sample, its functional form and prior can change the posterior for τ when baseline values are imbalanced or the outcome model is misspecified, so unadjusted and prior-sensitivity comparisons remain necessary. The **treatment-effect prior is tiered by outcome** (`measures.DISTAL_OUTCOMES`): proximal directly-taught and decoding outcomes keep the wider `Normal(0, 0.5)`; the broad standardised-transfer outcomes (vocabulary, grammar, concepts) take a tighter `Normal(0, 0.3)`, because that is where a large effect is least expected and where the wide prior is worst on the item scale. The tier resolves in `factories._tau_sigma_for` and applies to the single-outcome ITT `tau`, the gain-factors `beta_trt`, the level-factors t2 group contrast and the waitlist-crossover model's randomised `tau_t2`; `arm_gap_t1`, `arm_gap_t3` and `delta_crossover` are balance quantities or randomised schedule contrasts rather than additional treated-versus-untreated effects. Two terms are **exempted** and keep the common `Normal(0, 0.5)` — the joint model's `tau_k` (untiered across all outcomes) and the aligned family's `beta_cohort` — as documented in `docs/models/PRIORS.md`. A distal outcome's joint `tau_k` may therefore differ from its tiered single-outcome `tau` by a prior-driven amount. In general, priors may be informed by **measurement and scale properties (ceilings, denominators, test–retest reliability), external independent-sample literature, expert judgement from the education team, and prospectively stated substantive constraints**, but not by the posterior of the same fitted model if the analysis is to be described as prior-to-data. The distal tier in this project was adopted after the full ITT posterior table existed. It is therefore disclosed as **post-data, scale-calibrated regularisation**, not a blind prior; the sweep in `scripts/tau_prior_sensitivity.py` shows whether conclusions are stable across plausible prior scales, but that stability does not make the original choice prospective. Every prior is checked by **prior-predictive simulation and an estimand-scale pushforward** — does it generate plausible scores, gains and floor / ceiling patterns? Full rationale and the per-parameter inventory live in `docs/models/PRIORS.md` and `notes/202607011600-issue-141-prior-audit.md`.

**The Beta-Binomial dispersion prior is a modelling choice, not a nuisance (#605).** With `alpha + beta = kappa`, the Beta-Binomial variance inflation over Binomial is `(kappa + n) / (kappa + 1)`, so being within 10 % of Binomial variance needs `kappa >= 10(n - 1) - 1` — 779 at `n = 79` (word reading) and 1689 at `n = 170` (the vocabulary tests). The shared `kappa ~ HalfNormal(50)` gives those regions vanishing mass, so on a high-denominator outcome it **enforces a floor on overdispersion** (about 3.3× and 5.9× at its own median) rather than leaving the near-Binomial limit — for a bounded count the ordinary hypothesis "no extra-Binomial variation beyond the child random intercept" — available. The alternative is a change of _shape_, not scale: `1/sqrt(kappa) ~ HalfNormal(sigma)`, on which the no-dispersion limit is simply zero. `level_factors` adopted it as its default under #584 decision 4; `itt`, `level_factors`, `did` and `mechanism` all accept it via the shared `kappa_prior_family` setting, and the `did` (#576) and `mechanism` (#605) families each carry registered low- and high-denominator sensitivity companions. Mechanism fits publish a `dispersion_summary.csv` recording the prior it was drawn against, `kappa`'s posterior, the implied variance inflation and `P(within 10 % of Binomial)`, so a reader can see whether the data moved the parameter or the prior carried it. Dispersion widens intervals; it does not change the direction of a coefficient.

For the post-hoc P/N floor-transition estimands, a power-scaling conflict for `tau` triggers an explicit estimand-matched sensitivity grid rather than the ordinary graded-outcome sweep: the same observed-baseline-floor subgroup and Bernoulli off-floor likelihood are fitted under `tau ~ Normal(0, 0.5/1.0/1.5)` with the linear-age precision term both present and absent. All six fits must pass the all-free-variable convergence gate and retain content-addressed traces. The release evaluator binds each trace to its exact prior/age cell, data, analysis set, primary fit, free-variable set and sampling contract, then recomputes convergence and every reported effect summary from the draws; a self-declared CSV flag is not sufficient. Reports show the full risk-difference and `P(benefit)` range; completing the grid is a computational release gate, not evidence that the result is prior-insensitive.

**Sign convention:** `G = 1` is the immediate-intervention arm after the `2 − group` recode (`group == 1` = immediate intervention, `group == 2` = wait-list control), so **positive τ means the fitted intervention-arm outcome is higher**; interpreting that contrast as an effect of intervention requires the selection and missing-data assumptions below.

**ITT analysis population and estimand hierarchy.** Burgoyne et al. (2012, DOI [10.1111/j.1469-7610.2012.02557.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2012.02557.x)) randomised 57 children: 29 to immediate intervention and 28 to waiting control. Its CONSORT diagram reports three post-randomisation losses to follow-up—one intervention child who moved school and two waiting-control children, one who moved school and one whose reason is recorded as “refused to participate in testing, school withdrawn”—leaving 28 and 26 children analysed. It separately reports that two children in each arm discontinued the intervention after moving school but were followed and retained in the analyses. The repository's analysis file contains those 54 analysed children, and each model then uses the children with the outcome and required baseline/covariates observed; the fitted sample is commonly 54, is 53 for outcomes with an unavailable t2 score, and is smaller for the baseline-floor subgroup estimands. The original ANCOVAs used Mplus full-information maximum likelihood for the small amount of missing data within the reported analyses; that does not show that the three children lost to follow-up entered the paper's 54-child analysed cohort and is distinct from the current Bayesian complete-case restrictions. Assignment is always analysed as randomised for the observed children, including the four children who discontinued but were followed, but this sequence is `57 randomised → 3 lost to follow-up → 54 analysed and available in the repository → model-specific fitted sample`, so the current suite is an available-case modified ITT. Randomisation establishes exchangeability at assignment for the full cohort; it does not automatically preserve exchangeability after loss to follow-up or further observed-data restrictions. Interpreting even the fitted-sample arm contrast causally therefore assumes that loss to follow-up, outcome observation and any additional complete-case restriction did not select children differently between arms according to their potential outcomes, after allowing for the modelled pre-treatment information. Interpreting that selected-sample contrast as the effect for all 57 randomised children requires further assumptions about the missing outcomes and an estimand-matched attrition sensitivity. The emitted extreme-case raw post-score bounds are a useful model-free benchmark, but they are neither the covariate-adjusted Bayesian average marginal effect nor a missing-at-random or missing-not-at-random model. Reports must show the denominator and exclusions by arm. The published 2012 analysis described four primary outcome measures—single-word reading (`W`), letter-sound knowledge (`L`), phoneme blending (`B`) and taught expressive vocabulary (`TE`). This reanalysis makes `W` its single headline primary and treats the remaining outcomes as a family. That is an explicit **reanalysis hierarchy**, not the original trial's outcome hierarchy; both must be stated when interpreting multiplicity or comparing conclusions with the publication.

**Word-reading missing-data sensitivity.** `lrp-rli-itt-010` remains the model of record: it fits the 53 observed t2 word-reading outcomes using the t1 79-item word-reading baseline and t1 age. Its release additionally requires a trace-backed secondary sub-fit based on the complete screening word-reading (30-item) and screening-age profiles recovered for all 57 randomised children from the checksum-pinned UK Data Service archive (DOI [10.5255/UKDA-SN-852291](https://doi.org/10.5255/UKDA-SN-852291)). The companion's intercept prior is `Normal(mean all-57 pre-randomisation screening-W logit, 1)`, so its outcome level is anchored without using a t2 outcome; 1,000 prior and prior-predictive draws and their estimand-scale check are persisted with the trace. The secondary first reports a common-profile bridge estimate over the same 53 observed-outcome profiles, making the change of baseline instrument and timing visible, then standardises both arm surfaces over all 57 profiles under missing at random. Here MAR means that outcome observation is independent of the unseen t2 score conditional on assigned arm, screening word reading and screening age, together with the fitted logit Beta-Binomial outcome model and covariate overlap; this identifying assumption cannot be tested by the posterior-predictive check. The reference and delta analyses deliberately switch to factual randomised-arm completion: they compare predicted outcomes over the 29 assigned-intervention profiles with those over the 28 assigned-control profiles and modify only missing outcomes in their assigned arm. The zero-delta cell is the factual-arm MAR anchor. The no-benefit restriction gives the documented intervention non-starter the control mean surface; it is not classical distributional reference-based multiple imputation. The complete fixed item-shift grid `{-8, -4, 0, +4, +8}` in each arm applies to the one intervention and three control profiles with missing t2 word reading. Those step sizes are a deliberately broad diagnostic stress test, not an empirically calibrated distribution. The grid shifts are identifying assumptions, not estimated missingness parameters, and any clipping at the 0/79 test bounds is reported. With only four unavailable W outcomes, a fitted response-selection model or arm-specific missing-not-at-random parameters would be overwhelmingly prior-driven. The MAR, reference and delta scenarios therefore remain adjacent to the model-free sharp bounds, and no favourable scenario may be released alone. The archive is **committed to this repository** as `data/dse-rli-trial-data-archive.csv`, verbatim and under its upstream filename. Down Syndrome Education International deposited the ReShare collection and holds the rights in it, so it is distributed here under the repository's own CC BY 4.0 data licence; the earlier run-time-supply arrangement existed because the deposit's item-level licence field is blank, which constrains third parties rather than the depositor. The loader resolves that committed file by default, so a clean checkout runs this mandatory bundle with no extra step, and `--rli-randomised-archive` now overrides it for a local re-check rather than being required. The loader verifies the pinned checksum and a privacy-preserving 71-field reconciliation with the repository on every use. It discards the archive's own published anonymised subject labels and generates internal row labels only after every allocation, screening and outcome-count assertion passes, so returned model data and emitted artefacts carry no source-to-repository subject-ID crosswalk. Incorporating the archive supplies pre-randomisation screening profiles for the three children lost to follow-up; because it holds no t1 or later measurement for them, it does **not** recover their outcomes and does not make any fit a full-cohort ITT analysis.

**Fit.** NUTS via `nutpie`, with `dev` / `test` / `reporting` sampling presets (the last is 6 chains × 6000 draws); `--target-accept` overrides when a funnel needs it.

**Evaluate.** Check computation _before_ reading any estimate. The automatic clean-pass gate requires **0 divergences, BFMI ≥ 0.3, R-hat ≤ 1.01 and bulk/tail ESS ≥ 400**, evaluated unrounded over the model's free random variables plus the headline scalars. The pipeline records that measured verdict in `diagnostics_summary.json` and renders it before any estimate. Any R-hat, ESS or BFMI failure, or any unreviewed divergence, withholds findings. This is only the sampling gate: `pareto_k.csv` separately flags importance-sampled leave-one-out points above ArviZ's reliability threshold and makes that predictive score unreliable. In single-period ITT/joint models, where each point is one child, `scripts/influence_sensitivity.py` performs the required direct treatment-effect refit without all flagged children before a robustness claim. It standardises the full and leave-out posteriors over the same retained children, so the headline refit shift is not contaminated by changing the covariate-averaging population; the smaller composition shift and the total full-sample-to-leave-out shift are reported separately. This checks whether the treatment estimate is sensitive to those children; it does not repair or replace the unreliable LOO score. Where LOO holds out a child-by-period row conditional on the child's fitted intercept, use a sensitivity or exact/moment-matched LOO for that same target. Other families hold out children; follow the declared plan rather than applying the ITT/joint runner to every repeated-measures fit. Prior- and posterior-predictive checks assess whether the working likelihood reproduces the outcome by arm, baseline band and distribution shape; a material shape flag remains a qualification even when convergence passes. The 1,000 prior draws are persisted onto `trace.nc` so the report can show the prior-predictive check, the prior-vs-posterior overlay, the estimand-scale prior pushforward and power-scaling sensitivity without refitting.

**Divergent-transition qualification.** A divergence warns that Hamiltonian Monte Carlo may have missed posterior mass; its count or percentage alone cannot establish safety. Zero therefore remains the only automatic clean pass. A reporting-tier fit with a genuinely small absolute number of divergences may exceptionally be labelled **QUALIFIED, NOT PASSED** only after a trace- and estimand-specific review; it is never converted to `diagnostics_summary.passed = true`. The Stan diagnostic guidance likewise says that a few divergences with otherwise healthy diagnostics can sometimes be adequate after careful investigation, but cannot be assumed harmless ([Stan Development Team](https://mc-stan.org/learn-stan/diagnostics-warnings.html); Betancourt 2017, DOI [10.48550/arXiv.1701.02434](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.02434)). Qualification requires divergences to be the only failed check; a documented geometry-remediation attempt and independent-seed reporting run; stable named headline summaries within Monte Carlo uncertainty; and a diagnostic run that deliberately generates enough transitions from the same problematic geometry to map their relationship to those estimands and their stochastic ancestors. A visually unpatterned handful and agreement between seeds that might both miss the same region are not positive evidence of safety. The review must be content-addressed and name the trace, config, data, source, sampling contract, permitted outputs, reviewer, owner, issue and date. Deleting divergent draws is not evidence about posterior mass the sampler never reached. Any causal or model-of-record treatment effect, mediation decomposition, floor/survival estimand, nonlinear knee or shape, dose-heterogeneity slope, horseshoe ranking, covariance or latent-structure quantity remains zero-divergence-only. Qualified fits are excluded from model comparisons unless their predictive geometry is separately reviewed, and qualification never propagates to sensitivity or leave-one-out refits. The full policy and its supersession of the earlier percentage guidance and permanent model-spec waiver are recorded in `notes/202608021625-divergence-qualification-policy.md`; until a trace-bound verifier is implemented and a fit-specific review is approved, every divergent fit fails closed.

**Reading diagnostics.** Call `statistical_models.sampling_quality.sampling_quality(trace, var_names=…)` for unrounded R-hat, ESS, BFMI and divergence counts. Do not reimplement extraction in pipelines or one-off scripts. Rounded R-hat can conceal a gate failure: at two significant figures, 1.011 through 1.049 all display as `1.0`.

When using `az.summary` elsewhere, `round_to="none"` is the string that disables rounding; `None` and `"auto"` do not. Gate ESS on both `ess_bulk` and `ess_tail`. The helper extracts signals but does not decide pass/fail; each caller retains its variable set and missing-BFMI policy. The fixes are recorded in dseinternational/research#65 and #440.

**Compare.** Use ArviZ PSIS-LOO to compare models fitted to the same ordered rows, with the same holdout unit and prediction target. Read `elpd_diff` with its standard error (`dse`); `scripts/compare_statistical_models.py` collects the nested and cross-model comparisons. Conditional row-level LOO predicts a held-out row given the child's fitted intercept and remaining observations. It is not new-child prediction.

Gain-factor plans declare `loo_unit="child"`: PSIS removes all of a child's transition likelihood contributions together, since one transition's post-score can be the next transition's baseline. The score conditions on the supplied baseline predictors; it does not evaluate prediction before those baselines are observed. Check the declared plan and Pareto diagnostics rather than inferring the holdout unit from the presence of a random intercept.

**A declared prediction target, for the joint families (#626).** A LOO unit says what is held out; it does not by itself say what the held-out unit is a draw _of_. The three joint families (`joint`, `joint_mechanism`, `historical_joint`) now declare that separately as `prediction_target`, and the declared target is **a new child in a replicate cohort** — same size, same group composition, observed covariates, a child the model has never met. Children are the sampling unit in these designs; the waves are a fixed balanced design rather than a sample of occasions. The alternative target, a new occasion for an already observed child, is a different holdout and is refused rather than silently served by the new-child machinery.

The declaration matters because a child-aggregated PSIS-LOO does _not_ on its own answer it. Where the model carries a child-level latent — the LKJ residual block, a bivariate child intercept, stable and within-child departures — the importance weights reweight a posterior in which that child's own random effect is still fitted to its own data. The matching validation therefore **integrates that latent out**, re-drawing it from its population distribution at each posterior draw, and runs PSIS on the integrated term so both the ELPD and its Pareto-$k$ belong to the declared target. A family must name its child-level free variables; one carrying a declared child dimension and left undeclared **fails the fit**, because reverting to the conditional answer under a new-child label is the defect this exists to stop.

Two error sources gate publication, not one. An unacceptable Pareto-$k$ withholds the estimate, as always. So does a latent integral whose own Monte-Carlo error — measured by a half-split of the re-draws — rivals the ELPD's standard error, because an estimate whose numerical error is the size of its sampling error is not measuring the model. `historical_joint` fails both by a wide margin (Pareto-$k$ in the tens; the ELPD still drifting by tens of nats at 256 latent draws), so it takes the alternative estimator the same declaration supports: **grouped child-level K-fold refits**, stratified by group, each fold rebuilt through the family's own factory and its held-out children scored with latents drawn from the population. A partial K-fold is reported but is not the declared estimate.

The calibration half is now at the same unit. Each measure's per-child total gets a randomised PIT under that new-child predictive (from the refits where K-fold is used, so with no importance weights at all). The existing per-outcome LOO-PIT plots remain, still labelled **conditional leave-one-cell-out**: they keep the child's other cells observed, they answer a different question, and neither validates the other. See `notes/202609011600-joint-new-child-prediction-target-626.md`.

Two separate questions govern whether a nested contrast may be read, and the comparison CSVs record them in separate columns (#438). **Reliability** — `comparison_valid` — asks whether the number can be trusted at all: a comparison is refused whenever the models do not share ordered rows, or when importance sampling is unreliable (`pareto_k` above ArviZ's `good_k`). The HSGP-curve mechanism models each carry one or two such influential rows out of ~150, because the basis coefficients plus a child random intercept at this sample size make a single child-phase row pivotal for the curve near its own exposure value; the linear-mechanism pairs carry none. Those points are repaired by **exact refit** (`reloo`, at most `_RELOO_MAX_REFITS` per model), and `loo_method` / `n_exact_refits` record that the repair happened rather than leaving a reader to infer it from a silently-valid row. **Discrimination** — `elpd_verdict` — is the different question of whether a trustworthy number separates the models, and follows the standing `|elpd_diff| < 4` rule. A nested pair differing by one regularised coefficient sits near the floor of what predictive comparison can resolve at n ≈ 54, so **an inconclusive contrast is the expected outcome and is a pass, not a failed comparison**: it is recorded and reported as "LOO does not distinguish these models at this sample size", and the scientific claim rests on the coefficient's own posterior (median, 50 %/89 % intervals, direction probability, ROPE) in the usual way. Do not read the `|elpd_diff| / dse` ratio in that regime — when two models are near-identical pointwise the standard error is itself unreliable, and a ratio above 5 on a difference of ~1 elpd says the models agree, not that one wins.

**Interpret.** Report the posterior median, an inner 50% and outer 89% equal-tailed credible interval, and the full posterior. The 50% band is a visual aid, not a decision threshold. An 89% highest posterior density interval (HPDI) is a per-scale sensitivity check. The [interval decision](notes/202607172359-credible-interval-standard.md) records the house convention and retirement of the former 90% sensitivity band.

For a continuous scalar distribution, a strictly monotone transformation maps its median and equal-tailed interval to the corresponding summaries on the transformed scale. An HPDI need not transform this way. An average marginal effect combines several uncertain coefficients, so calculate it within each posterior draw before summarising. Substituting median coefficients can give a different answer; the [worked example](docs/learning/itt-model-walkthrough.md#calculate-a-difference-within-every-draw) demonstrates this.

Coverage alone does not establish simulation precision. The 89% interval uses the 5.5% and 94.5% quantiles. Standard tail ESS uses the 5% and 95% quantiles, so it is a nearby diagnostic, not an exact check of those limits. When endpoint precision matters, assess quantile-specific ESS or Monte Carlo error at the reported probabilities. See the [tail-ESS definition](https://mc-stan.org/posterior/reference/ess_tail.html), [quantile Monte Carlo error](https://mc-stan.org/posterior/reference/mcse_quantile.html), and Vehtari et al. (2021), DOI [10.1214/20-BA1221](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-BA1221). Neither 89% nor 95% guarantees stable endpoints; the original reporting rationale cites Kruschke (2021), DOI [10.1038/s41562-021-01177-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01177-7).

Read intervention direction from `P(AME > 0)`, where AME is the average marginal effect over the fitted sample's observed covariate profiles. Also report the conditional logit coefficient and its secondary `P(τ_logit > 0)`. Their signs agree in a constant-effect model but can differ under moderation. Give probability/items-scale effects averaged over observed profiles, not a constructed mean child. If the 89% interval includes zero, describe the posterior mass on both sides and avoid a decisive directional claim or a significant/not-significant label. There are no p-values.

Separate direction from meaningful size. Report `P(benefit ≥ δ)` against the education-lead-approved minimally important difference and the mandatory δ-sensitivity sweep. The initial rule was chosen after inspecting results and remains post-hoc for those analyses; later outcomes follow the standing rule in the glossary. A high probability of a positive effect can accompany a practically negligible effect. Selected large point estimates can exaggerate magnitude, so report their uncertainty.

If using evidence labels (inconclusive, suggestive, moderate, strong, very strong), put them after the probability and odds and name the claim. Say "moderate evidence that the intervention helps", not "a moderate effect". Orient the label to the favoured direction, including harm. See [the evidence and ROPE guidance](notes/202606261304-evidence-strength-and-rope-reporting.md).

## Causal interpretation and its limits

**What is causal:** within the randomised window, assignment licenses a causal treatment contrast for the assigned cohort under the causal model; baseline floor status can define a pre-randomisation subgroup. Once the analysis is restricted to archived children, observed outcomes or complete cases, calling the fitted contrast causal additionally requires the available-case selection assumption stated above. The same conditions apply to the waitlist-crossover model's immediate-minus-waitlist t2 contrast, `tau_t2`, because it uses the same randomised comparison; agreement is not independent evidence because the models share data. **The t3 arm gap is a separate case and was misclassified until 2026-08-24 (#576 finding 3).** Original assignment remains randomised at t3, so `arm_gap_t3` identifies the effect of assignment to the early-start treatment history versus the delayed-start one, under the same available-case and model assumptions as `tau_t2`; latent ability does not become a confounder of a randomised assignment merely because the waiting list has crossed over, so calling it a latent-ability-confounded association was wrong in both directions — it understated the identification and overstated the confounding. What is genuinely unavailable is the **mechanism**: treatment duration, carryover, maturation, ceiling effects and the different taught blocks are inseparable within that one contrast. `delta_crossover` is correspondingly a change between two randomised regime contrasts, and attributing it to waiting-list catch-up, or reading it as the intervention "wearing off", requires assumptions this design does not supply. Report both t3 quantities as randomised _regime_ contrasts with no mechanistic reading. The causal licence does not extend at all to dose or heterogeneity quantities, which are not randomised. Randomisation balances arms in expectation at assignment, but it does not repair exclusions or post-randomisation missing outcomes, and the effect for all 57 children needs the further missing-data assumptions and sensitivity stated above. The own-baseline term is in an ITT model for _precision_, not missing-data identification—say so when writing up an ITT result, and remember that its finite-sample prior and functional form can still influence the numerical posterior for τ.

**What is not:** every association we did not randomise — the `gamma_cross` "baseline X predicts later Y" terms, the mechanism `f_mech` slope, and the mediator → outcome arrow. These have cross-lagged-panel structure and can be inflated by a stable trait shared across measures or by ordinary measurement noise (which alone can manufacture a spurious "earlier → later" path). The mechanism models already include the recommended repair — a per-child random intercept, but two leaks remain: the mechanism is measured at the _same wave_ as the outcome, and we do not separately model measurement error. So report these as _adjusted associations_, never as "X drives Y".

**The mechanism family's declared natural-scale estimand (#602).** The family used to publish two different natural-scale answers to the same question in the same report — a full-observed-range contrast that averaged fitted probabilities across rows, and an interquartile worked example computed for a constructed "typical child" — over different intervals and under different standardisations, with nothing saying which was the headline. On `lrp-rli-mech-058` those were +6.6 and +2.3 items. There is now **one** headline, and every artefact computes it: the predicted outcome difference between the **25th and 75th percentile of the fitted exposure** (`items_ref_quantiles`, per-model configurable), **standardised over the fitted rows** — each row keeps its own period, covariates, autoregressive baseline and **its child's own fitted random intercept**, and only the exposure is moved; any moderator is held at its standardised mean. Formally `y(x) = N * mean_i expit(eta_base[i] + f_i(x))`, so the answer is "averaged over the children actually analysed", not "for a typical child", and probabilities are averaged after the link rather than the link applied to an average. The full observed range is retained as an explicitly labelled **secondary** contrast, because the observed extremes are order statistics that move with a single child and sit exactly where an HSGP curve is least constrained. `mechanism_summary.csv` carries both rows with a machine-readable `estimand` on each (headline first), `mechanism_curve_items.csv` plots that same standardised quantity so the worked-example points lie on the curve, `key_findings.json` records the headline under `headline_estimand`, and the cross-model `mechanism_forest.csv` reports each model's secant across the same declared interval per SD of the exposure logit — which for a linear fit is exactly `beta_mech`. Both scales of the steepest-interval statistic are published: the latent-logit interval (`scale`) and, under the same reference population, the **expected-items** interval (`items_*`), because `dE[y]/dx = N p(1-p) d(eta)/dx` can peak elsewhere and "where do items rise fastest?" is a different question from "where does the logit rise fastest?".

**Reading a blend, a between coefficient and a within coefficient (#603).** A single exposure coefficient fitted over a child random intercept is a **precision-weighted blend** of two associations that answer different questions: _between_ children (do children whose average exposure is higher score higher?) and _within_ a child (when their own exposure moves, does their outcome move with it?). The random intercept does not separate them — it models repeated-measures dependence under an independence assumption, is not permitted to correlate with the exposure, and does not decompose the exposure — so the weights come from the variance ratio, not the question. Where a fit declares `decompose_between_within` the two are estimated separately (`beta_between`, `beta_within`, the Mundlak split `pooled_levels` already uses) and published in `mechanism_slope_summary.csv` with the question each answers. **Read a blend as neither**: quote it only when the between and within estimates are close, and otherwise quote the one that matches the claim. A within-child coefficient removes _stable_ between-child confounding, including the stable part of latent general ability; it does **not** make the exposure temporally prior to the outcome (both are still same-wave) and does not remove time-varying confounding or reverse causation. Neither coefficient is causal.

**How reliably measured ability is, and what adjusting for it can and cannot do.** Every family that partials out measured ability uses `blocks`, WPPSI-III Block Design, a single subtest. Object Assembly, the second non-verbal subtest the original trial reported, is now also in the analysis files, and the two correlate at **0.664** over the 54 analysed children. That number is the honest measure of what a single-subtest adjustment achieves: treating the subtests as two indicators of what they share, **Block Design alone is roughly a 0.66-reliable measure of it and their sum roughly 0.80-reliable**, so adjusting for Block Design leaves about a third of the shared factor's variance unadjusted, and the composite about a fifth. Report that limit rather than implying the adjustment is complete. The published subtest alphas (0.84 and 0.85) are internal consistency of each subtest and are **not** its reliability as a measure of the common factor; do not quote them for this purpose. Two consequences follow. First, the gap is small in practice: a screening regression over the fitted mechanism rows moved the letter-sound → word-reading slope from +0.250 (Block Design) to +0.257 (composite) against a standard error of 0.089, and the negative control from +0.075 to +0.074, so the registered composite companions (`LRP306`–`LRP312`, one per ability-adjusted parent) exist to make that check available rather than because the answer is expected to change. Second, and more important, **neither measure is a better proxy for the DAG's latent general ability `GA`**: Block Design and Object Assembly are both perceptual-organisation tasks, so what they share is a _narrow visuospatial factor_ — the domain of relative strength in the Down syndrome profile — while `GA` remains unmeasured and structurally unblockable. A composite-adjusted association is better adjusted for one domain, not closer to a causal effect. `notes/202607172345-design-lessons-for-future-studies.md` asks a future study for a proper multi-indicator general-ability battery; two subtests from one domain is not that.

**Reading a phase-varying slope (#604).** The family stacks the randomised t1→t2 transition with two post-crossover ones under one common exposure slope. Where a fit declares `phase_varying_slope` that slope is estimated per period, partially pooled toward a shared mean (`mu_mech`, spread `sigma_mech_phase`), with a nested PSIS-LOO comparison against the pooled comparator and the per-period exposure support published beside it. A difference between periods is **evidence against pooling, not evidence that the relationship changed over time**: the periods differ in age, treatment history and measurement position at once, only the first is randomised-arm-clean, and the exposure is randomised in none of them.

**Characterising non-responders — the honest ladder.** The intuitive recipe — classify children into responders and non-responders on their observed change, then compare the groups' characteristics — manufactures a subgroup from noise and finds predictors of that artefact (Senn 2016, doi:10.1002/sim.7059). LRPDID13 is retained only as an **exploratory heterogeneity sensitivity**, not as a clean test of whether treatment-response variance exists. Only waitlist children change treatment status, each supplies one untreated-to-treated transition, and immediate-arm random intercepts and treatment slopes are not separately identified from their rows; consequently, the variance component can also absorb heterogeneous maturation, period shocks and measurement noise. Any subsequent moderation must therefore be one-stage, strongly regularised, limited to pre-randomisation moderators and interpreted as exploratory. The observable companion question — who is at persistent low attainment on the floored phonics measures — is answered descriptively and with the floor-sitter survival family (`lrp-rli-surv-009`/`011`), not by a latent non-responder label.

## Reporting results

Write for a science undergraduate familiar with frequentist statistics but new to Bayesian methods. Lead with the finding and uncertainty, then explain its practical meaning.

- **Translate the Bayesian parts.** Expand shorthand on first use (τ = conditional logit-scale treatment coefficient, AME = fitted-sample average marginal treatment effect, `gamma_own` = baseline coupling, `f_mech` = mechanism curve). Read an equal-tailed 89% credible interval in plain words — "given the model and data, the value lies in [a, b] with 89% probability" — prefer a posterior tail probability for the named estimand (`P(AME > 0) = 0.97` for the intervention-help claim) to anything that reads like a p-value, and restate the sign convention wherever τ appears.
- **Pair every estimate with its uncertainty** — never a bare posterior mean or a bare importance rank.
- **Avoid verbal evidence labels ("strong", "moderate") wherever possible** — report the odds and probabilities and let them speak. Where prose forces a label, append the word _evidence_ and name the claim ("strong evidence that the intervention helps"), show the odds beside it, and use the round-odds ladder in `notes/202606261304-evidence-strength-and-rope-reporting.md` — run separately for _direction_ (`pd`) and _meaningful benefit_ (`P(benefit ≥ δ)`). A label must never connote effect _size_.
- **Cite primary sources, verify them before committing them to text, and always include a DOI** when one exists.

## Conventions

- **No outlier exclusion** unless justified on the model page.
- **`GroupKFold` by `subject_id`** wherever longitudinal leakage would otherwise inflate performance.
- **Mechanism-model β_G is not a direct-effect estimate** — both arms are on intervention during phases 1 and 2, so the pooled β_G averages over phases where group is no longer a treatment contrast (documented in each mechanism report).
- **RLI intervention scope** — the intervention targets vocabulary and grammar as well as reading, so null effects on receptive/expressive vocabulary are substantive findings, not by-design predictions.
- **Decisions a future reader might question get a dated note in `notes/`** before they leave your head — the question, the choice, the rationale, the result. Mark AI-drafted notes with a `> [!NOTE]` admonition naming the tool and model (per the AI-authorship rule in `CLAUDE.md`).

## Glossary

- **Posterior** — the distribution of a parameter after combining prior and data; summarised by a mean/median, a credible interval, and tail probabilities.
- **Credible interval (CrI)** — a range holding the parameter with stated probability _given the model and data_ (the reading people wrongly attach to a confidence interval).
- **Posterior tail probability** — e.g. `P(AME > 0)` or `P(τ_logit > 0)`, the posterior mass for a named estimand or parameter on one side of a value; it must say which quantity is being evaluated.
- **Probability of direction (`pd`)** — the posterior mass in the favoured direction for the named quantity. The ITT headline uses `P(AME > 0)`; `P(τ_logit > 0)` is a secondary coefficient read and can differ under moderation. It answers direction, not magnitude. For a symmetric posterior `p ≈ 2(1 − pd)`, so treating a `pd` threshold as a discovery rule would merely disguise a p-value threshold.
- **ROPE / minimally-important difference (δ)** — a band [−δ, +δ] around zero wide enough that an effect inside it is practically equivalent to none (the frequentist SESOI / clinical MID); `P(|effect| < δ)` is its coverage, `P(effect > δ)` the probability of a meaningful benefit. δ is set to **half the period's natural-maturation gain** (floored at 1 item) and was signed off by the education lead after the initial results had been examined (issue #144), so for the analyses fitted to date it is a transparent post-hoc reanalysis judgement rather than a prospectively specified threshold. On 2026-08-19 the rule was confirmed as the project's **standing** practical-difference rule (`notes/202608191130-practical-difference-rule-confirmed.md`): a new outcome's δ is derived from the waiting-list arm's untreated period-1 gain by the rule and recorded in `measures.ROPE_DELTA` before the outcome is fitted, so from that point the threshold is pre-specified. Each ITT report also carries a **δ-sensitivity table** giving every outcome's `P(benefit ≥ δ)` at its adopted δ and a stricter δ (word reading at δ = 1 and 2; the floored outcomes at 10 / 15 / 20 pp), so the meaningful-benefit claim's robustness to the δ choice is explicit rather than hidden in a single number.
- **Type-S / Type-M error** — among effects declared "significant", the rate of wrong _sign_ (S) and the average _magnitude_ exaggeration (M); both worsen at low power.
- **τ (tau)** — the randomised-window assigned-arm contrast in the fitted analysis sample (positive ⇒ intervention arm higher); reading it as a fitted-sample causal effect requires the available-case selection assumption above, and extending it to all 57 randomised children requires further missing-data assumptions and sensitivity.
- **`tau_t2`** — the waitlist-crossover arm-by-wave model's immediate-minus-waitlist contrast at t2; randomised and causal under the same selection and missing-data assumptions as the corresponding available-case modified ITT estimate, but not independent evidence.
- **`delta_crossover`** — `tau_t2 - arm_gap_t3`, the modelled closure of the immediate-minus-waitlist arm gap after crossover; the change between two randomised regime contrasts; never an identified catch-up mechanism.
- **`gamma_own` / `gamma_cross`** — a measure's own-baseline coupling / its cross-baseline couplings, on the logit scale.
- **`f_mech`** — the nonparametric dose-response curve in a mechanism model.
- **ITT (intention-to-treat)** — analyse every randomised child in the arm assigned, regardless of adherence. The current suite preserves assigned arm but starts from 54 of the 57 randomised children and then applies model-specific observed-data requirements, so it is described as available-case modified ITT rather than full ITT; causal interpretation of the resulting selected-sample contrast is conditional on ignorable archive inclusion and outcome observation.
- **Mediation / NDE / NIE** — how much of an effect flows through a mediator (natural direct / natural indirect effect). Here these name the _functionals the software computes_, not identified causal effects: under this study's DAG the natural effects are not identified (latent general ability, plus exposure-induced confounding by dose `IS`). See the Mediation family above.
- **IDE / IIE** — the **interventional** direct / indirect effects (MED-078/186/187): the mediator is drawn from its arm-specific covariate-conditional distribution rather than a cross-world counterfactual. Weaker assumptions than NDE/NIE — the exposure-induced-confounding obstacle drops away — but still not identified here, since no-unmeasured-mediator–outcome-confounding is still required.
- **Exposure-induced (treatment-induced) confounder** — a variable caused by treatment that then confounds the mediator → outcome relation (here: dose `IS`). It blocks identification of natural direct/indirect effects, and adjusting for it does _not_ fix the problem.
- **PSIS-LOO** — Pareto-smoothed importance-sampling leave-one-out cross-validation, the Bayesian model-comparison score (`elpd`).
- **HSGP** — Hilbert-space approximation to a Gaussian process; a fast smooth nonlinear term.
- **Regression to the mean** — extreme scores drift toward the average on retest; visible here as `gamma_own` below 1.
