# Methods

This document describes the methods used in this project. See [README.md](README.md) for an introduction to the project.

## Overview

We use two processes for exploring predictors of language and reading outcomes:

1. **Gradient boosting algorithms** to identify the most influential predictors for each
   outcome.
2. **Bayesian statistical models** to estimate interpretable quantities with quantified uncertainty — treatment effects, dose-response curves, mediation — answering specific questions.

## 1. Gradient-boosting models

**Purpose:** rank predictors by how much they help out-of-sample prediction, and read the direction and consistency of each effect.

**Fit and cross-validate.** LightGBM with an MAE objective, `GroupKFold` by `subject_id`. One module per problem; level and gain targets are separate models.

**Tune.** `scripts/tune_model.py` runs an Optuna TPE study under the same `GroupKFold`. Inside each training fold it carves an inner `GroupShuffleSplit` for early stopping, so the outer validation fold is never seen by `early_stopping` and the reported CV RMSE and best iteration stay independent; the mean best iteration becomes the tuned `n_estimators`. Results land in `output/tuning/{model_id}/`. Tuning does **not** mutate the registry — applying tuned parameters is a manual, reviewable edit, so the source stays the single source of truth.

**Rank predictors.** The gradient-boosting layer no longer performs hard feature selection. Each model fits the full default predictor set for its target family and treats the ordered, clustered ranking as the deliverable. Per-fit diagnostics — Spearman correlation, a distance-correlation dendrogram, a mutual-information heatmap, and importance pairing — guard against over-reading a single predictor that is collinear with another. Gain-model defaults exclude intervention-period process measures (`attend`, `tascore`, `tachang`) so the ranking is about baseline/state predictors of progress rather than variables observed during the gain interval.

**Interpret.** Read the SHAP beeswarm (`output/models/{model_id}/shap_summary.png`) _together with_ the permutation-importance ranking: importance is _how much_ a feature contributes, the beeswarm is _which direction_ and _how consistently_. They often disagree in interpretively important ways — a top-ranked predictor can run opposite to every other, be non-monotonic or sit beside a similar-importance predictor of opposite sign. For each top predictor, check whether low (blue) and high (red) values separate cleanly across zero, how tight the effect is, and whether any tail acts against the dominant direction. Always state the direction; never let a reader infer it from importance alone. A predictive importance on a `_GAIN` or `_NEXT` variable is _not_ a causal claim.

## 2. Bayesian statistical models

**Undergraduate route.** [`docs/report/chapters/methods-models.qmd`](docs/report/chapters/methods-models.qmd) follows `lrp-rli-itt-001` from its estimand and DAG through the dataset columns, transformed predictors, complete equation, priors, Beta-Binomial working likelihood, posterior sampling, average marginal effect, convergence gate and posterior-predictive checks. It includes small synthetic examples for standardisation, partial pooling, the likelihood, credible intervals, tail probabilities and predictive checking, plus a common-misinterpretations checklist. The integrated [glossary](docs/report/glossary.qmd) defines the terms without assuming prior Bayesian training.

**Specification contract.** Each module in a converted family declares immutable typed settings — all twenty-three registered families now have them: `itt`, `joint`, `gain_factors`, `level_factors`, `did`, `concurrent`, `aligned`, `growth`, `historical_growth`, `historical_joint`, `mechanism`, `survival`, `block_exposure`, `dose_response`, `horseshoe`, `long_corr_factor`, `corr_factor`, `mediation`, `mediation_multi`, `adjusted`, `lcsm`, `joint_mechanism` and `pooled_levels`. The mediation declarations share `mediation_settings.py` because `mediation.py` already owns the g-formula algorithms. Before the pipeline creates a fit context or reads data, the family's `resolve_<family>_run_plan()` rejects unknown keys, mixed typed-and-legacy declarations and contradictory choices, fills model-level defaults such as the outcome to load, and creates one concrete run plan. Constraints that depend only on the declared settings are checked at this point rather than in the model factory, which runs after the output directory has been reset and the data read. The family module turns that plan into loader arguments, the effective adjustment set, factory arguments and diagnostic-variable names — and for ITT the analysis-set audit and posterior-predictive calibration as well; the general pipeline supplies only the shared execution stages. For ITT, influence refits and metadata consume the same plan, reducing the risk that two paths silently interpret the specification differently. Every such run saves the machine-readable plan under `resolved_run_plan` in `config.json` and generates `model_recipe.md`, which explains the research question, analysis rows, likelihood, treatment contrast, non-causal precision or adjustment terms, and diagnostic checks in introductory language.

**Replication gates and publication transaction.** Every fit writes to a hidden same-filesystem staging directory. The final report stage promotes that complete directory to `output/statistical_models/models/{model_id}-{config}/`; a failed refit leaves the previous successful publication intact rather than deleting it or mixing old and partial artefacts. Each published directory includes `environment-lock.json`, a deterministic snapshot of the resolved `uv.lock` digest, every installed Python distribution version and direct-install source metadata, referenced by filename and SHA-256 digest from `config.json`. CI runs a fixed-seed real PyMC/nutpie posterior-sampling smoke test, an incremental mypy gate over the typed specification/reporting boundaries, and a registry-derived documentation-count check in addition to the deterministic unit tests.

**Purpose:** estimate interpretable quantities with full uncertainty. `definitions.KINDS` is the authoritative family list. The fit CLI first builds a lightweight, filename-derived map of `LazyModel` entries and imports only a selected model when it is fitted; listing models or rejecting an unknown identifier does not import every convention-named model module. The common order — attach the built model, sample, diagnose, draw posterior predictions, write metadata and finish the report — lives in `stages.py`, and every secondary or sensitivity sub-fit a family runs (a flagged cross-check, a prior-width sweep, a complete-case or later-wave refit) is sampled by the one runner in `subfits.py`, which records its convergence verdict, fitted rows and sampling settings to `subfit_provenance.csv`. Whether a finished fit may publish findings is one ordered decision in `release.py` — inputs, then the sampling-quality gate, then required-artefact completeness, then the treatment-effect robustness policy — written to `release_decision.json` before the key-findings box that follows from it is built. Family code decides which rows, equation and summaries those stages operate on. Every family's orchestration now lives in its own module under `pipelines/`, one per `ModelSpec.kind`; registered model modules import the matching family entry point directly, with no aggregate compatibility facade. All registered families resolve typed run plans while retaining the existing `factories.py` boundary, so this organisational refactor does not imply an equation change. The eight detailed below carry the headline estimands. The remaining registered families cover associations, cross-checks and reproductions: `adjusted`, `corr_factor`, `dose_response`, `lcsm`, `mediation_multi`, `horseshoe`, `growth`, `historical_growth`, `historical_joint`, `joint_mechanism`, `survival`, `block_exposure`, `concurrent` and `long_corr_factor`.

- **ITT** — the randomised-phase assigned-arm contrast (τ) on one outcome (the uniform DAG-faithful **LRPITT01–11** suite; the own baseline and linear age are _precision_ terms only, no cross-baselines enter, and covariate adjustment is not what creates exchangeability). These fits preserve assigned arm but use the available analysis data rather than every randomised child, so they are **available-case modified ITT analyses**, not a complete ITT analysis of all 57 children. Randomisation balances the original assigned arms in expectation, but a causal interpretation after restricting to archived and observed cases requires the selection and missing-outcome assumptions stated below. Heavily-floored outcomes (phonetic spelling, nonword reading) use a **post-hoc, arm-blind, data-adaptive floor rule** (≥ 40 % of post-scores at zero at t2): the exploratory headline is the off-floor transition risk difference `Pr(post > 0 | observed at floor at t1)`, fit on the baseline-floored at-risk children only. Baseline floor status is pre-randomisation, so this remains a randomised subgroup contrast among children whose floor status and post-score are observed, but its causal reading still requires the same available-case assumption; because the rule and its 40 % threshold were chosen after examining this trial's outcome distribution, it is an exploratory reanalysis estimand rather than a prospectively pre-specified trial primary. A graded Beta-Binomial over all available children and a graded contrast among the off-floor children (an untruncated proxy for the conditional-above-floor mean) are flagged, detection-limited secondaries read only beside per-arm mover counts. Phoneme blending (`B`) uses the ordinary logit mean link in the working model, which permits fitted means below the one-third guessing level of its three-alternative items; any headline `B` interpretation must therefore be accompanied by the mechanically motivated link sensitivity `mu = 1/3 + (2/3) * inverse_logit(eta)` and remains qualified until that companion has passed the same computational and reporting gates. The coefficient priors are shared on the latent scale, but the inverse link maps them to different prior distributions for the items-scale effect, so reports show both prior pushforwards rather than attributing every posterior difference to the observed data alone.
- **Joint** — the registered joint specifications place several outcome models in one PyMC graph, but residual correlation is off and the outcome-specific priors and likelihoods factorise. They therefore reproduce separate marginal posteriors rather than learning within-child residual covariance. Current reports lead with per-draw contrasts between probability-scale average marginal effects, which puts tests with different denominators on the common proportion-correct scale; `tau_i - tau_j` conditional-logit contrasts are supplementary. Neither interval preserves within-child outcome covariance under the factorised model, so cross-outcome conclusions remain exploratory sensitivity results pending a paired child-level randomisation-inference/permutation analysis, bootstrap, sandwich, or defensible dependence-model analysis. In the taught-versus-not-taught models, a positive contrast establishes only that the taught effect is larger; describing transfer as limited additionally requires the marginal not-taught effect to be small against a substantively defined negligible-effect threshold.
- **Mechanism** — a dose-response curve `f_mech` of one measure on another (e.g. letter-sound → word reading), conditioned on a DAG-derived adjustment set and a per-child random intercept.
- **Mediation** — a direct/indirect decomposition through a mediator by counterfactual g-formula simulation from the posterior, not from coefficients. The software computes the NDE/NIE _functionals_, but under this study's DAG those are **not identified natural effects**, and no amount of extra data repairs that. Two obstacles stack: latent general ability confounds every mediator → outcome path, and intervention dose (`IS`) is a **treatment-induced (exposure-induced) mediator–outcome confounder** — a descendant of the randomised group pointing into both mediator and outcome — so natural effects fail to be identified even under randomisation, and are _not_ rescued by measuring and adjusting dose (VanderWeele, Vansteelandt & Robins 2014, DOI [10.1097/EDE.0000000000000034](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000000034)). Report every mediation quantity as a **model-based decomposition under stated assumptions**, never as a causal route. The **interventional** companions (MED-078 for $L\to W$, MED-186 for $L\to N$ and MED-187 for $L\to B$; `decompose(..., interventional=True)`) invoke no cross-world quantity and so escape the recanting-witness obstacle in the target estimand, but they still assume no unmeasured mediator–outcome confounding (Hejazi, Rudolph, van der Laan & Díaz 2022, assumption A5, DOI [10.1093/biostatistics/kxac002](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxac002)) — weaker-assumption targets, not identified effects in this study.
- **Waitlist-crossover arm-by-wave models** — a longitudinal sensitivity analysis alongside the available-case modified ITT estimates (the **LRPDID01–13** family): the binary-treatment models jointly model the bounded t1, t2 and t3 levels with separate immediate-minus-waitlist gaps at each wave. `tau_t2` is the clean randomised t2 arm contrast; `arm_gap_t1` checks pre-randomisation balance; `arm_gap_t3` compares arms after both have crossed into treatment and is associational; `delta_crossover = tau_t2 - arm_gap_t3` describes how much the arm gap closes after crossover, but is not a second randomised treatment effect. A child random intercept partially pools stable between-child differences; it does not make every child their own fixed-effect control. The models do not condition on each period's start outcome because t2 is already treatment-affected for the immediate arm when it becomes the P2 baseline. Dose variants retain the two transition rows, adjust for randomised arm, current treatment, pre-randomisation t1 outcome and t1 age, and estimate observational treated-centred session-dose associations. LRPDID07 resolves that association by period and LRPDID07base is its pooled-dose LOO comparator.
- **Gain factors** — a DAG-focused ANCOVA of a period's post-score on its own pre-score (the **LRPGF01–11** family, one model per outcome; LRPGF01–08 each have a treated-only companion), with a per-child random intercept that accounts for repeated observations and partially pools stable residual between-child heterogeneity. It does not control omitted latent general ability that is correlated with the included predictors. Only the randomised on-intervention term is causal; every other covariate (own baseline, age, cognitive ability, upstream DAG skills, focal interactions) is reported as an explicit _adjusted association_, never as "X drives Y". SES is excluded — not a DAG node, and statistically redundant. Phonetic spelling inherits the ITT floor rule (an off-floor `Pr(post > 0)` Bernoulli, the treatment marginal an off-floor risk difference).
- **Level factors** — the companion _levels_ view (the **LRPLF01–11** family): the score at each timepoint, with group-by-time and ability-by-time as per-timepoint coefficient vectors. Only the t2 group contrast is a clean randomised effect; later timepoints are post-crossover and reported as associations. In both factor families "factors" carries its plain-English sense — the observed covariates _associated with_ gains or levels — not the factor-analysis sense: these are regression models with no latent variables (the latent measurement model is the separate correlated-domain-factor model, LRP-RLI-MM-001).
- **Aligned** — an onset-aligned per-protocol single gain (the **LRPAL01–08** family, plus a dose sensitivity variant): both arms are aligned by intervention onset (immediate t1 → t3, wait-list t2 → t4) into one cross-sectional ANCOVA per child. This trades randomisation for a like-for-like dose comparison, so the cohort contrast is **not** the ITT effect — it is confounded by age-at-onset and cohort/timing, and every coefficient is reported as an association. Cumulative sessions (dose) are a collider and enter only the sensitivity variant.

**Likelihood and priors.** A Beta-Binomial likelihood is used as a **working model** for each bounded total score via a logit linear predictor. It respects the score bounds and permits extra-binomial variation, but it should not be read as a literal claim that every test item is an exchangeable Bernoulli trial: heterogeneous item difficulty, stopping rules and floor or ceiling compression can violate that interpretation. Posterior-predictive checks must therefore assess the full score distribution, including zero and ceiling mass, by arm and relevant baseline strata; material mismatch triggers a likelihood sensitivity analysis. Priors come from shared constructors in `priors.py` (so the factories cannot drift apart); smooth nonlinear terms use Hilbert-space Gaussian-process approximations (`hsgp.py`).

**Prior-setting protocol.** Priors are **weakly informative and scale-calibrated**, not "uninformative": on a bounded-count logit model a very broad coefficient prior implies implausibly large probability- and item-scale effects — a `Normal(0, 0.5)` on τ is a plausible ~4 readable items on the 32-item letter-sound test but a ~35-item 95 % swing on the 170-item vocabulary tests. Each prior is **role-specific** — the causal τ, the _precision_ covariates (own baseline, age), the adjusted _associations_ (`gamma_cross`, the mechanism and mediator slopes), the _nuisance_ terms (intercept, the dispersion `kappa`, random-effect scales) and the _GP_ amplitudes / lengthscales — and its role is documented per parameter in every report's `priors_table.csv` (a guard test refuses any prior the table cannot describe). Calling a term a precision covariate describes its causal role, not immunity from modelling choices: in a finite Bayesian sample, its functional form and prior can change the posterior for τ when baseline values are imbalanced or the outcome model is misspecified, so unadjusted and prior-sensitivity comparisons remain necessary. The **treatment-effect prior is tiered by outcome** (`measures.DISTAL_OUTCOMES`): proximal directly-taught and decoding outcomes keep the wider `Normal(0, 0.5)`; the broad standardised-transfer outcomes (vocabulary, grammar, concepts) take a tighter `Normal(0, 0.3)`, because that is where a large effect is least expected and where the wide prior is worst on the item scale. The tier resolves in `factories._tau_sigma_for` and applies to the single-outcome ITT `tau`, the gain-factors `beta_trt`, the level-factors t2 group contrast and the waitlist-crossover model's randomised `tau_t2`; `arm_gap_t1`, `arm_gap_t3` and `delta_crossover` are balance or post-crossover quantities rather than additional treatment effects. Two terms are **exempted** and keep the common `Normal(0, 0.5)` — the joint model's `tau_k` (untiered across all outcomes) and the aligned family's `beta_cohort` — as documented in `docs/models/PRIORS.md`. A distal outcome's joint `tau_k` may therefore differ from its tiered single-outcome `tau` by a prior-driven amount. In general, priors may be informed by **measurement and scale properties (ceilings, denominators, test–retest reliability), external independent-sample literature, expert judgement from the education team, and prospectively stated substantive constraints**, but not by the posterior of the same fitted model if the analysis is to be described as prior-to-data. The distal tier in this project was adopted after the full ITT posterior table existed. It is therefore disclosed as **post-data, scale-calibrated regularisation**, not a blind prior; the sweep in `scripts/tau_prior_sensitivity.py` shows whether conclusions are stable across plausible prior scales, but that stability does not make the original choice prospective. Every prior is checked by **prior-predictive simulation and an estimand-scale pushforward** — does it generate plausible scores, gains and floor / ceiling patterns? Full rationale and the per-parameter inventory live in `docs/models/PRIORS.md` and `notes/202607011600-issue-141-prior-audit.md`.

**The Beta-Binomial dispersion prior is a modelling choice, not a nuisance (#605).** With `alpha + beta = kappa`, the Beta-Binomial variance inflation over Binomial is `(kappa + n) / (kappa + 1)`, so being within 10 % of Binomial variance needs `kappa >= 10(n - 1) - 1` — 779 at `n = 79` (word reading) and 1689 at `n = 170` (the vocabulary tests). The shared `kappa ~ HalfNormal(50)` gives those regions vanishing mass, so on a high-denominator outcome it **enforces a floor on overdispersion** (about 3.3× and 5.9× at its own median) rather than leaving the near-Binomial limit — for a bounded count the ordinary hypothesis "no extra-Binomial variation beyond the child random intercept" — available. The alternative is a change of _shape_, not scale: `1/sqrt(kappa) ~ HalfNormal(sigma)`, on which the no-dispersion limit is simply zero. `level_factors` adopted it as its default under #584 decision 4; `itt`, `level_factors` and `mechanism` all accept it via `kappa_prior_family`. Every Beta-Binomial fit that publishes a `dispersion_summary.csv` records the prior it was drawn against, `kappa`'s posterior, the implied variance inflation and `P(within 10 % of Binomial)`, so a reader can see whether the data moved the parameter or the prior carried it. Dispersion widens intervals; it does not change the direction of a coefficient.

For the post-hoc P/N floor-transition estimands, a power-scaling conflict for `tau` triggers an explicit estimand-matched sensitivity grid rather than the ordinary graded-outcome sweep: the same observed-baseline-floor subgroup and Bernoulli off-floor likelihood are fitted under `tau ~ Normal(0, 0.5/1.0/1.5)` with the linear-age precision term both present and absent. All six fits must pass the all-free-variable convergence gate and retain content-addressed traces. The release evaluator binds each trace to its exact prior/age cell, data, analysis set, primary fit, free-variable set and sampling contract, then recomputes convergence and every reported effect summary from the draws; a self-declared CSV flag is not sufficient. Reports show the full risk-difference and `P(benefit)` range; completing the grid is a computational release gate, not evidence that the result is prior-insensitive.

**Sign convention:** `G = 1` is the immediate-intervention arm after the `2 − group` recode (`group == 1` = immediate intervention, `group == 2` = wait-list control), so **positive τ means the fitted intervention-arm outcome is higher**; interpreting that contrast as an effect of intervention requires the selection and missing-data assumptions below.

**ITT analysis population and estimand hierarchy.** Burgoyne et al. (2012, DOI [10.1111/j.1469-7610.2012.02557.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2012.02557.x)) randomised 57 children: 29 to immediate intervention and 28 to waiting control. Its CONSORT diagram reports three post-randomisation losses to follow-up—one intervention child who moved school and two waiting-control children, one who moved school and one whose reason is recorded as “refused to participate in testing, school withdrawn”—leaving 28 and 26 children analysed. It separately reports that two children in each arm discontinued the intervention after moving school but were followed and retained in the analyses. The repository's analysis file contains those 54 analysed children, and each model then uses the children with the outcome and required baseline/covariates observed; the fitted sample is commonly 54, is 53 for outcomes with an unavailable t2 score, and is smaller for the baseline-floor subgroup estimands. The original ANCOVAs used Mplus full-information maximum likelihood for the small amount of missing data within the reported analyses; that does not show that the three children lost to follow-up entered the paper's 54-child analysed cohort and is distinct from the current Bayesian complete-case restrictions. Assignment is always analysed as randomised for the observed children, including the four children who discontinued but were followed, but this sequence is `57 randomised → 3 lost to follow-up → 54 analysed and available in the repository → model-specific fitted sample`, so the current suite is an available-case modified ITT. Randomisation establishes exchangeability at assignment for the full cohort; it does not automatically preserve exchangeability after loss to follow-up or further observed-data restrictions. Interpreting even the fitted-sample arm contrast causally therefore assumes that loss to follow-up, outcome observation and any additional complete-case restriction did not select children differently between arms according to their potential outcomes, after allowing for the modelled pre-treatment information. Interpreting that selected-sample contrast as the effect for all 57 randomised children requires further assumptions about the missing outcomes and an estimand-matched attrition sensitivity. The emitted extreme-case raw post-score bounds are a useful model-free benchmark, but they are neither the covariate-adjusted Bayesian average marginal effect nor a missing-at-random or missing-not-at-random model. Reports must show the denominator and exclusions by arm. The published 2012 analysis described four primary outcome measures—single-word reading (`W`), letter-sound knowledge (`L`), phoneme blending (`B`) and taught expressive vocabulary (`TE`). This reanalysis makes `W` its single headline primary and treats the remaining outcomes as a family. That is an explicit **reanalysis hierarchy**, not the original trial's outcome hierarchy; both must be stated when interpreting multiplicity or comparing conclusions with the publication.

**Word-reading missing-data sensitivity.** `lrp-rli-itt-010` remains the model of record: it fits the 53 observed t2 word-reading outcomes using the t1 79-item word-reading baseline and t1 age. Its release additionally requires a trace-backed secondary sub-fit based on the complete screening word-reading (30-item) and screening-age profiles recovered for all 57 randomised children from the checksum-pinned UK Data Service archive (DOI [10.5255/UKDA-SN-852291](https://doi.org/10.5255/UKDA-SN-852291)). The companion's intercept prior is `Normal(mean all-57 pre-randomisation screening-W logit, 1)`, so its outcome level is anchored without using a t2 outcome; 1,000 prior and prior-predictive draws and their estimand-scale check are persisted with the trace. The secondary first reports a common-profile bridge estimate over the same 53 observed-outcome profiles, making the change of baseline instrument and timing visible, then standardises both arm surfaces over all 57 profiles under missing at random. Here MAR means that outcome observation is independent of the unseen t2 score conditional on assigned arm, screening word reading and screening age, together with the fitted logit Beta-Binomial outcome model and covariate overlap; this identifying assumption cannot be tested by the posterior-predictive check. The reference and delta analyses deliberately switch to factual randomised-arm completion: they compare predicted outcomes over the 29 assigned-intervention profiles with those over the 28 assigned-control profiles and modify only missing outcomes in their assigned arm. The zero-delta cell is the factual-arm MAR anchor. The no-benefit restriction gives the documented intervention non-starter the control mean surface; it is not classical distributional reference-based multiple imputation. The complete fixed item-shift grid `{-8, -4, 0, +4, +8}` in each arm applies to the one intervention and three control profiles with missing t2 word reading. Those step sizes are a deliberately broad diagnostic stress test, not an empirically calibrated distribution. The grid shifts are identifying assumptions, not estimated missingness parameters, and any clipping at the 0/79 test bounds is reported. With only four unavailable W outcomes, a fitted response-selection model or arm-specific missing-not-at-random parameters would be overwhelmingly prior-driven. The MAR, reference and delta scenarios therefore remain adjacent to the model-free sharp bounds, and no favourable scenario may be released alone. The external source is supplied at run time rather than redistributed because its ReShare item-level licence is blank. The importer and loader jointly verify the pinned ZIP and CSV checksums plus a privacy-preserving 71-field reconciliation with the repository. The gitignored local raw CSV retains the upstream source identifiers; returned model data and emitted artefacts omit them, and no source-to-repository crosswalk is persisted.

**Fit.** NUTS via `nutpie`, with `dev` / `test` / `reporting` sampling presets (the last is 6 chains × 6000 draws); `--target-accept` overrides when a funnel needs it.

**Evaluate.** Check computation _before_ reading any estimate. The automatic clean-pass gate requires **0 divergences, BFMI ≥ 0.3, R-hat ≤ 1.01 and bulk/tail ESS ≥ 400**, evaluated unrounded over the model's free random variables plus the headline scalars. The pipeline records that measured verdict in `diagnostics_summary.json` and renders it before any estimate. Any R-hat, ESS or BFMI failure, or any unreviewed divergence, withholds findings. This is only the sampling gate: `pareto_k.csv` separately flags importance-sampled leave-one-out points above ArviZ's reliability threshold and makes that predictive score unreliable. In single-period ITT/joint models, where each point is one child, `scripts/influence_sensitivity.py` performs the required direct treatment-effect refit without all flagged children before a robustness claim. It standardises the full and leave-out posteriors over the same retained children, so the headline refit shift is not contaminated by changing the covariate-averaging population; the smaller composition shift and the total full-sample-to-leave-out shift are reported separately. This checks whether the treatment estimate is sensitive to those children; it does not repair or replace the unreliable LOO score. In repeated-measures random-intercept families, each point is a child-by-period row conditional on that child's fitted intercept; use an observation-level sensitivity or exact/moment-matched LOO for that same predictive target, not the whole-child ITT/joint runner. Prior- and posterior-predictive checks assess whether the working likelihood reproduces the outcome by arm, baseline band and distribution shape; a material shape flag remains a qualification even when convergence passes. The 1,000 prior draws are persisted onto `trace.nc` so the report can show the prior-predictive check, the prior-vs-posterior overlay, the estimand-scale prior pushforward and power-scaling sensitivity without refitting.

**Divergent-transition qualification.** A divergence warns that Hamiltonian Monte Carlo may have missed posterior mass; its count or percentage alone cannot establish safety. Zero therefore remains the only automatic clean pass. A reporting-tier fit with a genuinely small absolute number of divergences may exceptionally be labelled **QUALIFIED, NOT PASSED** only after a trace- and estimand-specific review; it is never converted to `diagnostics_summary.passed = true`. The Stan diagnostic guidance likewise says that a few divergences with otherwise healthy diagnostics can sometimes be adequate after careful investigation, but cannot be assumed harmless ([Stan Development Team](https://mc-stan.org/learn-stan/diagnostics-warnings.html); Betancourt 2017, DOI [10.48550/arXiv.1701.02434](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.02434)). Qualification requires divergences to be the only failed check; a documented geometry-remediation attempt and independent-seed reporting run; stable named headline summaries within Monte Carlo uncertainty; and a diagnostic run that deliberately generates enough transitions from the same problematic geometry to map their relationship to those estimands and their stochastic ancestors. A visually unpatterned handful and agreement between seeds that might both miss the same region are not positive evidence of safety. The review must be content-addressed and name the trace, config, data, source, sampling contract, permitted outputs, reviewer, owner, issue and date. Deleting divergent draws is not evidence about posterior mass the sampler never reached. Any causal or model-of-record treatment effect, mediation decomposition, floor/survival estimand, nonlinear knee or shape, dose-heterogeneity slope, horseshoe ranking, covariance or latent-structure quantity remains zero-divergence-only. Qualified fits are excluded from model comparisons unless their predictive geometry is separately reviewed, and qualification never propagates to sensitivity or leave-one-out refits. The full policy and its supersession of the earlier percentage guidance and permanent model-spec waiver are recorded in `notes/202608021625-divergence-qualification-policy.md`; until a trace-bound verifier is implemented and a fit-specific review is approved, every divergent fit fails closed.

**Reading diagnostics — always via `sampling_quality`.** Never re-derive R-hat, ESS, BFMI or divergences from a trace by hand, in pipeline code or in a one-off script: call `statistical_models.sampling_quality.sampling_quality(trace, var_names=…)`, which returns the four signals unrounded and correctly coerced. Hand-rolled extraction has gone wrong twice for the same reason. `az.summary()` rounds to `rcParams["stats.round_to"]` (`"2g"`, two significant figures) unless passed `round_to="none"` — the **string**; `round_to=None` and `"auto"` both fall through to the rounded default, and omitting the argument returns a _string_-dtype frame that raises on float formatting. Rounding erases exactly the digits the gate turns on: across the whole gate-relevant range **every R-hat from 1.011 to 1.049 rounds to `1.0`**, so an `R-hat ≤ 1.01` test silently becomes `R-hat < 1.05` (dseinternational/research#65; found again in #440 in the exact-LOO-refit gate, which had been gating refits on rounded values since #438, and in a prototype script that reported four fits as `1.0000` when they were 1.0011–1.0022). ESS must be the minimum of `ess_bulk` and `ess_tail`, as the gate is defined, not `ess_bulk` alone. The helper deliberately does not decide pass/fail — call sites differ in which variables they gate over and in how they treat a missing BFMI — so those policies stay at the call site while the extraction stays in one place.

**Compare.** PSIS-LOO via ArviZ: prefer the higher-`elpd` model only when the difference clears its standard error (`elpd_diff` against `dse`). The interaction models are tested against their own no-interaction baselines as clean nested comparisons; `scripts/compare_statistical_models.py` collects the cross-model views. For the families with a per-child random intercept (mechanism, gain-/level-factors, DiD, dose-response, LCSM, growth) the pointwise unit is a child × phase/period row and the LOO is **conditional** (within-child, one-row-out) — elpd scores predicting a held-out row given that child's own fitted intercept, not new-child prediction — so it is comparable only across models sharing the same grouping on the same rows (which the nested comparators do).

Two separate questions govern whether a nested contrast may be read, and the comparison CSVs record them in separate columns (#438). **Reliability** — `comparison_valid` — asks whether the number can be trusted at all: a comparison is refused whenever the models do not share ordered rows, or when importance sampling is unreliable (`pareto_k` above ArviZ's `good_k`). The HSGP-curve mechanism models each carry one or two such influential rows out of ~150, because the basis coefficients plus a child random intercept at this sample size make a single child-phase row pivotal for the curve near its own exposure value; the linear-mechanism pairs carry none. Those points are repaired by **exact refit** (`reloo`, at most `_RELOO_MAX_REFITS` per model), and `loo_method` / `n_exact_refits` record that the repair happened rather than leaving a reader to infer it from a silently-valid row. **Discrimination** — `elpd_verdict` — is the different question of whether a trustworthy number separates the models, and follows the standing `|elpd_diff| < 4` rule. A nested pair differing by one regularised coefficient sits near the floor of what predictive comparison can resolve at n ≈ 54, so **an inconclusive contrast is the expected outcome and is a pass, not a failed comparison**: it is recorded and reported as "LOO does not distinguish these models at this sample size", and the scientific claim rests on the coefficient's own posterior (median, 50 %/89 % intervals, direction probability, ROPE) in the usual way. Do not read the `|elpd_diff| / dse` ratio in that regime — when two models are near-identical pointwise the standard error is itself unreliable, and a ratio above 5 on a difference of ~1 elpd says the models agree, not that one wins.

**Interpret.** Report the **posterior**, not a point estimate: the **median** (preferred over the mean — it is transformation-invariant across the logit and probability scales) with its **89 % equal-tailed credible interval**. A highest posterior density interval (HPDI) at the same coverage is reported alongside as a per-scale **sensitivity** check — useful when a bounded-scale posterior is skewed — but not as the primary interval, since the HPDI is not transformation-invariant. Posterior bands follow a fixed **two-band** convention (issue #177, revised 2026-07-17; `notes/202607172359-credible-interval-standard.md`): the **equal-tailed 89 % credible interval** is the headline uncertainty summary and the **central 50 %** interval (the middle half of the posterior mass) is a visual aid, not a decision threshold — both shown alongside the full posterior. The **89 %** outer coverage is deliberate, not 95 %: 95 % is an arbitrary convention carried over from frequentist NHST, and its 2.5 / 97.5 % limits are the noisiest quantiles to estimate from a finite MCMC sample, whereas an equal-tailed 89 % interval (5.5 / 94.5 %) is more stable per effective draw (Kruschke 2021 BARG, [doi:10.1038/s41562-021-01177-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01177-7): _"For reasonably stable estimates of limits of highest-density intervals (HDIs), I recommend that ESS ≥ 10,000. For stable estimates of limits of equal-tailed intervals, ESS can be lower."_). This is a per-effective-draw efficiency point, not low attained ESS — the reporting fits reach a Tail-ESS in the low tens of thousands on the headline terms, and the standard Tail-ESS is calibrated to the 5 %/95 % quantiles so it is the near-exact diagnostic for the 89 % limits (`notes/202607181200-ess-reporting-standard.md`). The earlier equal-tailed **90 %** "sensitivity" band is **retired**: a deliberately non-round 89 % headline plus the full posterior already discourage the bright-line reading that band existed to blunt. Read direction for the reported intervention effect from `P(AME > 0)`, the posterior probability that the fitted-sample average marginal effect is positive, not from whether a 50 % or 89 % band excludes zero. The secondary `P(τ_logit > 0)` describes the conditional logit coefficient; it has the same sign in a constant-effect model because the inverse-logit is monotone, but it can differ when the treatment effect is moderated across children. There are no p-values. Distinguish two questions: _direction_ — `P(AME > 0)` — and _magnitude_ — `P(benefit ≥ δ)` against an education-lead-approved minimally-important difference δ (a region of practical equivalence). δ is a **post-hoc** MID — the primary rule was chosen after inspecting fitted posteriors and its candidates' effect on the headline claim was tabulated — so it is always reported with a **mandatory δ-sensitivity sweep** (e.g. the 10/15/20 pp risk-difference grid on the floored outcomes) rather than rested on a single leniently-selected threshold. A high direction probability can sit on a practically negligible effect. Give both the logit scale (the natural parameter) and the probability / items scale as an **average marginal effect** over the fitted sample's observed covariate profiles (not at a single constructed sample-mean baseline). An 89 % credible interval that includes zero means the sign is not pinned down — avoid a decisive directional claim, and report where the posterior mass sits (both sides of zero) rather than collapsing to "significant / not". At this study's sample size a significant-looking point estimate is on average magnitude-inflated (a Type-M / winner's-curse effect), so lead with the interval, not the point. **Evidence labels** (inconclusive / suggestive / moderate / strong / very strong; issue #179) qualify the evidence for a **named claim** — "moderate evidence that the intervention helps", never "a moderate effect" — are reported _after_ the probability and odds, and are oriented to the **favoured direction**, so a clearly negative effect is reported as evidence of harm, not as inconclusive. See `notes/202606261304-evidence-strength-and-rope-reporting.md`.

## Causal interpretation and its limits

**What is causal:** within the randomised window, assignment licenses a causal treatment contrast for the assigned cohort under the causal model; baseline floor status can define a pre-randomisation subgroup. Once the analysis is restricted to archived children, observed outcomes or complete cases, calling the fitted contrast causal additionally requires the available-case selection assumption stated above. The same conditions apply to the waitlist-crossover model's immediate-minus-waitlist t2 contrast, `tau_t2`, because it uses the same randomised comparison; agreement is not independent evidence because the models share data. The licence does not apply to `arm_gap_t3`, `delta_crossover`, dose or heterogeneity quantities. Randomisation balances arms in expectation at assignment, but it does not repair exclusions or post-randomisation missing outcomes, and the effect for all 57 children needs the further missing-data assumptions and sensitivity stated above. The own-baseline term is in an ITT model for _precision_, not missing-data identification—say so when writing up an ITT result, and remember that its finite-sample prior and functional form can still influence the numerical posterior for τ.

**What is not:** every association we did not randomise — the `gamma_cross` "baseline X predicts later Y" terms, the mechanism `f_mech` slope, and the mediator → outcome arrow. These have cross-lagged-panel structure and can be inflated by a stable trait shared across measures or by ordinary measurement noise (which alone can manufacture a spurious "earlier → later" path). The mechanism models already include the recommended repair — a per-child random intercept, but two leaks remain: the mechanism is measured at the _same wave_ as the outcome, and we do not separately model measurement error. So report these as _adjusted associations_, never as "X drives Y".

**The mechanism family's declared natural-scale estimand (#602).** The family used to publish two different natural-scale answers to the same question in the same report — a full-observed-range contrast that averaged fitted probabilities across rows, and an interquartile worked example computed for a constructed "typical child" — over different intervals and under different standardisations, with nothing saying which was the headline. On `lrp-rli-mech-058` those were +6.6 and +2.3 items. There is now **one** headline, and every artefact computes it: the predicted outcome difference between the **25th and 75th percentile of the fitted exposure** (`items_ref_quantiles`, per-model configurable), **standardised over the fitted rows** — each row keeps its own period, covariates, autoregressive baseline and **its child's own fitted random intercept**, and only the exposure is moved; any moderator is held at its standardised mean. Formally `y(x) = N * mean_i expit(eta_base[i] + f_i(x))`, so the answer is "averaged over the children actually analysed", not "for a typical child", and probabilities are averaged after the link rather than the link applied to an average. The full observed range is retained as an explicitly labelled **secondary** contrast, because the observed extremes are order statistics that move with a single child and sit exactly where an HSGP curve is least constrained. `mechanism_summary.csv` carries both rows with a machine-readable `estimand` on each (headline first), `mechanism_curve_items.csv` plots that same standardised quantity so the worked-example points lie on the curve, `key_findings.json` records the headline under `headline_estimand`, and the cross-model `mechanism_forest.csv` reports each model's secant across the same declared interval per SD of the exposure logit — which for a linear fit is exactly `beta_mech`. Both scales of the steepest-interval statistic are published: the latent-logit interval (`scale`) and, under the same reference population, the **expected-items** interval (`items_*`), because `dE[y]/dx = N p(1-p) d(eta)/dx` can peak elsewhere and "where do items rise fastest?" is a different question from "where does the logit rise fastest?".

**Reading a blend, a between coefficient and a within coefficient (#603).** A single exposure coefficient fitted over a child random intercept is a **precision-weighted blend** of two associations that answer different questions: _between_ children (do children whose average exposure is higher score higher?) and _within_ a child (when their own exposure moves, does their outcome move with it?). The random intercept does not separate them — it models repeated-measures dependence under an independence assumption, is not permitted to correlate with the exposure, and does not decompose the exposure — so the weights come from the variance ratio, not the question. Where a fit declares `decompose_between_within` the two are estimated separately (`beta_between`, `beta_within`, the Mundlak split `pooled_levels` already uses) and published in `mechanism_slope_summary.csv` with the question each answers. **Read a blend as neither**: quote it only when the between and within estimates are close, and otherwise quote the one that matches the claim. A within-child coefficient removes _stable_ between-child confounding, including the stable part of latent general ability; it does **not** make the exposure temporally prior to the outcome (both are still same-wave) and does not remove time-varying confounding or reverse causation. Neither coefficient is causal.

**Reading a phase-varying slope (#604).** The family stacks the randomised t1→t2 transition with two post-crossover ones under one common exposure slope. Where a fit declares `phase_varying_slope` that slope is estimated per period, partially pooled toward a shared mean (`mu_mech`, spread `sigma_mech_phase`), with a nested PSIS-LOO comparison against the pooled comparator and the per-period exposure support published beside it. A difference between periods is **evidence against pooling, not evidence that the relationship changed over time**: the periods differ in age, treatment history and measurement position at once, only the first is randomised-arm-clean, and the exposure is randomised in none of them.

**Characterising non-responders — the honest ladder.** The intuitive recipe — classify children into responders and non-responders on their observed change, then compare the groups' characteristics — manufactures a subgroup from noise and finds predictors of that artefact (Senn 2016, doi:10.1002/sim.7059). LRPDID13 is retained only as an **exploratory heterogeneity sensitivity**, not as a clean test of whether treatment-response variance exists. Only waitlist children change treatment status, each supplies one untreated-to-treated transition, and immediate-arm random intercepts and treatment slopes are not separately identified from their rows; consequently, the variance component can also absorb heterogeneous maturation, period shocks and measurement noise. Any subsequent moderation must therefore be one-stage, strongly regularised, limited to pre-randomisation moderators and interpreted as exploratory. The observable companion question — who is at persistent low attainment on the floored phonics measures — is answered descriptively and with the floor-sitter survival family (`lrp-rli-surv-009`/`011`), not by a latent non-responder label.

## Reporting results

Write for a numerate reader who knows frequentist statistics but is newer to Bayesian methods — picture a science undergraduate. Lead with the finding and its uncertainty, then the "so what"; keep narratives as short as the content allows.

- **Translate the Bayesian parts.** Expand shorthand on first use (τ = conditional logit-scale treatment coefficient, AME = fitted-sample average marginal treatment effect, `gamma_own` = baseline coupling, `f_mech` = mechanism curve). Read an equal-tailed 89% credible interval in plain words — "given the model and data, the value lies in [a, b] with 89% probability" — prefer a posterior tail probability for the named estimand (`P(AME > 0) = 0.97` for the intervention-help claim) to anything that reads like a p-value, and restate the sign convention wherever τ appears.
- **Pair every estimate with its uncertainty** — never a bare posterior mean or a bare importance rank.
- **Avoid verbal evidence labels ("strong", "moderate") wherever possible** — report the odds and probabilities and let them speak. Where prose forces a label, append the word _evidence_ and name the claim ("strong evidence that the intervention helps"), show the odds beside it, and use the round-odds ladder in `notes/202606261304-evidence-strength-and-rope-reporting.md` — run separately for _direction_ (`pd`) and _meaningful benefit_ (`P(benefit ≥ δ)`). A label must never connote effect _size_.
- **Cite primary sources, verify them before committing them to text, and always include a DOI** when one exists.

## Conventions

- **No outlier exclusion** unless justified on the model page.
- **`GroupKFold` by `subject_id`** wherever longitudinal leakage would otherwise inflate performance.
- **Mechanism-model β_G is not a direct-effect estimate** — both arms are on intervention during phases 1 and 2, so the pooled β_G averages over phases where group is no longer a treatment contrast (documented in each mechanism report).
- **RLI intervention scope** — the intervention targets vocabulary and grammar as well as reading, so null effects on receptive/expressive vocabulary are substantive findings, not by-design predictions.
- **Decisions a future reader might question get a dated note in `notes/`** before they leave your head — the question, the choice, the rationale, the result. Mark AI-drafted notes with a `> [!NOTE]` admonition naming the tool and model (per the AI-authorship rule in `CLAUDE.md`).

## Glossary

- **Posterior** — the distribution of a parameter after combining prior and data; summarised by a mean/median, a credible interval, and tail probabilities.
- **Credible interval (CrI)** — a range holding the parameter with stated probability _given the model and data_ (the reading people wrongly attach to a confidence interval).
- **Posterior tail probability** — e.g. `P(AME > 0)` or `P(τ_logit > 0)`, the posterior mass for a named estimand or parameter on one side of a value; it must say which quantity is being evaluated.
- **Probability of direction (`pd`)** — the posterior mass in the favoured direction for the named quantity. The ITT headline uses `P(AME > 0)`; `P(τ_logit > 0)` is a secondary coefficient read and can differ under moderation. It answers direction, not magnitude. For a symmetric posterior `p ≈ 2(1 − pd)`, so treating a `pd` threshold as a discovery rule would merely disguise a p-value threshold.
- **ROPE / minimally-important difference (δ)** — a band [−δ, +δ] around zero wide enough that an effect inside it is practically equivalent to none (the frequentist SESOI / clinical MID); `P(|effect| < δ)` is its coverage, `P(effect > δ)` the probability of a meaningful benefit. δ is set to **half the period's natural-maturation gain** (floored at 1 item) and was signed off by the education lead after the initial results had been examined (issue #144), so for the analyses fitted to date it is a transparent post-hoc reanalysis judgement rather than a prospectively specified threshold. On 2026-08-19 the rule was confirmed as the project's **standing** practical-difference rule (`notes/202608191130-practical-difference-rule-confirmed.md`): a new outcome's δ is derived from the waiting-list arm's untreated period-1 gain by the rule and recorded in `measures.ROPE_DELTA` before the outcome is fitted, so from that point the threshold is pre-specified. Each ITT report also carries a **δ-sensitivity table** giving every outcome's `P(benefit ≥ δ)` at its adopted δ and a stricter δ (word reading at δ = 1 and 2; the floored outcomes at 10 / 15 / 20 pp), so the meaningful-benefit claim's robustness to the δ choice is explicit rather than hidden in a single number.
- **Type-S / Type-M error** — among effects declared "significant", the rate of wrong _sign_ (S) and the average _magnitude_ exaggeration (M); both worsen at low power.
- **τ (tau)** — the randomised-window assigned-arm contrast in the fitted analysis sample (positive ⇒ intervention arm higher); reading it as a fitted-sample causal effect requires the available-case selection assumption above, and extending it to all 57 randomised children requires further missing-data assumptions and sensitivity.
- **`tau_t2`** — the waitlist-crossover arm-by-wave model's immediate-minus-waitlist contrast at t2; randomised and causal under the same selection and missing-data assumptions as the corresponding available-case modified ITT estimate, but not independent evidence.
- **`delta_crossover`** — `tau_t2 - arm_gap_t3`, the modelled closure of the immediate-minus-waitlist arm gap after crossover; a post-crossover association, not a second treatment effect.
- **`gamma_own` / `gamma_cross`** — a measure's own-baseline coupling / its cross-baseline couplings, on the logit scale.
- **`f_mech`** — the nonparametric dose-response curve in a mechanism model.
- **ITT (intention-to-treat)** — analyse every randomised child in the arm assigned, regardless of adherence. The current suite preserves assigned arm but starts from 54 of the 57 randomised children and then applies model-specific observed-data requirements, so it is described as available-case modified ITT rather than full ITT; causal interpretation of the resulting selected-sample contrast is conditional on ignorable archive inclusion and outcome observation.
- **Mediation / NDE / NIE** — how much of an effect flows through a mediator (natural direct / natural indirect effect). Here these name the _functionals the software computes_, not identified causal effects: under this study's DAG the natural effects are not identified (latent general ability, plus exposure-induced confounding by dose `IS`). See the Mediation family above.
- **IDE / IIE** — the **interventional** direct / indirect effects (MED-078/186/187): the mediator is drawn from its arm-specific covariate-conditional distribution rather than a cross-world counterfactual. Weaker assumptions than NDE/NIE — the exposure-induced-confounding obstacle drops away — but still not identified here, since no-unmeasured-mediator–outcome-confounding is still required.
- **Exposure-induced (treatment-induced) confounder** — a variable caused by treatment that then confounds the mediator → outcome relation (here: dose `IS`). It blocks identification of natural direct/indirect effects, and adjusting for it does _not_ fix the problem.
- **PSIS-LOO** — Pareto-smoothed importance-sampling leave-one-out cross-validation, the Bayesian model-comparison score (`elpd`).
- **HSGP** — Hilbert-space approximation to a Gaussian process; a fast smooth nonlinear term.
- **Regression to the mean** — extreme scores drift toward the average on retest; visible here as `gamma_own` below 1.
