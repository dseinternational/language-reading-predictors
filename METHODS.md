# Methods

See [README.md](README.md) for the project introduction and [the model catalogue](docs/models/README.md) for registered analyses.

## Overview

We use two processes for exploring predictors of language and reading outcomes:

1. **Gradient boosting** ranks variables by their contribution to prediction.
2. **Bayesian models** estimate treatment contrasts and adjusted associations with uncertainty. The study design and assumptions determine which contrasts can be interpreted causally.

## 1. Gradient-boosting models

**Purpose.** Rank predictors by how much they help predict observations held out from fitting. Describe the direction and consistency of their predictive associations.

**Fit and cross-validate.** LightGBM with a Huber objective, `GroupKFold` by `subject_id`. The Huber threshold is a per-model constant, 1.345 times a robust scale estimate of the target (1.4826 × the median absolute deviation, or the mean absolute deviation from the median when more than half the rows sit at the floor), so residuals within it are fitted as under squared error and larger ones as under absolute error. Fixed-threshold Huber predictions need not equal conditional means for skewed or floored outcomes. RMSE scoring and RMSE-based permutation importance assess squared-error performance of those fitted predictions. Permutations align assessment waves and restrict donor children to the same observed-wave schedule. Importance is therefore conditional on that schedule; unique schedules cannot move, and `permutation_schedule_support.csv` records support. The [objective-sensitivity check](notes/202609221700-gb-objective-sensitivity.md) records why Huber replaced the earlier MAE policy. One module per problem; level and gain targets are separate models. Because hyperparameters are tuned on the same grouped folds (see **Tune**), the fit pipeline's cross-validation metrics — including the pooled out-of-fold R² — are **post-selection internal cross-validation** numbers, not independent generalisation estimates; `metrics.json` records this as `cv_post_selection: true`.

**Tune.** `scripts/tune_model.py` runs an Optuna TPE study under `GroupKFold`. Each training fold uses an inner `GroupShuffleSplit` for early stopping, keeping the outer validation fold out of that decision. The mean best iteration sets `n_estimators`. Hyperparameters are still selected on the grouped validation folds, so later cross-validation on those folds is post-selection. Results go to `output/tuning/{model_id}/`; applying them requires a manual, reviewable registry edit.

**Rank predictors.** The gradient-boosting layer no longer performs hard feature selection. Each model fits the full default predictor set for its target family and treats the ordered, clustered ranking as the deliverable. Per-fit diagnostics — Spearman correlation, a distance-correlation dendrogram, a mutual-information heatmap, and importance pairing — guard against over-reading a single predictor that is collinear with another. Gain-model defaults exclude intervention-period process measures (`attend`, `tascore`, `tachang`) so the ranking is about baseline/state predictors of progress rather than variables observed during the gain interval.

**Interpret.** Read the SHAP beeswarm (`output/models/{model_id}/shap_summary.png`) alongside permutation importance. Importance ranks predictive contributions; the beeswarm shows their direction and consistency. For each leading predictor, check whether low (blue) and high (red) values separate across zero, whether the pattern is monotonic, and whether tails or similarly ranked predictors point in another direction. State direction explicitly. Predictive importance for a `_GAIN` or `_NEXT` variable does not establish causation. In gain models `time` identifies the transition, so it can absorb the transitions' unequal lengths (see **Assessment intervals**); its importance mixes interval length with programme stage, treatment status and age.

## 2. Bayesian statistical models

**Undergraduate route.** The [model-methods chapter](docs/report/chapters/methods-models.qmd) follows `lrp-rli-itt-001` from its question and DAG through data, equations, priors, sampling, marginal effects and checks. It includes synthetic examples and a misinterpretations checklist. The [glossary](docs/report/glossary.qmd) assumes no prior Bayesian training.

**Specification contract.** Every registered family declares immutable typed settings. Before creating a fit context or reading data, `resolve_<family>_run_plan()` rejects unknown keys, mixed typed/legacy declarations and contradictory choices, then fills defaults. Check settings-only constraints here, before the factory runs or output is reset.

The family uses this plan for loader arguments, adjustment terms, factory arguments and diagnostic variables. ITT also uses it for analysis-set audits, predictive calibration, influence refits and metadata. Each fit saves `resolved_run_plan` in `config.json` and writes `model_recipe.md`, an introductory account of the question, rows, likelihood, treatment contrast, non-causal terms and checks. See [the architecture guide](AGENTS.md#statistical-models-statistical_models) for module ownership.

**Replication gates and publication transaction.** Every fit writes to a hidden same-filesystem staging directory. The final report stage promotes that complete directory to `output/statistical_models/models/{model_id}-{config}/`; a failed refit leaves the previous successful publication intact rather than deleting it or mixing old and partial artefacts. Each published directory includes `environment-lock.json`, a deterministic snapshot of the resolved `uv.lock` digest, every installed Python distribution version and direct-install source metadata, referenced by filename and SHA-256 digest from `config.json`. CI runs a fixed-seed real PyMC/nutpie posterior-sampling smoke test, an incremental mypy gate over the typed specification/reporting boundaries, and a registry-derived documentation-count check in addition to the deterministic unit tests.

**Execution and release.** Family code chooses the rows, equation and summaries. Models import their owning `pipelines/` entry point directly; the CLI imports only selected models. `stages.py` supplies shared execution, and `subfits.py` samples secondary and sensitivity fits and records their convergence, fitted rows and settings in `subfit_provenance.csv`.

`release/` checks inputs, sampling, required artefacts and treatment-effect robustness in that order. It writes `release_decision.json` before generating findings. `definitions.KINDS` and the [model catalogue](docs/models/README.md) list all families; the summaries below describe eight principal designs.

| Design                      | Question and interpretation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Available-case modified ITT | What is the assigned-arm difference at the end of the randomised period? Baseline and age improve precision. Exclusions and missing outcomes still require the assumptions below.                                                                                                   |
| Joint outcomes              | How do assigned-arm differences compare across outcomes? Parent models factorise by outcome. Registered dependence companions add within-child residual covariance. Compare natural-scale effects within posterior draws and inspect the covariance diagnostics.                    |
| Mechanism                   | How does one measured skill or exposure relate to another after adjustment? These curves remain associations under latent-ability confounding.                                                                                                                                      |
| Mediation                   | How does a fitted model divide the outcome contrast into direct and indirect components? These are model-based decompositions, not identified causal pathways in this study.                                                                                                        |
| Waitlist crossover (`did`)  | How do arm gaps differ at t1, t2 and t3? `tau_t2` is the adjusted t2 gap in levels. The later gap compares randomised treatment schedules. Its change does not identify catch-up. Dose variants are observational.                                                                  |
| Gain factors                | How does the period-end score relate to the starting score, treatment and other covariates? The treatment headline is averaged over period 1. Later periods inform the shared model, so the period-1-only sensitivity is required. Covariate and moderation terms are associations. |
| Level factors               | How do adjusted score levels and arm gaps change across waves? With the default t1 reference, `d_grp_time[t2]` is the randomised change in the gap. Later changes compare randomised schedules. Covariate terms are associations.                                                   |
| Aligned windows             | How do outcomes compare over windows aligned to intervention onset? Age, timing and unequal window lengths confound the cohort contrast, so every term is an association. Aligning windows equalises neither dose nor elapsed time.                                                 |

The [catalogue](docs/models/README.md) gives each model's equation, adjustment set, comparison models and interpretation limits. "Factors" in the gain and level families means observed predictors. These regressions include child random effects, but they do not estimate latent skill factors.

**Floor rule.** Phonetic spelling and nonword reading have many zero scores. Their ITT headline uses a Bernoulli model for moving off zero among children observed at the baseline floor. The ≥40% post-score zero threshold was chosen after inspecting this trial's outcome distribution. The resulting risk difference is an exploratory, data-adaptive reanalysis quantity. Graded secondary analyses remain detection-limited and must be read with the per-arm mover counts. The gain family instead models off-floor post-score status with binary off-floor pre-score adjustment.

**Mediation limits.** Each leg conditions on one common vector of pre-exposure variables. Count mediators are integrated over their complete finite support; normal mediators use checked numerical quadrature. The reported Monte Carlo error describes posterior sampling precision, not integration error.

Latent general ability confounds the mediator–outcome relationship. Intervention dose is also a treatment-induced mediator–outcome confounder. Randomisation therefore does not identify the natural direct and indirect effects, and measuring dose alone does not resolve that obstacle (VanderWeele, Vansteelandt and Robins, 2014, DOI [10.1097/EDE.0000000000000034](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000000034)). Interventional targets avoid the cross-world requirement, but still require no unmeasured mediator–outcome confounding (Hejazi et al., 2022, DOI [10.1093/biostatistics/kxac002](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxac002)). Report both as model-based decompositions under the stated assumptions.

**Likelihood and priors.** A Beta-Binomial likelihood is used as a **working model** for each bounded total score via a logit linear predictor. It respects the score bounds and permits extra-binomial variation, but it should not be read as a literal claim that every test item is an exchangeable Bernoulli trial: heterogeneous item difficulty, stopping rules and floor or ceiling compression can violate that interpretation. Posterior-predictive checks must therefore assess the full score distribution, including zero and ceiling mass, by arm and relevant baseline strata; material mismatch triggers a likelihood sensitivity analysis. Priors come from shared constructors in `priors.py` (so the factories cannot drift apart); smooth nonlinear terms use Hilbert-space Gaussian-process approximations (`hsgp.py`).

**The phoneme-blending guessing floor binds every `B` fit, in every family (#608).** The requirement above follows from the _link_, not from the estimand: the ordinary inverse-logit score mean permits expected scores anywhere in (0, 1), while ten three-alternative forced-choice items put a child answering at random at about 3.3 out of 10. Under the assumed random-guessing mechanism, expected performance below chance is inappropriate for both randomised contrasts and adjusted associations. The policy therefore covers **every registered model with `outcome_symbol="B"`**, and the release gate is keyed on that symbol rather than on the model's family — a new `B` model in any family fails closed until it is paired or carries a recorded, dated exemption.

The [scope decision](notes/202608242000-blending-guessing-floor-scope-608.md) records the fitted evidence behind this rule. It applies to natural-scale associations as well as treatment contrasts because both depend on the score link. Matching latent-scale coefficient priors can imply different score-scale priors under the two links, so show both prior distributions of the reported contrast.

**Blending-pair evidence.** The [signed-off amendment to #608](notes/202608252100-blending-pair-binding-608-decision-2.md) keeps the content-addressed archive ITT-only. Other families use stored-artefact checks. Every pair must have matching resolved plans apart from the score-mean link and its pairing bookkeeping, and each stored plan must match its current specification. Record and show differences in source commit, dirty flag and environment lock; these need not match. Both evidence tiers enforce pairing.

**Prior-setting protocol.** Use scale-calibrated priors with a stated role and rationale. Check their implications for the actual probability, score or other reported quantity, not only for a coefficient. Record external sources, expert judgement, measurement limits and any use of these trial outcomes. A prior adopted after reviewing results remains a post-data choice even if a later sensitivity analysis supports it.

Every free variable has a descriptor recorded when the factory builds it. The fit's `priors_table.csv` and density panels describe the actual distribution, including overrides. The [priors guide](docs/models/PRIORS.md) explains the current inventory, treatment-prior tiers and checks. The [original audit](notes/202607011600-issue-141-prior-audit.md) preserves the decision timeline.

**Dispersion priors.** The Beta-Binomial concentration `kappa = alpha + beta` controls variation beyond the Binomial model. Its variance multiplier is `(kappa + n) / (kappa + 1)`, where `n` is the number of items. A multiplier within 10% of one requires `kappa >= 10(n - 1) - 1`: 779 for 79 items and 1689 for 170 items. `HalfNormal(50)` assigns negligible probability to those values. It therefore strongly favours extra variation for these outcomes, although it does not impose a strict lower bound on that variation.

A prior on `1/sqrt(kappa)` instead permits appreciable probability near the Binomial limit. The `itt`, `level_factors`, `did` and `mechanism` settings support this alternative; the level family uses it by default. Registered companions assess sensitivity in the crossover and mechanism families. Read `dispersion_summary.csv` for the prior, posterior, variance multiplier and probability of being within 10% of Binomial variance. Changing this prior can affect both interval width and coefficient estimates. Do not assume their signs will remain unchanged. The [dispersion decision](notes/202608241400-mechanism-batch-c-602-605.md) gives the calibration and comparisons.

For the post-hoc P/N floor-transition estimands, a power-scaling conflict for `tau` triggers an explicit estimand-matched sensitivity grid rather than the ordinary graded-outcome sweep: the same observed-baseline-floor subgroup and Bernoulli off-floor likelihood are fitted under `tau ~ Normal(0, 0.5/1.0/1.5)` with the linear-age precision term both present and absent. All six fits must pass the all-free-variable convergence gate and retain content-addressed traces. The release evaluator binds each trace to its exact prior/age cell, data, analysis set, primary fit, free-variable set and sampling contract, then recomputes convergence and every reported effect summary from the draws; a self-declared CSV flag is not sufficient. Reports show the full risk-difference and `P(benefit)` range; completing the grid is a computational release gate, not evidence that the result is prior-insensitive.

**Sign convention:** `G = 1` is the immediate-intervention arm after the `2 − group` recode (`group == 1` = immediate intervention, `group == 2` = wait-list control), so **positive τ means the fitted intervention-arm outcome is higher**; interpreting that contrast as an effect of intervention requires the selection and missing-data assumptions below.

**ITT analysis population and estimand hierarchy.** Burgoyne et al. (2012, DOI [10.1111/j.1469-7610.2012.02557.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2012.02557.x)) randomised 57 children: 29 to immediate intervention and 28 to waiting control. Its CONSORT diagram reports three post-randomisation losses to follow-up—one intervention child who moved school and two waiting-control children, one who moved school and one whose reason is recorded as “refused to participate in testing, school withdrawn”—leaving 28 and 26 children analysed. It separately reports that two children in each arm discontinued the intervention after moving school but were followed and retained in the analyses. The repository's analysis file contains those 54 analysed children, and each model then uses the children with the outcome and required baseline/covariates observed; the fitted sample is commonly 54, is 53 for outcomes with an unavailable t2 score, and is smaller for the baseline-floor subgroup estimands.

The original ANCOVAs used Mplus full-information maximum likelihood for the small amount of missing data within the reported analyses; that does not show that the three children lost to follow-up entered the paper's 54-child analysed cohort and is distinct from the current Bayesian complete-case restrictions. Assignment is always analysed as randomised for the observed children, including the four children who discontinued but were followed, but this sequence is `57 randomised → 3 lost to follow-up → 54 analysed and available in the repository → model-specific fitted sample`, so the current suite is an available-case modified ITT.

Randomisation establishes exchangeability at assignment for the full cohort; it does not automatically preserve exchangeability after loss to follow-up or further observed-data restrictions. Interpreting even the fitted-sample arm contrast causally therefore assumes that loss to follow-up, outcome observation and any additional complete-case restriction did not select children differently between arms according to their potential outcomes, after allowing for the modelled pre-treatment information. Interpreting that selected-sample contrast as the effect for all 57 randomised children requires further assumptions about the missing outcomes and an estimand-matched attrition sensitivity. The emitted extreme-case raw post-score bounds are a useful model-free benchmark, but they are neither the covariate-adjusted Bayesian average marginal effect nor a missing-at-random or missing-not-at-random model. Reports must show the denominator and exclusions by arm.

The published 2012 analysis described four primary outcome measures—single-word reading (`W`), letter-sound knowledge (`L`), phoneme blending (`B`) and taught expressive vocabulary (`TE`). This reanalysis makes `W` its single headline primary and treats the remaining outcomes as a family. That is an explicit **reanalysis hierarchy**, not the original trial's outcome hierarchy; both must be stated when interpreting multiplicity or comparing conclusions with the publication.

**Word-reading missing-data sensitivity.** `lrp-rli-itt-010` remains the model of record: it fits the 53 observed t2 word-reading outcomes using the t1 79-item word-reading baseline and t1 age. Its release additionally requires a trace-backed secondary sub-fit based on the complete screening word-reading (30-item) and screening-age profiles recovered for all 57 randomised children from the checksum-pinned UK Data Service archive (DOI [10.5255/UKDA-SN-852291](https://doi.org/10.5255/UKDA-SN-852291)). The companion's intercept prior is `Normal(mean all-57 pre-randomisation screening-W logit, 1)`, so its outcome level is anchored without using a t2 outcome; 1,000 prior and prior-predictive draws and their estimand-scale check are persisted with the trace.

The secondary first reports a common-profile bridge estimate over the same 53 observed-outcome profiles, making the change of baseline instrument and timing visible, then standardises both arm surfaces over all 57 profiles under missing at random. Here MAR means that outcome observation is independent of the unseen t2 score conditional on assigned arm, screening word reading and screening age, together with the fitted logit Beta-Binomial outcome model and covariate overlap; this identifying assumption cannot be tested by the posterior-predictive check.

The reference and delta analyses deliberately switch to factual randomised-arm completion: they compare predicted outcomes over the 29 assigned-intervention profiles with those over the 28 assigned-control profiles and modify only missing outcomes in their assigned arm. The zero-delta cell is the factual-arm MAR anchor. The no-benefit restriction gives the documented intervention non-starter the control mean surface; it is not classical distributional reference-based multiple imputation. The complete fixed item-shift grid `{-8, -4, 0, +4, +8}` in each arm applies to the one intervention and three control profiles with missing t2 word reading. Those step sizes are a deliberately broad diagnostic stress test, not an empirically calibrated distribution. The grid shifts are identifying assumptions, not estimated missingness parameters, and any clipping at the 0/79 test bounds is reported.

With only four unavailable W outcomes, a fitted response-selection model or arm-specific missing-not-at-random parameters would be overwhelmingly prior-driven. The MAR, reference and delta scenarios therefore remain adjacent to the model-free sharp bounds, and no favourable scenario may be released alone.

The archive is **committed to this repository** as `data/dse-rli-trial-data-archive.csv`, verbatim and under its upstream filename. Down Syndrome Education International deposited the ReShare collection and holds the rights in it, so it is distributed here under the repository's own CC BY 4.0 data licence; the earlier run-time-supply arrangement existed because the deposit's item-level licence field is blank, which constrains third parties rather than the depositor. The loader resolves that committed file by default, so a clean checkout runs this mandatory bundle with no extra step, and `--rli-randomised-archive` now overrides it for a local re-check rather than being required.

The loader verifies the pinned checksum and a privacy-preserving 71-field reconciliation with the repository on every use. It discards the archive's own published anonymised subject labels and generates internal row labels only after every allocation, screening and outcome-count assertion passes, so returned model data and emitted artefacts carry no source-to-repository subject-ID crosswalk. Incorporating the archive supplies pre-randomisation screening profiles for the three children lost to follow-up; because it holds no t1 or later measurement for them, it does **not** recover their outcomes and does not make any fit a full-cohort ITT analysis.

**Fit.** NUTS via `nutpie`, with `dev`, `test`, `rep-lite` and `reporting` presets. Reporting uses 6 chains × 6,000 draws. An explicit `--target-accept` overrides the model default and then the preset; diagnose sampling problems before choosing an override.

**Evaluate.** Check computation _before_ reading any estimate. The automatic clean-pass gate requires **0 divergences, BFMI ≥ 0.3, R-hat ≤ 1.01 and bulk/tail ESS ≥ 400**, evaluated unrounded over the model's free random variables plus the headline scalars. The pipeline records that measured verdict in `diagnostics_summary.json` and renders it before any estimate. Any R-hat, ESS or BFMI failure, or any unreviewed divergence, withholds findings.

This is only the sampling gate: `pareto_k.csv` separately flags importance-sampled leave-one-out points above ArviZ's reliability threshold and makes that predictive score unreliable.

In single-period ITT/joint models, where each point is one child, `scripts/influence_sensitivity.py` performs the required direct treatment-effect refit without all flagged children before a robustness claim. It standardises the full and leave-out posteriors over the same retained children, so the headline refit shift is not contaminated by changing the covariate-averaging population; the smaller composition shift and the total full-sample-to-leave-out shift are reported separately. This checks whether the treatment estimate is sensitive to those children; it does not repair or replace the unreliable LOO score.

Where LOO holds out a child-by-period row conditional on the child's fitted intercept, use a sensitivity or exact/moment-matched LOO for that same target. Other families hold out children; follow the declared plan rather than applying the ITT/joint runner to every repeated-measures fit.

Prior- and posterior-predictive checks assess whether the working likelihood reproduces the outcome by arm, baseline band and distribution shape; a material shape flag remains a qualification even when convergence passes. The 1,000 prior draws are persisted onto `trace.nc` so the report can show the prior-predictive check, the prior-vs-posterior overlay, the estimand-scale prior pushforward and power-scaling sensitivity without refitting.

**Divergent-transition qualification.** A divergence warns that the sampler may have missed part of the posterior. A small count or percentage does not establish safety. Zero divergences remains the only automatic clean pass. A reporting-tier fit can be labelled **QUALIFIED, NOT PASSED** only after a review of its exact trace and named quantities. Its `diagnostics_summary.passed` remains false. See the [Stan guidance](https://mc-stan.org/learn-stan/diagnostics-warnings.html) and Betancourt (2017), DOI [10.48550/arXiv.1701.02434](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.02434).

Qualification requires divergences to be the only failed check. The review must document an attempt to repair the sampling geometry, an independent-seed reporting run, stable headline summaries within Monte Carlo uncertainty, and a diagnostic run with enough divergent transitions to assess the affected regions. Agreement between seeds or removing divergent draws cannot establish that neither run missed posterior mass.

The content-addressed review must identify the trace, configuration, data, source, sampling contract, permitted outputs, reviewer, owner, issue and date. Causal or model-of-record treatment effects, mediation, floor/survival, nonlinear-shape, dose-heterogeneity, horseshoe-ranking, covariance and latent-structure results always require zero divergences. Qualification does not extend to sensitivity or leave-one-out refits. It permits model comparison only after a separate review of predictive geometry. Until a trace-bound verifier and fit-specific approval exist, every divergent fit fails closed. The [full policy](notes/202608021625-divergence-qualification-policy.md) replaces earlier percentage rules and permanent model waivers.

**Reading diagnostics.** Call `statistical_models.sampling_quality.sampling_quality(trace, var_names=…)` for unrounded R-hat, ESS, BFMI and divergence counts. Do not reimplement extraction in pipelines or one-off scripts. Rounded R-hat can conceal a gate failure: at two significant figures, 1.011 through 1.049 all display as `1.0`.

When using `az.summary` elsewhere, `round_to="none"` is the string that disables rounding; `None` and `"auto"` do not. Gate ESS on both `ess_bulk` and `ess_tail`. The helper extracts signals but does not decide pass/fail; each caller retains its variable set and missing-BFMI policy. The fixes are recorded in dseinternational/research#65 and #440.

**Compare.** Use ArviZ PSIS-LOO to compare models fitted to the same ordered rows, with the same holdout unit and prediction target. Read `elpd_diff` with its standard error (`dse`); `scripts/compare_statistical_models.py` collects the nested and cross-model comparisons. Conditional row-level LOO predicts a held-out row given the child's fitted intercept and remaining observations. It is not new-child prediction.

Gain-factor plans declare `loo_unit="child"`: PSIS removes all of a child's transition likelihood contributions together, since one transition's post-score can be the next transition's baseline. The score conditions on the supplied baseline predictors; it does not evaluate prediction before those baselines are observed. Check the declared plan and Pareto diagnostics rather than inferring the holdout unit from the presence of a random intercept.

**Prediction for new children.** The `joint`, `joint_mechanism` and `historical_joint` families declare `prediction_target` as a new child in a replicate cohort, with the same design and observed covariates. Holding out another observation from a child already used to fit the model answers a different question.

Aggregating likelihood contributions by child does not by itself produce new-child prediction. A fitted child effect still contains information from that child's outcomes. The calculation must integrate child-level latent variables over their population distribution before applying PSIS-LOO. Every child-level free variable must be declared; an undeclared one stops the fit.

Require finite, complete diagnostics and validation schema version 2. Withhold the predictive estimate if Pareto-k exceeds its threshold in the full or either split integration batch. Compare likelihood discrepancies before averaging over posterior draws, and recompute PSIS scores for each batch. Withhold if the largest per-draw discrepancy times the child count, or the largest sum of absolute pointwise score differences, exceeds the ELPD standard error. These are stability checks, not error bounds; both batches can miss the same region. See the [correction record](notes/20260923-statistical-review-corrections.md). Grouped child-level K-fold refits provide an alternative when these checks fail. Each fold uses the family's factory and scores held-out children with latent variables drawn from the population. All folds are required. The [prediction-target decision](notes/202609011600-joint-new-child-prediction-target-626.md) records the historical joint model's failures that motivated this alternative.

Calibration must use the same target. The new-child probability integral transform (PIT) assesses each child's total for each measure; K-fold predictions use the refitted models. Retain existing per-outcome LOO-PIT plots with the label "conditional leave-one-cell-out". Those plots use the child's other observations and cannot validate new-child prediction.

**Reliability and discrimination.** `comparison_valid` asks whether a predictive comparison is trustworthy. The models must use the same ordered rows, and importance sampling must pass the Pareto-k check. Mechanism pipelines can repair a limited number of flagged points by exact refits (`reloo`); `loo_method` and `n_exact_refits` record the repair. Unrepaired failures invalidate the comparison.

`elpd_verdict` asks whether a valid comparison distinguishes the models. The project's standing rule treats `|elpd_diff| < 4` as inconclusive. This is a reporting rule, not a universal significance threshold. A near-identical pair can have both a small difference and an unstable standard error, so do not use their ratio to declare a winner in that range. Report the inconclusive comparison alongside the coefficient's posterior, intervals and practical-difference probabilities.

**Interpret.** Report the posterior median, an inner 50% and outer 89% equal-tailed credible interval, and the full posterior. The 50% band is a visual aid, not a decision threshold. An 89% highest posterior density interval (HPDI) is a per-scale sensitivity check. The [interval decision](notes/202607172359-credible-interval-standard.md) records the house convention and retirement of the former 90% sensitivity band.

For a continuous scalar distribution, a strictly monotone transformation maps its median and equal-tailed interval to the corresponding summaries on the transformed scale. An HPDI need not transform this way. An average marginal effect combines several uncertain coefficients, so calculate it within each posterior draw before summarising. Substituting median coefficients can give a different answer; the [worked example](docs/learning/itt-model-walkthrough.md#calculate-a-difference-within-every-draw) demonstrates this.

Coverage alone does not establish simulation precision. The 89% interval uses the 5.5% and 94.5% quantiles. Standard tail ESS uses the 5% and 95% quantiles, so it is a nearby diagnostic, not an exact check of those limits. When endpoint precision matters, assess quantile-specific ESS or Monte Carlo error at the reported probabilities. See the [tail-ESS definition](https://mc-stan.org/posterior/reference/ess_tail.html), [quantile Monte Carlo error](https://mc-stan.org/posterior/reference/mcse_quantile.html), and Vehtari et al. (2021), DOI [10.1214/20-BA1221](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-BA1221). Neither 89% nor 95% guarantees stable endpoints; the original reporting rationale cites Kruschke (2021), DOI [10.1038/s41562-021-01177-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01177-7).

Read intervention direction from `P(AME > 0)`, where AME is the average marginal effect over the fitted sample's observed covariate profiles. Also report the conditional logit coefficient and its secondary `P(τ_logit > 0)`. Their signs agree in a constant-effect model but can differ under moderation. Give probability/items-scale effects averaged over observed profiles, not a constructed mean child. If the 89% interval includes zero, describe the posterior mass on both sides and avoid a decisive directional claim or a significant/not-significant label. There are no p-values.

Separate direction from meaningful size. Report `P(benefit ≥ δ)` against the education-lead-approved minimally important difference and the mandatory δ-sensitivity sweep. The initial rule was chosen after inspecting results and remains post-hoc for those analyses; later outcomes follow the standing rule in the glossary. A high probability of a positive effect can accompany a practically negligible effect. Selected large point estimates can exaggerate magnitude, so report their uncertainty.

If using evidence labels (inconclusive, suggestive, moderate, strong, very strong), put them after the probability and odds and name the claim. Say "moderate evidence that the intervention helps", not "a moderate effect". Orient the label to the favoured direction, including harm. See [the evidence and ROPE guidance](notes/202606261304-evidence-strength-and-rope-reporting.md).

## Causal interpretation and its limits

**Randomised contrasts.** Original assignment supports causal contrasts under the stated model, selection and missing-data assumptions. This applies to the t2 treated-versus-untreated comparison and to a subgroup defined by pre-randomisation floor status. Report each fitted sample and its exclusions. Extending a selected-sample result to all 57 children needs further missing-outcome assumptions and sensitivity analyses.

Assignment remains randomised after crossover. The t3 arm gap compares assignment to early-start and delayed-start treatment schedules. It does not isolate treatment presence, duration, carryover, maturation or taught content. `delta_crossover` describes the change between two randomised schedule contrasts; it does not identify waiting-list catch-up or an effect wearing off. Latent ability does not confound the original assignment simply because both arms have received treatment. The [crossover sign-off](notes/202608241100-did-t2-estimand-signoff.md) records the corrected interpretation.

The own-baseline and age terms improve precision; they do not repair selection or create identification. Their priors and functional form can still affect an estimate in a small sample. Agreement between ITT and crossover estimates is a sensitivity check on shared trial data, not independent replication.

**Assessment intervals.** The data record age in whole months and no assessment dates, so every interval is a difference of two whole-month ages. Assessments followed a common calendar: t1→t2 averaged 7.1 months, t2→t3 8.4 months and t3→t4 5.4 months. Within a transition the spread (SD about 0.6 months) includes rounding error, and t1→t4 spans 20–22 months for every child. A smaller raw gain over t3→t4 therefore partly reflects a shorter window. Separate transition intercepts, or arm-by-transition intercepts, allow different mean changes. They do not account for slopes that depend on elapsed time. A common skill coefficient assumes the same association across these unequal windows and describes their observed mixture, not a common per-month association. The gradient-boosting gain models (through `time`), `gain_factors`, `mechanism`, `dose_response`, `mediation`, `adjusted`, `joint_mechanism`, `block_exposure` and `survival` have one, and `growth` uses age as its time axis. `lcsm-067` pools a single change intercept; `lcsm-167` is its arm × window companion. Whole-month ages do not separate rounding from actual timing differences. A per-child interval covariate needs a justified measurement-error treatment and a clear time-scale target; an ordinary adjustment is not guaranteed to remove timing bias. Time-varying slopes or a continuous-time sensitivity would address a different limitation from transition intercepts.

The arms were not assessed at identical spacing. Relative to the wait-list arm, the immediate arm's t2 assessment came about two weeks later: its t1→t2 interval was 0.44 months longer (89% child-bootstrap interval 0.21 to 0.66) and its t2→t3 interval 0.51 months shorter, while t1→t3 was level (difference −0.08 months). Every t2 contrast — the ITT headline, `tau_t2`, `d_grp_time[t2]` and the period-1 `gain_factors` headline — therefore includes slightly more elapsed time in the immediate arm. The t1→t3 spacing is much closer between arms, but this does not prove that all timing influences vanish. Multiplying the gap by each arm's average per-month gain gives two constant-rate scenarios. These assume that the rate over the full interval also applies during the extra weeks and transfers to a common assessment time. They are not bounds or estimated timing corrections. For word reading the scenarios are 0.14 to 0.31 words beside a stored effect of 2.37 words. Comparing these point estimates cannot show that posterior conclusions would remain unchanged. State the assumptions and scenarios beside a t2 headline.

The onset-aligned windows are also unequal: the immediate arm's t1→t3 window averages 15.5 months and the wait-list's t2→t4 window 14.2 months. Under constant-rate scenarios, the 1.3-month difference corresponds to 0.67–0.77 words of the 2.12-word word-reading contrast, and about a fifth to a third of the letter-sound and receptive-vocabulary contrasts. Those scenarios do not identify the timing contribution or guarantee how a timing-adjusted contrast would change. `scripts/assessment_interval_check.py` computes these quantities; the [interval note](notes/202609212100-assessment-interval-lengths.md) records the measurements and decisions.

**Adjusted associations.** Baseline-to-later skill coefficients, mechanism curves and mediator-to-outcome terms concern exposures that were not randomised. Stable shared traits and measurement error can affect these relationships. A child random intercept models dependence between repeated observations; it does not by itself separate within-child from between-child associations or remove confounding. The mechanism exposure and outcome are also measured at the same wave. Report these results as adjusted associations, not as evidence that one skill causes another.

**Mechanism headline.** The default headline compares predicted outcomes at the fitted exposure's 25th and 75th percentiles (`items_ref_quantiles`). Calculate both predictions for every fitted row, keeping its period, covariates, baseline and fitted child intercept; hold any moderator at its standardised mean. Transform each prediction to the outcome scale before averaging. For the ordinary logit link, `y(x) = N * mean_i expit(eta_base[i] + f_i(x))`. This describes the fitted children, not a new child or a constructed average child.

`mechanism_summary.csv`, `mechanism_curve_items.csv` and `key_findings.json` must use that same interval and reference population. The full observed range is a labelled secondary contrast because its endpoints depend on extreme observations. The cross-model forest reports the slope across the declared interval per SD of exposure logit, which equals `beta_mech` for a linear model. Report steepest intervals separately on latent-logit and expected-items scales; they need not coincide. The [estimand decision](notes/202608241400-mechanism-batch-c-602-605.md) records the equations and artefact fields.

**Within-child and between-child associations.** A single exposure coefficient above a child random intercept can combine two relationships. The between-child question asks whether children with higher average exposure have higher outcomes. The within-child question asks whether a child's outcome differs when their exposure differs from their own mean. A random intercept alone does not separate them.

With `decompose_between_within`, the model estimates `beta_between` and `beta_within` separately and reports them in `mechanism_slope_summary.csv`. Use the coefficient that matches the question. A pooled coefficient should not stand in for either when they differ. The within-child comparison removes stable differences under its model assumptions, but it does not establish temporal order or remove time-varying confounding, measurement error or reverse causation. Neither coefficient is causal.

**Measured ability.** The RLI models that adjust for ability generally use WPPSI-III Block Design. Seven mechanism companions use its sum with Object Assembly. Both are perceptual-organisation tasks; neither specification establishes that the DAG's latent general ability has been controlled.

The [7 September comparison](notes/202609071900-nonverbal-ability-composite.md) records a correlation of 0.664 between the subtests. That correlation alone does not identify either test's reliability or the proportion of confounding removed. Interpreting it as reliability requires assumptions about the shared construct, equal measurement quality and uncorrelated errors. Under a parallel-test approximation, the Spearman-Brown formula gives a composite reliability of `2r/(1+r)`, about 0.80. This is an assumption-dependent illustration, not a validated measurement model for these children. Published subtest internal-consistency coefficients do not establish reliability for general ability either. See Eisinga et al. (2013), DOI [10.1007/s00038-012-0416-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00038-012-0416-3).

Use the registered companion fits to assess sensitivity to the observed ability measure. A small change in the screening regression does not show what perfect measurement or adjustment for unmeasured general ability would do. The [future-study recommendations](notes/202607172345-design-lessons-for-future-studies.md) therefore still call for several measures spanning ability domains.

**Period-specific associations.** `phase_varying_slope` estimates a slope for each transition, with partial pooling towards a shared mean (`mu_mech`, spread `sigma_mech_phase`). Read the nested PSIS-LOO comparison and exposure-support table alongside the slopes. A supported difference challenges the common-slope assumption. It does not identify a change in mechanism because periods differ in length, age, treatment history and measurement position. The exposure is not randomised in any period.

**Individual response.** Classifying children by observed change can confuse treatment response with measurement noise and other sources of change (Senn, 2016, DOI [10.1002/sim.7059](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.7059)). `lrp-rli-did-013` is an exploratory heterogeneity sensitivity. Only waitlist children change treatment status, with one untreated-to-treated transition each. Immediate-arm rows cannot separately identify their child intercepts and treatment slopes. The estimated variance can therefore include differences in maturation, period shocks and noise.

Any moderation analysis must use a single fitted model, strong regularisation and pre-randomisation moderators, and remain exploratory. Persistent low attainment is a separate descriptive question addressed by the floor-sitter survival models (`lrp-rli-surv-009` and `lrp-rli-surv-011`). It does not establish an individual non-response category.

## Reporting results

Write for a science undergraduate familiar with frequentist statistics but new to Bayesian methods. Lead with the finding and uncertainty, then explain its practical meaning.

- **Translate the Bayesian parts.** Expand shorthand on first use (τ = conditional logit-scale treatment coefficient, AME = fitted-sample average marginal treatment effect, `gamma_own` = baseline coupling, `f_mech` = mechanism curve). Read an equal-tailed 89% credible interval in plain words — "given the model and data, the value lies in [a, b] with 89% probability" — prefer a posterior tail probability for the named estimand (`P(AME > 0) = 0.97` for the intervention-help claim) to anything that reads like a p-value, and restate the sign convention wherever τ appears.
- **Pair every estimate with its uncertainty** — never a bare posterior mean or a bare importance rank.
- **Avoid verbal evidence labels ("strong", "moderate") wherever possible** — report the odds and probabilities and let them speak. Where prose forces a label, append the word _evidence_ and name the claim ("strong evidence that the intervention helps"), show the odds beside it, and use the round-odds ladder in `notes/202606261304-evidence-strength-and-rope-reporting.md` — run separately for _direction_ (`pd`) and _meaningful benefit_ (`P(benefit ≥ δ)`). A label must never connote effect _size_.
- **Cite primary sources, verify them before committing them to text, and always include a DOI** when one exists.

## Conventions

- **No outlier exclusion** unless justified on the model page.
- **`GroupKFold` by `subject_id`** wherever longitudinal leakage would otherwise inflate performance.
- **Mechanism-model β_G is not a direct-effect estimate** — both arms are on intervention during phases 1 and 2, so the pooled β_G averages over phases where group is no longer a treatment contrast (documented in each mechanism report).
- **RLI intervention scope** — the intervention targets vocabulary and grammar as well as reading, so null effects on receptive/expressive vocabulary are substantive findings, not by-design predictions.
- **Decisions a future reader might question get a dated note in `notes/`** before they leave your head — the question, the choice, the rationale, the result. Mark AI-drafted notes with a `> [!NOTE]` admonition naming the tool and model (per the AI-authorship rule in `CLAUDE.md`).

## Glossary

- **Posterior** — the distribution of a parameter after combining prior and data; summarised by a mean/median, a credible interval, and tail probabilities.
- **Credible interval (CrI)** — a range holding the parameter with stated probability _given the model and data_ (the reading people wrongly attach to a confidence interval).
- **Posterior tail probability** — e.g. `P(AME > 0)` or `P(τ_logit > 0)`, the posterior mass for a named estimand or parameter on one side of a value; it must say which quantity is being evaluated.
- **Probability of direction (`pd`)** — the posterior mass in the favoured direction for the named quantity. The ITT headline uses `P(AME > 0)`; `P(τ_logit > 0)` is a secondary coefficient read and can differ under moderation. It answers direction, not magnitude. Do not treat `pd` as a p-value or as evidence of a practically important effect. Numerical correspondences with p-values depend on the model and prior; posterior symmetry alone does not establish one.
- **ROPE / minimally-important difference (δ)** — a band [−δ, +δ] around zero wide enough that an effect inside it is practically equivalent to none (the frequentist SESOI / clinical MID); `P(|effect| < δ)` is its coverage, `P(effect > δ)` the probability of a meaningful benefit. δ is set to **half the period's natural-maturation gain** (floored at 1 item) and was signed off by the education lead after the initial results had been examined (issue #144), so for the analyses fitted to date it is a transparent post-hoc reanalysis judgement rather than a prospectively specified threshold. On 2026-08-19 the rule was confirmed as the project's **standing** practical-difference rule (`notes/202608191130-practical-difference-rule-confirmed.md`): a new outcome's δ is derived from the waiting-list arm's untreated period-1 gain by the rule and recorded in `measures.ROPE_DELTA` before the outcome is fitted, so from that point the threshold is pre-specified. Each ITT report also carries a **δ-sensitivity table** giving every outcome's `P(benefit ≥ δ)` at its adopted δ and a stricter δ (word reading at δ = 1 and 2; the floored outcomes at 10 / 15 / 20 pp), so the meaningful-benefit claim's robustness to the δ choice is explicit rather than hidden in a single number.
- **Type-S / Type-M error** — among effects declared "significant", the rate of wrong _sign_ (S) and the average _magnitude_ exaggeration (M); both worsen at low power.
- **τ (tau)** — the randomised-window assigned-arm contrast in the fitted analysis sample (positive ⇒ intervention arm higher); reading it as a fitted-sample causal effect requires the available-case selection assumption above, and extending it to all 57 randomised children requires further missing-data assumptions and sensitivity.
- **`tau_t2`** — the waitlist-crossover arm-by-wave model's immediate-minus-waitlist contrast at t2; randomised and causal under the same selection and missing-data assumptions as the corresponding available-case modified ITT estimate, but not independent evidence.
- **`delta_crossover`** — `tau_t2 - arm_gap_t3`, the modelled closure of the immediate-minus-waitlist arm gap after crossover; the change between two randomised regime contrasts; never an identified catch-up mechanism.
- **`gamma_own` / `gamma_cross`** — a measure's own-baseline coupling / its cross-baseline couplings, on the logit scale.
- **`f_mech`** — the nonparametric dose-response curve in a mechanism model.
- **ITT (intention-to-treat)** — analyse every randomised child in the arm assigned, regardless of adherence. The current suite preserves assigned arm but starts from 54 of the 57 randomised children and then applies model-specific observed-data requirements, so it is described as available-case modified ITT rather than full ITT; causal interpretation of the resulting selected-sample contrast is conditional on ignorable archive inclusion and outcome observation.
- **Mediation / NDE / NIE** — how much of an effect flows through a mediator (natural direct / natural indirect effect). Here these name the _functionals the software computes_, not identified causal effects: under this study's DAG the natural effects are not identified (latent general ability, plus exposure-induced confounding by dose `IS`). See the Mediation family above.
- **IDE / IIE** — the **interventional** direct / indirect effects (MED-078/186/187): the mediator is drawn from its arm-specific covariate-conditional distribution rather than a cross-world counterfactual. Weaker assumptions than NDE/NIE — the exposure-induced-confounding obstacle drops away — but still not identified here, since no-unmeasured-mediator–outcome-confounding is still required.
- **Exposure-induced (treatment-induced) confounder** — a variable caused by treatment that then confounds the mediator → outcome relation (here: dose `IS`). It blocks identification of natural direct/indirect effects, and adjusting for it does _not_ fix the problem.
- **PSIS-LOO** — Pareto-smoothed importance-sampling leave-one-out cross-validation, the Bayesian model-comparison score (`elpd`).
- **HSGP** — Hilbert-space approximation to a Gaussian process; a fast smooth nonlinear term.
- **Regression to the mean** means that people selected for extreme observed scores tend to have less extreme scores on retesting when measurement noise contributes to selection. It is not a causal recovery process. A baseline coefficient below one is not by itself a diagnosis; its interpretation also depends on scaling and the model.
