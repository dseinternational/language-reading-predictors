## #602 — one declared natural-scale estimand

### The defect

The family published two different natural-scale answers to the same question in the same report. For `lrp-rli-mech-058` as it stood:

| artefact                               | exposure interval              | standardisation                                                           | answer                    |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| `mechanism_summary.csv` → key findings | 2 → 32 letter sounds (min/max) | mean of `expit(·)` over fitted rows, keeping each row's own intercept     | **+6.64** [+2.38, +10.99] |
| `mechanism_curve_items` worked example | 17 → 28 (interquartile)        | `expit` of the row-averaged linear predictor, child intercept **removed** | **+2.32** [+0.64, +3.92]  |

Neither was wrong. They were different estimands over different intervals, and — because averaging probabilities is not applying the link to an average — they would have disagreed even on an identical interval. Nothing in the report said which to quote.

### The declaration

The family now declares **one** headline, and every artefact computes it:

- **Reference population** — standardised over the **fitted rows**: each row keeps its own period, covariates, autoregressive baseline and **its child's own fitted random intercept**; only the exposure moves; any moderator is held at its standardised mean. Formally `y(x) = N · mean_i expit(eta_base[i] + f_i(x))`. The answer is therefore "averaged over the children actually analysed", not "for a constructed typical child", and the child intercept's treatment is stated rather than left implicit.
- **Exposure interval** — the **interquartile range of the fitted exposure** (`items_ref_quantiles`, per-model configurable, `(0.25, 0.75)`). The observed extremes are order statistics of a ~156-row sample: they move with a single child entering or leaving, and on a curve fit they sit exactly where the HSGP is least constrained.
- **Secondary** — the full observed range, retained and explicitly labelled `secondary_observed_range`.

`mechanism_summary.csv` now has two rows, headline first, each carrying `estimand`, `contrast`, `reference_population`, `child_intercept` and the reference quantiles. `mechanism_curve_items.csv` plots the same standardised quantity, so the worked-example points lie on the curve. `key_findings.json` records the headline under `headline_estimand`. `mechanism_curve.csv` (the analyst's logit view) is the same standardisation on the logit scale, deduplicated to one row per distinct exposure value.

### Verification: the secondary reproduces the old headline exactly

Over the stored `reporting` traces, the new observed-range contrast is bit-for-bit the previously published number, which is what makes this a relabelling-plus-addition rather than a silent restatement:

| model              | old published headline | new `secondary_observed_range` | new headline (interquartile) |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| `lrp-rli-mech-058` | +6.643302              | +6.643302                      | **+2.76** [+0.77, +4.61]     |
| `lrp-rli-mech-097` | +16.292061             | +16.292061                     | **+3.96** [+2.22, +5.72]     |
| `lrp-rli-mech-101` | +9.790293              | +9.790293                      | **+2.56** [+1.57, +3.53]     |

The new mech-058 headline (+2.76) sits close to, but not on, the old worked example (+2.32). The gap is exactly the reference-population change — averaging probabilities over the fitted children rather than applying the link to their average, with the child intercepts retained.

### The cross-model forest

`mechanism_forest` plotted the fitted-row average derivative of `f_mech` over the _whole_ observed range for curve models beside `beta_mech` for linear ones — a second, undeclared interval. It now plots the secant across the **declared interquartile interval**, per SD of the exposure logit, which for a linear fit is exactly `beta_mech` (that identity is what makes the two shapes commensurable). The whole-range average derivative is kept as `secondary_row_average_slope_median`. On the stored traces the linear rows are unchanged by construction and mech-058 moves from +0.232 to +0.267 per SD.

### The items-scale derivative

`_readiness_knee` locates the steepest interval on the **outcome-logit** scale. The expected-items derivative is `dE[y]/dx = N · p(1-p) · d(eta)/dx` and can peak elsewhere, so the question "where do predicted items rise fastest?" is a different one. Rather than record a decision to leave it unanswered, it is now **published**: `readiness_threshold.csv` gains `items_*` columns computed by binning the standardised expected-items curve under the same declared reference population, with the same qualification rules (rise, interior, curvature) and its own `items_scale = "expected_items"` marker. Both are reported; neither replaces the other. On the stored `mech-058` trace both land at the same location (29.5 letter sounds) and both are boundary-pinned and **not** qualified as a knee — consistent with the finding that no stored HSGP fit has a qualified knee.

## #603 — the between/within (Mundlak) split

`decompose_between_within` (typed, default off, linear-only, rejected alongside a moderator, all validated before any I/O) splits the standardised exposure into each child's **fitted-row** mean and their deviation from it, following the implementation `pooled_levels` already uses. Both vectors are `pm.Data` nodes and sum back to the exposure exactly; a regression test pins both properties.

**`lrp-rli-mech-301`** is the registered variant — `lrp-rli-mech-101` with nothing else changed.

| coefficient    | median | 89 % CrI         | P(> 0) | question                                                   |
| -------------- | ------ | ---------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `beta_between` | +0.438 | [+0.300, +0.603] | 1.000  | do children with a generally higher exposure score higher? |
| `beta_within`  | +0.033 | [−0.109, +0.173] | 0.643  | when a child's own exposure moves, does their outcome?     |

The pooled comparator `lrp-rli-mech-101` reports **+0.27** per SD — a blend sitting between a strongly positive between-child association and a within-child one that is not resolved from zero. **This is the substantive result of #603**: on the letter-sound → word-reading relation the mechanism family's headline association is, on these data, almost entirely between-child.

Two qualifications belong with that, and are in the report prose:

- The mechanism design is an ANCOVA conditioning on the outcome's own period-start score, which already absorbs much of the within-child signal; a near-null `beta_within` here is not the same claim as a near-null within-child association in a levels model. The `pooled_levels` family, which has no own baseline, finds 0.81 between against 0.45 within for the same pair.
- Neither coefficient is causal. `beta_within` removes _stable_ between-child confounding, latent general ability's stable part included; it does not make the exposure temporally prior to the outcome, and it does not remove time-varying confounding or reverse causation.

A child-fixed-effect comparator was considered and **not** registered: at three transitions per child it would cost the between-child signal entirely for a noisy within estimate, and the partial-pooling assumption is not what the split is testing. Recorded here so the omission is a decision rather than an oversight.

## #604 — the phase-stability sensitivity

`phase_varying_slope` (typed, default off, linear-only, mutually exclusive with `phase_specific_mechanism`, validated before any I/O) replaces the pooled slope with `beta_mech_phase = mu_mech + sigma_mech_phase · z_phase` — partial pooling, as `dose_response` does for its period-varying dose, because three periods of ~52 rows will not support independent slopes.

Registered variants **`lrp-rli-mech-302`** (L → W, against `101`) and **`lrp-rli-mech-303`** (L → R, against `097`), each with a nested PSIS-LOO comparison written to the comparison directory and copied beside both runs.

| model              | t1→t2                 | t2→t3                 | t3→t4                 | `sigma_mech_phase` |
| ------------------ | --------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------ |
| `mech-302` (L → W) | +0.328 [+0.17, +0.52] | +0.294 [+0.17, +0.42] | +0.186 [+0.06, +0.31] | 0.143 [0.02, 0.51] |
| `mech-303` (L → R) | +0.060 [−0.03, +0.14] | +0.144 [+0.08, +0.21] | +0.104 [+0.04, +0.17] | 0.086 [0.01, 0.38] |

Read as the family's own prose insists: a period difference is **evidence against pooling, not evidence about mechanism change over time**. The three transitions differ in age, treatment history and measurement position at once, only the first is randomised-arm-clean, and the exposure is randomised in none of them. The intervals overlap substantially in both channels, so the pooled headline is not obviously mis-stated — but the assumption is now tested rather than assumed, and `exposure_support.csv` is rendered beside the slopes so the overlap the comparison rests on is visible.

**Sampling note.** The non-centred per-period slope vector is a small hierarchical funnel: `sigma_mech_phase` is informed by three periods and its lower tail pinches the geometry. At `rep-lite` the fit shows 8 divergences at `target_accept` 0.90, 2 at 0.95 and 0 at 0.995, so both modules declare `target_accept=0.995`. This is a diagnosed raise for a specific geometry, not a blanket escalation.

## #605 — a dispersion prior that can reach the near-Binomial limit

### The calibration

With `alpha + beta = kappa`, the Beta-Binomial variance inflation over Binomial is `(kappa + n) / (kappa + 1)`, so being within 10 % of Binomial needs `kappa ≥ 10(n − 1) − 1`. Measured over 2 000 000 prior draws:

| prior                                     | median `kappa` | P(within 10 % of Binomial), n = 79 | n = 170 |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------- |
| `kappa ~ HalfNormal(50)` **(registered)** | 33.8           | 0.000                              | 0.000   |
| `1/sqrt(kappa) ~ HalfNormal(0.25)`        | 35.2           | 0.114                              | 0.077   |
| `1/sqrt(kappa) ~ HalfNormal(0.50)`        | 8.8            | 0.057                              | 0.039   |

At the registered prior's own median the _prior_ already enforces **3.24×** inflation at n = 79 and **5.86×** at n = 170; the concentrations that would avoid it (779 and 1689) have effectively no mass. The alternative at 0.25 is calibration-preserving on the observable scale — a near-identical median concentration — while opening the tail the registered prior closes. That is why the sensitivity varies the prior's _family_ and not its scale: the earlier `kappa_sigma` sweep's widest cell, `HalfNormal(200)`, gives the near-Binomial region 0.000 mass just as `HalfNormal(50)` does.

### The result

`lrp-rli-mech-304` (L → W, n = 79) against `101`, and `lrp-rli-mech-305` (L → R, n = 170) against `097`:

| pair                    | headline contrast, registered prior | headline contrast, dispersion prior | `kappa` median |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------- |
| `mech-101` / `mech-304` | +2.557 [+1.568, +3.529]             | +2.557 [+1.605, +3.503]             | 81.9 → 91.0    |
| `mech-097` / `mech-305` | +3.955 [+2.220, +5.725]             | +4.015 [+2.321, +5.715]             | 99.2 → 111.1   |

The posteriors do move `kappa` well above the prior median in both channels (to VIF ≈ 1.85 at n = 79 and ≈ 2.5 at n = 170), so the data have something to say about dispersion — but the estimand does not move: the headline contrasts agree to within Monte-Carlo noise and the intervals are essentially unchanged.

### Decision

**Sensitivity only.** The registered mechanism fits keep `kappa ~ HalfNormal(50)`; `lrp-rli-mech-304` / `305` are retained as the companions that show the headline estimand is insensitive to it, and every mechanism fit now publishes `dispersion_summary.csv` so a reader can see the prior, the posterior and the implied variance inflation without having to reconstruct them. The family does **not** follow `level_factors` (#584 decision 4) in adopting the dispersion scale as its default, because the evidence for that change — a materially different coefficient or a materially better predictive calibration — is not present here, and refitting 41 models for a numerically identical answer is not warranted.

This decision is scoped to the **mechanism** family and to these two denominators. It should be revisited if a mechanism model is registered on a new high-denominator outcome, or if a future fit's `dispersion_summary.csv` shows `kappa` pinned against the prior rather than well inside it.
