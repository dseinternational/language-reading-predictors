# Findings: the `growth` family — trajectories across the whole study

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 4 models in the `growth` family. **Nothing here is causal.**

## The data

**Both studies.** Three models use the RLI trial across **all four timepoints** (54 children, 216 rows). One uses the historical cohort across **waves 1 to 3** (87 children and 261 possible child-wave cells, with missing outcome cells masked). Data are stacked, not collapsed — the whole point is the shape of each child's trajectory.

## What the model is for

Instead of comparing timepoints two at a time, a growth-curve model describes each child's **trajectory** as a starting level and a rate of change, linear in standardised age. In the three trial models both the starting level and rate are allowed to vary between children. The historical model gives children random intercepts but deliberately omits child-specific random slopes because one outcome over three waves cannot support 87 of them reliably; its group-average rates can instead vary with baseline verbal reasoning.

The question is then about the trajectory parameters: **does a child's baseline ability predict how _fast_ they progress**, as distinct from how high they start? Two quantities carry this — one linking baseline ability to the growth _rate_, one linking it to the _level_.

Some variants add a shared "growth tempo" factor allowing all measures' rates to move together, or an age-by-ability interaction to ask whether older-and-more-able children progress differently.

## What was found

In the **trial cohort**, the clearest relationship is between baseline non-verbal ability and growth in receptive grammar: **+0.11 logit units per SD** (89% +0.02 to +0.20). The shared-tempo variant gives the same +0.11 [+0.01, +0.21], and the age-by-ability variant gives +0.15 [+0.06, +0.25].

These are modest and consistent, and grammar is the outcome where the relationship is clearest — not reading. That is worth noting given that reading is the project's focus.

In the **historical cohort**, baseline verbal reasoning is associated with BAS word-reading growth at **−0.13 logit units per SD** (89% −0.25 to −0.01) — _negative_, meaning children with higher baseline verbal reasoning grew **more slowly** in word reading over waves 1 to 3.

## The negative result needs care, not a story

A negative ability-to-growth relationship is counter-intuitive and it would be easy to construct an explanation. The fit and its sensitivity support a narrower reading.

This is not the ordinary setup in which the same noisy outcome is subtracted from itself to make a change score: the predictor is wave-1 BAS Similarities, while the repeated outcome is BAS word reading, and the model estimates the reading level and rate jointly. Regression to the mean therefore does **not** by itself imply that the ability-to-rate coefficient must be biased negative. Similarities is nevertheless a noisy proxy, the reading-matched group was selected using baseline reading, and similarities is correlated with initial reading level, so measurement and selection could still affect the association in ways this model cannot separate.

**Ceiling and shape effects remain plausible, not demonstrated.** Children who read nearer the top of a bounded test have less observed headroom, and a linear trajectory may inadequately describe their development. The Beta-Binomial logit model respects the score boundary, but it cannot establish that headroom rather than a genuine association produced the negative rate coefficient.

The observation-influence sensitivity is the more direct warning. It masked nine high-Pareto child-wave cells across seven children, fully excluding one child. The `gamma` median stayed negative and the 89% intervals overlapped, but it moved from −0.13 [−0.25, −0.01] to **−0.10 [−0.22, +0.02]**; the posterior probability of a negative association fell from 0.956 to 0.906. The direction is stable under this check, but the evidence weakens from moderate to suggestive and the sensitivity interval includes zero.

The honest summary: **in this historical cohort, higher baseline verbal reasoning went with slower measured word-reading growth in the primary fit, and the direction survived a targeted influence sensitivity with greater uncertainty.** Regression to the mean, selection, bounded-scale behaviour and a genuine association are possible explanations rather than findings from this model. It is not evidence that verbal reasoning impedes reading development.

The same caution applies with the opposite sign to the trial cohort's positive grammar result, which is small.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Baseline ability is not a lever.** It was not manipulated, and the coefficient is an adjusted association.

**A linear trajectory is an assumption.** Real development is not linear, and forcing a straight line makes the estimated rate an average that may not describe any child.

**Growth rates are hard to estimate from four waves.** Separating a child's individual rate from measurement noise needs more data than most of these models have; the trial models get four points per child.

**The trial models pool across arms**, so they describe development in the study as a whole, not treatment effects.
