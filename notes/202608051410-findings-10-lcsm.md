# Findings 10 — the latent change-score (LCSM) family

Reports every model in the `lcsm` family from the 2026-08-04/05 `reporting` refit. **5 models, all passing the convergence gate.** Reading conventions in note 00. Preliminary research data — all estimates provisional.

## What these models do

Everything else in the suite looks at one transition at a time. These lay all four waves out as a panel and ask a **lead–lag** question: does a child's level of one skill at one wave predict their _change_ in another skill over the next interval?

**Design.** A latent change-score model on the child × wave panel. Each skill has a change term per interval; the change is regressed on the prior level of the same skill (self-feedback), the prior level of other skills (cross-coupling), age, and exogenous covariates (hearing, phonological memory, speech production). All measures are stacked into one likelihood, so per-measure quantities are selected by the persisted cell map.

**Everything here is an association.** The temporal ordering — prior level predicting later change — is often mistaken for causal evidence. It is not. Latent general ability drives both the prior level and the later change, and no adjustment in these models removes it. Lead–lag ordering rules out reverse _simultaneity_, nothing more.

## `lcsm-067` — letter sounds and vocabulary as leading indicators of reading change

| Coupling                                          | Estimate (89%)                |  P(>0) |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | -----: |
| Prior expressive vocabulary → word-reading change | **+0.288** (+0.104 to +0.479) |  0.994 |
| Prior letter sounds → word-reading change         | **+0.136** (+0.048 to +0.231) |  0.993 |
| Word-reading self-feedback                        | −0.220 (−0.291 to −0.153)     |  0.000 |
| Age → word-reading change                         | −0.148 (−0.209 to −0.087)     | 0.0001 |

Both prior letter sounds and prior expressive vocabulary lead word-reading change, with very strong evidence.

**The negative self-feedback is the term most likely to be misread.** −0.22 does _not_ mean better readers get worse. It is the standard regression-to-the-mean / equilibrium term of a change-score model: children further above their own trajectory at one wave change less over the next. Every model in this family has it, and it should not be reported as a finding.

**Age is negatively associated with reading change**, consistent with the gain-factor family (note 03) and with the same interpretive limit: this cannot separate developmental timing from trajectory selection.

## `lcsm-091` — does prior _change_ add anything over prior _level_?

The same structure, but adding prior **change** terms alongside prior levels.

| Coupling                                                     | Estimate (89%)            | P(>0) |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- | ----: |
| Prior letter-sound **level** → word-reading change           | +0.224 (+0.123 to +0.335) | 1.000 |
| Prior expressive vocabulary **level** → word-reading change  | +0.248 (+0.058 to +0.442) | 0.982 |
| Prior letter-sound **change** → word-reading change          | +0.047 (−0.178 to +0.257) | 0.648 |
| Prior expressive vocabulary **change** → word-reading change | −0.005 (−0.488 to +0.476) | 0.493 |

**Levels carry the signal; changes add nothing.** Both change-on-change couplings are flatly inconclusive with intervals spanning ±0.2–0.5. Where a child _is_ predicts their next interval's progress; how fast they were just moving does not add information beyond that. This is a useful negative result — it argues against momentum or acceleration stories.

## `lcsm-082` — which way does the blending/reading relationship run?

Fits phoneme blending and word reading with lagged couplings in **both** directions, so dominance can be assessed within one model.

| Coupling                                  | Estimate (89%)                | P(>0) |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ----: |
| Prior letter sounds → word-reading change | **+0.246** (+0.143 to +0.367) | 1.000 |
| Prior letter sounds → blending change     | **+0.156** (+0.042 to +0.285) | 0.985 |
| Prior word reading → blending change      | +0.053 (−0.037 to +0.149)     | 0.828 |
| Prior blending → word-reading change      | +0.039 (−0.059 to +0.135)     | 0.749 |

**Letter sounds lead both; blending and word reading do not clearly lead each other.** The two reciprocal couplings between blending and reading are both inconclusive and of similar small size, so this model does not identify a dominant direction between them. What it does show clearly is that letter-sound knowledge precedes change in both — consistent with the mediation family's finding (note 08) that the letter-sound route carries the effect and blending does not compete with it.

## `lcsm-081` and `lcsm-181` — the taught-vocabulary reverse coupling

`lcsm-081` asks whether prior **word reading** leads change in taught vocabulary — a reverse-direction check. `lcsm-181` is its matched comparator with that coupling removed.

| Coupling (`lcsm-081`)                             | Estimate (89%)                | P(>0) |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ----: |
| Prior taught receptive → taught expressive change | **+0.423** (+0.251 to +0.618) | 1.000 |
| Prior word reading → taught receptive change      | +0.084 (+0.050 to +0.122)     | 1.000 |
| Prior word reading → taught expressive change     | +0.062 (+0.016 to +0.113)     | 0.986 |
| Phonological memory → taught vocabulary change    | +0.121 (+0.045 to +0.197)     | 0.994 |
| Speech production → taught vocabulary change      | −0.112 (−0.180 to −0.043)     | 0.004 |

The reverse couplings are **small but clearly resolved** (+0.06 and +0.08, at P = 0.986 and 1.000). Prior word reading does lead taught-vocabulary change — five to seven times smaller than the receptive→expressive coupling (+0.42), but not zero. Removing it (`lcsm-181`) raises the receptive→expressive coupling from +0.42 to +0.45, so the two share some explanatory work.

The direction of causality between reading and vocabulary is **not** settled by this. What the models show is mutual lead–lag association, with the within-vocabulary coupling much the largest.

**Phonological memory and speech production** appear consistently: memory positively associated with taught-vocabulary change, speech production negatively. The negative speech-production coupling is worth flagging as unexpected and unexplained; it survives in both models and deserves a look before it is either reported or dismissed.

## Caveats

- **Associations only.** Lead–lag ordering is not causal identification; latent ability drives both sides.
- **Negative self-feedback is a modelling artefact of change scores**, not a finding.
- **These are the most heavily-parameterised models in the suite** relative to their data (four waves, ~54 children, several skills), so individual couplings are correspondingly uncertain.
- **Predictive calibration.** 50% bands cover about 85% of observations — the highest in the suite alongside `historical_growth`, expected for a panel model whose in-sample check conditions on fitted child effects.
- These five models now emit one posterior-predictive overlay **per measure** rather than none; previously the pooled overlay was skipped because the stacked likelihood mixes scales with different maxima.
