## Finding 1 (statistical/reporting, moderate): lrp-rli-itt-012's analysis population is misdescribed — the ten-baseline complete-case intersection drops one control child from all ten outcomes, and the recipe describes the opposite mechanism

`JointRunPlan.prepare_kwargs()` passes only `phase_mode="itt"` and the outcome list, so `load_and_prepare` applies its default `pre_required=None` — **every declared outcome's baseline must be non-missing for a row to be kept**. On the current CSV exactly one waiting-list child is missing exactly one of the ten baselines (the single-word reading `W` t1 score, all other nine present), and the ten-outcome joint therefore drops that child from **every** outcome: the fitted set is 28 + 25 = 53 children on all ten rows of `analysis_set.csv`, versus 28 + 26 = 54 in each single-outcome counterpart except W itself (lrp-rli-itt-010 also fits 53, because its own-baseline term genuinely needs the missing score). Two consequences:

- **The persisted recipe text states the opposite selection mechanism.** `resolve_joint_run_plan` writes, for every joint fit, "using outcome-specific available cases in the randomised t1-to-t2 window. Different outcomes may therefore contribute different observed child sets." That sentence describes post-score availability (which the flattened-cell likelihood does handle per outcome), not what actually bound here: a **cross-outcome baseline complete-case intersection** that makes the child sets _identical_ across outcomes and uniformly one control child _smaller_ than the single-outcome fits'. The 54-child cohort figure in the same paragraph is also not the fitted count for this model.
- **The joint-vs-single consistency check differs from the singles by population as well as by prior.** The module comment in `lrp_rli_itt_012.py` documents the deliberate untiered Normal(0, 0.5) tau (versus the distal tier's Normal(0, 0.3) in the singles), but nothing — module, recipe, `_results_joint.qmd`, or the `itt_vs_joint` comparison CSV — records that nine of the ten joint taus are additionally fitted on one fewer control child. The partial's how-to-read line ("the two markers should land close together... A large gap flags an outcome where the joint and single-outcome estimates disagree") names neither expected source of a small gap. On the current fits the gaps are small (e.g. R: 0.008 single vs 0.034 joint on the logit scale), so this is a framing defect, not a wrong number.

Structurally the drop is an artefact of the dense pre-score matrix: with `use_cross_baselines=False` outcome k's linear predictor uses only its own baseline column, so the other nine outcomes never consume the missing W score — but a NaN anywhere in `pre_logit` would poison `eta`, and the observation mask is post-score-only, so the loader's all-baselines rule is what protects the graph. Keeping the child for the nine unaffected outcomes would need per-cell pre-missingness masking plus NaN-filling of the unused matrix entry (a real design change for one child). **Recommendation:** fix the words, not the model — make `analysis_population` / the recipe state the joint baseline complete-case rule when more than one outcome is declared (and drop or condition the "different outcomes may contribute different observed child sets" sentence on actual post missingness), and add one line to `_results_joint.qmd`'s consistency-check paragraph naming the two expected gap sources (prior tier, one-child population difference). The two-outcome contrast fits are unaffected (both block-1 baselines are complete on all 54 children; their analysis sets match the singles).

## Finding 2 (reporting, moderate): the contrast models' key-findings box never states the declared contrast

`_kf_build_joint` reads only `joint_treatment_marginal.csv`; `tau_difference.csv` is never consulted. For the six contrast models (lrp-rli-itt-015/016/115 and the 215/216/315 companions) the gate-interlocked findings box — the authoritative source for note and report numbers (#320) — therefore headlines the _range of the two marginal effects_ and the clearer marginal's direction, but omits the model's declared estimand entirely. For lrp-rli-itt-015 the box says "ranged from +0.4 to +1.5 items" and "for taught expressive vocabulary... 98% probability the true effect is positive", while the taught-versus-not-taught contrast the model exists to estimate (AME[TE] − AME[UE] = +3.4 percentage points, 89% CrI −4.3 to +11.1, P(> 0) = 0.76 — a _much_ less resolved quantity than the box's 98% sentence) appears nowhere, and neither does the declared transfer read (assess the marginal UE effect against a negligible-effect threshold) nor the dependence caveat. A reader of the box alone would take away "the intervention helps taught vocabulary" from a model registered to answer "did it help taught words _more than_ untaught words", whose answer is "probably, but far from resolved". The full detail is in the report body (`_results_joint.qmd` renders `tau_difference.csv` with the interpretation, transfer and dependence metadata faithfully) — but the box is what `key_findings.json` consumers quote. **Recommendation:** when `tau_difference.csv` is present, lead the box with the contrast (median, 50%/89% intervals, P(> 0), the metadata's interpretation sentence), demote the range sentence to context, and append the transfer-read sentence when `transfer_outcome` is declared. This can be regenerated without a refit via `scripts/regenerate_key_findings.py` once the builder changes.

## Finding 3 (policy consistency, minor): the parent-companion dependence pairing is advisory prose, not a release rule

Every parent contrast's `dependence_note` instructs: "treat this contrast as dependence-checked only once that companion has passed the house gate" (015 → 215, 115 → 315, 016 → 216). Nothing enforces it: `release.evaluate_publication` has no joint-pair bundle — a parent releases its contrast unqualified even if its companion fails the gate, was never fitted, or its stored trace goes stale. This is exactly the pattern the phoneme-blending 008/108 pair closed in the ITT review (finding 1 there; the B evaluator now fails closed on a missing or stale trace-backed pair). Today nothing is wrongly released — all three companions pass their gates with zero divergences and clear tau psense, and the feared sigma-at-zero-boundary geometry did not recur — so this is a latent gap, not a live one. **Recommendation:** either register a paired-bundle robustness check for the three parent/companion pairs analogous to `_blending_pair_release_failures` (parent withholds or qualifies its `tau_difference` when the companion bundle is absent, unconverged or stale), or record a dated decision that the pairing stays advisory for the joint family and why the B precedent does not carry over.

## Finding 4 (statistical communication, minor): the joint key-findings headline pools incommensurable item units, unqualified for P and B

The box's headline for lrp-rli-itt-012 — "estimates ranged from **−0.1 to +3.5 items**" — takes the minimum and maximum of `items_median` across outcomes whose denominators run from 12 to 170 items: one "item" is not one unit of anything across those tests, which is precisely why the family's headline scale is the proportion-correct AME and why `_kf_most_resolved_row`'s own docstring refuses to rank raw magnitudes. Two of the pooled entries also carry qualifications the box does not: P's graded AME is shape-flagged by the fit's own predictive check, and `_results_joint.qmd` says in terms "Do not use P's graded joint-model AME unqualified in cross-outcome rankings"; B's ordinary-logit AME is subject to the mandatory 008/108 response-link sensitivity, on which ground `compare_statistical_models.py` excludes B from the ITT-vs-joint forest — yet both feed the box's range with no flag. **Recommendation:** state the range in percentage points (the scale built for cross-outcome comparability), or per outcome with symbols; for lrp-rli-itt-012 either exclude P and B from the range or append the two existing one-line qualifications. Also fix the subject-verb slip in the ROPE sentence ("1 were more likely than not").

## Observations (no action required)

- **PSIS-LOO on the correlated companions is conditional on the per-child offsets.** `_joint_log_likelihood_by_child` sums the flattened cells within child, which for the factorised fits makes the LOO unit exactly one child. For 215/216/315 the summed likelihood is still conditional on that child's latent `u_i`, so "leave-one-child-out generalisation" is approximated, not exact — the standard conditional-versus-marginal caveat for random-effect models, shared with every child-intercept family in the codebase. The importance sampling holds (all Pareto-k ≤ 0.66 across the three companions), so nothing is unreliable today.
- **The contrast fits give the distal-tier outcomes (UE, UR) the proximal Normal(0, 0.5) tau prior.** A uniform prior on both arms of a within-model contrast is the defensible choice — tier-differential shrinkage would attenuate the untaught effect more and push the taught-minus-untaught contrast up mechanically — but the choice is documented only in lrp-rli-itt-012's settings comment; a line in the 015/115 docstrings would stop the wider-than-single-outcome UE/UR marginals reading as an oversight (the box's transfer read quotes exactly those marginals).
- Power scaling on the companions flags `sigma_outcome` / `u_corr_pair` as "potential prior-data conflict"; the release class deliberately reads only `tau`, and the partial's dependence section plus the prior-vs-posterior overlay carry the block-identification story. Consistent with the #551 design.
- The 50% posterior-predictive interval covers 67.7% of cells on lrp-rli-itt-012 — expected over-coverage from discrete counts under the closed-interval convention, not misfit.
- The `use_age_gp` path (partial-pooled HSGP) is registered nowhere and retained for sensitivity fits only; its mutual exclusion with the linear age term is validated both in settings and in the factory.

## Verified sound (checked, no issue)

- **The LKJ residual block.** `pm.LKJCholeskyCov` with `sd_dist=HalfNormal(0.5)` bakes the per-outcome scales into `chol` (the residual covariance is `chol @ chol.T`) with no outer scale term — the historical double-scaling bug stays fixed; the non-centring `u = Z @ chol.T` gives row-wise `u_i = chol @ z_i` with covariance `chol @ chol.T`; `u_corr_pair` uses strictly-lower-triangle indices whose labels ("UE|TE") match `corr[i, j]`; the `outcome2` coordinate avoids the duplicate-dim error; the discarded-sd gate pollution that bit `corr_factor` does not apply because the sds are load-bearing here.
- **The AME core.** `_joint_ame_draws` nets `tau_k·G` out per draw and pushes every retained row through both arms (`expit(eta0 + tau) − expit(eta0)`), averaging over the outcome's observed rows via the persisted `y_post_cell_row`/`y_post_cell_outcome` map, with validated bounds, an outcome-with-no-observations guard, and boolean/integer `row_mask` semantics; with the residual block on, the fitted `u_i` rides along in both counterfactual arms, which is the correct paired treatment. `tau_difference_summary` differences per draw on both scales, so the companions' contrast intervals genuinely carry the estimated within-child covariance.
- **The flattened-cell likelihood.** Explicit `np.nonzero` cell indices; per-outcome denominators via `n_trials_vec[idx_col]`; the cell map persisted as constant data and used consistently by the per-outcome prior/posterior predictive plots, LOO-PIT trees, and PPC calibration — each of which raises rather than pooling counts with different denominators when the map cannot be resolved.
- **Child-aggregated LOO.** Cells are summed within child before `az.loo` (one row per child in `itt` phase mode), Pareto-k rows align to subject identities, and the same identities gate the influence-refit machinery, which consumes the same resolved plan and factory kwargs as the primary fit and refuses on any row-identity or checksum drift.
- **Plan discipline.** Unknown setting keys, typed/`extra` splits, empty or duplicate outcome lists, contrast outcomes outside the declared set, `transfer_outcome` without its interpretation (and vice versa), and a `joint_structure` contradicting `use_residual_correlation` all fail before `make_context` resets the output directory or data are read; the three companions are derived from their parents with `dataclasses.replace`, and a test pins that they match except the block and the note.
- **Release gating.** `joint` is in `GATED_KINDS`; the vector-valued `tau` aggregates worst-first over `tau[...]` psense rows (lrp-rli-itt-012 correctly qualifies as `prior_data_conflict` driven by L/B/P with the attenuation note attached; the six two-outcome fits release clear); the `✓` tick convention is handled.
- **Reporting conventions.** `ci_prob=0.89` is passed explicitly to every summary (no 0.95 default leaks); medians lead; the tau forest is explicitly labelled the secondary conditional-logit view; the per-outcome prior pushforward runs the identical AME core on the prior group with a labelled degradation row on failure; the priors table names and explains `u_chol`/`u_z` with sensible roles.
- **Loader integrity.** Duplicate (subject, time) keys and within-subject group changes fail loud; group codes validated before recoding to the positive-equals-benefit convention; Haldane-corrected logits; count ceilings enforced per measure.
- **Report partial.** `_results_joint.qmd` reads the persisted plan (not `spec.extra`) for both the dependence prose and the outcome list, renders the E/P shape-misfit callout from the fit's own calibration table, quotes the contrast with its interpretation/transfer/dependence metadata, and states the factorised-contrast caveat in both directions (omitted covariance can widen or narrow).
