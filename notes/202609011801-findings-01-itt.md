# Findings: the `itt` family — did the intervention work?

**Read `findings-00-overview` first** (`notes/202609011800-findings-00-overview.md`) for the study designs, the two datasets, and how to read credible intervals and evidence labels. This note covers the 31 models in the `itt` family, which carry the project's headline trial results. Every number below is read from the 2026-09-01 full rebuild (`notes/202609011030-full-rebuild-both-layers.md`), in which all 31 fits passed the convergence gate with zero divergences and all 31 are publishable.

## The data

**RLI trial only.** All 31 models use the randomised **timepoint 1 to timepoint 2 window** — the first period, before the waiting-list group started the intervention. This is the single window in the whole study where one randomised group has been treated and the other has not.

Each model uses **one row per child** from timepoint 2. The ordinary score models include the child's own timepoint-1 score on the same measure as a precision covariate. The phonetic-spelling and nonword-reading headline models restrict the analysis to children observed at zero on that measure at timepoint 1, model whether the timepoint-2 score is above zero, and do not include the graded baseline score. Nothing is stacked across periods, and later timepoints are not used.

Most ordinary primary models fit **54 children**; word reading and expressive grammar each fit 53 because one archived child lacks the required outcome. Phonetic spelling fits 41 and nonword reading 36, because their post-hoc headline estimands include only children observed at the floor at baseline and with the required post-score. The SES-adjusted comparisons drop to 33–34 because socio-economic data are missing for about a third of the sample.

## What the model is for

The question is deliberately narrow: **for children assigned to the intervention rather than the waiting list, how different was the timepoint-2 score — or, for the two exploratory floor-rule models, the chance of moving above zero?**

For the ordinary score outcomes the design is an ANCOVA: the outcome regressed on assigned group, adjusting for the child's own baseline score and for age. Those two covariates are there for **precision**, not to repair confounding — randomisation already handles that on average. Each unadjusted primary model uses the outcome's own baseline and no other skill measure, because adding post-treatment skills could condition on the causal path. The registered ability-, SES- and site-adjusted robustness models add only their declared pre-treatment covariates.

Ordinary scores are modelled as bounded counts with a Beta-Binomial likelihood and reported in **items**. Phonetic spelling and nonword reading use a Bernoulli likelihood for moving above zero and are reported as risk differences in percentage points. One likelihood detail changed since the August notes: standardised expressive vocabulary (`itt-006`) now uses the inverse-square-root dispersion prior adopted for high-denominator outcomes under the #577 review (#579), which is why its interval is narrower than receptive vocabulary's despite the same 170-item scale.

## How to read the results

Each row gives the estimated difference between arms in items (percentage points for the two off-floor outcomes), with the 89% credible range and the posterior probability that the effect is positive. **Positive means the intervention arm did better.** These are **available-case modified intention-to-treat estimates**: they start from the children in the archive with the required observations, so they do not automatically estimate the effect for all 57 randomised children.

"pp" is percentage points: for the two floored measures the quantity is the arm difference in the chance of scoring above zero at timepoint 2 **among children observed at zero at baseline**, not a change in score for the full analysed cohort. Expressive information is modelled on its doubled half-mark scale (out of 80); the whole-mark comparator `itt-129` gives +0.1 of 40 (89% −1.6 to +1.8), the same answer.

**The pattern is compatible with effects concentrated on directly taught skills.** The largest and best-supported estimates are for letter-sound knowledge, the specific vocabulary items taught, phoneme blending and word reading. Estimates are smaller for material that was not taught, and the broad standardised vocabulary estimates sit at zero with intervals several items wide in both directions.

**Size and direction are separate claims.** Against the project's post-hoc practical thresholds, the probability that the benefit is at least the threshold is 91% for letter sounds (threshold 2 items), 90% for word reading (1 item), 78% for taught expressive vocabulary and 69% for taught receptive vocabulary (1 item each). For broad receptive and expressive vocabulary the probabilities of a 2-item benefit are 24% and 12%, and 58% and 79% of the posterior sits inside the ±2-item region of practical equivalence. A benefit of that size is unlikely under these models, not ruled out.

**Taught versus not-taught vocabulary.** Taught vocabulary (TR +1.4, TE +1.5) is better supported than the not-taught items from the same blocks (UR +0.6, UE +0.3). Not-taught receptive vocabulary reaches moderate directional evidence with 84% of its posterior inside the 1-item equivalence region; not-taught expressive vocabulary has 95% inside it. Some generalisation beyond the taught items is permitted by these data without establishing how much; the fitted taught-minus-not-taught contrasts are in the `joint` note.

**Phoneme blending needs its pair.** Blending is a three-choice task, so a guessing child scores about a third. `itt-008` uses the ordinary model (+1.0 items) and `itt-108` holds the expected score at or above chance (+0.5 items, 89% −0.1 to +1.1, P = 0.89, suggestive). Neither may be quoted without the other — the release gate requires the validated trace-backed pair — and the honest summary is that blending improved, with the size halved by how guessing is handled.

## Robustness

- **Ability adjustment** (`itt-017`–`024`) leaves the picture unchanged: letter sounds +3.5 (identical), word reading +2.2 against +2.4, taught expressive +1.5 (identical), taught receptive +1.3 against +1.4, and the four remaining vocabulary outcomes within 0.1 items of their primaries. It covers 8 of the 15 primaries; blending, the two floored outcomes, basic concepts, grammar and the two APT scales have no ability-adjusted counterpart.
- **Socio-economic adjustment** on the reduced sample: word reading +2.5 (89% +0.3 to +4.7) adjusted against +2.4 (+0.5 to +4.3) on the same 33 children; letter sounds +3.4 (+1.2 to +5.6) adjusted against +3.9 (+1.8 to +6.0) on the same 34. The matched comparators rule out the sample restriction as the source of any difference.
- **Site adjustment**: word reading +2.6 (+0.8 to +4.3), letter sounds +3.5 (+1.7 to +5.3) — unchanged.
- **Prior sensitivity.** The power-scaling check classifies the treatment prior as pulling towards zero in 13 fits (including L, B, P, N, F and their adjusted variants); their key findings say the size is best read as a lower bound. No treatment term is prior-dominant.

## The word-reading caveat you should not skip

Four of the 57 randomised children have no timepoint-2 word-reading score, and the missing-outcome analysis materially qualifies the headline:

| Analysis                                                 | Estimate (items) | 89% range    |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------ |
| Headline (53 observed outcomes)                          | +2.4             | +0.7 to +4.1 |
| Same 53, using screening word reading and age            | +2.0             | 0.0 to +4.0  |
| All 57 standardised under a missing-at-random assumption | +2.0             | 0.0 to +4.0  |
| Completing both randomised arms, zero-delta anchor       | +1.7             | −0.3 to +3.7 |
| Intervention non-starter given the control mean surface  | +1.7             | −0.3 to +3.6 |
| Fixed delta grid, range of posterior medians             | +0.7 to +2.4     | —            |
| **Model-free extreme case**                              | **−6.3 to +4.9** | —            |

Under reasonable missingness assumptions the effect stays around +2 items and positive. The final row is the honest bound: if the four missing children could have had any outcome, the direction can reverse. The result is not assumption-free and should not be quoted without this envelope.

**The other headline outcomes have the same model-free bound, and it is firmer.** Letter sounds and the two taught-vocabulary tests are complete at timepoint 2 for all 54 analysed children, so only the three children lost to follow-up are missing, and completing them at the floor or ceiling in the least and most favourable ways cannot reverse the direction:

| Outcome                              | Observed raw t2 arm difference | Extreme-case bound (items) |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Letter sounds (of 32)                | +5.9                           | **+4.1 to +7.4**           |
| Taught expressive (of 24)            | +1.7                           | **+0.2 to +2.7**           |
| Taught receptive (of 24)             | +1.5                           | **+0.2 to +2.8**           |
| Word reading (of 79), for comparison | +1.6                           | −6.3 to +4.9               |

These bound the raw, unadjusted arm difference, not the covariate-adjusted estimates above, so they speak to direction rather than size.

## The Action Picture Test outcomes

**Expressive grammar** is the strongest lean among the outcomes the intervention did not teach directly: +1.0 marks of 37 (89% −0.5 to +2.5), P = 0.85, suggestive, with a 49% chance of reaching the 1-mark threshold. The arm-by-wave `did-015` fit gives +1.6 marks (−0.2 to +3.4) at P = 0.93, moderate. Neither clears zero; this is a consistent lean across two designs on a measure of everyday expressive language, not a finding.

**Expressive information** is inconclusive under both encodings (+0.3 of 80 half marks; +0.1 of 40 whole marks), with 65–66% of the posterior inside the practically-negligible region. The two encodings agree because the overdispersion parameter absorbs the doubled trial count, as the August analysis established.

## What changed since the August notes

The rebuild matches the August randomised-contrast numbers to within a tenth of an item for every outcome except expressive vocabulary, whose interval narrowed from about ±3.3 to ±2.6 items under the #579 dispersion prior; its direction probability is unchanged at 0.53. No direction, evidence label or release status changed in this family.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Only the assigned-group contrast is causal**, subject to the analysis-set assumptions above. Baseline, age and ability coefficients are precision terms, not effects. **These are estimates for the analysed children**; extending them to all 57 needs the missing-data assumptions above. **Nothing here explains _why_ it worked** — see the `mediation` and `mechanism` families, whose answers are associations. **Two measures required exploratory subgroup analyses** (P and N), so their headlines describe only children observed at the floor at baseline.
