# One runner for every sub-fit (#394, design point 5)

**Date:** 2026-08-08. **Issue:** #394 (complete the statistical pipeline family split and artefact lifecycle refactor), design point 5 and acceptance criterion "every secondary or sensitivity fit records convergence and sampling provenance through one shared runner". **Change:** behavioural, not a relocation — every published number is preserved byte-for-byte, and each affected fit gains one new artefact.

## What a sub-fit is, and what was wrong with how we ran them

A _sub-fit_ is any posterior the suite samples besides the primary one. There are eleven call sites across five families: the two floor-rule secondaries (the graded Beta-Binomial cross-check over all children, and the graded contrast among the off-floor subset), the mediation temporal-ordering sensitivity at t3, the adjusted family's per-predictor bivariate refits, its prior-slope sweep and its SES complete-case refit (twice over, since the Byrne port has the same three), and the concurrent and joint-mechanism non-anchor waves. Every one of them publishes numbers into the report exactly as the primary fit does, and every one of them bypasses `diagnostics_summary.json`, because the convergence gate covers `ctx.trace` alone.

They were run three different ways. Eight went through `diagnostics.sample_subfit`, which sampled and handed back a `(trace, verdict)` pair, leaving the caller to remember to publish the verdict. Three — the two floor secondaries and the t3 sensitivity — wrote out their own `pm.sample` call, so a sampling argument could drift between them with nothing to catch it. And none of them recorded two things a reader auditing a published sensitivity estimate needs: **what data the sub-fit was actually fitted to**, and **at what sampling settings**. A sub-fit that differs from the primary in its _rows_ — the SES refit drops children with missing SES, the off-floor secondary keeps 19 of 53 — looked identical in the output to one that differs only in its prior.

There was a third gap, quieter than either. `subfit_convergence` absorbs its own failures: it warns on the console and returns `converged=None`. In a published CSV that is an empty cell, which reads exactly like a column nobody filled in. A sub-fit whose gate could not be _evaluated_ was indistinguishable from one that was never checked.

## What is in the runner

`statistical_models/subfits.py` holds `run_subfit`, now the only way to sample a sub-fit, and the typed `SubfitResult` it returns:

- the **trace**, and the **convergence verdict verbatim** — the `subfit_convergence` dict with its key order intact, because families merge it straight into a published row and that key order is the CSV's column order;
- the **fitted-data identity**: the prepared frame's child and observation counts, the observed nodes with their shapes, and a short SHA-256 digest over the observed arrays themselves. The digest is the part that earns its place: two sub-fits can produce identically shaped observations from different children, and counting rows cannot tell them apart;
- the **sampling settings actually used**, so "sampled as its parent run was" is a recorded fact rather than an assumption;
- the **persisted trace filename**, where the family persists one;
- a **structured failure**, which names the two distinguishable ways a verdict can come back unusable (`convergence_unavailable` when the R-hat/ESS summary itself failed, `divergences_unavailable` when `sample_stats` carries no `diverging` variable). Both are derived from the verdict dict the check already returns, so nothing about the check changed.

Each call appends a row to `subfit_provenance.csv`. That table is rewritten on every call rather than assembled at finalisation, deliberately: a record of what a run did is worth least if it is the first casualty of the failure it exists to document.

Two choices are worth stating because they were choices. `convergence_scope` keeps both scans available — `free_rvs` (the sub-model's free random variables, which ten of the eleven sites use) and `all` (unrestricted, which is _stricter_, since ArviZ then summarises deterministics too, and which the t3 sensitivity has always used). Imposing one would have silently changed a published verdict. And sub-fit PSIS-LOO is deliberately **not** wired: the issue lists it as optional and no sub-fit computes one — a sensitivity refit is read against the primary's estimate, not ranked against it — so wiring it would have added an untested branch.

`diagnostics.sample_subfit` is gone, replaced by a comment where it stood. `subfit_convergence` stays in `diagnostics.py`: the prior-sensitivity sweep scripts and the influence/blending sensitivity modules check traces they sampled themselves.

## Scope, stated rather than implied

The runner covers every sub-fit **inside a family pipeline**. It does not cover the post-hoc sweep tools — `influence.py` and the seven `scripts/*_prior_sensitivity.py` runners — which sample their own refits outside any family fit and carry their own provenance conventions, including the hash-suffixed trace validation from #489. Bringing them onto the runner is separate work; the new structural guard is scoped to `pipelines/` so it cannot quietly assert otherwise.

## Two guards

`test_no_family_module_samples_a_posterior_of_its_own` asserts no module under `pipelines/` contains a `pm.sample(` call. Exactly two modules in the package sample: `diagnostics.sample_posterior` for the primary fit and `subfits.run_subfit` for sub-fits. An inline `pm.sample` in a family module is how three of them drifted apart in the first place, so it now fails a test.

`test_subfits.py` covers the bookkeeping without sampling: the digest separating same-shaped different-row fits, the provenance row matching a schema derived from the row builder itself (so the two cannot drift), the uncheckable verdict being named rather than left blank, a missing prepared frame degrading to nulls rather than failing a fit, and the log surviving a context with no output directory — which is design point 6's testability requirement applied to this module.

`subfits.py` joins the incremental MyPy gate.

## Verification

Seven dev fits covering all eleven sites — `itt-009` (both floor secondaries), `med-059` (t3 sensitivity), `adj-065` and `rlm-adj-001` (bivariate, prior sweep, SES, in both cohorts), `ca-001` (per-wave and per-predictor bivariate), `jm-001` (per-wave) — plus `itt-001` as a sub-fit-free control, run from `main` in a detached worktree and again from this branch.

**113 of 113 pre-existing CSVs are byte-identical**, with one new table per fit that has sub-fits and nothing else added or lost. The published verdicts match to the last digit: `itt-009`'s graded secondary reports `converged=True` at `max_rhat=1.003323200430603` in both runs, and `med-059`'s t3 sensitivity reports `converged=False` in both (a dev-tier artefact of short chains — min ESS 391 against the 400 threshold — that predates this change and is exactly the kind of flag the provenance table now explains rather than merely asserts).

**55 sub-fits are recorded** across the six models: 27 in `ca-001` (four waves plus every wave-predictor bivariate), 14 in `adj-065`, 8 in `rlm-adj-001`, 3 in `jm-001`, 2 in `itt-009`, 1 in `med-059`, and none in the control. The tables are coherent — one row per sub-fit, a digest on every row, one distinct value per sampling setting within a fit, and no recorded verdict absent from the family tables. `adj-065` is the clearest illustration of what was missing: its eleven bivariate refits and two prior-sweep refits share one data digest on 51 children, and the SES complete-case refit carries a different digest on 39. Nothing in the published output distinguished them before.

The two persisted floor-rule traces move from `untracked` to `written` in `artifact_manifest.json`, since the runner records them through `record_artifact` the way `save_trace` always has. No manifest's untracked set grew: the figure-data sidecars and the shared prior/posterior-predictive writers are untracked on both sides of this change, and bringing them onto the interface is step 2's remaining tail, not this tranche's.

Full suite, `ruff check src/ scripts/ tests/`, `mypy`, `npm run format:check` and `npm run spellcheck` pass.
