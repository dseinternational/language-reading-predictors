# Findings 02 — the joint models (cross-outcome consistency and the taught/not-taught contrasts)

Reports every model in the `joint` family from the 2026-08-04/05 `reporting` refit. **4 models, all passing the convergence gate.** Reading conventions in note 00; the single-outcome results these extend are in note 01. Preliminary research data — all estimates provisional.

## What these models do

The ITT suite fits one model per outcome. That answers "did the intervention move letter sounds?" but cannot answer "**did it move taught words more than matched not-taught words?**" — because comparing two separately fitted models has no principled uncertainty for the difference. The joint models fit several outcomes together, so a within-model contrast between outcomes is available with a proper posterior.

**Design.** The same randomised t1→t2 window, the same Beta-Binomial likelihood and the same precision terms as the single-outcome models, but with the outcomes stacked into one likelihood and an optional LKJ residual correlation block linking a child's outcomes. Because the flattened likelihood pools measures with different maxima, every per-outcome quantity is selected by the persisted cell map rather than read off the pooled node.

**What is causal.** The per-outcome τ, on the same available-case terms as note 01. The **contrasts between outcomes** are also randomisation-anchored — both outcomes are measured on the same children under the same assignment — which is what makes these models worth fitting.

## `itt-012` — the full ten-outcome joint model

Fits TR, TE, UR, UE, RV, EV, LS, PA, PS and WR together. Its purpose is consistency: does estimating the outcomes jointly move any conclusion from the single-outcome suite?

| Outcome                    | Joint effect (89%)  | P(>0) | Single-outcome (note 01) |
| -------------------------- | ------------------- | ----: | ------------------------ |
| Letter sounds (LS)         | +3.6 (+1.7 to +5.4) | 0.998 | +3.5                     |
| Word reading (WR)          | +2.4 (+0.7 to +4.1) | 0.988 | +2.4                     |
| Phonetic spelling (PS)     | +1.8 (−1.2 to +4.8) | 0.839 | floor-rule branch        |
| Taught expressive (TE)     | +1.6 (+0.4 to +2.7) | 0.986 | +1.5                     |
| Taught receptive (TR)      | +1.3 (+0.1 to +2.5) | 0.961 | +1.4                     |
| Receptive vocabulary (RV)  | +1.0 (−3.0 to +5.1) | 0.656 | +0.2                     |
| Phoneme blending (PA)      | +0.9 (+0.1 to +1.7) | 0.971 | +1.0 (link-sensitive)    |
| Not-taught receptive (UR)  | +0.7 (−0.0 to +1.4) | 0.941 | +0.6                     |
| Not-taught expressive (UE) | +0.4 (−0.4 to +1.1) | 0.796 | +0.3                     |
| Expressive vocabulary (EV) | −0.1 (−3.5 to +3.3) | 0.484 | +0.2                     |

**The suite is internally consistent.** Nine of the ten outcomes have an items-scale counterpart in note 01 (PS does not — its registered estimand there is the floor-rule risk difference), and **seven of those nine agree to within about 0.1 items**. The two that move — RV (+0.2 → +1.0) and EV (+0.2 → −0.1) — are precisely the two whose single-outcome results were already inconclusive with ±3–4-item intervals; the joint model shifts them within that noise and they remain inconclusive (P = 0.66 and 0.48). Nothing here changes a conclusion.

Note PS appears here as a graded count (+1.8 items, inconclusive) because the joint likelihood is graded throughout. Its **registered** estimand is the floor-rule risk difference in note 01, not this row. The cross-model comparison also excludes PA from the τ-consistency table by design, because its conclusion is response-link sensitive and the joint model uses only the ordinary logit.

## The three contrast models — and the generalisation question

These are the models that answer the transfer question directly. Each fits exactly two outcomes and reports the posterior probability that one arm-effect exceeds the other.

| Model     | Contrast                                                | P(first > second) | Reading      |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------: | ------------ |
| `itt-015` | Taught expressive (TE) **>** not-taught expressive (UE) |          **0.76** | suggestive   |
| `itt-115` | Taught receptive (TR) **>** not-taught receptive (UR)   |          **0.47** | inconclusive |
| `itt-016` | Taught expressive (TE) **>** taught receptive (TR)      |          **0.57** | inconclusive |

**This is more cautious than the single-outcome table looks, and it is the finding to carry forward.** Reading note 01 alone, taught receptive gains +1.4 items against not-taught +0.6, which invites the conclusion that the intervention's vocabulary effect is teaching-specific. The properly-estimated contrast does not support that for the receptive modality: **P(TR > UR) = 0.47 — the taught and not-taught receptive words are indistinguishable.** For the expressive modality the contrast leans the expected way but only suggestively (P = 0.76).

The reason the two readings differ is that the taught and not-taught sets are measured on the same children, so their arm-effects are correlated; the difference has less uncertainty attached to it than two independent intervals suggest, but it also has a much smaller point estimate than the raw gap between two separately-fitted numbers implies. **Comparing two credible intervals by eye is not a test of their difference**, and here it would have given the wrong answer.

`itt-016` finds no modality difference: taught expressive and taught receptive vocabulary move about equally (P = 0.57, inconclusive). The intervention does not preferentially help production over comprehension of the words it teaches, or vice versa.

## What this family establishes

1. **The single-outcome ITT results are robust to joint estimation.** No conclusion changes.
2. **Direct evidence of teaching-specific vocabulary transfer is weak.** Suggestive for expressive words, absent for receptive. Any claim that the intervention's vocabulary gains are specific to taught items should be stated at that strength — this is the model built to answer exactly that question, and it declines to give a clean answer.
3. **No modality asymmetry** in the taught words.

## Caveats

- Same available-case caveat as note 01: these are effects in the fitted sample, not for all 57 randomised children. This family additionally writes `attrition_bounds.csv`, which is where to look before extending any claim to the full randomised set.
- The LKJ residual-correlation block is included where the design calls for it, but at n ≈ 54 it is weakly identified; the τ estimates are the deliverable here, not the residual correlations.
- Items are not comparable across measures — the contrasts above are _within_ a matched taught/not-taught pair of the same length (24 vs 12 items), which is exactly why those pairs were constructed. Do not extend the comparison to, say, LS versus WR.
- All the note-01 caveats about δ being post-hoc, winner's curse and predictive overcoverage apply unchanged.
