# Findings: the `lcsm` family — do changes in one skill precede changes in another?

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 5 latent change-score models. **Nothing here is causal.**

## The data

**RLI trial only**, using **all four timepoints**: 54 children contributing 216 rows (four waves each). Data are stacked, not collapsed — the model needs the full sequence per child, because it is built around the transitions between consecutive waves.

## What the model is for

The `concurrent` family measures everything simultaneously and therefore cannot say what precedes what. This family adds **temporal ordering**: does a child's level of one skill at one wave predict how much _another_ skill **changes** by the next wave?

The model works in changes rather than levels. For each child and each interval it forms a latent change score — how much the underlying skill moved — and asks whether that change is predicted by a _prior_ level (a "g" coupling) or by a _prior change_ (an "h" coupling). "Latent" here means the model separates the underlying skill from measurement noise, using the Beta-Binomial structure to describe how observed test scores arise from it.

Coefficients are reported in log-odds units per standard deviation of the predictor. Couplings are held constant across arms and transitions.

## What was found

| Coupling                                                                   | Estimate (logit units) | 89% range      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------- |
| Prior taught receptive vocabulary → change in taught expressive vocabulary | **+0.42**              | +0.25 to +0.62 |
| Prior letter sounds → change in word reading (`lcsm-082`)                  | **+0.24**              | +0.14 to +0.36 |
| Prior letter-sound _change_ → change in word reading (`lcsm-091`)          | **+0.22**              | +0.12 to +0.33 |
| Age → change in reading                                                    | **−0.15**              | −0.21 to −0.09 |

**Letter-sound knowledge leads word reading in time.** Both the level-to-change coupling (+0.24) and the change-to-change coupling (+0.22) are positive with intervals clear of zero. This is the temporal pattern the letter-sound story predicts, and it adds something the `concurrent` family could not: at least the ordering runs the expected way.

**Taught receptive vocabulary precedes taught expressive vocabulary** (+0.42, the largest coupling in the family) — children recognise taught words before they produce them. That is developmentally unsurprising and is a useful sanity check that the machinery detects a relationship whose direction is independently known.

**Age predicts _slower_ reading change** (−0.15). Read carefully: this does not mean children get worse. Within this sample and window, older children gained less than younger children, conditional on everything else in the model. With 54 children across a narrow age band, and age correlated with starting level, this is a fragile quantity and should not be built on.

A comparator model (`lcsm-181`) that removes the reverse coupling gives +0.45 for the vocabulary relationship against +0.42 with it, so the vocabulary result does not depend on modelling the reverse path.

## How much weight temporal precedence carries

Less than it appears. Temporal ordering rules out the simplest reverse-causation story, but it does not establish causation. A stable third factor — general ability, home literacy environment, schooling — that drives both letter-sound growth and reading growth will produce exactly this pattern, because it was already raising letter sounds before it raised reading.

The `mechanism` family's negative-control result, where letter sounds tracked oral-language outcomes they should not have, is direct evidence that such a common factor is present in these data. That evidence applies here too.

There is also a statistical caution specific to change-score models: they are demanding of data. Four waves and 54 children is modest, and separating latent change from measurement noise consumes information rapidly. The intervals above reflect that.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Temporal precedence is not causation.** A common cause acting earlier produces the same pattern.

**Couplings are held constant across arms and transitions.** If the relationship differed during intervention versus after crossover, this model would average over that difference rather than reveal it.

**Nothing here is a treatment effect.** These are within-child associations pooled across arms.

**The age coefficient is not a claim about ageing.** It is a within-sample association over a narrow band.

## Model inventory

All 5 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and are publishable: `lcsm-067` (letter sounds and vocabulary as predictors of reading change), `081` (reverse coupling from word reading), `082` (reciprocal blending and word reading), `091` (change-on-change), `181` (no-reverse-coupling comparator).
