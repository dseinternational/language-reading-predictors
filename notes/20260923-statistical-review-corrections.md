## Decisions that affect future runs

**Permutation scope.** Restricting donors to identical schedules avoids imputation and cross-wave substitution. It changes the question to importance conditional on the observed schedule. A predictor constant within a schedule cannot move, even if it differs across schedules. A child with a unique schedule also stays fixed. The pooled score still weights rows equally, as before. `permutation_schedule_support.csv` reports eligible donor support; `stability_permutation_support.csv` records each bootstrap's support and whether it was used. A bootstrap with no alternative donors is excluded. This restriction and the exclusions must be read beside the ranking.

**Integration checks.** The largest absolute split discrepancy over posterior draws and children, multiplied by the number of children, must not exceed the ELPD standard error. Neither may the largest sum of absolute pointwise ELPD differences across the full and split batches. Every batch needs valid Pareto diagnostics. These are conservative stability checks, not bounds on integration error. Two finite batches can miss the same region. A failing run needs more integration evidence or the existing grouped child-level refit route. The stricter rule may withhold results that the former check allowed.

**Dependence summaries.** The exact identity is `Var(A - B) = Var(A) + Var(B) - 2 Cov(A, B)`. The new table stores sample moments from the same paired posterior draws and marks their method as `paired_ame_draws_v1`. The reader checks that identity and the covariance constraint. The difference in variance between two fits can then be split algebraically into marginal-variance and covariance changes. This does not decompose equal-tailed interval widths or identify which model component caused a change. An SD ratio remains only a comparison of spread.

**Prior sensitivity.** The existing release categories and requirements for sensitivity fits are retained. The correction removes unsupported claims from their explanation. A power-scaling flag does not establish a stable sign, attenuation towards zero or a lower bound. The response of the named estimand to justified prior changes still needs examination. See Kallioinen et al., DOI [10.1007/s11222-023-10366-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-023-10366-5).

**Prediction and timing targets.** Fixed-threshold Huber loss need not target the conditional mean; RMSE scoring does not change that fact. See Feng and Wu, DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2009.12755](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2009.12755). For unequal assessment intervals, a separate intercept does not establish a common time-scale coefficient. Voelkle et al. discuss the distinction between discrete and continuous-time panel models, DOI [10.1037/a0027543](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0027543). These references support the methodological distinctions, not a claim that the study's fitted effects have changed by a known amount.
