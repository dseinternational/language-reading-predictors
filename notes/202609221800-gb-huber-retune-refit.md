# Gradient-boosting retune and refit under the Huber objective, 2026-09-22

> [!NOTE]
> Drafted by a LLM-based AI tool (Claude Code/Fable 5.1).

Date: 2026-09-22 — **Status: EXECUTION RECORD** of an author decision. The author adopted the Huber objective for both boosting families after the [objective-sensitivity check](202609221700-gb-objective-sensitivity.md) (PR #686). This note records the retune of all 50 LightGBM models under that objective, the promotion of the tuned parameters, the reporting-tier refit and render, and the figure changes made in the same batch. It covers the gradient-boosting layer only. Nothing was published to the public research site.

## Decision and what changed

1. **Objective.** Every `lrp-rli-gbg` and `lrp-rli-gbl` model now uses LightGBM's Huber objective in place of MAE. Huber loss is quadratic for residuals within a threshold and linear beyond it, so fitted values are robust conditional means: the bulk of the sample is fitted as under squared error while the few extreme scorers that motivated MAE cannot dominate the trees. The tuning score is RMSE, so the tuning metric, the objective and the RMSE-based permutation importance now target the same quantity. The sensitivity check found that MAE placed the word-reading gain model's age effect on a single threshold, made the word-reading level model's partial dependence a staircase, and tuned that model to 11 trees; Huber and squared error spread those effects into continuous curves and raised out-of-fold R² in all four models checked.
2. **Threshold.** The Huber threshold `alpha` is a per-model constant, not a searched hyperparameter: 1.345 × 1.4826 × MAD of the target the tuner sees (the same rows the fit uses), falling back to 1.345 × the mean absolute deviation from the median when the MAD is zero. The 1.345 factor is Huber's tuning constant giving 95% asymptotic efficiency at the normal (Huber, 1964, DOI [10.1214/aoms/1177703732](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732)); 1.4826 makes the MAD a consistent estimate of the normal standard deviation. `models/objective.py` implements the rule, `scripts/tune_model.py --alpha-rule robust-mad` applies it and records the derivation in `best_params.json`, and the batch orchestrator checks it on resume. Three targets have a zero MAD because more than half their rows sit at the floor and take the fallback: phonetic spelling gain (`gbg-011`, alpha 12.37), phonetic spelling level (`gbl-011`, 25.40) and nonword reading level (`gbl-013`, 1.66). The four thresholds used in the sensitivity check are reproduced exactly.
3. **Figures.** The permutation-importance box plot, both SHAP bar plots and the default SHAP scatter set now show the ten leading predictors (by mean importance for the box plot and scatters, by mean |SHAP| for the bars, with the remainder collapsed into one row). The CSV tables still list every predictor. `plot_top_n` is recorded in each `config.json`. The 15-predictor partial-dependence grid and the beeswarm are unchanged.
4. **Waterfalls.** Each model now has three further SHAP waterfalls, for the observations whose observed outcome lies nearest the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles of the fitted target (ties go to the smallest absolute in-sample residual, then the earliest row). `shap_waterfall_observations.csv` records the child, wave, observed and predicted values for each, alongside the existing representative-observation waterfall. The word-reading gain template now globs its baseline-coloured scatters by rank instead of naming five predictors, so it follows the fitted top ten.
5. **Wording.** The 50 report templates, `METHODS.md`, the model catalogue and the two GB skills describe the Huber objective; the tuner and batch scripts default to it.

## Review and promotion

**Complexity.** The median tree count barely moves (126 under MAE, 132 under Huber), but individual models move a great deal: 11 gain models and 13 level models have fewer trees than before, and the three largest reductions are not-taught expressive vocabulary gain (`gbg-004`, 597 → 53), vowel articulation gain (`gbg-021`, 382 → 41) and not-taught receptive vocabulary gain (`gbg-003`, 366 → 59). The three largest increases are word-reading level (`gbl-012`, 11 → 142; the staircase model that prompted the check), nonword reading gain (`gbg-013`, 109 → 1191 at learning rate 0.011) and total repetition gain (`gbg-019`, 10 → 76). Two models remain very small: receptive grammar gain (`gbg-015`) at 12 trees and phoneme-blending level (`gbl-010`) at 20. Under the standing policy a small tree count is read as weak predictive signal, not as a reason to change the search. The fold-to-fold standard deviation of the tuning RMSE is about half its mean in the typical model, as expected with near leave-one-child-out folds; it is descriptive, not a confidence interval.

**Boundaries.** Thirty models have at least one tuned value within 2% of a search-space limit. The common ones are `max_depth` at its floor of 3 (10 models), `min_child_samples` at its floor of 4 (7) and `subsample` at its floor of 0.6 (6); `max_depth` at 12 (4), `num_leaves` at either limit (6), the regularisation floors (2), and one each of `learning_rate` at 0.2 and 0.01, `colsample_bytree` at either limit and `subsample` at 1.0 make up the rest. Shallow trees, small leaves and strong row subsampling are the conservative end of the space and are plausible for 150–215 rows; they were accepted as tuned. No model reached the 2000-tree ceiling, and no value sits at a limit that would call for widening the search.

**Verdict.** All 50 tuned parameter sets were promoted without manual adjustment, keeping one uniform policy, as in the July batch. The promotion script wrote each module's values in place, preserving its key schema (18 modules carry `random_state`), inserted `alpha` after `objective`, renamed `_LGBM_MAE_PARAMS` to `_LGBM_HUBER_PARAMS`, replaced the provenance comment, and rewrote the MAE wording in docstrings, descriptions and notes. `tests/test_models.py`, `tests/test_borrowed_params.py` and `tests/test_type_coverage.py` pass, and a development-tier fit of all 50 models ran clean. The comparison of old and new settings under the same grouped evaluation is the refit below: the 22 September MAE fits and the Huber fits both report pooled out-of-fold metrics at the reporting tier on the same child-grouped folds.

## What changed in the fitted models

`notes/assets/202609221800-huber-vs-mae-fits.csv` (with a Markdown rendering and the comparison script beside it) sets each Huber fit against the backed-up 22 September MAE fit of the same model: pooled out-of-fold metrics at the reporting tier on the same child-grouped folds, permutation-importance rank agreement, the five leading predictors, replicated labels (permutation z ≥ 2 and top-five appearance ≥ 0.5 over the bootstrap draws) and SHAP direction. Both sides are post-selection cross-validation numbers.

**Fit quality.** Pooled out-of-fold R² rises in 34 of 50 models and RMSE falls in the same 34. Gain models move by a median +0.018 in R² (16 of 22 improve; range −0.069 to +0.096) and level models by +0.009 (18 of 28; range −0.050 to +0.091). The largest gains are expressive information gain (`gbg-007`, +0.096), basic concept gain (`gbg-014`, +0.095), phonetic spelling gain (`gbg-011`, +0.092) and language-sample mean length of utterance level (`gbl-024`, +0.091); the largest losses are not-taught expressive vocabulary gain (`gbg-004`, −0.069), total repetition gain (`gbg-019`, −0.059), language-sample maximum utterance length level (`gbl-025`, −0.050) and taught receptive vocabulary level (`gbl-001`, −0.045). Mean absolute error barely moves (median change +0.001 in gains, +0.007 in levels; lower in 24 of 50), as expected when a median-targeting objective is replaced by a mean-targeting one. The median in-sample R² rises from 0.65 to 0.69, so the train-to-test gap widens slightly. Word-reading level, the model that prompted the check, goes from 11 to 142 trees and from R² 0.578 to 0.610; word-reading gain from 0.083 to 0.127.

**Rankings.** The leading predictor is unchanged in 44 of 50 models. The six changes are between predictors that were already near the top: average articulation gain (`gbg-022`, fine articulation → average articulation), receptive vocabulary level (`gbl-005`, Block 1 receptive total → expressive vocabulary), phoneme-blending level (`gbl-010`, word reading → nonword reading), basic concept level (`gbl-014`, Block 1 receptive total → expressive vocabulary), and the two language-sample levels `gbl-024` and `gbl-025`, which swap word repetition and initial-consonant articulation. The median top-five overlap is four of five in both families. Spearman agreement of the full permutation-importance vectors has a median of 0.59 in gain models and 0.65 in level models, with seven models below 0.4 (`gbg-014`, `gbg-016`, `gbg-017`, `gbg-020`, `gbg-022`, `gbl-019`, `gbl-028`). The sensitivity check's noise floor, re-tuning the same objective, gave 0.53–0.95 on four models, so these agreements are within the range that re-tuning alone produces; differences below the leading places should not be read as objective effects. The number of replicated labels falls from 137 to 134 model–predictor pairs. Twelve predictors in ten models change SHAP direction among those with z ≥ 2 in either fit; most are predictors that cross z = 2 in only one fit. The one already noted in the sensitivity check is repeated: receptive taught vocabulary in the nonword level model (`gbl-013`) is positive under MAE and negative under Huber.

**Shapes.** The two figures the sensitivity check singled out did not both behave as that check's Huber arm did, which is itself the tuning-noise point. For word-reading level (`gbl-012`, `-huber-gbl012-pdp.png`), the letter-sound partial dependence is now a smooth accelerating curve, but phonetic spelling remains a staircase because that model still splits it at a handful of thresholds. For word-reading gain (`gbg-012`, `-huber-gbg012-age.png`), the age contribution is still banded: three bands (+1.0 items below 79 months, +0.45 between 80 and 87 and −1.0 above 95) rather than the two under MAE, not the continuous decline the sensitivity check's Huber arm showed. That arm tuned to 94 trees with 19 leaves; the promoted retune, under the same protocol and seed but with the threshold at full precision rather than rounded to 3.99, tuned to 81 trees with 46 leaves at depth 9 and a minimum of 38 rows per leaf, which is what produces flat bands. The direction and span of the age effect are the same in every fit (Spearman of feature against SHAP −0.84 under MAE, −0.90 here). Expressive vocabulary is now second in that model, as the sensitivity check found, and letter sounds falls to sixth. Captions should keep describing the plotted bands as properties of the fitted trees, not as age thresholds.

**Waterfalls.** Every model has its three percentile waterfalls. For word-reading gain they explain a child-wave at the floor (observed 0, predicted −0.4), one at the median gain of 2 (predicted 2.1) and one at the upper quartile of 6 (predicted 5.7).

## Interpretation limits

Pooled held-out R² is internal performance after tuning on the same child-grouped folds, not independent validation, and the retune selected the reported parameters on those folds. Huber fits a robust conditional mean, so the predicted values, the partial-dependence curves and the SHAP base values are on the mean scale, not the median scale of the earlier MAE fits; in-sample and out-of-fold MAE can be slightly higher than under MAE tuning for the same model. Permutation importance and SHAP values describe the fitted predictions: they are associations, not effects of changing a predictor. Same-skill predictors can restate a level outcome, and gain models include the baseline score, so a negative baseline association can reflect score limits or regression to the mean. The sensitivity check found that rankings below the leading places move as much under re-tuning as under a change of objective; differences from the MAE fits below the top few predictors should be read with that in mind. The three percentile waterfalls explain single observations chosen by their outcome value; they illustrate how the model builds a low, a typical and a high prediction and are not a summary of the sample.
