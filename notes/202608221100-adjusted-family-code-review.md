## Finding 1 (functional, major, cross-family): power-scaling likelihood sensitivity is computed on a doubled log-likelihood wherever child-level LOO aggregation is active

`compute_log_likelihood_and_loo` calls `_joint_log_likelihood_by_child`, and when the trace carries `loo_child_idx` (the stacked Byrne transition model) or `y_post_cell_row` (the joint ITT likelihood) it writes the child-summed array back into the trace as `trace.log_likelihood["y_post_child"]` — alongside the row-level `y_post` it was summed from — so PSIS-LOO can run on the child unit. `psense_artifacts` then calls `arviz_stats.psense_summary(trace, var_names=...)` with no `likelihood_var_names`, and arviz-stats sums **every** variable in the `log_likelihood` group for the likelihood power-scaling. The likelihood is therefore counted twice. Reproduced against the stored `lrp-rlm-adj-006-reporting/trace.nc`: the published `psense_summary.csv` gives likelihood sensitivities of 2.078 (`gamma_own`), 3.140 (`sigma_child`) and 3.177 (`kappa`); recomputing on a view that keeps only `y_post`, or only `y_post_child`, gives 1.042 / 1.577 / 1.592 — identical to each other and half the published values, as the doubling predicts (`beta_age` 1.074 → 0.538). A scan of the output tree shows both variables in the `log_likelihood` group of nine fits: `lrp-rlm-adj-006` and the eight joint / joint-mechanism fits `lrp-rli-itt-012`, `-015`, `-016`, `-115`, `-215`, `-216`, `-315` and `lrp-rli-jm-002` (maximum published likelihood sensitivity 1.07–1.39 in `itt-215`, `-216` and `-315`). The diagnosis column is a threshold rule (`prior > 0.05` and `likelihood > 0.05` → "potential prior-data conflict"; `prior > 0.05` and `likelihood < 0.05` → "potential strong prior / weak likelihood"), so a doubled likelihood column can flip the second verdict into the first; in `adj-006` the three flagged rows stay flagged either way, so no verdict changes in this family, but the published numbers are wrong and the joint fits need the same check — the 2026-08-21 joint review did not look for this. Note that the corrected `adj-006` values (1.0–1.6) are still very large: with 225 rows the likelihood dominates those three parameters, which is the expected reading and not a concern. **Recommendation:** pass `likelihood_var_names=["y_post"]` (the row-level node; `run_psense` can read the primary node from the plan) in `psense_artifacts`, or keep the child aggregate out of the `log_likelihood` group (hand `az.loo` a separate `DataTree` view); add a test that a trace with both variables yields the single-variable sensitivities; then regenerate the nine fits' `psense_summary.csv` / `psense.png` with `scripts/regenerate_psense.py` (no refit).

## Finding 2 (statistical reporting, major): every publishable RLM adjusted fit's key-findings headline names the model title as the outcome

`_kf_build_adjusted` builds the headline as "… associated with **{med:+.1f} items** of difference in {outcome_label}", where `outcome_label = _kf_outcome_label(config)`. That helper says it mirrors the `_setup.qmd` derivation, but it only consults the RLI `measures.MEASURES` map and falls back to `config["title"]`; `_setup.qmd` goes on to `resolve_dataset(study_id)` for a non-RLI study and finds the Byrne label. The stored `key_findings.json` therefore reads, verbatim, "a 1-SD increase was associated with **-2.0 items** of difference in Byrne Down-syndrome-only predictors of word-reading gain, waves 1-3 (reduced mutually adjusted set) (89% credible range -4.5 to +0.8)" for `lrp-rlm-adj-002`, and the same title-as-outcome construction for `adj-003` ("… of difference in Byrne wave-1 predictors of receptive-vocabulary gain, waves 1-3 (confirmed-input, mutually adjusted)"), `adj-004`, `adj-005` and `adj-006`; only the RLI `adj-065` ("Word reading (WR)") and the withheld `adj-001` are unaffected. The findings-first box is what most readers see and what `notes/202608181009-findings-20-adjusted.md` quotes, and here it names a model where it should name BAS word reading, BPVS receptive vocabulary, TROG receptive grammar or BAS recall of digits. The other RLM families' builders happen to avoid the helper (horseshoe reports "logit units", concurrent "outcome items", growth names the measure itself), so the defect surfaces in this family. **Recommendation:** give `_kf_outcome_label` the same `resolve_dataset` fallback as `_setup.qmd` (keyed on `config["study_id"]`), add a test with an RLM config, and regenerate the five boxes with `scripts/regenerate_key_findings.py` — remembering the `_partials` sync, since the stored directories carry their fit-time `_setup` / `_diagnostics` / `_priors` copies.

## Finding 3 (statistical reporting, major): the missing-data indicator rows are filtered out of the associations table but not out of the natural-scale table the headline is ranked on

`fit_adjusted` deliberately drops every `*_missing` coefficient from `predictor_associations.csv` and the forest plot — they are subgroup mean-offsets under the missing-indicator method, labelled `nuisance` in the priors table on Frank's #384 decision, and the prior pushforward is driven off the filtered table for exactly that reason. But `_natural_scale_contrasts(ctx, ctx.prepared, headline, …)` is fed the unfiltered `headline`, so `predicted_gain_words.csv` carries the indicator rows: the stored `lrp-rli-adj-065` file has 11 rows against the associations table's 9, including "Hearing missing (indicator)" (+0.3 words) and "Speech missing (indicator)" (−2.0 words, 89% −4.0 to −0.0, P(>0) = 0.053). Two consequences. First, the report's "Natural-Scale Differences" table publishes two rows its own associations table and priors table say are not interpretable predictor associations, directly under the sentence that explains the table "translates a predictor's association into a difference in outcome items". Second, and worse, `_kf_build_adjusted` ranks **this** table by `_kf_most_resolved_row` to choose the headline: in the stored fit `deapp_c_missing` is the second-most resolved row (0.053 against age's 0.004), so had the age association been any less resolved the findings-first box would have headlined "The clearest adjusted predictor was Speech missing (indicator)". Nothing in the tests pins the filter on either artefact. **Recommendation:** apply the same `_missing` mask to the natural-scale table (or pass the filtered predictor list to `_natural_scale_contrasts`) and, belt-and-braces, have `_kf_build_adjusted` ignore `*_missing` predictors; add a test with an indicator row. Regenerable from the stored trace without a refit.

## Finding 4 (statistical, moderate): the `kappa ~ HalfNormal(50)` shape defect diagnosed for the historical families is present in the RLM adjusted span fits on the low-denominator outcomes

The 2026-08-21 historical-families review (finding 8) established that a HalfNormal directly on the Beta-Binomial concentration gives the near-Binomial limit `kappa >> n` essentially zero prior mass at the Byrne denominators (n = 18–90), and Frank's decision reparameterised the **historical** families onto `1/sqrt(kappa) ~ HalfNormal(0.25)` (`inv_sqrt_kappa_prior`), explicitly scoping the change to them. `build_rlm_adjusted_model` and `build_rlm_transition_adjusted_model` still use `kappa_prior()` and the stored posteriors show the same signature on the three confirmed-input wider-outcome fits: `adj-003` (BPVS, n = 32) κ median 96.1, 89% [58, 148], posterior SD 0.94 of the prior's, P(κ > 100) = 0.45 against the prior's 0.046 and P(κ > n) = 1.00; `adj-004` (TROG, n = 20) 89.0 [51, 142], ratio 0.96, P(κ > 100) = 0.36; `adj-005` (digit recall, n = 34) 86.2 [51, 137], ratio 0.90, P(κ > 100) = 0.31. `psense_summary.csv` flags `kappa` in all three at prior sensitivity 0.24–0.26. That is a likelihood wanting "no extra-Binomial dispersion beyond the covariates" and being held back by the prior's shape — the same diagnosis as before, now on the adjusted span model. The word-reading fits are unaffected (n = 90: κ 14.9 in `adj-001`, 45.7 in `adj-002`, data-identified), and `adj-006` is intermediate (74.8, ratio 0.60, P(κ > 100) = 0.11). The predictor slopes — the deliverable — are robust to this (κ is a dispersion nuisance; the historical refits moved growth medians by ≤ 0.1 items), but the posterior-predictive ranges, the PPC coverage statistic and the prior-predictive check are dispersion-driven and are what the prior is pinning. **Recommendation:** a decision from Frank on whether the 2026-08-21 reparameterisation extends to the RLM adjusted factories (and, by the same argument, the RLM horseshoe and concurrent factories on the same denominators); the calibration already worked out there (`HalfNormal(0.25)` on the dispersion scale reproduces the old median variance inflation within 3% at n = 18–90) transfers unchanged. Refit of `adj-003/004/005` (and `006`) if adopted.

## Finding 5 (statistical, moderate): `gamma_own` is the one mean-informative prior, its posterior sits well below the prior mean in every fit, and the family's sensitivity sweep never varies it

`gamma_own_prior` is `Normal(1, 0.25)` — centred on a 1:1 post-on-pre logit coupling and, by its own docstring, "exposed for the required 0.25-vs-0.5 sensitivity rather than treating the tighter value as known". In the stored fits the posterior medians are 0.60 [0.47, 0.74] (`adj-065`), 0.68 (`adj-001`), 0.84 (`adj-002`), 0.60 (`adj-003`), 0.80 (`adj-004`), 0.69 (`adj-005`) and 0.82 (`adj-006`) — one to 1.6 prior SDs below the prior mean — and `psense_summary.csv` flags `gamma_own` as "potential prior-data conflict" in six of the seven fits (prior sensitivity 0.066–0.137). The family's `prior_sensitivity.csv` sweeps only `predictor_slope_sigma` (0.3 → 0.5 → 0.7), and none of the `scripts/*_prior_sensitivity.py` runners covers the adjusted family, so the 0.25-vs-0.5 check is simply never run here. It matters more in this family than in most: the baseline the slope conditions on is itself a member of the correlated general-ability cluster the predictors come from (the module's own DAG draws `g → wpre`), so a prior that pulls the baseline slope toward 1 redistributes shared variance into the partial slopes of whichever predictors correlate with `W_pre` — exactly the coefficients this family reports. **Recommendation:** add a `gamma_own_sigma` row set to the prior sweep (0.25 against 0.5, one sub-fit each, under `run_subfit` with the existing provenance) and report it in `prior_sensitivity.csv`; no primary refit is needed. Whether the family should adopt the wider scale is a decision for Frank once the sweep exists.

## Finding 6 (statistical reporting, moderate): the `adj-006` box calls a repeated-transition random-intercept model "between-child", and the natural-scale contrasts' operating point is never stated and differs between the two designs

_(a)_ `_kf_build_adjusted` ends every box with "This is a between-child adjusted association; it does not identify what would happen if the predictor were changed." The resolved plan for `lrp-rlm-adj-006` records `causal_status = "Adjusted repeated-transition association, not an intervention effect."` and `model_recipe.md` says the same; the builder ignores `transition_waves`, so the findings-first box contradicts the plan and the results partial's own opening sentence for that fit. _(b)_ The span fits' "Natural-Scale Differences" are evaluated at a single operating point (all standardised predictors at 0, own baseline at its sample mean: `alpha + gamma_own · mean(pre_logit)`), whereas the transition fit's are the average over the 225 observed rows of `expit(eta_fixed + β) − expit(eta_fixed)` with the child intercept set to zero — a row-averaged median-child contrast. Neither the partial ("how many items the outcome differs by for a typical change in each predictor") nor the CSV says which, and they are different functionals of the same coefficient. Quantified on the stored traces: for `adj-065` the at-mean age contrast is −2.88 words and the row-averaged one −3.07 (the fitted probability at the operating point is 0.15 against an observed-row mean of 0.21, so the difference is Jensen's inequality at work); for `adj-006` including the child intercept moves the age contrast from −2.74 to −2.70 (σ_child is only 0.20). _(c)_ The #381 prior pushforward uses `convention="forward"`, whose docstring requires it to "match the transform the same family's posterior summary uses"; it is the row-averaged form, so it matches `adj-006` and not the six span fits. None of this changes a direction or an interval materially, but the estimand text should say where the contrast is taken. **Recommendation:** branch the causal sentence on the plan's `transition_waves`; state the operating point in the partial and in `recipe_markdown`; pick one convention for the span fits (at-mean is the cleaner "two children at the sample mean" reading the docstring already gives — then the pushforward should use it too).

## Finding 7 (functional / reporting, moderate): the shared results partial publishes orphaned prose for every RLM fit, and `adj-006`'s child-level LOO is unreliable for 19% of children without the report saying what that means

`_results_adjusted.qmd` ends with a "Sensitivity Checks" SES block and an "Influence" section that read `ses_sensitivity.csv` and `influence.csv` — both written only by the RLI pipeline. The RLM pipelines write neither (they rely on the shared `pareto_k.csv` from `write_loo_influence`, which the partial does not read), so the rendered `lrp-rlm-adj-002` and `-006` reports print "No SES sensitivity table." followed by "_How to read the table:_ when present, it contrasts the associations with and without adjusting for SES…", and "No per-child influence table." followed by "_How to read the table:_ rows are the most influential children (top ten), ranked by how much dropping each one would shift the fit" (verified in the rendered HTML). This is the same orphaned-prose class the `_priors` / `_diagnostics` partials were taught to gate on file presence in the 2026-08-21 reviews. The influence gap is not cosmetic for `adj-006`: its child-level PSIS-LOO (leave one whole child out, with that child's random intercept integrated away) has **16 of 84 children with Pareto-k in (0.7, 1]** and p_loo = 43.7 — the textbook PSIS failure mode for a marginal new-child target under a per-child intercept — and the report's only statement of it is the generic "PSIS-LOO is unreliable" callout in the collapsed technical section. Because `adjusted` is not in the diagnostics partial's `_RE_KINDS`, the conditional-versus-marginal caveat the other random-intercept families get does not appear (for `adj-006` the LOO **is** the marginal child target, which deserves a sentence saying so rather than the row-level caveat), and the results section's "Influence" heading sits empty. The RLI fit is fine on this point (1 of 51 flagged, k = 0.86, and `influence.csv` is present). **Recommendation:** make the Influence block read `pareto_k.csv` (or have the RLM pipelines write `influence.csv` from the same `influence_diagnostics` call the RLI pipeline makes), gate the SES and influence prose on file presence, and add a sentence to `_results_adjusted.qmd` / the `adj-006` template naming the child-level target and the flagged share.

## Finding 8 (functional, minor): `alpha_transition` is published with role `other` and a generic rationale, and no adjusted factory is under the prior-completeness guard

## Finding 9 (functional, minor): the two ports do not publish the shared artefact schema the module docstring claims

`pipelines/adjusted.py` says the two ports "publish the same `predictor_associations.csv` schema, so they live together here". They do not: the RLI writer's convergence columns are `adj_converged` / `biv_converged`, the RLM writers' are `adjusted_converged` / `bivariate_converged`; the RLI `prior_sensitivity.csv` has `sigma` plus the full `beta_summary` (`median`, `mean`, `lo`, `hi`, `lo50`, `hi50`, `prob_pos`, `converged`), the RLM one has `predictor_slope_sigma`, `mean`, `lo`, `hi`, `prob_pos`, `subfit_converged` — no median (the house standard) and no 50% interval. ## Finding 11 (functional, minor): RLI covariates and age are standardised on the loader's row set, not the fitted rows

`load_and_prepare(phase_mode="span")` keeps every child with a t4 row and at least one of the six requested outcomes observed at t4 — 52 children — and standardises `blocks`, `behav`, `hs`, the `_missing` indicators, `deapp_c`, `erbto` and age on those rows; `build_adjusted_model` then drops the one child whose word reading is missing at t4 and standardises the skill predictors and the language composite on the remaining 51.
