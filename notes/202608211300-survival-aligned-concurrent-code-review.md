## Finding 1 (statistical, major): the survival `tau` is identified only by the randomised first interval, and the zero-centred per-interval baseline-hazard prior manufactures the published direction — for phonetic spelling it flips the sign

The design codes `treated_ik = 1` for the immediate arm in every interval and for the wait-list arm from interval 2. That coding is factually right (session records show both arms receiving sessions through t3→t4), but it means **every person-period row in intervals 2 and 3 has `treated = 1`**: rebuilding the panel confirms there is no treated-versus-untreated contrast anywhere except interval 1 (P: 7/24 = 29% immediate vs 2/17 = 12% wait-list; N: 10/21 = 48% vs 2/15 = 13% — both raw contrasts favour treated). The likelihood therefore depends on `tau` only through the four identified combinations `alpha_1`, `alpha_1 + tau`, `alpha_2 + tau`, `alpha_3 + tau`: beyond the interval-1 contrast, the split of the pooled post-crossover hazard between `tau` and `alpha_2`/`alpha_3` is decided **entirely by the priors**. And the priors decide it badly: `alpha_k ~ Normal(0, 1.5)` on the cloglog scale centres each interval's off-floor probability at 63%, while the observed pooled hazards run 8–29%, so the prior pulls each `alpha_k` up toward zero and — through the identified sums — pulls `tau` down. In effect intervals 2 and 3 act as two pseudo-observations saying `tau ≈` (the pooled interval hazard on the cloglog scale, i.e. ≈ −1.8 for P), each weighted by the alpha prior's precision; a four-term precision-weighted sketch reproduces the fitted P `tau` to within 0.03. The posterior corroborates the weak identification (cor(`tau`, `alpha[t2→t3]`) = −0.64 in surv-009; the fitted alphas sit at −1.4 to −1.8, ~1σ below their prior mean) and power scaling flags **every** parameter of both fits, `tau` included, for potential prior-data conflict.

The consequence is visible in the released numbers. A scratch refit on the interval-1 rows only — identical priors, covariates and link, zero divergences — gives **P: `tau` = +0.14** (89% −0.52 to +0.80, P(>0) = 0.63) versus the published pooled **−0.17** (89% −0.78 to +0.44, P(>0) = 0.33), and **N: +0.46** (89% −0.17 to +1.09, P(>0) = 0.88) versus the published **+0.30** (P(>0) = 0.80). The randomised-window information leans _faster_ for both outcomes — consistent with the ITT floor-rule companions (itt-009: +4 pp, P = 0.72; itt-011: +10 pp, P = 0.88) — and surv-009's published "slower" lean is produced by the prior-mediated split, not by any observed comparison. Three published texts misattribute this: the run plan's estimand ("the pooled intervention-aligned log-hazard shift **across** the immediate and post-crossover intervals") implies the post-crossover intervals contribute contrast information they cannot contain; surv-009's `key_findings.json` reports "67% probability of a negative association" as if it were evidence about the association; and `notes/202608181004-findings-11-survival.md` states "an evidence label attaches to whichever direction **the data** lean towards" and counts the survival estimate in its phonetic-spelling straddling-zero triangulation — for P the data (in the only window with a contrast) lean the other way. A secondary casualty: the `baseline off-floor prob [t2→t3]` / `[t3→t4]` rows in `survival_summary.csv` describe an _untreated_ child in intervals where no untreated children exist; they are counterfactual extrapolations whose value depends one-for-one on the prior-set `tau`, and the key-findings highlight quotes them unqualified.

Nothing here is a released causal claim — the family is correctly outside `GATED_KINDS` and everything is labelled prognostic — but the direction sentences are wrong as _descriptions of the data_. **Recommendation:** pick one of (a) re-anchor the `alpha_k` prior mean at a plausible floored-cohort per-interval hazard from an admissible external source — `priors.py`'s own rationale says a level-carrying intercept (no `gamma_own` term, as here) should be mean-anchored, so the current zero-centred `alpha` violates the documented house rule; (b) reparameterise so the post-crossover cells are saturated (e.g. `treated` enters interval 1 only, making `tau` explicitly the randomisation-anchored interval-1 hazard contrast and letting `alpha_2`/`alpha_3` absorb the treated levels — same likelihood support, no prior-mediated split); or (c) register the interval-1-only fit as a required companion. In all cases fix the estimand/causal-status text to say the post-crossover intervals carry no arm contrast, flag the untreated post-crossover "baseline" rows as extrapolations (or report the identified treated-cell probabilities instead), regenerate the key-findings boxes, and correct the two direction sentences in the 2026-08-18 findings note.

## Finding 2 (statistical, moderate): the aligned off-floor variant (al-005) keeps the graded pre-logit with the `Normal(1, 0.25)` tracking prior that #391 retired for exactly this situation

`build_aligned_model`'s `bernoulli_offfloor` branch swaps the likelihood but keeps the linear predictor unchanged, so al-005 regresses the off-floor indicator on `gamma_own · logit_*(pre)` with `gamma_own ~ Normal(1, 0.25)` — a prior whose stated calibration ("post-logit tracks pre-logit 1:1", a graded test–retest fact) does not transfer to a Bernoulli off-floor outcome. The gain-factors family resolved precisely this in the #391 finding-2 decision: the graded pre-logit of a heavily floored measure is a near-degenerate spike, and the honest baseline main effect for the off-floor likelihood is the **binary off-floor-at-pre indicator** (`gamma_own_offfloor ~ Normal(0, 1)`). The aligned floor-rule variant was never updated. Concretely: 40 of al-005's 53 fitted children sit at the P floor at onset (pre-logit −5.22, the Haldane floor value), so for the at-floor majority the effective intercept prior is `alpha + gamma_own·(−5.22) ~ Normal(−5.2, 2.0)` — a prior off-floor probability centred at ~0.5% against an observed 30% (12/40) — and the fit resolves the conflict by pushing `alpha` to +3.11 (89% +1.88 to +4.47), more than 2σ above its own `Normal(0, 1.5)` prior, with `alpha` and `gamma_own` near-collinear (the graded term is informed mostly by the 13 off-floor-at-onset children). Power scaling duly flags `alpha` and `gamma_own`. The fit converges and `beta_cohort` (+3.2 pp, P = 0.70) is probably not much moved — the flexible intercept absorbs the conflict — but the prior is informative in an unintended direction, the `factor_summary` row labels the substantially prior-informed `gamma_own` "very strong", and the family is internally inconsistent with the suite's own floor-rule decision. **Recommendation:** adopt the #391 parameterisation for the aligned `bernoulli_offfloor` branch (binary off-floor-at-pre main effect; drop the graded `gamma_own`), or record a dated decision why the graded term stays for this family. Only al-005 is affected (no aligned model exists for N).

## Finding 3 (reporting, moderate): the aligned recipe misdescribes the off-floor model — the fitted likelihood never appears in `model_recipe.md`

`resolve_aligned_run_plan` builds `design` as a fixed string — "a cross-sectional **Beta-Binomial** regression of the aligned post-score…" — regardless of `likelihood`, and `recipe_markdown`'s Terms section lists outcome/ability/cohort/dose but never the likelihood. al-005's persisted `model_recipe.md` therefore describes a Beta-Binomial on the post-score while the fitted model is a Bernoulli on the off-floor indicator with no `kappa`. The machine-readable plan fields (`likelihood`, `off_floor`, `obs_node`) are correct, and the report template describes the off-floor model properly — but the recipe is the plain-language audit document (#394 pillar 4) and it contradicts them. **Recommendation:** condition the `design` text on `off_floor` and add the likelihood (and, when off-floor, the risk-difference reading of the cohort marginal) to the Terms section; the recipe regenerates at the next refit and can be rewritten in place for the stored fit without sampling.

## Finding 5 (functional robustness, minor): validation asymmetries in the bespoke loaders and the pre-I/O promise

- `prepare_survival` reads the CSV directly and skips the shared loaders' integrity checks: group codes are taken as `int(by_time[GROUP].iloc[0])` with no {1, 2} validation and no within-child stability check (an invalid code would silently produce `G = −1` and wait-list-like treated coding rather than failing loud), and a duplicated (subject, time) row makes `by_time[col].get(t)` return a Series, failing later with a cryptic truth-value error. `load_and_prepare_aligned` validates group codes and count ranges but also takes `pre.iloc[0]`/`post.iloc[0]` without a duplicate-row check. The main loader fails loud on both; the current CSV is clean, so these are latent.
- The RLI concurrent plan validates the missing-covariate policy pre-I/O but not the predictor/outcome symbols: an unknown RLI predictor fails only inside `load_and_prepare`, after `make_context` has reset the output directory — the RLM branch validates its measures in `resolve_concurrent_run_plan`, before any I/O, which is the family promise. The aligned plan likewise accepts any `outcome_symbol`/`ability_covariate` string and fails post-reset in the loader.
- `load_rlm_concurrent_frames` validates count ranges but not integer-ness; `build_rlm_concurrent_model`'s `astype(np.int64)` would silently truncate a fractional count. The RLI loaders reject fractional counts explicitly.

## Finding 6 (consistency, minor): the aligned cohort prior is untiered while its intercept is tiered

`build_aligned_model` gives `beta_cohort` the proximal `tau_prior()` scale (`Normal(0, 0.5)`) for every outcome, including the distal-tier ones (R, E) whose ITT `tau` was tightened to `Normal(0, 0.3)` in #141 and whose aligned `alpha` _is_ tiered via `_alpha_sigma_for`. For an associational, non-gated term the wider prior is the conservative direction and psense is clean across the family, so nothing is being distorted today — but the half-applied tiering reads as drift rather than decision. A dated one-line rationale (or applying the tier) would close it.
