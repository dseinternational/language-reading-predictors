# Findings 15 — the latent growth-curve family

Reports every model in the `growth` family from the 2026-08-04/05 `reporting` refit. **3 models, all passing the convergence gate.** Reading conventions in note 00. Preliminary research data — all estimates provisional.

## What these models do

A different shape of question from the rest of the suite: rather than transitions between adjacent waves, these fit each child's **trajectory** across all four waves as a latent intercept (where they start) and slope (how fast they move), for several measures jointly.

The headline question is whether **baseline non-verbal ability predicts trajectory shape**:

- **δ (delta)** — ability's association with the baseline **level**;
- **γ (gamma)** — ability's association with the growth **rate**. This is the estimand of interest.

**Design.** Joint multivariate latent growth curves over the measures, with a masked Beta-Binomial observation model. `gc-070` adds a rank-1 shared growth-tempo factor coupling the slopes; `gc-085` adds an age × ability interaction on the growth rate.

**Every term is an adjusted, latent-ability-confounded association.** There is no treatment term and no causal reading anywhere in this family.

## Results — ability predicts where you start, not how fast you move

`gc-069`, the core model:

| Outcome                    | γ (ability → growth rate)     | Favoured direction               | δ (ability → baseline level)         |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Receptive grammar (RG)     | **+0.115** (+0.024 to +0.206) | **positive, strong** (P = 0.977) | **+0.226** (P = 0.999, very strong)  |
| Letter sounds (LS)         | −0.146 (−0.333 to +0.045)     | negative, suggestive (0.892)     | +0.135 (P = 0.790, suggestive)       |
| Word reading (WR)          | −0.047 (−0.230 to +0.128)     | inconclusive (0.667)             | +0.221 (P = 0.879, suggestive)       |
| Receptive vocabulary (RV)  | −0.035 (−0.094 to +0.023)     | negative, suggestive (0.832)     | **+0.186** (P = 0.9999, very strong) |
| Expressive vocabulary (EV) | −0.017 (−0.079 to +0.045)     | inconclusive (0.667)             | **+0.223** (P = 1.000, very strong)  |

**The δ/γ contrast is the finding.** Baseline non-verbal ability predicts the **starting level** decisively for the three language measures (δ = +0.19 to +0.23, P ≥ 0.999) while its association with the **growth rate** never reaches even moderate strength except for receptive grammar. Higher-ability children start higher; they do not, on this evidence, move faster.

Two qualifications keep this honest. δ is only _suggestive_ for the two reading measures (word reading 0.88, letter sounds 0.79), so "ability predicts level" is a claim about the language measures in this model, not about all five. And on the favoured-direction reading γ is genuinely inconclusive for only two of the five — receptive vocabulary and letter sounds both lean negative at suggestive strength, which is a lean _away_ from ability predicting faster growth rather than toward it.

This is the same pattern the horseshoe family finds by a completely different route (note 13: levels selected at P ≈ 1.00, gains selected at nothing) and the adjusted family finds by a third (note 12: non-verbal mental age adds almost nothing to predicting gain). Three independent methods agreeing is worth more than any one of them.

**One exception, and it points the "wrong" way for the others.** Receptive grammar has a clearly positive γ (+0.115, P = 0.977) — higher-ability children _do_ gain grammar faster. Meanwhile letter sounds leans negative (−0.146, suggestive). If anything, this is a mild hint that the alphabetic skills are the ones least tied to general ability's growth trajectory, which sits comfortably with the decoding-specificity picture in note 07 — but at suggestive strength it should not be leaned on.

`gc-070` adds a shared growth-tempo factor and reproduces the pattern almost exactly (grammar γ = +0.110, letter sounds −0.150). The tempo factor does not change the conclusions.

## `gc-085` — do older children with higher ability grow differently?

Adds an age × ability interaction to the growth rate. Under it, the grammar association strengthens (γ = +0.155, P = 0.994, very strong) and word reading moves from inconclusive-negative to **suggestive-positive** (γ = +0.096, P = 0.830).

That word-reading sign flip between `gc-069` and `gc-085` is worth flagging rather than picking a side. Both estimates are inside each other's intervals and neither is well resolved; the flip reflects how age and ability trade off in a model with few children and four waves, not a substantive disagreement. **Do not quote either word-reading γ as a finding.**

## Caveats

- **Associations only**, latent-ability-confounded throughout. No treatment term.
- **Heavily parameterised for the data available** — five measures × four waves × ~54 children, with intercepts and slopes per child. The intervals reflect that.
- **Priors are the most diffuse in the suite.** The prior-predictive spread is about 3.5× the data's spread, against 1.2–1.9× for most other families. That is admissible — the prior covers the observed range and a growth model needs to allow for trajectories the data may not contain — but it means these posteriors are doing more work per data point than elsewhere.
- **Power-scaling sensitivity is high** in this family, with several parameters flagged for prior-data tension. Read the γ estimates as the least prior-robust in the suite.
- **Predictive calibration.** 50% bands cover about 76% of observations.
- These three models now emit one posterior-predictive overlay **per measure**; previously the pooled overlay was skipped because the stacked likelihood mixes measures with different maxima.
