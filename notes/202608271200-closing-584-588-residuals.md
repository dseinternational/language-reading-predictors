## Residual 1 — the cross-model comparisons were never re-run

Every artefact in `output/statistical_models/comparison/` still dated from 2026-08-20/23, so none of it had seen the batch. The clearest case: `mediation_family.csv` recorded `lrp-rli-med-059`'s total effect as **2.061 items**, which is the value the #585 leg-contract fix superseded — the refit gives **2.319**.

Two further consequences that were not obvious from the file dates:

- **#586 Batch A's "copy the mech-058/071 and mech-072/172 LOO comparison CSVs beside both model runs" was unsatisfied.** The copy is implemented (`_copy_compare_beside_runs` in `scripts/compare_statistical_models.py`), but a refit resets its fit directory, so the batch deleted every previously written copy and nothing rewrote them.
- **The L×N nested LOO could newly validate.** The stored comparison recorded `comparison_valid=False` with "rebuilt frame has 155 rows but the stored trace has 151 … refusing to refit". The #586 Batch B row-contract fix moved `mech-063/163` to 155 rows, which removes that blocker.

`gb_vs_statistical.csv` and the four `horseshoe_vs_gb_*.csv` files come from two other scripts and were equally stale on their statistical side.

## Residual 2 — `lrp-rlm-jc-002`'s prior-sensitivity binding could never open

#588's last release-and-interpretation work item was to "run and provenance-bind the `lrp-rlm-jc-002` `HalfNormal(1.0)` sensitivity, or record a dated decision withdrawing the requirement". The batch ran it: `lrp-rlm-jc-102` is fitted, converged and `ok`. But jc-002's stored decision still read:

> the registered within-scale prior sensitivity (lrp-rlm-jc-102) is not release-ready beside this fit (its own release decision withholds publication)

which is false — and jc-002 was the only fit in the registry carrying any publication qualification. The immediate cause is fit ordering: jc-002 finished at 16:00:26 and jc-102 at 16:02:46, so the parent's decision was computed before the companion existed. The movement record spotted this and proposed re-evaluating the decision.

Re-evaluation is not enough. It only moves the complaint:

> … is not release-ready beside this fit (**the input data checksum is not recorded on both fits**)

`release._HISTORICAL_JOINT_PRIOR_BINDING` requires `data_sha256` on both fits and fails closed when either is absent. `PreparedData` and `WavePanel` have always carried that field; `LongitudinalPanel` — the container behind the whole `historical_growth` and `historical_joint` families — carried neither it nor `data_path`, so `reporting.write_run_metadata` read `None` off it and wrote `data_sha256: null`. Across the registry, 24 of the 25 RLM reporting fits record no checksum (the exception, `lrp-rlm-gc-001`, is a `growth` fit built from a different container).

A fail-closed check that can never open is not a strict check; it is a broken one. Every fixture in the binding's test suite hands both fits a `data_sha256`, so the suite passed against input production never produced — which is why this survived review.

**Fix.** `LongitudinalPanel` gains `data_path` / `data_sha256`, populated from `_data_provenance` in `load_longitudinal_panel` exactly as the sibling loaders do, and the two `jc` fits were refit so both record it. The new regression test asserts _satisfiability against the real loader_ rather than against a fixture: it builds the panel the `historical_joint` pipeline builds, resolves `lrp-rlm-jc-002`'s own plan, and requires every binding reader to find a value. It fails without the change.

The nine `historical_growth` fits and `lrp-rlm-jc-001` still carry no checksum and will record one at their next fit. Nothing reads it for them: `jc-001` has no within-child block and is out of the binding's scope, and no other check consumes the field for this study.

## What was run

### The tail: 25 fits, 4h46m, 25 ok / 0 failed

The 13 plan-stale fits and the two provenance blemishes, plus the two `jc` fits the checksum fix needs, plus the eight remaining `mediation` fits so all twenty carry `extra.simulation` — `med-059/060/062/064/066/068/074/075/076/078/079/080/086/087/092/176/186/187/276/387`, `mm-002`, `mm-102`, `adj-065`, `rlm-jc-002`, `rlm-jc-102`.

Every one now resolves a plan identical to what its module produces today, records `dirty: false`, and is `ok` at the robustness stage. **Twenty-four of the twenty-five reproduce their pre-tail posterior byte-for-byte**, which is what a metadata-only tail should look like: the persisted plan and provenance change, the science does not.

The fits span four commits (`200ca657`, `a26261ad`, `0d439687`, `edb1766f`) rather than one. Three code changes and a documentation commit landed while the sweep ran, each committed immediately rather than held — an uncommitted tree would have recorded `dirty: true` on every remaining fit, which is the exact blemish this tail exists to repair. The four differ only in documentation, a maintenance script's constant, and a behaviour-identical refactor; no fit output depends on which one it ran at. Freezing code before a sweep is the better discipline and was not followed here.

### The one fit that did not reproduce

`lrp-rlm-jc-002`'s summaries moved by up to ~1e-2 and its max R-hat went 1.00329 → 1.00430, while `jc-102` reproduced to the digit. Since "seeded, therefore byte-identical" is the premise the whole tail rests on, this was worth settling rather than waving through.

A third, independent fit into a scratch output root **reproduces the new result exactly** — 0.000e+00 across every column of `within_scale_summary.csv` and `measure_correlation_summary.csv`, identical max R-hat — and differs from the stored one by the same amount as the tail fit did. Sampling is therefore deterministic, and the _pre-tail artefact_ was the outlier, most plausibly a residue of the batch's post-hoc #628 re-derivation over the nine LKJ fits. Nothing in the conclusion moves: `basread` stays resolvable, `bpvs` and `basdig` stay unresolvable, under both priors. A fit that could not be reproduced from its own recorded commit is a good argument for having refit it.

### The ordering hazard, twice

`jc-002` ran at 24/25 and `jc-102` at 25/25, so the parent's decision was again computed before its companion existed and the stored qualification still read "the input data checksum is not recorded on both fits" — the same shape of staleness the batch produced, now under the repaired binding. Re-evaluating over the finished pair returns **empty**: the binding clears, and jc-002 publishes unqualified for the first time. Its `release_decision.json`, `key_findings.json` and rendered report were regenerated.

`regenerate_key_findings.py` already re-evaluates and rewrites the decision, so the capability was never missing — only the _noticing_ was. `run_refit_sweep.py` now reports, at end of sweep, any completed fit whose qualification names a model the same sweep fitted afterwards, and names the command that fixes it. It stays quiet for a genuinely unfitted companion, which is the case the gate is right to withhold on.
