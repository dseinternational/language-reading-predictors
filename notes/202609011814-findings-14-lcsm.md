# Findings: the `lcsm` family — do levels or changes in one skill precede changes in another?

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 5 latent change-score models. The cross-process couplings are associations, not causal paths; only the immediate-minus-wait-list contrast in latent change during the first, randomised window carries a causal reading, and only as a consistency check. All 5 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and are publishable (2026-09-01 rebuild). Four of the five could not fit at all until PR #650 declared their inline priors, and `lcsm-081` needed a refit at a higher acceptance target to bring the R-hat of five per-child latent offsets under 1.01.

## The data

**RLI trial only**, all four timepoints: 54 children contributing 216 rows. The model needs the full sequence per child because it is built around the transitions between consecutive waves.

## What the model is for

For each child and interval the model forms a latent change score and asks whether that change is predicted by a **prior level** of another skill (a "g" coupling) or by a **prior change** in it (an "h" coupling), separating the underlying skill from measurement noise through the Beta-Binomial structure. Couplings are reported on the latent log-odds scale per unit of the source's prior latent value; because sources have different latent spreads, every fit also writes a per-SD standardised table, which is what to use for comparing sources. Couplings are held constant across arms and transitions.

**Prior letter-sound level predicts later word-reading change; prior letter-sound change does not.** The level-to-change coupling is resolved in every model that carries it, while the change-on-change coupling in `lcsm-091` is +0.05 (89% −0.17 to +0.26), inconclusive. The data support temporal ordering of prior level and later change, not the stronger claim that one interval's letter-sound gain predicts the next interval's reading gain.

**Letter sounds versus vocabulary as predictors of later reading change, on one scale.** Per unit of latent log-odds the vocabulary coupling looks larger than the letter-sound one in `lcsm-067` (+0.29 against +0.14), but letter-sound levels spread about three times more widely (latent SD 1.46 against 0.47), so per SD the ordering reverses: +0.64 SD of later reading change per SD of prior letter-sound level against +0.43 per SD of prior vocabulary, contrast +0.21 (89% −0.38 to +0.76, P = 0.72, inconclusive). In `lcsm-091`, which also conditions on each skill's prior change, the same comparison is +0.72 against +0.26, contrast +0.47 (+0.05 to +0.86, P = 0.96, moderate). If either source predicts later reading change more strongly per SD of where a child stands, it is letter sounds.

**Reciprocal blending and reading (`lcsm-082`).** Neither lagged cross-coupling between blending and word reading is resolved (blending → reading +0.03; reading → blending +0.06), the dominance contrast is inconclusive (P = 0.70), and letter sounds predict later change in both. The letter-sound-to-reading coupling dominates the blending-to-reading one by −0.69 SD (89% −1.03 to −0.28).

**Reading predicts later taught-vocabulary change, a little (`lcsm-081`).** Prior word reading carries small but resolved couplings into later taught receptive (+0.08, P = 1.00) and taught expressive (+0.06, P = 0.98) vocabulary change, beside the family's largest coupling, prior taught receptive vocabulary → taught expressive change (+0.42; +0.44 in the no-reverse-coupling comparator `lcsm-181`). The nested predictive comparison between `081` and `181` is inconclusive (elpd difference 0.0 ± 4.0, with several high Pareto-k children), so the reverse edge neither earns nor loses its place predictively. The taught-vocabulary result is an across-child prior-level-to-later-change association; it does not show that individual children first recognise and then produce the same words.

**Age predicts slower reading change** in all three reading models (−0.15, −0.09, −0.11 logits per SD of age, the family's one standardised covariate). Within this narrow age band older children gained less, conditional on everything else; `lcsm-067` has no window intercepts, so its coupling also absorbs any tendency for later windows to deliver less change. A fragile quantity, not a claim about ageing.

**The randomised window-1 contrasts** — immediate minus wait-list latent change in the first window — are +0.42 (89% +0.11 to +0.74) for word reading, +0.50 (+0.09 to +0.92) for blending and +0.68 (+0.31 to +1.06) for letter sounds in `lcsm-082`, +0.39 for word reading in `lcsm-091` and +0.29 (+0.02 to +0.57) for taught expressive vocabulary in `lcsm-081`. They inherit randomisation as consistency checks on the same trial window; the later-window arm contrasts occur after crossover.

## What changed since the August notes

The couplings match the August values to within a hundredth or two (e.g. `lcsm-082` letter sounds → reading +0.25 [+0.14, +0.38] then and now; `lcsm-081`'s taught-vocabulary coupling +0.42 against August's +0.43). The changes are computational: the four fits blocked by undeclared priors, and the `lcsm-081` acceptance-target refit, which moved nothing of substance.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Temporal precedence is not causation** — a stable common cause acting earlier produces the same level-to-change pattern, and the `mechanism` family's negative controls show part of the letter-sound association is non-specific. **Couplings are held constant across arms and transitions.** **They are not treatment effects** and pool between-child and within-child variation. **Four waves and 54 children is modest** for a change-score model.

## Model inventory

All 5 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and are publishable: `lcsm-067` (letter sounds and vocabulary as predictors of reading change), `081` (reverse coupling from word reading into taught-vocabulary change), `082` (reciprocal blending and word reading), `091` (change-on-change), `181` (no-reverse-coupling comparator for `081`). `lcsm-081` was refitted at `target_accept` 0.99 (R-hat 1.0119 → 1.0078 on the per-child offsets, zero divergences throughout).
