## Finding 2 (statistical reporting, major): `posterior_population_mean` is the _median-child_ cell mean, and the report tells the reader to compare it with the observed mean

`cell_summary` publishes two different fitted quantities per cell. `posterior_mean` is the average of `fitted_mean_items_obs` over the cell's rows — the population-average expected count, and the right calibration target. `posterior_population_mean` is `mean_items = n · sigmoid(eta_cell)`, the expected count for a child at the group-centred zero offset — the _median_ child, not the population mean, because `sigmoid` is nonlinear and `sigma_subject` here runs 1.05–1.41 logits. The report's "how to read the table" paragraph (in both `_results_historical_growth.qmd` and `_results_historical_joint.qmd`) says `posterior_population_mean` "is the model's expected item score for that cell … `observed_complete_case_mean` is the real data mean it should match", and the bullet under the table says it is the expected score "on the population mean". Verified on the stored `lrp-rlm-hg-001` cell summary: for the Down-syndrome wave-1 cell the observed mean is 16.91, `posterior_mean` is 16.89 (calibration gap −0.02 items, an excellent reproduction) and `posterior_population_mean` is **11.12** — a 5.8-item, 34% apparent shortfall against the quantity the reader has just been told it should match. The sign flips above p = 0.5 exactly as Jensen's inequality predicts (Average readers wave 3: observed 65.31, population-mean column 69.02). A careful reader following the report's own instructions concludes the Table 2 reproduction failed by several items in every cell. The gap column itself is computed from the correct quantity, so the number in `posterior_mean_minus_observed_mean` is right; only the column the prose points at is wrong. **Recommendation:** rename the column to what it is (`posterior_median_child_mean`, or keep `mean_items` in the name), and rewrite both partials' prose so the calibration comparison is stated against `posterior_mean` — with one sentence explaining why the median-child level sits below (or above) the cell average on a logit-scale random-intercept model. This is a rename plus prose; the CSVs regenerate from the stored traces without a refit.

## Finding 3 (statistical reporting, major): five of six publishable growth headlines quote an attrition-selected extension interval without saying so, and the selection among tied rows is arbitrary

`_kf_build_historical_growth` filters `posterior_growth_summary.csv` to the within-group rows, ranks them by `_kf_most_resolved_row` (distance of `P(>0)` from 0.5) and wraps the winner in a fixed sentence. It never reads the `window` column. Verified across the stored fits: `hg-001` headlines "Wave 3 to wave 4" (extension, `n_subjects` = 19 of 23), `hg-004`/`005`/`006` headline "Wave 1 to wave 4" (extension), `hg-007` headlines "Wave 1 to wave 5" (extension, Down-syndrome-only tail); only `hg-009` headlines a core interval, and only because its declared core _is_ waves 3–4. The whole #338 extension policy — stated in the model docstrings, the Overview prose and the results partial's own reading guide ("rows tagged `extension` … should be read as within-cohort follow-up, not as the audited core") — is that these cells are an attrition-selected tail that must be read alongside their `n`. The findings-first box, which is what most readers see and what the findings notes quote, drops exactly that qualification. Two aggravating details. First, the ranking metric saturates: `hg-001` has **eight** rows tied at `P(>0)` = 1.000000, and `_kf_most_resolved_row` breaks the tie with `pd.DataFrame.sort_values`, whose default `kind="quicksort"` is not stable — so the specific interval published as _the_ headline is arbitrary and not reproducible across pandas versions or row orderings. The +8.5-item Down-syndrome wave-3-to-4 interval is published as the finding while the +37.1-item Average-readers waves-1-to-4 interval is equally resolved. Second, the `readgrp_label`-non-empty filter excludes the between-group `total_growth_*` contrasts entirely — the quantities the family's own prior pushforward (#381) treats as _the_ reported estimands — so the box can never headline the descriptive comparison the cohort design exists to make. **Recommendation:** carry `window` and `n_subjects` into the sentence (or prefer a core row when one is available and say so when none is); break ties deterministically (largest `|median|` within the tied set, or `kind="stable"` with a documented row order); and add a contrast sentence so the box and the pushforward describe the same estimand. All of this regenerates via `scripts/regenerate_key_findings.py` without a refit — but note the `_partials` sync requirement when doing so.

## Finding 6 (statistical, moderate): `sigma_within` overstates the fitted within-child departure scale by ~26%, and — because the within branch drops kappa — it also absorbs measurement noise, attenuating the headline correlation by an unidentifiable amount

Two separate points about `lrp-rlm-jc-002`, the family's within-child companion, both affecting the parameter its resolvability gate and its key-findings box are built on.

_(a) The double sum-to-zero projection deflates the realised residual._ `within_offset` is `z_within @ within_chol.T` swept of subject means and then of group-by-wave cell means, times `sigma_within`. That projection removes `n_subjects + G·T − G` of the `n_subjects·T` degrees of freedom, so the realised SD of the fitted departures is `sqrt(1 − 77/213) = 0.799` times `sigma_within`. Verified empirically on the stored trace: the ratio of `sd(within_offset)` to `sigma_within` is 0.7956 / 0.7941 / 0.7952 for the three measures, matching the prediction. The report publishes `sigma_within` as "the wave-specific residual standard deviation" and the key-findings box quotes "median 0.32 logits" for BAS word reading — the realised departure SD is 0.25. The same argument applies to `sigma_subject` in both families, but there the factor is `sqrt(1 − 1/m_g)` with group sizes 21–32, i.e. a 2% inflation that is not worth reporting. **The headline correlation is not affected**: the projection is applied identically to every measure column, so cross-measure correlations of the projected residuals equal those of the unprojected ones exactly — worth stating explicitly in the report, because a reader who spots the constraint will otherwise wonder.

_(b) The correlation is attenuated by an unmodelled measurement-error share._ The within branch replaces the Beta-Binomial with a Binomial (documented in the factory docstring: the logistic-normal residual supplies the extra-Binomial variance, and fitting both made the correlation prior-dominated in development). The consequence is that `sigma_within` is a _compound_ of true wave-to-wave state fluctuation and all extra-Binomial measurement/item noise, and those two components cannot be separated within this model. Measurement noise is very unlikely to be correlated across three different instruments, so it attenuates `within_corr` toward zero by an amount the model cannot estimate. Neither `_results_historical_joint.qmd` nor the resolved run plan's estimand text says so; the partial describes `within_corr` as asking "whether a wave when a child is above their own stable level on one measure is also an above-level wave on another", which is the pure-state reading. The stored fit shows how much this bites: BPVS and BAS recall-of-digits have residual scales of 0.03 and 0.04 logits, both below the 0.05-logit resolvability threshold, so no pair is `pair_resolvable` and the key-findings box correctly reports "the model did not resolve a within-child correlation" — the resolvability gate is working, and it is the right design. But the accompanying causal sentence ("not evidence that skills are causally unrelated") stops one step short of the sharper statement: with measurement noise inside the same term, a small `sigma_within` cannot distinguish "these skills do not co-fluctuate" from "they do, but the measurements are too noisy for this design to see it". **Recommendation:** report the realised departure SD alongside (or instead of) `sigma_within` in `within_scale_summary.csv`, note the projection factor in the partial, and add the attenuation caveat to the estimand text in `resolve_historical_joint_run_plan` so it reaches `model_recipe.md` and `config.json`. The threshold itself is fine at 0.05 (the ~26% inflation makes it marginally anti-conservative; the effect is negligible at that scale).

## Finding 8 (statistical, moderate): the overdispersion prior's SHAPE excluded the answer the data prefer — resolved by reparameterising onto the dispersion scale

`psense_summary.csv` flags `sigma_subject` in **all eleven** fits, for every group and every measure, at prior sensitivities of 0.20–0.58 against the 0.05 threshold — nine of them as "potential prior-data conflict" and the rest as "potential strong prior / weak likelihood". `kappa` is flagged in eight of the nine `hg` fits and eight of nine `jc-001` cells. That pattern is exactly what a weakly-separable variance partition looks like: `sigma_subject` (persistent, between-child) and `kappa` (independent, per-row) compete for the same extra-Binomial dispersion, and with 76 children over 3–5 waves the data constrain their sum far better than either alone. It is worth naming for three reasons. First, `sigma_subject`'s posterior is what drives the median-child-versus-population-mean gap in Finding 2 — a prior-driven heterogeneity parameter propagates straight into the cell levels a reader compares with Table 2. Second, #383 widened this prior from HalfNormal(0.5) to HalfNormal(1.0) on the argument that the posteriors sat at the old prior's 99th percentile; the power-scaling diagnostic is now saying the widened prior is itself doing substantial work, which is a different diagnosis of the same parameter and deserves an explicit decision rather than being left as a table row. Third, `kappa ~ HalfNormal(50)` is a very diffuse prior on a parameter the data barely identify; a reparameterisation on `1/kappa` or `log kappa`, or a joint prior on the total dispersion with a partition, would be the conventional fix. The psense table is published in the collapsed technical section, so the information is not hidden — but no report sentence, and none of the 2026-08-18 findings notes, tells a reader that the between-child spread these models report is substantially prior-carried. **Recommendation:** state it in the historical-growth and historical-joint findings notes, and record a decision on whether the current `sigma_subject` / `kappa` priors stand. (For contrast, `measure_corr_pairs` in `jc-001` — the actual headline — is flagged with prior sensitivity 0.065–0.095 against likelihood sensitivity 0.17–0.32, i.e. predominantly data-driven. That one is fine.)

**Diagnosis, on the evidence.** Separating the two flagged parameters settles which one is actually carrying the prior. `sigma_subject` is **well identified**: its posterior standard deviation is 11–46% of the prior's across all 27 group-by-measure cells, so the data are doing most of the work, and its psense flag comes from the shrinkage geometry of a zero-anchored prior on a narrow posterior rather than from prior domination. Tightening it would have reversed #383 (which widened it 0.5 → 1.0 on measured truncation evidence) and re-truncated the four Down-syndrome measures now sitting at 1.39–1.60. `kappa` is the opposite: its posterior standard deviation is **90–99% of the prior's in 20 of 27 cells**, and the posterior piles up against wherever the prior stops — P(κ > 100) is 0.046 under `HalfNormal(50)` but 0.37–0.78 in those same posteriors. That is a likelihood monotone in κ, wanting the near-Binomial limit (no extra-Binomial dispersion beyond the child random intercept), held back only by the prior.

The defect is the prior's **shape, not its scale**. Expressed on the observable scale, `HalfNormal(50)` gives the event "Beta-Binomial variance within 10% of Binomial" a prior probability of **0.000–0.001 at every RLM denominator** (n = 18–90). The answer was excluded a priori. `kappa_prior`'s own docstring records that exclusion as deliberate — but reasons explicitly about RLI's _high_-denominator outcomes (n up to 170), where holding κ below n enforces a sensible minimum dispersion. The RLM measures run n = 18–34, so the fitted κ ≈ 110 is κ ≫ n, exactly the region the prior was shaped to exclude. The argument does not transfer. Note also that shrinking `HalfNormal(50)` — the literal "tighten" — would have made this worse: it pulls κ _down_ at the measures where the data say there is no extra-Binomial dispersion, pushes that variance into `sigma_subject`, and raises the prior's share further.

**Decision (Frank, 2026-08-21): reparameterise onto the dispersion scale.** `1/sqrt(kappa) ~ HalfNormal(0.25)`, with `kappa` retained as a Deterministic so the reports, diagnostics and prior-vs-posterior overlay keep speaking in the family's documented units. On this scale the no-dispersion limit is simply `u = 0`, where a HalfNormal has its mode. The scale 0.25 was chosen to be **calibration-preserving on the observable scale**: it reproduces the old prior's median Beta-Binomial variance inflation at every RLM denominator to within 3% (n = 18: 1.47 against 1.49; n = 32: 1.85 against 1.89; n = 90: 3.45 against 3.56), with comparable upper limits. It therefore asserts no new belief about _how much_ dispersion there is — it only stops forbidding "none", raising P(inflation < 1.1) from ~0.000 to 0.11–0.24 and P(κ > 100) from 0.046 to 0.311. Every κ in the current suite (28–121) sits within 0.4–0.75 prior standard deviations of the mode, so neither tail is truncated. Scoped to the RLM historical families: `kappa_prior` is unchanged for every other family, and the settings field is renamed `kappa_prior_sigma` → `dispersion_prior_sigma` so a stale declaration fails loudly instead of being silently reinterpreted on the new scale.

**Measured, on real fits** (`test` preset, before against after, `lrp-rlm-hg-001`). κ is released exactly where it was pinned and left alone where it was not: Down syndrome median 93.5 → 147.8 with the 89% upper limit moving 137.8 → 480 (the cell whose likelihood wanted the near-Binomial limit), while Average readers (56.0 → 55.6) and Reading-matched (31.2 → 29.4) — the cells where the data already identified κ — do not move. `sigma_subject` is unchanged (1.392 → 1.373, 1.141 → 1.143, 1.032 → 1.024), so the variance split did not shift; κ simply stopped being held down. **The deliverable is untouched: the maximum |Δ median| across every published growth quantity is 0.094 items** on a 90-item scale, the same order as #383's ≤ 0.10. On `lrp-rlm-hg-004` — the most κ-unidentified model — psense prior sensitivity for κ falls from 0.284/0.285/0.286 to 0.038/0.093/0.123, with likelihood sensitivity now dominant. Sampling is unaffected: both fits keep zero divergences, and a controlled before/after at `test` preset gives max R-hat 1.0144 against 1.0136 with the same, pre-existing, `z_subject` failing parameter.

## Finding 10 (functional, minor): resolver validation gaps and one asymmetric run-plan record

- Neither resolver validates the declared waves against the catalogue's `available_waves`, although `concurrent` and `growth` both do exactly that in their own resolution and the RLM catalogue records it per measure (`basnum` = 1–4, `basmat` = 3–5). A core wave a measure was never administered at would fail late and loudly (complete-case selection empties the panel); an _extension_ wave that was never administered fails silently — `load_longitudinal_panel` simply appends no rows, and the reports then show a wave column of zero `n` and NaN means that reads as total attrition rather than as a declaration error. The current registry declarations are all correct; nothing enforces that.
- Nothing requires `extension_waves` to lie _after_ the core window. `waves=(3, 4), extension_waves=(1,)` resolves and fits; the plan text, the `window` labels and the "attrition-selected follow-up tail" prose would all be wrong for it.
- `HistoricalJointModelSettings(within_correlation=True, kappa_prior_sigma=25.0)` is accepted and the value silently discarded (the resolver maps it to `None`). #455's rule is that incoherent cross-field combinations fail at resolution; this one cannot be detected perfectly in a dataclass with defaults, but it is checkable against the class default.
- The joint run plan is asymmetric about unused settings: it nulls `kappa_prior_sigma` when `within_correlation` is true, but records live `sigma_within_prior_sigma: 0.5` and `within_lkj_eta: 2.0` when it is false. Verified in `lrp-rlm-jc-001-reporting/config.json`, which names two prior scales the fitted model does not contain — the failure mode CLAUDE.md's "`config.json` never names a coefficient the posterior lacks" rule exists to prevent. Null them the same way.

## Finding 11 (provenance, minor): the influence script overwrites the fit's own required `pareto_k.csv` and then hashes its own output as the "primary" binding

`scripts/historical_growth_influence.py` recomputes `historical_growth_pareto_table(panel, loo, ...)` and writes it over `model_dir / "pareto_k.csv"` (lines 164–166) before hashing that file as `primary_pareto_k_sha256`. Two consequences. First, `pareto_k.csv` is a **required**, manifest-recorded fit artefact: `lrp-rlm-hg-001-reporting/artifact_manifest.json` records it with `n_rows: 300` and the five columns the fit-time `influence_diagnostics` writer produced (`observation_index, subject_id, pareto_k, good_k_threshold, loo_reliable`). The script replaces it with a nine-column table sorted differently, so the fit's own manifest no longer describes the file on disk, and `release.evaluate_publication` — which checks required artefacts for presence but not schema — would still pass over the mutated directory. Second, the bundle evaluator's `primary_pareto_k_sha256` check is therefore self-referential: it binds to a file the script itself just wrote, not to an artefact of the primary fit. The substance is fine (the table is a deterministic function of `trace.nc`, which _is_ hash-bound, and the extra columns are a genuine improvement in row traceability), but the provenance chain claims more than it delivers. **Recommendation:** write the enriched table under its own filename (`pareto_k_rows.csv`) and hash the fit's untouched `pareto_k.csv` as the primary binding; or, better, have the fit itself emit the enriched table so the schema is the fit's own and the manifest matches.

## Finding 12 (labelling and robustness, minor)

- `historical.growth_summary` raises `ValueError: No observations for group=…, wave=…` if any interval's two endpoint cells share no children. The trace has already been persisted by then, but the exception is unguarded in a post-sampling stage, so the fit ends without `config.json`, `key_findings.json` or a release decision. Unlikely with nested follow-up, but cheap to guard (skip the interval, record the reason).
- The `observed_complete_case_mean` / `_sd` / `n_complete` column names are applied to extension cells too, which are available-case by construction. The `window` and `n_obs` columns carry the truth; the column names contradict them.
- The key-findings "reproduction check" sentence takes `max` over `posterior_mean_minus_observed_mean` across _all_ cells, core and extension. The Table 2 audit is the core cells only. For `hg-001` the maximum happens to fall on a core cell (0.42 items, Reading-matched wave 1), but that is luck, not construction.
- The between-group contrast rows publish no per-group components and `n_subjects = NA`, so `hg-001`'s "Total growth (waves 1–4): Average readers minus Down syndrome = +18.6 items" cannot be reconstructed from any visible row (the Down-syndrome waves-1-to-4 total is computed internally and never published; the table shows waves 1-to-5 instead). Publishing the common-window per-group totals, and the per-group `n_subjects` behind each contrast, would close it.
- The model's `growth_*_items` deterministics are median-child, common-window quantities and are _not_ the published growth summary. With `hg-001`'s four-wave common window they diverge materially: `growth_first_last_items` is 20.7 / 43.0 / 38.4 items by group, implying an Average-readers-minus-Down-syndrome contrast of 22.3 against the published 18.6; `growth_first_next_items` for Average readers is 20.7 against the published waves-1-to-2 value of 16.5. These deterministics are what `psense_summary.csv` and `diagnostics.csv` report, so the family's power-scaling diagnostic is run on a different estimand from the one the prior pushforward checks and the results table publishes. Nothing in the report explains the distinction, and `growth_next_last_items` silently spans two intervals (waves 2→4) once the common window has four waves, despite a name that reads as one.
- `_cell_obs_index` returns `df.index[mask]` and feeds it to `.isel(obs=...)`, i.e. it assumes `panel.long` always carries a 0..n−1 `RangeIndex`. Both construction paths do reset the index, so this is correct today; an `.reset_index(drop=True)` at the top of the helper (or an assertion) would make the coupling explicit rather than implicit.
- The all-free-RV convergence gate scans `measure_corr_chol` / `within_corr_chol`, whose upper-triangular structural zeros and unit `[0,0]` entry yield `r_hat = NaN`; the shared gate takes `np.nanmax`, so those entries are silently dropped. Correct behaviour for genuine constants, but it means a _pathological_ NaN R-hat (a chain frozen at a constant) would also pass unremarked. This is shared-gate behaviour, not family code, and it is the same shape as the `corr_factor` LKJ observation already in the repo memory — noted here because these are the two historical fits where it fires.

## Observations (no action required)

- **The influence bundle is currently absent everywhere.** `hg-001` has five Pareto-k values above 0.7 (max 0.972) and no `historical_growth_influence_sensitivity.csv`, so its report prints the "PSIS-LOO requires observation-level sensitivity" warning with the exact command to fix it. `hg-009`'s bundle from the 2026-08-16 audit was cleared by the August full refit (the fit resets its output directory). The design is sound and fails closed — `evaluate_historical_growth_influence_bundle` re-derives the flagged rows from the current Pareto table and rejects a stale or partial bundle on eight separate grounds — it simply has not been re-run since the refit. Unlike the `growth` family, this bundle is advisory (report-partial only) rather than a release-gate input; that asymmetry looks deliberate given these families publish nothing causal, but it is worth an explicit sign-off.
- **The inputs gate works.** `hg-002` (BAS spelling), `hg-003` (WORD comprehension) and `hg-008` (BAS number skills) are withheld at the inputs stage on unconfirmed bounded-count denominators, `key_findings.json` is empty with the reason, and the rendered reports show the "Findings withheld: scientific inputs are unresolved" callout with every result table replaced by "No growth summary." Verified in `lrp-rlm-hg-002-reporting/index.html`. The denominator is the Beta-Binomial `n`, so it scales every reported items-scale quantity — withholding is right.
- **The within-child double centring is exact, and it is enforced.** The subject sweep followed by the cell sweep leaves subject means at exactly zero _only_ on a balanced panel (the leftover per-group constant is `(1/T)·Σ_w cell_means[g, w]`, which is identically zero when every child contributes all `T` waves). `HistoricalJointModelSettings.__post_init__` rejects `within_correlation` with extension waves, the factory rejects duplicate child-wave rows and an unbalanced wave set, and `test_build_rlm_joint_growth_within_child_layer_is_double_centred` pins both zero-mean conditions. This is well built.
- **The prior on the reported estimand is effectively non-informative.** `hg-001`'s `prior_pushforward.csv` puts the 89% prior range for the between-group total-growth contrast at −72 to +71 items on a 90-item test. That is a legitimate choice for a descriptive audit model with independent `Normal(0, 1.5)` cell levels, and the report prints it — but the accompanying calibration sentence ("wide but not absurd — it should not concentrate mass on implausibly large effects") reads oddly against a prior that spans essentially the whole possible range. Worth a sentence acknowledging that these contrasts are entirely likelihood-driven by design.
- **`historical_growth_pareto_table` is unused by the fit.** It is called only from the influence script; the fit-time table comes from the shared `influence_diagnostics`, which has a dedicated `LongitudinalPanel` branch mapping `pareto_k` onto `panel.long` rows. Both are correct and both preserve row order; see Finding 11 for the consequence.
- **`_results_historical_joint.qmd` leads with the between-child correlation for both models**, although `jc-002`'s resolved plan names the within-child matrix as its headline and the between-child one as "reported alongside it". The section title does say "companion headline"; a reader who stops at the first table gets the wrong one for `jc-002`.
- **The joint fits are over-dispersed against their own data.** Wiring up the per-measure coverage statistic (Finding 4) makes this visible for the first time. Recomputed over `lrp-rlm-jc-001`'s stored trace: the 90% prediction ranges contain 839 of 852 observations (98%, expected ≈ 90%) and the 50% ranges 697 of 852 (82%, expected ≈ 50%). Coverage that far above nominal means the fitted `kappa` and child random effect together spread the predictive distribution wider than the data need — consistent with Finding 8's diagnosis that `kappa` was held below where the likelihood wanted it. It is not a defect (an under-confident model is the safe direction), but it is a substantive property of these fits that no report has ever stated, and it will now appear in the coverage sentence. Worth re-reading once the refits land under the reparameterised dispersion prior, which releases `kappa` upward and should move coverage toward nominal.
