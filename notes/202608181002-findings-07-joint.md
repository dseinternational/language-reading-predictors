# Findings: the `joint` family — all outcomes in one model

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 4 models in the `joint` family, which fit several outcomes simultaneously instead of one at a time.

## The data

**RLI trial only**, the randomised **timepoint 1 to timepoint 2 window**, one row per child and outcome — the same window as the `itt` family. `itt-012` uses one common 53-child analysis set across all 10 outcomes, giving 530 fitted child-outcome cells. Each of the three two-outcome fits uses 54 children. Nothing is stacked across periods.

## What the model is for

Fitting outcomes one at a time, as the `itt` family does, makes it awkward to organise several assigned-arm effects and their contrasts in one model. The same children often produced the outcomes, so their residuals could be dependent.

These particular fits use a **factorised multi-outcome likelihood**: each outcome has its own marginal model, and the fit does **not** estimate within-child residual covariance. The shared fit keeps definitions and posterior calculations aligned and can generate a difference between outcome-specific effects, but that difference pairs factorised marginals. It therefore does not, by itself, account for the dependence created when the same child supplies both outcomes.

## How to read the results

Each outcome still gets its own available-case modified intention-to-treat assigned-arm effect, read exactly as in the `itt` family. The additional quantity is a **contrast** between two average marginal effects on the proportion-correct scale. Its credible interval omits paired within-child residual covariance and requires a paired randomisation, bootstrap, sandwich or dependence-model sensitivity before being treated as a confirmed paired contrast.

## What was found

**The joint estimates reproduce the single-outcome estimates almost exactly**, which is the main thing to check. On the model's log-odds scale:

| Outcome                | Single-outcome (`itt`) | Joint (`itt-012`)      |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Letter-sound knowledge | 0.582 [0.278, 0.877]   | 0.580 [0.271, 0.880]   |
| Word reading           | 0.354 [0.101, 0.611]   | 0.355 [0.101, 0.611]   |
| Receptive vocabulary   | 0.008 [−0.128, 0.145]  | 0.034 [−0.104, 0.172]  |
| Expressive vocabulary  | 0.007 [−0.119, 0.134]  | −0.003 [−0.132, 0.126] |

Agreement to three decimal places for the two strong effects is a useful numerical consistency check. Because the multi-outcome likelihood factorises, close agreement is also expected; it does not prove that either model is correctly specified or test cross-outcome dependence.

Across all 10 outcomes in `itt-012` the medians range from −0.1 to +3.5 items, with individual 89% ranges extending as far as −3.4 and +5.4 items. This matches the `itt` pattern: the directly taught skills have the largest resolved positive estimates, while broad standardised measures have near-zero medians but intervals that still permit material effects in either direction.

**The two generalisation contrasts do not establish limited transfer.** For expressive vocabulary, `itt-015` estimates taught minus not-taught at +0.034 proportion correct (89% −0.043 to +0.111), P(>0) = 0.762: suggestive in the positive direction but wide. For receptive vocabulary, `itt-115` gives −0.003 [−0.080, +0.071], P(>0) = 0.471: inconclusive. A positive taught-minus-not-taught contrast would not by itself show negligible generalisation; that requires comparing the not-taught marginal effect with a substantively defined negligible-effect threshold.

`itt-016` is not a generalisation model. It compares taught expressive with taught receptive vocabulary: +0.008 proportion correct [−0.060, +0.076], P(>0) = 0.572, an inconclusive modality contrast. All three contrast intervals also carry the factorised-dependence limitation above.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Only the outcome-specific assigned-arm contrasts have the randomisation basis**, and even those are available-case modified intention-to-treat estimates for the observed analysis cases under the stated missing-data assumptions. Randomisation does not repair selection into the archived and observed samples; precision terms remain adjusted associations.

**These fits do not estimate residual correlation between outcomes.** They therefore say nothing about whether outcome residuals move together, still less whether one skill causes another. Association and mediation questions belong to other families.

**The shared fit does not make a paired contrast automatically valid.** Its main benefit here is a common modelling and reporting framework; confirmation of an outcome difference still requires a dependence-aware analysis.

## Model inventory

All 4 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and are publishable: `itt-012` (10 factorised outcome marginals), `itt-015` and `itt-115` (expressive and receptive taught-versus-not-taught generalisation contrasts), and `itt-016` (taught expressive-versus-receptive modality contrast).
