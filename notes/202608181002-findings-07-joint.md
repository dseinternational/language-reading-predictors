# Findings: the `joint` family — all outcomes in one model

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 4 models in the `joint` family, which fit several outcomes simultaneously instead of one at a time.

## The data

**RLI trial only**, the randomised **timepoint 1 to timepoint 2 window**, one row per child — the same data as the `itt` family. `itt-012` fits 10 outcomes together for 53 children; the three generalisation models fit 2 outcomes each for 54 children. Nothing is stacked across periods.

## What the model is for

Fitting outcomes one at a time, as the `itt` family does, treats them as unrelated. They are not: the same child produced all of them, so a child having an unusually good day, or being unusually able, moves several scores together.

Fitting them jointly does two things. It lets the model estimate the **correlation between outcomes** and use it, which can sharpen the individual estimates. More importantly, it makes **comparisons between outcomes** possible. Asking "is the effect on taught vocabulary bigger than on untaught vocabulary?" requires both effects to come from one posterior; comparing two separately-fitted models cannot properly account for the fact that the same children generated both.

## How to read the results

Each outcome still gets its own effect, read exactly as in the `itt` family. The additional quantity is a **contrast**: the difference between two outcomes' effects, with its own credible interval.

## What was found

**The joint estimates reproduce the single-outcome estimates almost exactly**, which is the main thing to check. On the model's log-odds scale:

| Outcome                | Single-outcome (`itt`) | Joint (`itt-012`)      |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Letter-sound knowledge | 0.582 [0.278, 0.877]   | 0.580 [0.271, 0.880]   |
| Word reading           | 0.354 [0.101, 0.611]   | 0.355 [0.101, 0.611]   |
| Receptive vocabulary   | 0.008 [−0.128, 0.145]  | 0.034 [−0.104, 0.172]  |
| Expressive vocabulary  | 0.007 [−0.119, 0.134]  | −0.003 [−0.132, 0.126] |

Agreement to three decimal places for the two strong effects. This is a genuine consistency check rather than a formality: if modelling the outcomes together had shifted the estimates, it would mean the single-outcome models were mis-specified in some way that mattered. They were not.

Across all 10 outcomes in `itt-012` the effects range from −0.1 to +3.5 items, with individual 89% ranges spanning −3.4 to +5.4 items — the same picture as the `itt` family: large effects on directly taught skills, nothing on broad standardised measures.

**The generalisation contrasts** (`itt-015`, `itt-016`, `itt-115`) fit taught and not-taught vocabulary together to ask whether learning transferred beyond the specific items taught. Effects range +0.4 to +1.5 items across the pairs. The consistent pattern is that taught items moved more than not-taught items, which is what the `itt` family showed separately — but fitting them jointly is what allows that comparison to be stated as a difference with an interval rather than as two numbers side by side.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Only the arm contrasts are causal**, exactly as in the `itt` family.

**Correlation between outcomes is not causation between them.** The model estimates that the outcomes' residuals move together; it does not say one skill causes another. That question belongs to the `mechanism` and `mediation` families.

**A joint model does not add information about any single outcome.** It mainly buys the ability to compare outcomes properly.

## Model inventory

All 4 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and are publishable: `itt-012` (10 outcomes with residual correlation), `itt-015`, `itt-016`, `itt-115` (taught versus not-taught generalisation contrasts).
