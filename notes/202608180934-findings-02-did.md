# Findings: the `did` family — difference-in-differences across the crossover

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 18 models in the `did` family, which re-estimate the intervention effect using the waiting-list crossover structure rather than a single post-treatment comparison.

## The data

**RLI trial only.** These models use **timepoints 1, 2 and 3** — not just the randomised window. Data are **stacked, not collapsed**: each child contributes one row per timepoint, so a typical fit has 54 children and about 162 rows. The dose companions use fewer rows (105–107) because they need session-count records.

Using three timepoints is the entire point of the design, but only one of the three comparisons contrasts treated children with untreated ones.

## What the model is for

Despite the family name, the implemented headline is **not** a literal timepoint-2-minus-timepoint-1 difference in each arm. It is an arm-by-wave repeated-level model: timepoint 1 is modelled as an outcome level, and the immediate-minus-waiting arm gap is estimated separately at each wave. The child random intercept partially pools stable between-child differences, but it does not difference them away or make each child their own fixed-effect control.

Here the model estimates the gap between arms **separately at each timepoint**, which splits into three quantities that must be read very differently:

- **`arm_gap_t1`** — the gap _before_ anyone was treated. This is a balance check, not an effect. Randomisation should make it near zero; if it is not, that is informative about chance imbalance.
- **`tau_t2`** — the gap at timepoint 2, when the immediate group has had roughly 20 weeks of intervention and the waiting group none. **This is the effect of being taught rather than not, and it is the only quantity here that is.**
- **`arm_gap_t3`** — the gap at timepoint 3, when _both_ groups have been treated, just for different durations (about 40 weeks versus 20). This is **still a comparison of randomly assigned groups** — assignment does not stop being random once the waiting list crosses over — but it compares a _different thing_: starting the intervention earlier rather than later. It is not the effect of being taught at all, because by timepoint 3 both groups have been.

A fourth quantity, `delta_crossover` (`tau_t2` minus `arm_gap_t3`), describes how much the gap between the two randomly assigned groups changed once the waiting group received the intervention. What it cannot supply is the reason: a shrinking gap is consistent with the waiting group benefiting after crossover, and equally consistent with ceiling effects, with ordinary development, or with the immediate group's earlier advantage decaying. Those explanations are inseparable here.

> [!NOTE]
> An earlier version of this note followed the model code in calling the timepoint-3 quantities "no longer randomised" and "associations". Issue #576 (2026-08-24) found that wrong in both directions: it understated what randomisation still identifies at timepoint 3, and overstated the confounding — latent ability does not confound a randomly assigned comparison. The real limitation is that the timepoint-3 contrast cannot tell you _why_ the groups differ. The wording above is the corrected version.

Each child also gets a random intercept, which partially pools their stable individual level. This is a shrunken estimate of between-child differences, **not** a full control for each child's unmeasured ability.

## What was found

**The timepoint-2 arm-gap estimates broadly agree with the headline trial estimates.** Letter-sound knowledge is +3.5 items here against +3.5 in the `itt` family; word reading +2.2 against +2.4; taught expressive vocabulary +1.5 against +1.5; phoneme blending +0.9 against +1.0. These are different statistical specifications and this family also models timepoints 1 and 3, but both timepoint-2 estimates derive their causal interpretation from the same randomisation. Their agreement is therefore a useful specification check, not independent causal identification.

The ordering is also similar: the largest positive estimates are on directly taught material. Broad standardised vocabulary remains unresolved (R −0.1, E +0.8, both with intervals several items wide in each direction), rather than showing evidence of no effect.

The DiD intervals are generally **wider** than the corresponding `itt` intervals — word reading spans −0.3 to +4.7 here versus +0.7 to +4.1 there. That is expected: this model estimates more quantities from the same children and does not use the baseline score as a precision covariate in the same way. Wider intervals are not a defect; they reflect what this design can support.

A sensitivity variant (`did-101`) with a different prior on the intercept gives +2.1 for word reading against +2.2, so the result is not an artefact of that choice. A variant with a **wider prior on the treatment effect itself** (`did-102`; the earlier description of it as "different adjustment" was wrong — nothing else in that model changes) gives +4.3 for letter-sound against +3.5 — the largest disagreement in the family, and a reminder that letter-sound's exact magnitude is less settled than its direction. That is precisely the attenuation the wider prior was registered to test for: the reference estimate should be read as conservative.

## The two Action Picture Test outcomes

`did-014` and `did-015` were added after the main run, once the test's ceilings were confirmed (see the `itt` note). Expressive grammar gives the clearer signal: a randomised timepoint-2 contrast of **+1.6 marks of 37** (89% −0.2 to +3.4) with a 0.926 probability of being positive, which is moderate evidence and a little stronger than the same outcome's ITT estimate. Expressive information is inconclusive at +0.9 whole marks (probability 0.741).

Two things to read carefully here. For information, the **pre-treatment** arm gap is itself 0.757 probability positive, so some of the timepoint-2 gap may be a starting-point difference rather than an effect — this is exactly the balance quantity the arm-by-wave design exposes and a single post-treatment comparison hides. And for both outcomes the `delta_crossover` term reaches strong directional evidence, which is **not** a treatment result: by timepoint 3 both groups have been taught, so that term describes how the difference between two randomly assigned groups changed, without identifying why it changed.

## An important caveat on how these results cleared review

Five focal terms were initially withheld because a diagnostic showed greater sensitivity to the prior than to the data. Four are randomised timepoint-2 arm contrasts (`did-001`, `did-003`, `did-013`, `did-101`); the fifth is the observational session-dose slope `mu_dose` in `did-007`, not a treatment coefficient. They were released only after an estimand-matched prior-sensitivity sweep established how the conclusion varied across a range of defensible priors.

That is a narrower guarantee than it may appear. For `did-001` (word reading), widening the treatment prior moves the estimate from **1.31 items to 2.51 items** — nearly double — while every one of those 89% intervals includes zero:

| Prior width | Effect (items) | 89% range      | P(>0) |
| ----------- | -------------- | -------------- | ----- |
| 0.25        | 1.31           | −0.66 to +3.24 | 0.858 |
| 0.50        | 2.19           | −0.31 to +4.68 | 0.920 |
| 0.75        | 2.51           | −0.20 to +5.16 | 0.932 |

So the DiD word-reading result should be read as: **the direction is robust, the magnitude is not well determined by these data alone.** The `itt` estimate for the same outcome does not have this problem, which is a reason to treat the `itt` number as the headline and this one as corroboration of direction.

## The dose companions

Three models (`did-006`, `did-007`, `did-107`) relate outcomes to how many intervention sessions a child actually received. **None reports a causal headline, by design.** Children who received more sessions differ from those who received fewer in ways that are not random — attendance reflects health, family circumstances and engagement — so a dose-outcome relationship cannot be read as "more sessions cause more progress". `did-007` in particular leans substantially on its prior and is flagged as exploratory. Treat these as descriptive.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Only `tau_t2` compares treated with untreated.** `arm_gap_t3` and `delta_crossover` compare groups that have both been treated, so although they are still comparisons of randomly assigned groups, they answer a different question — the effect of an earlier rather than a later start. A narrowing gap after the waiting group received the intervention is compatible with catch-up, ceiling effects, ordinary development or decay of the earlier advantage; this model cannot distinguish those explanations and does not show that the intervention "wore off".

**The child random intercept is not a fixed-effect control.** It partially pools stable differences; it does not make each child their own control.

**The model does not condition on the timepoint-2 score** when estimating later periods, deliberately — that score is itself affected by treatment, and conditioning on it would bias the later comparisons.

## Model inventory

All 18 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and are publishable. Primary arm-by-wave: `did-001` (W), `002` (L), `003` (B), `004` (TE), `005` (R), `008` (TR), `009` (E), `010` (F), `011` (P), `012` (N), `013` (W), `014` (EI), `015` (EG). ## What issue #576 changed, and what these numbers still are

The 2026-08-24 review (#576) corrected several things about how this family is described, gated and reported, and registered four new companions (`103`, `104`, `105`, `106`) that have **not yet been fitted**. The numbers in this note come from the reporting fits as they stood before that change, so read them with three caveats.

- **Phoneme blending (`B`, +0.9 items) is now withheld pending its pair.** Blending has ten three-alternative items, so scoring by chance gives about 3.3 of 10, and the model used here places no floor at chance. The new companion `did-103` refits it with the floor the test actually has. On the ITT side the same change roughly halved the estimate, so treat +0.9 as an upper bound until the pair is fitted and reported together.
- **The timepoint-3 wording above has been corrected** (see the note in "What the model is for"); the estimates themselves are unchanged.
- **The dose companions' reported numbers will move.** `did-006`, `007` and `107` now publish one named quantity — the session-dose marginal averaged over on-intervention rows — checked identically by their prior check, their prior-sensitivity sweep and the release gate. Before this, `did-007`'s release gate followed a different quantity from the one its report published.

See `notes/202608241130-did-family-576-remediation.md` for the full change list and which fits need regenerating.
