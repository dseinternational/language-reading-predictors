# Findings: the `did` family — difference-in-differences across the crossover

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 16 models in the `did` family, which re-estimate the intervention effect using the waiting-list crossover structure rather than a single post-treatment comparison.

## The data

**RLI trial only.** These models use **timepoints 1, 2 and 3** — not just the randomised window. Data are **stacked, not collapsed**: each child contributes one row per timepoint, so a typical fit has 54 children and about 162 rows. The dose companions use fewer rows (105–107) because they need session-count records.

Using three timepoints is the entire point of the design, but only one of the three comparisons is randomised.

## What the model is for

Despite the family name, the implemented headline is **not** a literal timepoint-2-minus-timepoint-1 difference in each arm. It is an arm-by-wave repeated-level model: timepoint 1 is modelled as an outcome level, and the immediate-minus-waiting arm gap is estimated separately at each wave. The child random intercept partially pools stable between-child differences, but it does not difference them away or make each child their own fixed-effect control.

Here the model estimates the gap between arms **separately at each timepoint**, which splits into three quantities that must be read very differently:

- **`arm_gap_t1`** — the gap _before_ anyone was treated. This is a balance check, not an effect. Randomisation should make it near zero; if it is not, that is informative about chance imbalance.
- **`tau_t2`** — the gap at timepoint 2, when the immediate group has had roughly 20 weeks of intervention and the waiting group none. **This is the randomised, causal quantity, and it is the only one.**
- **`arm_gap_t3`** — the gap at timepoint 3, when _both_ groups have been treated, just for different durations (about 40 weeks versus 20). This is no longer a randomised comparison, and it is reported as an association.

A fourth quantity, `delta_crossover` (`tau_t2` minus `arm_gap_t3`), describes how much the waiting group caught up once they received the intervention. It is descriptive: a shrinking gap is consistent with the waiting group benefiting after crossover, but it is also consistent with ceiling effects or with the immediate group's advantage decaying.

Each child also gets a random intercept, which partially pools their stable individual level. This is a shrunken estimate of between-child differences, **not** a full control for each child's unmeasured ability.

## What was found

**The timepoint-2 arm-gap estimates broadly agree with the headline trial estimates.** Letter-sound knowledge is +3.5 items here against +3.5 in the `itt` family; word reading +2.2 against +2.4; taught expressive vocabulary +1.5 against +1.5; phoneme blending +0.9 against +1.0. These are different statistical specifications and this family also models timepoints 1 and 3, but both timepoint-2 estimates derive their causal interpretation from the same randomisation. Their agreement is therefore a useful specification check, not independent causal identification.

The ordering is also similar: the largest positive estimates are on directly taught material. Broad standardised vocabulary remains unresolved (R −0.1, E +0.8, both with intervals several items wide in each direction), rather than showing evidence of no effect.

The DiD intervals are generally **wider** than the corresponding `itt` intervals — word reading spans −0.3 to +4.7 here versus +0.7 to +4.1 there. That is expected: this model estimates more quantities from the same children and does not use the baseline score as a precision covariate in the same way. Wider intervals are not a defect; they reflect what this design can support.

A sensitivity variant (`did-101`) with a different prior on the intercept gives +2.1 for word reading against +2.2, so the result is not an artefact of that choice. A variant with different adjustment (`did-102`) gives +4.3 for letter-sound against +3.5 — the largest disagreement in the family, and a reminder that letter-sound's exact magnitude is less settled than its direction.

## An important caveat on how these results cleared review

Five focal terms were initially withheld because a diagnostic showed greater sensitivity to the prior than to the data. Four are randomised timepoint-2 arm contrasts (`did-001`, `did-003`, `did-013`, `did-101`); the fifth is the observational session-dose slope `mu_dose` in `did-007`, not a treatment coefficient. They were released only after an estimand-matched prior-sensitivity sweep established how the conclusion varied across a range of defensible priors.

That is a narrower guarantee than it may appear. For `did-001` (word reading), widening the treatment prior moves the estimate from **1.31 items to 2.51 items** — nearly double — while every one of those 89% intervals includes zero:

| Prior width | Effect (items) | 89% range      | P(>0) |
| ----------- | -------------- | -------------- | ----- |
| 0.25        | 1.31           | −0.66 to +3.24 | 0.858 |
| 0.50        | 2.19           | −0.31 to +4.68 | 0.920 |
| 0.75        | 2.51           | −0.20 to +5.16 | 0.932 |

So the DiD word-reading result should be read as: **the direction is robust, the magnitude is not well determined by these data alone.** The `itt` estimate for the same outcome does not have this problem, which is a reason to treat the `itt` number as the headline and this one as corroboration of direction.

## The dose companions

Three models (`did-006`, `did-007`, `did-107`) relate outcomes to how many intervention sessions a child actually received. **None reports a causal headline, by design.** Children who received more sessions differ from those who received fewer in ways that are not random — attendance reflects health, family circumstances and engagement — so a dose-outcome relationship cannot be read as "more sessions cause more progress". `did-007` in particular leans substantially on its prior and is flagged as exploratory. Treat these as descriptive.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Only `tau_t2` is causal.** `arm_gap_t3` and `delta_crossover` compare groups that have both been treated. A narrowing gap after the waiting group received the intervention is compatible with catch-up, ceiling effects or decay of the earlier advantage; this model cannot distinguish those explanations and does not show that the intervention "wore off".

**The child random intercept is not a fixed-effect control.** It partially pools stable differences; it does not make each child their own control.

**The model does not condition on the timepoint-2 score** when estimating later periods, deliberately — that score is itself affected by treatment, and conditioning on it would bias the later comparisons.
