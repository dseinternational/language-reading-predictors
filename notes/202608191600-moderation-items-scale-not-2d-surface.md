# Moderation on the items scale: the two-dimensional letter-sound × decoding surface dropped

**Date:** 2026-08-19. **Decision:** the two-dimensional HSGP surface over letter sounds and nonword decoding (or blending) proposed in section 8 of the question-organised findings report (`notes/202608182200-findings-by-question.md`) is **dropped**. The question it was meant to settle — whether the negative letter-sound × decoding and letter-sound × blending interactions are substitution or an artefact of the bounded scale — is a question about the link, not the functional form, and is answered from the stored posteriors without a refit. Every moderated `mechanism` fit now publishes the interaction re-expressed in outcome items (`moderation_items.csv`), the key-findings box carries that sentence, and a wording defect in the moderated key findings is corrected. Confirmed by the author on 2026-08-19 after the premise check below.

## The premise, checked

The section-8 bullet read: "The moderation fits impose a linear interaction on the logit scale, which builds in substitution; a two-dimensional HSGP … would show the joint surface without that constraint and settle whether the negative interactions are real or a scale artefact."

**What is right.** A product term on the latent logit scale does not carry its sign over to items on a bounded outcome. On the fitted rows of these models word reading sits in the bottom fifth of its range (mean 14 of 79 words, p ≈ 0.17). Below the midpoint the logit is concave, so two positive effects that are _additive in words_ must show a negative logit product, and logit-additivity (`gamma_int = 0`) is _synergy_ in words (the inverse logit is convex there). A negative `gamma_int` therefore cannot by itself distinguish substitution from additivity in words — the bullet's worry is real.

**What is wrong.** The bilinear form does not "build in" a sign; the ambiguity is the link. A two-dimensional HSGP fitted through the same logit link inherits it exactly — reading "substitution" off its surface needs the same re-expression in items. That re-expression is a pure function of the stored posterior: evaluate the fitted surface in items at chosen cells, every other term held at its fitted value for each row and averaged over rows — the family's own end-to-end curve-contrast convention — and compare the letter-sound increment at a low and at a high moderator cell.

## Why a two-dimensional surface would not have added what the bullet wanted

A 2-D surface adds freedom from the bilinear _form_, and the data cannot support it where the form question lives. The joint support is a ridge (letter sounds and each moderator correlate about 0.6): on the `mech-063` rows no child has 12 or fewer sounds with six or more nonwords, one has 12 or fewer sounds with any nonword, and half the rows are at N = 0; on the `mech-061` rows no child has 12 or fewer sounds with eight or more blending items. The "low letter sounds, high decoding" corner a synergy-versus-substitution surface needs is empty and would be prior; the interaction is identified by the 100 rows at 21 or more sounds where the moderator varies. Out of sample the data barely distinguish even the bilinear interaction from none — nested PSIS-LOO with exact refits for the one high-Pareto-k child: `mech-061` against `161` +2.0 ± 1.4 elpd, `mech-063` against `163` 0.0 ± 0.7 — and a surface with two lengthscales and 36–100 basis functions on about 150 rows from 53 children would be less distinguishable still. This family already had to cut its one-dimensional basis to six functions with a tighter lengthscale prior (`notes/202607251500-mech-hsgp-reparameterisation.md`) to stop its leave-one-out refits diverging.

## A caution found on the way (not a finding)

Every moderated letter-sound → word-reading fit returns a negative `gamma_int` of about the same size whatever the moderator — blending −0.11, taught expressive −0.09, expressive −0.07, taught receptive −0.06, age −0.06, decoding −0.05, receptive −0.05 — including age, whose own main effect is negative. The Haldane-corrected logit stretches the letter-sound ceiling: on the fitted rows the standardised exposure is +0.55 at 28 sounds and +2.18 at 32, so the top four items span 1.6 SD while the middle 23 (5 → 28) span 2.4, and the 18% of rows at 30 or more sounds carry between about half and four-fifths of Σ(z<sub>L</sub>·z<sub>M</sub>)² (60% for blending, 80% for decoding); z<sub>L</sub>·z<sub>M</sub> correlates about 0.7 with z<sub>L</sub>². Some of the uniform negativity may therefore be curvature at the letter-sound ceiling that a six-function curve with a tight lengthscale cannot express, rather than moderation. This is a hypothesis about the exposure axis — one-dimensional — and a two-dimensional surface over the same standardised axis would inherit it. The cheap check, if it is ever wanted, is one moderated refit with the interaction built on the count-standardised or top-clipped letter-sound score; it is optional and unscheduled, and refits are in any case held until the author's review of the August findings is complete.
