# Findings by question: the intervention, word reading and its predictors

**This report answers seven questions using the 2026-08-20 full refit (242 Bayesian models across 23 families, all passing the convergence gate with zero divergences; 236 publishable) — see `notes/202608200800-full-refit-both-layers-2026-08.md`.** It was re-read from the new artefacts on 2026-08-20; the changes are the `level_factors` re-parameterisation (#552), four new `pooled_levels` exposures (#553) and small movements throughout from the corrected hearing coding. It reorganises the per-family findings notes (`notes/202608180931-findings-00-overview.md` and its companions) around the questions a reader actually asks, so the same fit may appear under several headings. Every number below is read from a stored fit's own artefacts; the model identifiers are given so each can be traced. **Read the overview note first** for the study designs, the vocabulary and the evidence ladder — in brief: intervals are 89% equal-tailed credible ranges around the posterior median; P is the posterior probability of the stated direction; the labels are suggestive (P ≥ 0.75), moderate (≥ 0.91), strong (≥ 0.97) and very strong (≥ 0.99), with "inconclusive" below 0.75; and only the randomised timepoint-2 arm contrasts carry a causal reading — every coefficient on a skill, a covariate or a dose is an adjusted association. All results are preliminary and exploratory.

**The trial in one paragraph.** 57 children with Down syndrome (aged roughly 5–10) were randomised to receive a reading and language intervention immediately (29) or after a 20-week wait (28); 54 are in the analysis files (28 immediate, 26 waiting), assessed at four timepoints. Only the timepoint-2 comparison — after 20 weeks, when one arm has been taught and the other has not — is a randomised contrast; by timepoint 3 both arms have been taught. Outcomes are bounded test scores ("k out of n items"), so results are reported in **items** on each test's own scale: word reading is out of 79 words, letter-sound knowledge out of 32, phoneme blending out of 10, nonword reading out of 6.

## 1. What effects did the intervention have on the outcomes of interest?

**Short answer.** The intervention's clearest effects are on what it taught. After 20 weeks the immediate arm knew about 3.5 more letter sounds (very strong evidence), read about 2.4 more words (strong), knew about 1.5 more of the taught expressive words and 1.4 more of the taught receptive words (strong / moderate), and blended about one more item (strong). Standardised vocabulary (receptive and expressive) shows no detectable effect: medians near zero with intervals several items wide in each direction. Between those poles sit modest leans — not-taught vocabulary from the taught blocks, basic concepts, expressive grammar, receptive grammar — none of which clears zero. The two heavily floored measures (phonetic spelling, nonword reading) are estimated as the chance of moving off the floor and are suggestive at best. Four different model families agree on the ordering, and where they disagree it is on the vocabulary measures, for reasons that are understood.

**The evidence.** The table gives the randomised timepoint-2 contrast from the four families that estimate it, in items (or percentage points for the two off-floor outcomes), positive meaning the immediate arm did better. The `itt` column is the headline (an available-case modified intention-to-treat estimate: one row per child at timepoint 2, adjusted for the child's own baseline and age). **Direction versus size.** The labels above describe how sure the direction is, not whether the effect is big enough to matter. Against each outcome's practical-difference threshold (half the waiting-list arm's natural first-period gain, floored at one item — a rule set after the initial results review, so post-hoc for this run, read with its sensitivity analysis, and since confirmed as the project's standing rule for future rounds), the probability that the benefit reaches that size is 0.91 for letter sounds (2 items), 0.90 for word reading (1 word), 0.78 for taught expressive and 0.69 for taught receptive vocabulary (1 item), 0.49 for blending and for expressive grammar (1 item), and 0.24 and 0.19 for receptive and expressive vocabulary (2 items). So letter sounds and word reading are the two outcomes where "almost certainly positive" and "almost certainly worth having" coincide; taught vocabulary is positive but of uncertain practical size; blending's ordinary-model estimate (+1.0) shrinks to +0.5 [−0.1, +1.1] (suggestive) once the one-third guessing rate of a three-choice task is modelled explicitly (`lrp-rli-itt-108`), and the project requires that pair to be reported together.

**Where the families disagree.** They agree closely on the taught skills, on word reading and on the ordering. Two points deserve a sentence each. First, the `did` word-reading estimate leans on its prior: widening the treatment prior moves it from 1.3 to 2.5 items with every interval spanning zero, so it corroborates the direction of the `itt` result rather than its size. Second, **the vocabulary disagreement that stood here in August has largely resolved.** It was an artefact of how `level_factors` reported its contrast: under the old free parameterisation that family reported the timepoint-2 arm gap itself, which for receptive vocabulary was −3.7 items — and that gap turned out to be almost entirely the gap the arms started with. Refitted on the timepoint-1-centred parameterisation (#552), the family reports the _change_ in the gap over the randomised window, which is +0.2 for receptive and +0.1 for expressive vocabulary, both inconclusive and in line with `itt` and `did`. The decomposition is exact: receptive vocabulary's timepoint-1 gap is −0.161 log-odds and its randomised change +0.008, summing to the −0.153 the levels view still reports. What remains is `gain_factors` at −2.0 items for receptive vocabulary with P(negative) = 0.80 — one family leaning weakly negative rather than two. Under every family a vocabulary benefit of two or more items is unlikely but not ruled out.

**Generalisation beyond the taught items.** The taught-versus-not-taught contrasts from the joint model are +0.19 log-odds [−0.18, +0.55] (P = 0.79) for expressive and −0.05 [−0.41, +0.31] for receptive vocabulary; the taught-expressive-versus-taught-receptive contrast is +0.07 [−0.25, +0.39] (`lrp-rli-itt-015/115/016`). The dependence caveat these carried in August has been discharged: companions that model the within-child residual correlation (`itt-215/315/216`, #551) move the point estimates by at most 0.006 log-odds and widen the intervals by 3–5%. They permit some generalisation to untaught words without establishing how much — the expressive contrast is suggestive at best and the other two are inconclusive. The block-2 exposure contrasts (`lrp-rli-bx-001–004`) are all inside ±1 item with intervals spanning zero.

**Later timepoints and dose.** After the waiting arm crossed over, the arm gaps at timepoint 3 are associations, not effects; for word reading the immediate arm is still ahead with P = 0.84 and the catch-up term is inconclusive (`lrp-rli-did-001`). Session dose is observational: one standard deviation more sessions (about 31 against a mean of about 54) goes with +1.2 words [+0.4, +2.0] and +0.8 letter sounds [+0.2, +1.4], with the period-varying and pooled versions agreeing (`lrp-rli-dose-077/177/277`). Children who attend more differ from those who attend less, so this describes who progressed, not what more sessions would cause. Whether the treatment effect on word reading depends on baseline ability or baseline reading was tested (`lrp-rli-gf-201`): both interactions are inconclusive (+0.01 [−0.18, +0.19]; −0.06 [−0.27, +0.14]).

**Scope.** These are available-case estimates for the children with the required observations, not estimates for all 57 randomised children; the word-reading section says how much that matters. The Action Picture Test scales were fitted only after their ceilings were confirmed on 2026-08-18; expressive grammar's lean (+1.0 in `itt`, +1.6 in `did`) is the strongest among outcomes the intervention did not teach directly, and expressive information's crossover model suggests the immediate arm may have started slightly ahead (pre-treatment gap P = 0.76), which is a reason for caution.

## 2. How did the intervention affect word reading?

**Short answer.** After 20 weeks the children taught immediately read about **2.4 more words out of 79** than the waiting arm (89% +0.7 to +4.1; P(> 0) = 0.986, strong), with a 0.90 probability that the gain is at least one word and a 0.09 probability that it is too small to matter either way. The estimate is stable across adjustment for ability (+2.2), socio-economic status (+2.5 on the 33 children with SES data), and site (+2.6), and across the other three treatment families (+2.2, +2.6, +1.7). It is not assumption-free: four of the 57 randomised children have no timepoint-2 word-reading score, and under unrestricted assumptions about them the direction can reverse. And the models say the effect ran almost entirely through letter-sound knowledge.

**The headline and its envelope** (`lrp-rli-itt-010`, 53 children: 28 immediate, 25 waiting):

| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------ |
| Headline: 53 observed outcomes, own baseline + age              | **+2.4**         | +0.7 to +4.1 |
| Same 53, screening word reading and age as covariates           | +2.0             | 0.0 to +4.0  |
| All 57 randomised, standardised under missing-at-random         | +2.0             | 0.0 to +4.0  |

Under reasonable assumptions the effect stays positive and around two words; the last row is the honest bound. The result should never be quoted without it.

**Robustness and corroboration.** Ability-adjusted +2.2 [+0.5, +4.0] (`itt-024`); SES-adjusted +2.5 [+0.3, +4.7] against +2.4 on the same 33 children unadjusted (`itt-013/014`); site-adjusted +2.6 [+0.8, +4.3] (`itt-027`). The arm-by-wave model gives +2.2 [−0.3, +4.7] (P = 0.92; prior-sensitive in magnitude, direction stable), the period-stacked gain model +2.6 [+0.9, +4.3] (P = 0.99), the levels model +1.7 [−1.0, +4.2] (P = 0.84), and the latent-change model's window-1 arm contrast in latent word-reading change is +0.40 log-odds [+0.09, +0.73] (P = 0.98; `lrp-rli-lcsm-082`). The onset-aligned per-protocol view — both arms lined up by when their own teaching started, a non-randomised comparison — gives +2.1 [−0.5, +4.8] (P = 0.91; `lrp-rli-al-001`).

The picture is consistent: the part of the word-reading effect that runs through letter-sound knowledge is well identified (+1.7 words, essentially all of the total in the single-window fits, about a quarter of it in the period-stacked fit), while vocabulary, blending and nonword decoding carry essentially none of it, and the negative-control mediator behaves as a negative control should. Two cautions. The proportion mediated is badly determined (0.83 with an 89% range from −0.9 to +3.3), because the total itself has an interval that touches zero. And the decomposition assumes no unmeasured confounding of the mediator–outcome link; a calibration against session-related confounding shows an amount of confounding that could plausibly account for the estimated indirect effect (the tipping point is about half the fitted mediator–outcome slope), so "runs through letter sounds" is the model's best reading, not a demonstrated channel. It is, however, exactly the pattern the letter-sound → word-reading associations in question 5 predict.

**Beyond timepoint 2.** The immediate arm's advantage is still visible at timepoint 3 (association only, P = 0.84) and the catch-up term is inconclusive (`did-001`), so the data neither show the effect wearing off nor the waiting arm overtaking. The observational dose relationship (+1.2 words per SD of sessions) points the same way as the randomised effect.

## 3. What predictors are associated with gains in word reading across all periods?

**Short answer.** Across the three periods, the children who gained most words were those who started lower on word reading (regression to the mean and the ceiling), were younger, and — given their starting point — had more letter-sound knowledge, more taught vocabulary, more nonword decoding, clearer speech, and attended more sessions. Age is the one baseline characteristic that predicts gain consistently and negatively in every family. Nothing about a child's general ability, hearing, blending or standardised vocabulary predicts word-reading gain reliably once the others are in the model. All of this is association: which children progressed, not what would change their progress.

The treatment term is the only causal row. Note that the "own baseline" row is a strong positive coefficient on the logit scale of a post-score-given-pre-score model, which is the same thing as saying that gains in items are largest for children starting low.

**Age, not time in the study.** Because every child ages about the same amount between assessments (the spacing varies by only ±0.6 months), age and wave are collinear within a child — so it is fair to ask whether the negative age term means _older children_ gain less or _later periods_ deliver less. The models already separate the two. Every stacked family that carries age (`gain_factors`, `mechanism`, `level_factors`, `dose_response`, `block_exposure`; `did` uses baseline age outright) also carries per-period or per-wave intercepts, and once wave is in the model 99.9% of the remaining age variation is between-child (between-child SD of age 15.7 months against a within-child residual of 0.5 months after wave means), so the age coefficient is identified from how old a child was at baseline, while any "later periods gain less" pattern sits in the period intercepts (in `gf-001` those run +0.23, −0.03, −0.23 log-odds across the three periods — a decline, but with intervals of about ±0.47). The single-row models agree without any collinearity to worry about: baseline age in `adj-065` is −2.9 words per SD, and age at intervention onset in the onset-aligned per-protocol frame (`lrp-rli-al-001`) is −0.18 log-odds per SD [−0.32, −0.04]. The one fit where age and window are genuinely confounded is `lcsm-067`, which has no window intercepts; its windowed siblings `lcsm-082`/`091` put the age coupling at −0.10/−0.11 instead of −0.15, so about a third of that estimate was the later-window decline and the rest is age. The finding is therefore about older children: at the same starting level, an older child gains less over a period. Two readings remain open and the data cannot separate them — older children at the same low starting level are a selected group (age and starting level are correlated, so "same score, older" partly means "further behind for their age"), and the age band is narrow (5–10 years) — so this is a description of who progressed, not a developmental law (checked 2026-08-19).

And a `mechanism` slope conditions on the outcome's own start-of-period score, which removes the stable between-child part of the association; these are within-period readings, and they are far smaller than the level associations in question 4 for exactly that reason. The mechanism family's own adjusters point the same way as the tables above: in the letter-sound curve fit, age −0.10 [−0.18, −0.02], speech production +0.10 [+0.02, +0.19], attendance +0.14 [+0.06, +0.22] and hearing +0.07 [−0.00, +0.15] on the word-reading logit per SD.

**Prior levels and later change** (latent change-score models, `lrp-rli-lcsm-067/082/091`, 54 children, all four waves; couplings are per unit of the source's latent logit, not per SD, so they are comparable in direction and evidence but not in size across sources): a higher prior letter-sound level goes with larger subsequent word-reading change (0.14 [+0.05, +0.23], 0.24 [+0.14, +0.36] and 0.22 [+0.12, +0.33] across the three specifications, all P ≥ 0.99); so does a higher prior expressive-vocabulary level (0.29 [+0.10, +0.48] and 0.25 [+0.06, +0.44], P ≥ 0.98) — and put on one scale (SDs of later reading change per SD of prior level, standardised by the model's own latent scales; `standardised_couplings.csv`, added 2026-08-19), the ordering reverses: letter sounds +0.64 [+0.23, +1.01] against vocabulary +0.43 [+0.15, +0.72] in `lcsm-067` (contrast +0.21 [−0.38, +0.76], P = 0.72, inconclusive) and +0.72 [+0.43, +1.00] against +0.26 [+0.06, +0.46] in `lcsm-091` (contrast +0.47 [+0.05, +0.86], P = 0.96, moderate), because letter-sound levels spread about three times as widely on the latent scale; prior blending does not (0.04 [−0.06, +0.14]); and prior letter-sound _change_ does not predict later reading change over and above level (0.05 [−0.17, +0.26]). Age is negative in every one of these (−0.10 to −0.15), and reading's own self-feedback is negative (−0.22 to −0.27), the latent-scale form of the ceiling and regression-to-the-mean effect.

**Two more views agree.** An errors-in-variables model that treats letter sounds, blending and nonword reading as indicators of one latent code factor puts that factor's association with word-reading gain at +0.35 log-odds per SD [+0.09, +0.60] (P = 0.985), with age −0.35 [−0.53, −0.17] and hearing +0.23 [+0.05, +0.40] alongside (`lrp-rli-mm-002`, 51 children). And baseline non-verbal ability does not predict the word-reading growth rate across the four waves (−0.05 [−0.23, +0.13], `lrp-rli-gc-069`), although it does predict grammar's (+0.12 [+0.02, +0.20]).

**What this does not say.** None of these coefficients is a lever. They were chosen and adjusted to make the treatment estimate clean, which generally makes them less interpretable individually (the Table 2 fallacy), and the between-child ones absorb whatever stable differences the children bring. The gradient-boosting layer's rankings for word-reading gain are not part of this run's stored artefacts, so they are not compared here.

**What the exploratory (step-1) models say about the same question.** The gradient-boosting model of word-reading gain (`lrp-rli-gbg-012`) and the Bayesian many-predictor ranking of the same target (`lrp-rli-hs-001`) were both refitted in the 2026-08-20 sweep and compared at construct level. **They agree that age matters and disagree about almost everything else** — the rank correlation between them is +0.36 across the eight shared constructs. Both put age first and negative; after that the tree model ranks background variables whose permutation importance is exactly zero, and the horseshoe ranks letter sounds second at P(|β| > δ) = 0.42, which is well below anything the project would call evidence.

The reason is visible in the model's own accuracy: `gbg-012` explains **0.08** of the out-of-sample variance in word-reading gain. Two honest methods asked to rank predictors of a target carrying that little signal will rank the noise differently, and they do. **The step-1 layer therefore corroborates the negative part of this section's answer — that gains are weakly predictable from anything measured here — and adds no predictor the statistical models missed.** It is not independent evidence for any positive claim in the table above.

## 4. What predictors are associated with levels of word reading at each timepoint?

**Short answer.** At every timepoint the strongest concurrent correlate of how many words a child reads is how many letter sounds they know — and the association is largest at timepoint 2 (about +9 words per SD of letter sounds, mutually adjusted for the other five skills, ability, hearing, speech and phonological memory) and still around +4 to +6 words at timepoints 3 and 4. Taught vocabulary, blending, and by timepoints 3 and 4 the broad vocabulary measures, are the next tier. Almost all of the letter-sound / word-reading level association lives _between_ children: children who know more letter sounds across the study read far more across the study, whereas within a child, being above one's own letter-sound average at a wave says almost nothing about being above one's own reading average — the pattern a shared cause predicts.

**Per-timepoint concurrent associations** (`lrp-rli-ca-001`, 53 children at t1–t3 and 51 at t4; word reading at that timepoint on the six skills at the same timepoint plus non-verbal ability, hearing, speech production and phonological memory, age and arm; entries are words per +1 SD of the predictor, mutually adjusted, with P(> 0)):

**Pooled across the four waves, and split** (`lrp-rli-pl-001`, 53 children, 210 child-waves, per-wave intercepts, adjusted for ability, hearing, speech and age): the _between-child_ letter-sound association is +1.61 log-odds per SD [+1.34, +1.87] — about +17 words per SD at the average fitted level — while the _within-child_ association is +0.04 [−0.06, +0.14] (P = 0.74, inconclusive). Without the wave intercepts (`pl-101`) the within-child term becomes +0.19 [+0.08, +0.29] with very strong evidence, which is what shared maturation alone manufactures when both skills rise across the study. In the same fit speech production is associated with the reading level (+0.26 log-odds per SD [+0.13, +0.40], P = 0.999), non-verbal ability weakly (+0.13 [−0.06, +0.33]), age weakly (+0.11 [−0.09, +0.31]) and the hearing flag not at all (−0.00 [−0.19, +0.18]). Taken together with the transition and latent-change models, the temporally ordered within-child question — does a rise in a child's own letter sounds precede a rise in their own reading — has been looked at from every angle the data support, and the answer is the same each time: the lagged, blended association of period-start letter sounds with the period's reading growth is modest (`gf-001`, +0.07 log-odds per SD [−0.01, +0.15], P = 0.92), the within-child change-on-change coupling is inconclusive (`lcsm-091`, +0.05 [−0.17, +0.26]), and the within-child level association is near zero (above); with only 15% of the variance in period-start letter sounds lying within child, a further lagged within-child model would add a fourth look with the same answer (checked 2026-08-19).

**Latent structure.** Treating the tests as indicators of three latent domains, the code domain (letter sounds, blending, nonword reading) correlates with the vocabulary domain at about 0.70 at every wave [roughly 0.57 to 0.79], with grammar at about 0.61, and vocabulary with grammar at about 0.88 (`lrp-rli-lcf-001`; the cross-sectional model `mm-001` gives 0.79, 0.70 and 0.83). These are disattenuated correlations between domains, and they say that whatever separates children on reading-related code skills also separates them substantially on language.

**What this does not say.** These are level associations at one moment, with no temporal ordering at all: they carry _less_ structure than the change-based models in question 3, and every one of them absorbs the stable between-child differences (ability, home, schooling) that measured ability only partly captures.

**What the exploratory (step-1) models say about the same question.** Levels are a different story from gains. The gradient-boosting model of word-reading level (`lrp-rli-gbl-012`) explains **0.56** of the out-of-sample variance, and its ranking is led by phonetic spelling and letter-sound knowledge, then the other reading measures — the same constructs this section's models put first. Its Bayesian counterpart (`lrp-rli-hs-002`) leads with letter sounds and expressive vocabulary, both at P(|β| > δ) ≈ 0.99. The rank correlation between the two methods is **+0.54**, and they share two of their top three constructs.

Two cautions travel with that agreement. The level models are **concurrent** — they describe a child's score using other scores taken at the same assessment, so part of what they capture is that the measures were taken from the same child on the same day, not that one predicts the other. And agreement between step 1 and step 2 says the ranking reflects something in the data rather than one method's quirks; it says nothing about direction of influence. Both layers are describing which children are ahead.

## 5. How far does letter-sound knowledge contribute to better word reading, and does that depend on decoding skill?

**Short answer.** Letter-sound knowledge is the best-supported correlate of word reading in this dataset on every view — levels, gains and change — and it is the only route through which the models can trace the intervention's word-reading effect. But the evidence for a _contribution_ in the causal sense is indirect: the association survives adjustment for measured general ability essentially unchanged, is much larger for nonword decoding than for word reading in the way a decoding route predicts, and is the channel the mediation models pick out; against that, it lives almost entirely between children rather than within them, and the intervention taught letter sounds and reading together, so the design cannot separate the two. On the second half of the question, the data point the _opposite_ way from "it depends on full decoding": adjusting for nonword decoding leaves most of the letter-sound / word-reading association standing, nonword decoding carries none of the intervention's word-reading effect over and above letter sounds, and the letter-sound association is if anything shallower, not steeper, where blending or decoding is higher.

**The association, and how robust it is.** In the transition models the letter-sound slope is +0.25 log-odds per SD [+0.15, +0.35] (`mech-101`), or +9.9 words [+6.1, +13.5] across the observed range linearly and +6.8 words [+2.5, +11.1] as a flexible curve (`mech-058`), which rises gently and then more steeply above about 25 letter sounds; concurrently, +9.0 words per SD at timepoint 2 (`ca-001`); across waves, +1.61 log-odds per SD between children (`pl-001`); and prior letter-sound level predicts later reading change in every latent-change specification (0.14 to 0.24 per unit latent logit, P ≥ 0.99). Adjusting additionally for the measured non-verbal ability proxy leaves the slope at +0.245 [+0.146, +0.343] and the curve's endpoint contrast at +6.5 words [+2.2, +10.9], a 4% shift with a maximum pointwise gap of 0.15 words between the two curves (`mech-201`, `mech-258`).

**Specificity: the decoding contrast.** The pre-specified test is whether letter sounds predict nonword decoding _more_ than word reading, which a pure general-ability account gives no reason to expect. They do: +1.03 log-odds per SD [+0.74, +1.34] for nonwords against +0.25 for words, a difference of +0.78 [+0.48, +1.10] from the single-outcome fits and +0.81 [+0.50, +1.13] from the identified bivariate model (`lrp-rli-jm-002`), with essentially all posterior mass above zero. The four negative-control outcomes — receptive and expressive vocabulary, grammar, basic concepts, which the causal diagram gives letter sounds no route to — are nonetheless clearly positive (0.10 to 0.29 log-odds per SD), so letter-sound knowledge also carries a broad non-specific association with every skill measured; adjusting for measured ability removes some of that (grammar drops from 0.12 to 0.07, receptive vocabulary and basic concepts lose about a seventh) but not most of it, while leaving the written-code slopes untouched (nonwords −0.3%, words −2.0%; contrast +0.78 → +0.78). So the shared cause behind the non-specific component is not, or not only, the ability this battery measures. Two more cautions: nonword reading is a six-item measure with most children at the floor, so its logit slope is not directly commensurate with a 79-item scale; and on the _level_ scale the same contrast is near zero or negative (letter sounds relate no more, and at some waves less, to nonword than to word-reading levels: Δ = −0.49 [−0.98, +0.02] at timepoint 1, −0.15, −0.26 and +0.06 [−0.29, +0.40] at timepoints 2–4 in the per-wave joint levels model `lrp-rli-jm-001`) — the change-based and level-based Δs are different estimands, and only the change-based one was pre-specified as the decoding signature.

**Between or within children?** The pooled-levels decomposition is the sharpest statement of the limit: +1.61 between children against +0.04 [−0.06, +0.14] within a child for word reading (`pl-001`), and +1.79 against +0.33 [−0.02, +0.69] (P = 0.93) for nonword decoding (`pl-002`). If letter-sound knowledge were driving word reading within a child, the within-child term is where it should show; a small within-child association is not evidence of no influence — within-child variation is small and levels are a blunt instrument for it, and the transition models are the better-powered within-child view — but it is the pattern a shared-cause account predicts. Note also that in `mech-058` the association is estimated given the child's own start-of-period reading, so it already excludes most stable between-child variation, and it is still clearly positive.

**Through the intervention.** The models trace the timepoint-2 word-reading effect through letter sounds (+1.79 words [+0.66, +3.37] of a +2.06 total; and +3.07 [+1.22, +5.45] when timepoint-2 letter sounds carry it to timepoint-4 reading in `med-076` — +3.14 [+1.31, +5.38] with timepoint-3 reading in `med-059`'s ordering sensitivity — though by then both arms have been treated, so neither later total is a randomised contrast), and through nothing else tested. That is consistent with letter-sound knowledge being on the path, but the intervention taught reading and letter sounds together, the confounding calibration says session-related confounding could account for the indirect effect, and the total effect's own interval touches zero.

**Does it depend on decoding skill (nonword reading) or blending?**

- _Adjustment._ Adding same-timepoint nonword decoding to the concurrent model reduces the letter-sound association with word reading from +11.0 to +7.2 words per SD at timepoint 2 (and from +9.1 to +6.8 at timepoint 3, +11.1 to +6.5 at timepoint 4), while nonword decoding takes +2.9 [+1.3, +4.6] rising to +6.3 [+3.8, +8.9] words per SD by timepoint 4 (`ca-010` vs `ca-011`). The per-wave joint levels model asks the same question within one likelihood: holding nonword decoding fixed, the letter-sound slope on word reading retains 0.91 [0.58, 1.06] of its size at timepoint 1, 0.77 [0.39, 0.94] at timepoint 2, 0.81 [0.55, 0.96] at timepoint 3 and 0.58 [0.16, 0.83] at timepoint 4 (`jm-001`, `share_retained`). So decoding accounts for a tenth to two-fifths of the letter-sound level association, growing as the children read more; it does not absorb it. In the period-stacked gain model both enter with similar, moderate support (letter sounds +1.0, nonword decoding +0.6 words per SD).
- _Moderation._ Whether the letter-sound slope is steeper for children with better decoding was tested directly (`mech-063`, letter sounds moderated by same-period nonword decoding): the interaction is −0.05 log-odds [−0.14, +0.03] (P(negative) = 0.85, suggestive) — if anything shallower where decoding is higher — while nonword decoding's own main effect given letter sounds is +0.10 [+0.02, +0.18] (`mech-163`). With phoneme blending as the moderator (`mech-061`) the interaction is −0.11 [−0.21, −0.02] (P(negative) = 0.97, strong), blending's main effect +0.10 [+0.01, +0.18]. On the logit scale a negative interaction between two positive predictors means substitution rather than synergy — but on a bounded outcome that sign is not by itself a statement about words: these children read in the bottom fifth of the scale (mean 14 of 79 words), where the logit is concave, so two effects that are additive in words would show a negative logit product, and logit-additivity would be synergy in words. Re-expressed in words at the interquartile cells of the fitted rows (`moderation_items.csv`, added 2026-08-19 without a refit), the letter-sound increment from 17 to 28 sounds is worth +2.9 words [+0.9, +4.8] at blending 4 and +1.7 [−0.5, +3.8] at blending 8 — a difference of −1.1 words [−2.3, −0.0] (P(negative) = 0.95, moderate), where logit-additivity would have shown +0.2 — so for blending the substitution is not an artefact of the scale; for decoding the same contrast is −0.7 words [−2.2, +0.7] (P = 0.79), inconclusive on both scales, as it is for the four vocabulary moderators. This is associational moderation, not causal effect modification. One caution on what these interaction terms measure: every moderated letter-sound fit returns a negative interaction of about the same size whatever the moderator — blending, decoding, the four vocabulary measures and age alike — and the letter-sound logit stretches the ceiling (the top four of 32 sounds span 1.6 SD of the standardised exposure, the middle 23 span 2.4), so the 18% of rows at 30 or more sounds carry between about half and four-fifths of the product term's leverage; some of that uniform negativity may be curvature at the letter-sound ceiling rather than moderation. Either way there is no support here for the idea that letter-sound knowledge only pays off once decoding is in place.
- _Mediation._ Nonword decoding carries none of the intervention's word-reading effect over and above letter sounds (sequential route L → N → W within the timepoint-2 window: NIE through N −0.06 [−0.58, +0.20]; nonword decoding alone +0.02 [−0.39, +0.51]), and neither does blending (−0.03). The forward-carrying version of the question — does later decoding carry an earlier letter-sound advantage into later reading — is answered, as far as these data can answer it, by two lagged associations already fitted: period-start letter sounds predict being off the decoding floor at period end strongly (+0.55 log-odds per SD [+0.29, +0.82], P = 0.9996; `gf-011`), and period-start decoding predicts period-end word reading given period-start letter sounds only weakly (+0.05 per SD [−0.01, +0.10], P = 0.92; `gf-001`) — a strong first link and a weak second, the same shape as the within-window chain; and by timepoint 3 the arms no longer differ in decoding (48% off the floor in each; adjusted gap −0.15 log-odds [−0.78, +0.48], `lf-011`), so there is no arm-driven decoding difference left for a later chain to carry.
- _Timing._ Children with more letter sounds at baseline leave the nonword-reading floor sooner (hazard ratio 1.39 per SD [1.02, 1.90], P = 0.95), as do children reading more words (1.65 [1.19, 2.31], P = 0.99; `lrp-rli-surv-011`, from the 2026-08-21 reparameterised refit — the covariate slopes barely moved), consistent with letter sounds preceding decoding rather than depending on it.

**Practical reading.** On these data letter-sound knowledge is the skill most tightly bound to word reading, the intervention raised both, and the models locate the reading effect on the letter-sound path; nonword decoding as measured here — a six-item, floored test — is a weaker and later correlate that neither explains the letter-sound association away nor gates it. The letter-sound → reading slope is also the same whether a child had few or many sessions in the period (a screening check, section 8), while more sessions go with more reading at the same letter-sound level (`mech-058` γ_attend +0.14 log-odds per SD [+0.06, +0.22], P = 0.997) — the programme's reading association is not only through letter sounds. What the data cannot do is establish that teaching letter sounds _causes_ the reading gain independently of the reading teaching delivered alongside it.

## 6. How far do receptive vocabulary, expressive vocabulary and word repetition each contribute to word reading?

**Short answer.** Each is associated with word reading, none as strongly as letter sounds, and each in a characteristic way. **Expressive vocabulary** is a moderately supported correlate on every view except the mutually adjusted gain model: its within-period slope is +0.12 log-odds per SD (P = 0.93), its prior _level_ predicts later reading change with strong evidence in the latent-change models, it ranks alongside letter sounds in the stacked-levels ranking, and its concurrent association appears at timepoints 3 and 4 rather than earlier. **Receptive vocabulary** is weaker throughout: +0.06 (P = 0.80) within period, inconclusive as a gain predictor, and only clearly associated concurrently at timepoint 3. **Word repetition** (the repetition battery total, read as phonological memory) is a moderately supported within-period correlate of word reading (+3.1 words across its range, P = 0.95) and a strong one of nonword decoding, and it neither moderates the letter-sound association nor predicts the whole-study gain. None of these carries any of the intervention's word-reading effect. Every one of these quantities is an adjusted association, and vocabulary and code share about half of their between-child variance, so the vocabulary coefficients partly stand in for general language and ability.

**How to read this.** Expressive vocabulary is the one of the three that keeps a positive association after conditioning on the child's own prior reading, and it predicts _later_ reading change from a prior level; that ordering is worth more than a concurrent correlation, but it still cannot separate "vocabulary helps reading" from "children developing faster on everything have both". Receptive vocabulary's associations appear where the children read more (timepoints 3–4) and are inconclusive as predictors of gain. Word repetition behaves like a code-adjacent skill: it tracks nonword decoding strongly (+2.5 nonwords across its range, P = 1.00, `mech-102`) and word reading modestly. And the vocabulary and code domains correlate at about 0.70 as latent factors at every wave, so a vocabulary slope in a model that also carries letter sounds is a slope on the part of vocabulary that is _not_ shared with code — which is part of why the standardised vocabulary coefficients are small.

## 7. How are hearing and speech clarity associated with word reading?

**Short answer.** **Speech production accuracy** is clearly associated with how many words a child reads (+0.26 log-odds per SD [+0.12, +0.40], P = 0.999, pooled across the four waves and adjusted for letter sounds, ability, hearing and age), modestly with within-period reading gains (+0.10 [+0.01, +0.18]), and strongly with nonword decoding (+2.2 nonwords across its range, P = 1.00); it does not predict the whole-study gain from baseline. **Hearing** is the surprise: the hearing-difficulty flag (impaired hearing or repeated ear infections at baseline) is associated with _larger_, not smaller, word-reading gains in every gain or change model that carries it (+2.2 words per SD of the flag over the whole study, P = 0.97; +0.21 log-odds per SD in the errors-in-variables gain model, P = 0.97; +0.07 per SD within period, P = 0.93; +0.07 for flagged versus clear in the latent-change model, P = 0.85), leans the same way for the concurrent level at timepoint 3 (P = 0.89), and is unrelated to the reading level pooled across waves. Neither result is causal, and both rest on imperfect measures.

**Speech clarity** (DEAP picture-naming composite — initial consonants + vowels + final consonants — measured at every timepoint):

| View                                                                 | Association with word reading                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pooled level across four waves, adjusted (`pl-001`)                  | +0.26 log-odds per SD [+0.12, +0.40], P = 0.999                |
| Same-period gain given start-of-period reading (`mech-058`)          | +0.10 log-odds per SD [+0.01, +0.18], P = 0.97                 |
| Concurrent level at timepoint 3, fully adjusted (`ca-001`)           | +0.04 [−0.23, +0.31], inconclusive                             |
| Baseline → t1–t4 gain, mutually adjusted (`adj-065`)                 | +0.6 words per SD [−1.9, +3.6], inconclusive                   |
| Latent code-factor gain model (`mm-002`); latent change (`lcsm-082`) | +0.06 [−0.19, +0.31]; −0.02 [−0.12, +0.07], both inconclusive  |
| Nonword decoding, same period (`mech-103`)                           | +2.2 nonwords [+1.4, +3.1] across the observed range, P = 1.00 |
| Nonword decoding level pooled across waves (`pl-002`)                | +0.50 log-odds per SD [+0.26, +0.75], P = 0.999                |

So clearer speech goes with reading more words at any given moment and, more strongly, with decoding more nonwords, but once a child's own prior reading is in the model the association with _gain_ is small, and as a baseline predictor of the whole-study gain it is absent. That is the profile of a stable between-child correlate — children who articulate more accurately are further along on the alphabetic skills — rather than of something that changes the rate of progress. Two measurement caveats: the speech score used here is the sum of the DEAP picture-naming initial-consonant, vowel and final-consonant scores from a lenient trial-era scoring — its consonant components run roughly 20 percentage points above the published percent-consonants-correct re-scoring of this same cohort (Burgoyne, Buckley and Baxter 2021, DOI 10.1111/jir.12890) — so treat it as a relative rather than an absolute accuracy scale; and it is missing for a few children at each wave (mean-imputed with an indicator).

**Hearing** (a baseline flag: 1 = impaired hearing or repeated ear infections, 0 = clear; as these fits were run it was observed for 44 of 54 children — 24 flagged, 20 clear — and the other 10 were imputed to clear with an indicator). In every model except the latent-change model the flag enters standardised, so those rows read per SD of the flag (SD ≈ 0.5), and the flagged-versus-clear contrast is roughly twice the quoted figure; the latent-change model carries it raw, so its row is already the flagged-versus-clear contrast:

| View                                                        | Association with word reading                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Baseline → t1–t4 gain, mutually adjusted (`adj-065`)        | **+2.2 words per SD of the flag [+0.3, +4.3]**, P = 0.97 |
| Latent code-factor gain model (`mm-002`)                    | +0.21 log-odds per SD [+0.04, +0.38], P = 0.97           |
| Same-period gain given start-of-period reading (`mech-058`) | +0.07 log-odds per SD [−0.01, +0.15], P = 0.93           |
| Latent reading/blending change (`lcsm-082`)                 | +0.07 flagged vs clear [−0.04, +0.18], P = 0.85          |
| Pooled level across four waves, adjusted (`pl-001`)         | −0.02 per SD [−0.20, +0.16], inconclusive                |
| Concurrent level at timepoint 3 (`ca-001`)                  | +0.15 per SD [−0.04, +0.33], P = 0.89                    |

The direction is counter-intuitive and consistent, which is why it should be reported rather than hidden: children flagged for hearing difficulty at baseline gained slightly more words over the study than children not flagged, at similar reading levels. It should not be read as hearing difficulty helping reading. The flag is coarse and heterogeneous (it merges a hearing impairment with a history of ear infections), a fifth of the children had it imputed in the fits as run (nine of 54 are unknown under the corrected coding), and the most likely explanations are chance in a sample of about fifty, or the flag proxying something else — such as which children were being monitored or supported more closely. Its association with reading _level_ is null. Every one of these coefficients was included as an adjuster to clean the treatment or mechanism estimates, not to estimate hearing's own effect, and it is exactly the kind of coefficient the Table 2 fallacy warns about. What the flag actually is (checked 2026-08-19): its 25 flagged children are 22 with the hearing-impairment indicator (11 impairment only, 10 with both, 1 impairment with no ear-infection record) and 3 with an ear-infection history alone, so it is in effect the impairment indicator, and the pattern is that indicator's — raw whole-study word-reading gain 12.6 words (n = 22) against 7.5 (n = 25) by impairment, 10.1 against 10.1 (13 against 31) by ear infections; the impairment-only children start a little above average (9.9 words) and gain most (13.7), so a lower starting point is not the explanation for them. The components cannot be separated by a model (three children carry ear infections alone), and dropping the unknown children cannot change the contrast: they are filled to the clear reference with their own indicator, so the flagged-versus-clear coefficient is estimated from the known children whatever is done with them.

**Did the intervention work less well for these children?** The question practice most wants answered cannot be resolved at this size, but the trial shows no indication that it did: in the randomised window (timepoint 1 → 2) the immediate arm gained 4.1 more words than the waiting arm among children flagged for hearing difficulty (SE 1.4; 14 against 11 children) and 3.0 more among the clear (SE 1.3; 12 against 7), and 3.6 more among children with speech at or below the median (SE 1.7) against 1.3 more above it (SE 1.5; an arm-unbalanced split, 16 against 9). The point estimates run, if anything, in favour of the flagged and less clear children, with standard errors of about 2 words on the differences; a model would turn these into intervals of roughly ±3–4 words either way (section 8).

**Is the flag an adequate adjustment?** Two checks on the data and the fitted rows (2026-08-19) say yes, for its purpose. It measures something hearing-like where the causal diagram says it should: flagged children score about −0.5 SD on speech production at both timepoint 1 and timepoint 4 and −0.6 SD on word/nonword repetition at timepoint 1 (the `HS → SP` and `HS → RW` edges), while its associations with the literacy and vocabulary _levels_ it is meant to de-confound are small (|d| ≤ 0.23 SD; word reading −0.19 at timepoint 1, +0.09 at timepoint 4) — so there is little hearing confounding of the skill associations to remove in these data. And the estimates do not depend on it: a screening check on the fitted rows of four mechanism models (clustered least squares with each model's own covariates, with and without the flag and its missing indicator) moves the focal slopes by 0–4% — letter sounds → word reading +0.434 without against +0.425 with, taught receptive vocabulary → word reading +0.226 against +0.230, expressive vocabulary → word reading +0.029 against +0.028, speech production → nonword reading +0.421 against +0.422. The verdict is therefore: adequate for its purpose — not because a binary, time-invariant baseline flag captures hearing well (it cannot follow fluctuating conductive loss, and it is three-grouped with nine unknowns), but because there is little hearing confounding of these associations to remove and the adjusted slopes are the same to within a few percent whether it is in or out; the same small flag-to-skill associations mean the mediation models' mediator–outcome confounding through hearing is small too. Where it is not adequate is the one use already ruled out above: estimating hearing's own association. (The check is a linear screening of Beta-Binomial fits, kept here as evidence about sensitivity, not as a result.)

An encoding fault found while checking this section (2026-08-19) has since been fixed and the affected models refitted. The stored composite had been derived upstream with a strict both-known OR, so one child recorded as hearing-impaired but with no ear-infection record was coded _unknown_ — and therefore imputed to _clear_. Logically "impaired or unknown" is impaired, so the correct split is 25 flagged, 20 clear and 9 unknown (`notes/202608191030-hearing-composite-three-valued-or.md`). **Every number in this section now comes from fits using the corrected coding** (the 2026-08-20 full refit, `notes/202608200800-full-refit-both-layers-2026-08.md`). The change was as small as predicted: all six coefficients moved slightly toward zero, none changed direction, and none changed its evidence label except the latent-change model, which slipped from 0.90 to 0.85. The counter-intuitive positive association therefore survives the correction, and so does every caveat above it.

## 8. Further models and analyses worth considering

Grouped by the question they would sharpen. Several are cheap because the family already exists; the expensive ones are marked.

**On the intervention's effects (questions 1–2).**

- _Make the taught-versus-not-taught contrasts dependence-aware_ — **confirmed 2026-08-19, tracked as issue #551, to run with the post-review refit batch (#554).** The joint fits set residual correlation off, so each contrast's interval is computed as if the two outcome effects were independent — the point estimate is fine, but the same children supply both outcomes, and a positive within-child residual correlation would make the current intervals too wide (a negative one, too narrow). The LKJ residual-correlation block already exists in the family; fitting `itt-015/115/016` with it on, as registered sensitivity companions, would replace the "provisional" label with a dependence-aware interval. Expect a modest change at most: an earlier attempt found the shared residual near zero once baselines are conditioned on, and even a correlation of 0.5 between the two effects would leave the expressive contrast suggestive (P ≈ 0.84 rather than 0.76). The cost is not compute but geometry — that attempt mixed at the edge of the convergence gate — and the ten-outcome `itt-012` block was prior-dominated when tried, so it is not worth repeating.
- _Pre-specify the practical-difference thresholds_ — **done (2026-08-19).** The half-natural-maturation rule (half the waiting-list arm's untreated first-period gain, rounded to whole items and floored at one) reproduces every adopted threshold and is now confirmed as the project's standing rule, to be applied to any new outcome before it is fitted (`notes/202608191130-practical-difference-rule-confirmed.md`). That settles future rounds; the thresholds behind this run's size statements were still derived after the results existed and are read with their sensitivity tables.
- _A prospectively powered follow-up._ With ~54 children the vocabulary contrasts have intervals seven to ten items wide. A Bayesian design analysis using these posteriors as priors would say what sample a follow-up trial needs to resolve a two-item vocabulary effect, and whether the expressive-grammar lean (+1.0 to +1.6 marks) is worth pursuing.
- _Baseline-balance-adjusted levels models_ — **confirmed 2026-08-19, tracked as issue #552, to run with the post-review refit batch (#554).** The `level_factors` vocabulary contrasts inherit the adjusted timepoint-1 arm gap, which is negative on every outcome in these data (the immediate arm is older and higher on block design, so once age and ability are adjusted for its equal raw scores read as a deficit); centring the group-by-time terms on that gap — a difference-in-differences of levels, with the t1 gap kept as a balance term — removes the artefact. Read from the stored traces, the t2 − t1 change is +0.03 log-odds for receptive vocabulary (P = 0.61) and +0.01 for expressive (P = 0.53), so the family's negative vocabulary lean is the baseline offset, and every other outcome moves into line with `itt`. The refit puts the prior on the change directly and publishes the items-scale marginals; `itt` remains the headline.
- _Missing-outcome sensitivity for the other headline outcomes._ Corrected on checking (2026-08-19): every `itt` fit already carries the model-free extreme-case bounds, and for letter sounds (+4.1 to +7.4 items), taught expressive (+0.2 to +2.7) and taught receptive vocabulary (+0.2 to +2.8) no completion of the three lost children reverses the direction — a firmer statement than word reading can make (−6.3 to +4.9, four missing). What only word reading has is the model-based envelope (screening-bridge model, MAR standardisation over all 57, jump-to-reference, δ grid); with one intervention and two control children missing, that envelope would move the other estimates by a few tenths of an item at most, well inside their posterior intervals, and taught vocabulary has no screening analogue to bridge on. **Done and closed (2026-08-19):** the existing bounds are now quoted in the ITT note's caveat section and in the key findings of every graded `itt` fit (appended to the available-case sentence, regenerated without a refit); the model-based extension is **dropped** — with three missing children it would move the estimates by a few tenths of an item inside their posterior intervals, and taught vocabulary has no screening measure to bridge on.

**On predictors of word-reading gains and levels (questions 3–4).**

- _Regenerate and reconcile the gradient-boosting layer_ — **confirmed 2026-08-19, tracked in issue #554 (the post-review batch).** The step-1 rankings for word-reading gain and level are not in the current output root; refitting `lrp-rli-gbg`/`gbl` for word reading at the reporting config and comparing the SHAP directions and permutation rankings with the horseshoe, gain-factor and pooled-levels coefficients closes the two-step loop the methodology promises, and is the one step-1 item outstanding for this refit.

What remains true is the point the horseshoe note already makes — with one coefficient per predictor across stacked waves, each ranking score blends between-child and within-child association, and the between-child part dominates, which is why the level rankings read as near-tautological. A between/within-split horseshoe would separate the two rankings, but `pl-001` already shows the within-child letter-sound association is near zero and both gain rankings are flat, so the within-child ranking would almost certainly be flat too. Not pursued.

- _Put the latent-change couplings on one scale_ — **done (2026-08-19, reporting-only).** Every `lcsm` fit now writes `standardised_couplings.csv` (each level → change coupling standardised per draw by the model's own latent scales, plus contrasts between sources of the same target), backfilled over the stored fits from their posteriors without a refit. Result: per SD of prior level, letter sounds predict later reading change more strongly than expressive vocabulary, not less (`lcsm-067` +0.64 vs +0.43, contrast P = 0.72, inconclusive; `lcsm-091` +0.72 vs +0.26, P = 0.96, moderate) — the raw per-unit comparison that suggests the opposite should not be made.
- _Disentangle age from time in the study_ — **already answered by the existing fits; closed 2026-08-19.** Age and wave are collinear within child, but every stacked family that carries age also carries per-period or per-wave intercepts (and `did` uses baseline age), and given wave 99.9% of the remaining age variation is between-child, so the age term is already the baseline-age effect and "later periods" sits in the period intercepts; the single-row models (`adj-065`, baseline age; `al-001`, age at onset) agree, and the one fit without window intercepts (`lcsm-067`, −0.15) shrinks to −0.10/−0.11 in its windowed siblings. The finding is about older children, not later periods — see question 3. No new model needed.

**On letter sounds and decoding (question 5).**

- _A lagged, within-child mechanism model_ — **dropped 2026-08-19.** `mechanism_at_pre` exists but the family has no between/within split, and the question is already answered from every angle the data support (question 5): the lagged blended association is modest (`gf-001` +0.07/SD, P = 0.92), the within-child change-on-change coupling inconclusive (`lcsm-091` `h_L` +0.05 [−0.17, +0.26]) and the within-child level association near zero (`pl-001`); only 15% of the variance in period-start letter sounds is within child, so a lagged split would return a fourth inconclusive within-child term. The lagged-exposure idea is folded into issue #553 as an optional `pooled_levels` setting rather than a mechanism-family extension.
- _A two-dimensional letter-sound × decoding surface_ — **dropped 2026-08-19; the scale question answered from the stored fits instead (`notes/202608191600-moderation-items-scale-not-2d-surface.md`).** The premise was half right: a negative logit-scale interaction is ambiguous on a bounded outcome, but the ambiguity is the link, not the bilinear form — a two-dimensional HSGP through the same logit link inherits it — and the data cannot support the off-ridge surface a synergy-versus-substitution reading needs: letter sounds and the moderator correlate about 0.6, the low-letter-sound/high-decoding corner is empty (no row with 12 or fewer sounds and 8 or more blending items; one with 12 or fewer sounds and any decoding), and out of sample the data barely distinguish even the bilinear interaction from none (`mech-061` against `161` +2.0 ± 1.4 elpd; `063` against `163` 0.0 ± 0.7). Every moderated `mechanism` fit now writes `moderation_items.csv` (the interquartile-cell letter-sound increments in outcome items at the low and high moderator cell, their difference, the logit-additive benchmark and the logit-scale interaction over the same cells), backfilled over the nine stored fits, with a words-scale sentence in the key findings and the old "not additivity in word counts" clause removed; the result is in question 5. The one-dimensional ceiling check noted there (a moderated refit with the interaction on the count-standardised or top-clipped letter-sound score) is optional and unscheduled.
- _Sequential mediation with temporal ordering_ — **dropped 2026-08-19.** The premise was wrong on both counts. The machinery does not exist: `med-076` is the single-mediator family with a lagged outcome, and the two-mediator chain family (`mediation_multi`, `med-060`) has neither an outcome wave nor per-mediator waves, so letter sounds at t2, decoding at t3 and reading at t4 would need a new loader path, factory branch and g-formula simulation in the slowest family (`med-064` took 42 minutes a fit). And the design could not show what it was meant to: the decomposition is of the arm contrast, and by timepoint 3 the waiting arm has had 20 weeks of the programme — 48% of each arm is off the decoding floor (adjusted gap −0.15 log-odds [−0.78, +0.48], `lf-011`), and the timepoint-4 word-reading contrast is a timing contrast (`med-076` total +1.4 [−2.0, +4.8]) — so a chain through decoding at timepoint 3 would be decomposing an early-versus-late contrast through a mediator on which the arms no longer differ, on 50 complete rows, with the crossover as time-varying treatment between the mediators on top of the non-identification `med-060` and `med-076` already carry. The two links it would multiply are already fitted as lagged associations (question 5: `gf-011` letter sounds → off-floor decoding +0.55 per SD, very strong; `gf-001` decoding → word reading given letter sounds +0.05 per SD, P = 0.92), and the design that could genuinely order the chain is the wave-unrolled cross-lagged model scoped in #250, not a g-formula through a post-crossover window.
- _A better decoding measure, or a better model of this one_ — **closed 2026-08-19: future data collection, not this repository.** Nonword reading is six items with most children at zero, and the archive holds only the total score (no item responses), so an item-response model cannot be fitted here; the Beta-Binomial and off-floor treatments already are the graded and floor-aware models of that total. A longer decoding measure, or item-level recording, in future data collection is the only way to test decoding's role without the floor dominating.
- _Dose-dependence of the letter-sound link_ — **dropped 2026-08-19.** The fit is one module (`mechanism` with `moderator_symbol="attend"`, `moderator_is_covariate=True`, as `mech-104` enters phonological memory), but three things argued against spending it. Sessions on the mechanism rows is zero for the 25 waiting-arm period-1 rows and 10–94 everywhere else (both arms receive sessions in periods 2 and 3), so 69% of its variance is the untreated-versus-on-programme contrast and only 31% dose within the programme. A screening check on the fitted `mech-058` rows (clustered least squares of the word-reading logit on its baseline, letter sounds, sessions, their product, arm, age and period) puts the interaction at −0.06 per SD·SD (SE 0.05) — the same small negative every other moderator gives — with the letter-sound slope identical below and above the median dose within treated periods (+0.43 and +0.44 logits per SD) and if anything steeper on the zero-dose rows (+0.67 ± 0.20, n = 28); a Bayesian fit would return the same under the ceiling caution noted in question 5. And the test would not discriminate the hypothesis: a steeper slope under more dose is equally what responder heterogeneity produces, and a quickly learned conversion strategy would show none. The taught-route question proper is the mediation one already answered (`med-059`, `med-092`), and the main-effect version is in hand — given letter sounds, more sessions go with more reading (`mech-058` γ_attend +0.14 [+0.06, +0.22], P = 0.997).

**On vocabulary and word repetition (question 6).**

- _Adjust the taught-vocabulary mechanism fits for sessions_ — **already done; premise corrected 2026-08-19.** `mech-088/089` have adjusted for the sessions delivered in the period since 2026-07-17 (`attend` in `adjust_for`, `gamma_attend` in the posterior; the #309 handling was reversed after the d-separation review in `notes/202607171700-mech-intervention-sessions-adjustment-collider-review.md`, which showed that the always-conditioned arm closes the collider path and that without sessions an observable backdoor stays open). - _Longitudinal-ordering mediation through expressive vocabulary_ (t2 vocabulary → t4 reading, mirroring `med-076`) — **dropped 2026-08-19: there is no first leg.** A mediation needs the intervention to move the mediator, and it did not move expressive vocabulary at timepoint 2: `itt-006` τ = +0.2 items of 170 [−3.1, +3.5], P(> 0) = 0.53. The randomised-window two-mediator fit already ran this leg and got what a null first leg produces — `med-064` indirect effect through expressive vocabulary +0.03 words [−0.47, +0.74], P = 0.58, beside +1.87 [+0.61, +3.65] through letter sounds. Moving the outcome to timepoint 4 changes the second leg but not the first, so the indirect effect stays the product of about zero and something, with a post-crossover timing contrast for a total. The observation behind the bullet — that prior expressive vocabulary predicts later reading change (`lcsm-067` +0.43 SD of later reading change per SD of prior level [+0.15, +0.72], P = 0.99; inconclusive once the other period-start skills are held, `gf-001` γ_E +0.01 [−0.24, +0.27]) — is an observational coupling and does not become an intervention route unless the intervention changes vocabulary.

**On hearing and speech (question 7).**

- _Use the two hearing indicators separately_ — **dropped 2026-08-19; answered from the data (question 7).** Both of the bullet's questions resolve without a model: the composite's 25 flagged children are 22 with the impairment indicator and 3 with an ear-infection history alone, so a separate ear-infection coefficient would rest on three children and the association is the impairment indicator's by arithmetic (raw whole-study gains 12.6 against 7.5 words by impairment, 10.1 against 10.1 by ear infections); and a `require_observed` sensitivity is moot by construction — `hs` is filled to the clear reference with `hs_missing` marking the unknown nine, the three groups are saturated, and the flagged-versus-clear contrast is estimated from the known children only, so dropping the unknown rows can move it only through second-order changes in the other adjusters. The flag stays in the observational adjustment sets the DAG requires (a coarse proxy still closes part of the hearing backdoor, and it is absent from every randomised fit), is never headlined as a hearing effect, and takes the three-valued-OR coding in the held refit.
- _Re-score speech production to the published standard_ — **closed 2026-08-19: out of scope for this repository.** The archive holds only the trial-era summary percentages (initial, vowel and final consonants per wave; 41 distinct initial-consonant values at timepoint 1) — no item-, word- or phoneme-level data and no transcriptions — and percent-consonants-correct needs what Burgoyne, Buckley and Baxter (2021, DOI 10.1111/jir.12890) used: blind IPA transcription of the video recordings with every consonant token scored. Re-scoring is therefore a data request to the 2021 team (their per-child file covers timepoint 1 with about 50 children as far as the notes record; wave coverage would need confirming), not a model change. It would also change little in the associations: every fit enters speech standardised, so a rank-preserving re-scoring leaves the coefficients where they are and the published figures (initial 46.8%, final 40.0%, vowels 91% against 68 / 60 / 96 here) look like a level shift; what it would change is the absolute reading of the scale and the one place speech is reported in its own units (`mech-103`'s range contrast). The standing caveat in question 7 stays. If the 2021 file is ever obtainable, the one cheap use is a rank correlation of the timepoint-1 trial-era composite against PCC per child, which would settle whether the lenient scoring reorders children.
- _Hearing and speech as treatment moderators_ — **dropped 2026-08-19; the practical answer recorded in question 7.** The route as written does not exist (`gain_factors` interactions can name only treatment, age, own baseline, ability and the skills, not the `adjust_for` covariates; the `itt` tau-moderator could take them, one module each), and at 53 children it would only bound the question at roughly ±3–4 words either way. The raw randomised-window arithmetic (timepoint 1 → 2 word-reading gain, immediate minus waiting) already says what practice needs: +4.1 words (SE 1.4) among children flagged for hearing difficulty (14 against 11) against +3.0 (SE 1.3) among the clear (12 against 7); +3.6 (SE 1.7) among children with speech at or below the median (12 against 14) against +1.3 (SE 1.5) above it (16 against 9); +2.6 (SE 1.0) overall. No sign that either group benefited less — the point estimates run the other way, by +1.1 and +2.3 words — but the differences carry standard errors of about 2 words and the speech split is arm-unbalanced, so neither direction is resolvable; `gf-201`'s treatment × ability interaction (+0.01 [−0.17, +0.19] log-odds per SD) is the existing bound for general ability.
