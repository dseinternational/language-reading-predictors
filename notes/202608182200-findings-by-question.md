# Findings by question: the intervention, word reading and its predictors

**This report answers seven questions using the August 2026 statistical refit (235 Bayesian models across 23 families, all passing the convergence gate; 229 publishable).** It reorganises the per-family findings notes (`notes/202608180931-findings-00-overview.md` and its companions) around the questions a reader actually asks, so the same fit may appear under several headings. Every number below is read from a stored fit's own artefacts; the model identifiers are given so each can be traced. **Read the overview note first** for the study designs, the vocabulary and the evidence ladder — in brief: intervals are 89% equal-tailed credible ranges around the posterior median; P is the posterior probability of the stated direction; the labels are suggestive (P ≥ 0.75), moderate (≥ 0.91), strong (≥ 0.97) and very strong (≥ 0.99), with "inconclusive" below 0.75; and only the randomised timepoint-2 arm contrasts carry a causal reading — every coefficient on a skill, a covariate or a dose is an adjusted association. All results are preliminary and exploratory.

**The trial in one paragraph.** 57 children with Down syndrome (aged roughly 5–10) were randomised to receive a reading and language intervention immediately (29) or after a 20-week wait (28); 54 are in the analysis files (28 immediate, 26 waiting), assessed at four timepoints. Only the timepoint-2 comparison — after 20 weeks, when one arm has been taught and the other has not — is a randomised contrast; by timepoint 3 both arms have been taught. Outcomes are bounded test scores ("k out of n items"), so results are reported in **items** on each test's own scale: word reading is out of 79 words, letter-sound knowledge out of 32, phoneme blending out of 10, nonword reading out of 6.

## 1. What effects did the intervention have on the outcomes of interest?

**Short answer.** The intervention's clearest effects are on what it taught. After 20 weeks the immediate arm knew about 3.5 more letter sounds (very strong evidence), read about 2.4 more words (strong), knew about 1.5 more of the taught expressive words and 1.4 more of the taught receptive words (strong / moderate), and blended about one more item (strong). Standardised vocabulary (receptive and expressive) shows no detectable effect: medians near zero with intervals several items wide in each direction. Between those poles sit modest leans — not-taught vocabulary from the taught blocks, basic concepts, expressive grammar, receptive grammar — none of which clears zero. The two heavily floored measures (phonetic spelling, nonword reading) are estimated as the chance of moving off the floor and are suggestive at best. Four different model families agree on the ordering, and where they disagree it is on the vocabulary measures, for reasons that are understood.

**The evidence.** The table gives the randomised timepoint-2 contrast from the four families that estimate it, in items (or percentage points for the two off-floor outcomes), positive meaning the immediate arm did better. The `itt` column is the headline (an available-case modified intention-to-treat estimate: one row per child at timepoint 2, adjusted for the child's own baseline and age). **Direction versus size.** The labels above describe how sure the direction is, not whether the effect is big enough to matter. Against each outcome's practical-difference threshold (half the waiting-list arm's natural first-period gain, floored at one item — a rule set after the initial results review, so post-hoc for this run, read with its sensitivity analysis, and since confirmed as the project's standing rule for future rounds), the probability that the benefit reaches that size is 0.91 for letter sounds (2 items), 0.90 for word reading (1 word), 0.78 for taught expressive and 0.69 for taught receptive vocabulary (1 item), 0.49 for blending and for expressive grammar (1 item), and 0.24 and 0.19 for receptive and expressive vocabulary (2 items). So letter sounds and word reading are the two outcomes where "almost certainly positive" and "almost certainly worth having" coincide; taught vocabulary is positive but of uncertain practical size; blending's ordinary-model estimate (+1.0) shrinks to +0.5 [−0.1, +1.1] (suggestive) once the one-third guessing rate of a three-choice task is modelled explicitly (`lrp-rli-itt-108`), and the project requires that pair to be reported together.

**Where the families disagree.** They agree closely on the taught skills, on word reading and on the ordering. Two disagreements deserve a sentence each. First, the `did` word-reading estimate leans on its prior: widening the treatment prior moves it from 1.3 to 2.5 items with every interval spanning zero, so it corroborates the direction of the `itt` result rather than its size. Second, the two vocabulary measures lean negative in the `level_factors` fits (and receptive vocabulary in `gain_factors`), where `itt` and `did` sit at zero. That is specification sensitivity, not a demonstrated harm: the levels model carries no baseline covariate, and its own timepoint-1 arm coefficient for receptive vocabulary is already −0.15 log-odds — the same size as its timepoint-2 one — so the negative contrast is inheriting a chance starting-point difference rather than measuring an effect. Under every family a vocabulary benefit of two or more items is unlikely but not ruled out.

**Generalisation beyond the taught items.** The taught-versus-not-taught contrasts from the joint model are +0.19 log-odds [−0.18, +0.55] (P = 0.79) for expressive and −0.05 [−0.41, +0.31] for receptive vocabulary; the taught-expressive-versus-taught-receptive contrast is +0.07 [−0.25, +0.39] (`lrp-rli-itt-015/115/016`). These are provisional — the joint model does not yet model within-child residual correlation, so they are dependence-limited — but they permit some generalisation to untaught words without establishing how much. The block-2 exposure contrasts (`lrp-rli-bx-001–004`) are all inside ±1 item with intervals spanning zero.

**Later timepoints and dose.** After the waiting arm crossed over, the arm gaps at timepoint 3 are associations, not effects; for word reading the immediate arm is still ahead with P = 0.84 and the catch-up term is inconclusive (`lrp-rli-did-001`). Session dose is observational: one standard deviation more sessions (about 31 against a mean of about 54) goes with +1.2 words [+0.4, +2.0] and +0.8 letter sounds [+0.2, +1.4], with the period-varying and pooled versions agreeing (`lrp-rli-dose-077/177/277`). Children who attend more differ from those who attend less, so this describes who progressed, not what more sessions would cause. Whether the treatment effect on word reading depends on baseline ability or baseline reading was tested (`lrp-rli-gf-201`): both interactions are inconclusive (+0.01 [−0.18, +0.19]; −0.06 [−0.27, +0.14]).

**Scope.** These are available-case estimates for the children with the required observations, not estimates for all 57 randomised children; the word-reading section says how much that matters. The Action Picture Test scales were fitted only after their ceilings were confirmed on 2026-08-18; expressive grammar's lean (+1.0 in `itt`, +1.6 in `did`) is the strongest among outcomes the intervention did not teach directly, and expressive information's crossover model suggests the immediate arm may have started slightly ahead (pre-treatment gap P = 0.76), which is a reason for caution.

## 2. How did the intervention affect word reading?

**Short answer.** After 20 weeks the children taught immediately read about **2.4 more words out of 79** than the waiting arm (89% +0.7 to +4.1; P(> 0) = 0.986, strong), with a 0.90 probability that the gain is at least one word and a 0.09 probability that it is too small to matter either way. The estimate is stable across adjustment for ability (+2.2), socio-economic status (+2.5 on the 33 children with SES data), and site (+2.6), and across the other three treatment families (+2.2, +2.6, +1.7). It is not assumption-free: four of the 57 randomised children have no timepoint-2 word-reading score, and under unrestricted assumptions about them the direction can reverse. And the models say the effect ran almost entirely through letter-sound knowledge.

**The headline and its envelope** (`lrp-rli-itt-010`, 53 children: 28 immediate, 25 waiting):

| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------ |
| Headline: 53 observed outcomes, own baseline + age              | **+2.4**         | +0.7 to +4.1 |
| Same 53, screening word reading and age as covariates           | +2.0             | 0.0 to +4.0  |
| All 57 randomised, standardised under missing-at-random         | +2.0             | 0.0 to +4.0  |
| Both arms completed, zero-delta anchor                          | +1.7             | −0.3 to +3.7 |
| Model-free extreme case (missing children at either test bound) | **−6.3 to +4.9** | —            |

Under reasonable assumptions the effect stays positive and around two words; the last row is the honest bound. The result should never be quoted without it.

**Robustness and corroboration.** Ability-adjusted +2.2 [+0.5, +4.0] (`itt-024`); SES-adjusted +2.5 [+0.3, +4.7] against +2.4 on the same 33 children unadjusted (`itt-013/014`); site-adjusted +2.6 [+0.8, +4.3] (`itt-027`). The arm-by-wave model gives +2.2 [−0.3, +4.7] (P = 0.92; prior-sensitive in magnitude, direction stable), the period-stacked gain model +2.6 [+0.9, +4.3] (P = 0.99), the levels model +1.7 [−1.0, +4.2] (P = 0.84), and the latent-change model's window-1 arm contrast in latent word-reading change is +0.40 log-odds [+0.09, +0.73] (P = 0.98; `lrp-rli-lcsm-082`). The onset-aligned per-protocol view — both arms lined up by when their own teaching started, a non-randomised comparison — gives +2.1 [−0.5, +4.8] (P = 0.91; `lrp-rli-al-001`).

The picture is consistent: the part of the word-reading effect that runs through letter-sound knowledge is well identified (+1.7 words, essentially all of the total in the single-window fits, about a quarter of it in the period-stacked fit), while vocabulary, blending and nonword decoding carry essentially none of it, and the negative-control mediator behaves as a negative control should. Two cautions. The proportion mediated is badly determined (0.83 with an 89% range from −0.9 to +3.3), because the total itself has an interval that touches zero. And the decomposition assumes no unmeasured confounding of the mediator–outcome link; a calibration against session-related confounding shows an amount of confounding that could plausibly account for the estimated indirect effect (the tipping point is about half the fitted mediator–outcome slope), so "runs through letter sounds" is the model's best reading, not a demonstrated channel. It is, however, exactly the pattern the letter-sound → word-reading associations in question 5 predict.

**Beyond timepoint 2.** The immediate arm's advantage is still visible at timepoint 3 (association only, P = 0.84) and the catch-up term is inconclusive (`did-001`), so the data neither show the effect wearing off nor the waiting arm overtaking. The observational dose relationship (+1.2 words per SD of sessions) points the same way as the randomised effect.

## 3. What predictors are associated with gains in word reading across all periods?

**Short answer.** Across the three periods, the children who gained most words were those who started lower on word reading (regression to the mean and the ceiling), were younger, and — given their starting point — had more letter-sound knowledge, more taught vocabulary, more nonword decoding, clearer speech, and attended more sessions. Age is the one baseline characteristic that predicts gain consistently and negatively in every family. Nothing about a child's general ability, hearing, blending or standardised vocabulary predicts word-reading gain reliably once the others are in the model. All of this is association: which children progressed, not what would change their progress.

**Prior levels and later change** (latent change-score models, `lrp-rli-lcsm-067/082/091`, 54 children, all four waves; couplings are per unit of the source's latent logit, not per SD, so they are comparable in direction and evidence but not in size across sources): a higher prior letter-sound level goes with larger subsequent word-reading change (0.14 [+0.05, +0.23], 0.24 [+0.14, +0.36] and 0.22 [+0.12, +0.33] across the three specifications, all P ≥ 0.99); so does a higher prior expressive-vocabulary level (0.29 [+0.10, +0.48] and 0.25 [+0.06, +0.44], P ≥ 0.98); prior blending does not (0.04 [−0.06, +0.14]); and prior letter-sound _change_ does not predict later reading change over and above level (0.05 [−0.17, +0.26]). Age is negative in every one of these (−0.10 to −0.15), and reading's own self-feedback is negative (−0.22 to −0.27), the latent-scale form of the ceiling and regression-to-the-mean effect.

**Two more views agree.** An errors-in-variables model that treats letter sounds, blending and nonword reading as indicators of one latent code factor puts that factor's association with word-reading gain at +0.35 log-odds per SD [+0.09, +0.60] (P = 0.985), with age −0.35 [−0.53, −0.17] and hearing +0.23 [+0.05, +0.40] alongside (`lrp-rli-mm-002`, 51 children). And baseline non-verbal ability does not predict the word-reading growth rate across the four waves (−0.05 [−0.23, +0.13], `lrp-rli-gc-069`), although it does predict grammar's (+0.12 [+0.02, +0.20]).

**What this does not say.** None of these coefficients is a lever. They were chosen and adjusted to make the treatment estimate clean, which generally makes them less interpretable individually (the Table 2 fallacy), and the between-child ones absorb whatever stable differences the children bring. The gradient-boosting layer's rankings for word-reading gain are not part of this run's stored artefacts, so they are not compared here.

## 4. What predictors are associated with levels of word reading at each timepoint?

**Short answer.** At every timepoint the strongest concurrent correlate of how many words a child reads is how many letter sounds they know — and the association is largest at timepoint 2 (about +9 words per SD of letter sounds, mutually adjusted for the other five skills, ability, hearing, speech and phonological memory) and still around +4 to +6 words at timepoints 3 and 4. Taught vocabulary, blending, and by timepoints 3 and 4 the broad vocabulary measures, are the next tier. Almost all of the letter-sound / word-reading level association lives _between_ children: children who know more letter sounds across the study read far more across the study, whereas within a child, being above one's own letter-sound average at a wave says almost nothing about being above one's own reading average — the pattern a shared cause predicts.

The letter-sound row's jump at timepoint 2 is not an accident of the model: it is the wave at which one arm has just been taught letter sounds and reading together, so the two skills separate the children most sharply there. A less-adjusted version of the same question (`ca-010`: letter sounds, ability, hearing, age and arm only) gives +4.6, +11.0, +9.1 and +11.1 words per SD across the four timepoints, and adding nonword decoding to it (`ca-011`) reduces the letter-sound association to +3.9, +7.2, +6.8 and +6.5 while nonword decoding itself takes +2.2, +2.9, +4.7 and +6.3 — see question 5.

**Group and ability by timepoint** (`lrp-rli-lf-001`, the levels model, log-odds): the immediate-minus-waiting gap is −0.17 [−0.59, +0.23] before treatment, +0.25 [−0.15, +0.64] at timepoint 2 (the randomised contrast, +1.7 words), then +0.15 and +0.06 after crossover; non-verbal ability's association with the reading level is +0.27 [+0.02, +0.51] per SD at timepoint 1 (moderate) and about +0.15 to +0.18 (suggestive) at each later timepoint. A regularised-horseshoe ranking of the stacked levels (`lrp-rli-hs-002`, 210 child-waves, no child random intercept) ranks expressive vocabulary (+0.41 [+0.22, +0.61] log-odds per SD) and letter sounds (+0.33 [+0.19, +0.48]) at the top with near-certain non-negligible coefficients, then receptive grammar (+0.19), age (+0.19), receptive vocabulary (+0.15) and blending (+0.12); that ranking treats a child's four waves as independent and should be read as a cross-check.

**Pooled across the four waves, and split** (`lrp-rli-pl-001`, 53 children, 210 child-waves, per-wave intercepts, adjusted for ability, hearing, speech and age): the _between-child_ letter-sound association is +1.61 log-odds per SD [+1.34, +1.87] — about +17 words per SD at the average fitted level — while the _within-child_ association is +0.04 [−0.06, +0.14] (P = 0.74, inconclusive). Without the wave intercepts (`pl-101`) the within-child term becomes +0.19 [+0.08, +0.29] with very strong evidence, which is what shared maturation alone manufactures when both skills rise across the study. In the same fit speech production is associated with the reading level (+0.26 log-odds per SD [+0.13, +0.40], P = 0.999), non-verbal ability weakly (+0.13 [−0.06, +0.33]), age weakly (+0.11 [−0.09, +0.31]) and the hearing flag not at all (−0.00 [−0.19, +0.18]).

**Latent structure.** Treating the tests as indicators of three latent domains, the code domain (letter sounds, blending, nonword reading) correlates with the vocabulary domain at about 0.70 at every wave [roughly 0.57 to 0.79], with grammar at about 0.61, and vocabulary with grammar at about 0.88 (`lrp-rli-lcf-001`; the cross-sectional model `mm-001` gives 0.79, 0.70 and 0.83). These are disattenuated correlations between domains, and they say that whatever separates children on reading-related code skills also separates them substantially on language.

**What this does not say.** These are level associations at one moment, with no temporal ordering at all: they carry _less_ structure than the change-based models in question 3, and every one of them absorbs the stable between-child differences (ability, home, schooling) that measured ability only partly captures.

## 5. How far does letter-sound knowledge contribute to better word reading, and does that depend on decoding skill?

**Short answer.** Letter-sound knowledge is the best-supported correlate of word reading in this dataset on every view — levels, gains and change — and it is the only route through which the models can trace the intervention's word-reading effect. But the evidence for a _contribution_ in the causal sense is indirect: the association survives adjustment for measured general ability essentially unchanged, is much larger for nonword decoding than for word reading in the way a decoding route predicts, and is the channel the mediation models pick out; against that, it lives almost entirely between children rather than within them, and the intervention taught letter sounds and reading together, so the design cannot separate the two. On the second half of the question, the data point the _opposite_ way from "it depends on full decoding": adjusting for nonword decoding leaves most of the letter-sound / word-reading association standing, nonword decoding carries none of the intervention's word-reading effect over and above letter sounds, and the letter-sound association is if anything shallower, not steeper, where blending or decoding is higher.

**The association, and how robust it is.** In the transition models the letter-sound slope is +0.25 log-odds per SD [+0.15, +0.35] (`mech-101`), or +9.9 words [+6.1, +13.5] across the observed range linearly and +6.8 words [+2.5, +11.1] as a flexible curve (`mech-058`), which rises gently and then more steeply above about 25 letter sounds; concurrently, +9.0 words per SD at timepoint 2 (`ca-001`); across waves, +1.61 log-odds per SD between children (`pl-001`); and prior letter-sound level predicts later reading change in every latent-change specification (0.14 to 0.24 per unit latent logit, P ≥ 0.99). Adjusting additionally for the measured non-verbal ability proxy leaves the slope at +0.245 [+0.146, +0.343] and the curve's endpoint contrast at +6.5 words [+2.2, +10.9], a 4% shift with a maximum pointwise gap of 0.15 words between the two curves (`mech-201`, `mech-258`).

**Specificity: the decoding contrast.** The pre-specified test is whether letter sounds predict nonword decoding _more_ than word reading, which a pure general-ability account gives no reason to expect. They do: +1.03 log-odds per SD [+0.74, +1.34] for nonwords against +0.25 for words, a difference of +0.78 [+0.48, +1.10] from the single-outcome fits and +0.81 [+0.50, +1.13] from the identified bivariate model (`lrp-rli-jm-002`), with essentially all posterior mass above zero. The four negative-control outcomes — receptive and expressive vocabulary, grammar, basic concepts, which the causal diagram gives letter sounds no route to — are nonetheless clearly positive (0.10 to 0.29 log-odds per SD), so letter-sound knowledge also carries a broad non-specific association with every skill measured; adjusting for measured ability removes some of that (grammar drops from 0.12 to 0.07, receptive vocabulary and basic concepts lose about a seventh) but not most of it, while leaving the written-code slopes untouched (nonwords −0.3%, words −2.0%; contrast +0.78 → +0.78). So the shared cause behind the non-specific component is not, or not only, the ability this battery measures. Two more cautions: nonword reading is a six-item measure with most children at the floor, so its logit slope is not directly commensurate with a 79-item scale; and on the _level_ scale the same contrast is near zero or negative (letter sounds relate no more, and at some waves less, to nonword than to word-reading levels: Δ = −0.49 [−0.98, +0.02] at timepoint 1, −0.15, −0.26 and +0.06 [−0.29, +0.40] at timepoints 2–4 in the per-wave joint levels model `lrp-rli-jm-001`) — the change-based and level-based Δs are different estimands, and only the change-based one was pre-specified as the decoding signature.

**Between or within children?** The pooled-levels decomposition is the sharpest statement of the limit: +1.61 between children against +0.04 [−0.06, +0.14] within a child for word reading (`pl-001`), and +1.79 against +0.33 [−0.02, +0.69] (P = 0.93) for nonword decoding (`pl-002`). If letter-sound knowledge were driving word reading within a child, the within-child term is where it should show; a small within-child association is not evidence of no influence — within-child variation is small and levels are a blunt instrument for it, and the transition models are the better-powered within-child view — but it is the pattern a shared-cause account predicts. Note also that in `mech-058` the association is estimated given the child's own start-of-period reading, so it already excludes most stable between-child variation, and it is still clearly positive.

**Through the intervention.** The models trace the timepoint-2 word-reading effect through letter sounds (+1.70 words [+0.58, +3.24] of a +1.95 total; and +2.92 [+1.12, +5.32] when timepoint-2 letter sounds carry the effect to timepoint-3 reading), and through nothing else tested. That is consistent with letter-sound knowledge being on the path, but the intervention taught reading and letter sounds together, the confounding calibration says session-related confounding could account for the indirect effect, and the total effect's own interval touches zero.

**Does it depend on decoding skill (nonword reading) or blending?**

- _Adjustment._ Adding same-timepoint nonword decoding to the concurrent model reduces the letter-sound association with word reading from +11.0 to +7.2 words per SD at timepoint 2 (and from +9.1 to +6.8 at timepoint 3, +11.1 to +6.5 at timepoint 4), while nonword decoding takes +2.9 [+1.3, +4.6] rising to +6.3 [+3.8, +8.9] words per SD by timepoint 4 (`ca-010` vs `ca-011`). The per-wave joint levels model asks the same question within one likelihood: holding nonword decoding fixed, the letter-sound slope on word reading retains 0.91 [0.58, 1.06] of its size at timepoint 1, 0.77 [0.39, 0.94] at timepoint 2, 0.81 [0.55, 0.96] at timepoint 3 and 0.58 [0.16, 0.83] at timepoint 4 (`jm-001`, `share_retained`). So decoding accounts for a tenth to two-fifths of the letter-sound level association, growing as the children read more; it does not absorb it. In the period-stacked gain model both enter with similar, moderate support (letter sounds +1.0, nonword decoding +0.6 words per SD).
- _Moderation._ Whether the letter-sound slope is steeper for children with better decoding was tested directly (`mech-063`, letter sounds moderated by same-period nonword decoding): the interaction is −0.05 log-odds [−0.14, +0.03] (P(negative) = 0.85, suggestive) — if anything shallower where decoding is higher — while nonword decoding's own main effect given letter sounds is +0.10 [+0.02, +0.18] (`mech-163`). With phoneme blending as the moderator (`mech-061`) the interaction is −0.11 [−0.21, −0.02] (P(negative) = 0.97, strong), blending's main effect +0.10 [+0.01, +0.18]. On the logit scale a negative interaction between two positive predictors means substitution rather than synergy: a child high on either reads more, and the increments do not multiply. This is associational moderation, not causal effect modification, and the negative sign is partly what a bounded scale does; but there is no support here for the idea that letter-sound knowledge only pays off once decoding is in place.
- _Mediation._ Nonword decoding carries none of the intervention's word-reading effect over and above letter sounds (sequential route L → N → W: NIE through N −0.06 [−0.58, +0.20]; nonword decoding alone +0.02 [−0.39, +0.51]), and neither does blending (−0.03).
- _Timing._ Children with more letter sounds at baseline leave the nonword-reading floor sooner (hazard ratio 1.40 per SD [1.03, 1.90], P = 0.96), as do children reading more words (1.65 [1.18, 2.31], P = 0.99; `lrp-rli-surv-011`), consistent with letter sounds preceding decoding rather than depending on it.

**Practical reading.** On these data letter-sound knowledge is the skill most tightly bound to word reading, the intervention raised both, and the models locate the reading effect on the letter-sound path; nonword decoding as measured here — a six-item, floored test — is a weaker and later correlate that neither explains the letter-sound association away nor gates it. What the data cannot do is establish that teaching letter sounds _causes_ the reading gain independently of the reading teaching delivered alongside it.

## 6. How far do receptive vocabulary, expressive vocabulary and word repetition each contribute to word reading?

**Short answer.** Each is associated with word reading, none as strongly as letter sounds, and each in a characteristic way. **Expressive vocabulary** is a moderately supported correlate on every view except the mutually adjusted gain model: its within-period slope is +0.12 log-odds per SD (P = 0.93), its prior _level_ predicts later reading change with strong evidence in the latent-change models, it ranks alongside letter sounds in the stacked-levels ranking, and its concurrent association appears at timepoints 3 and 4 rather than earlier. **Receptive vocabulary** is weaker throughout: +0.06 (P = 0.80) within period, inconclusive as a gain predictor, and only clearly associated concurrently at timepoint 3. **Word repetition** (the repetition battery total, read as phonological memory) is a moderately supported within-period correlate of word reading (+3.1 words across its range, P = 0.95) and a strong one of nonword decoding, and it neither moderates the letter-sound association nor predicts the whole-study gain. None of these carries any of the intervention's word-reading effect. Every one of these quantities is an adjusted association, and vocabulary and code share about half of their between-child variance, so the vocabulary coefficients partly stand in for general language and ability.

And the vocabulary and code domains correlate at about 0.70 as latent factors at every wave, so a vocabulary slope in a model that also carries letter sounds is a slope on the part of vocabulary that is _not_ shared with code — which is part of why the standardised vocabulary coefficients are small.

## 7. How are hearing and speech clarity associated with word reading?

**Short answer.** **Speech production accuracy** is clearly associated with how many words a child reads (+0.26 log-odds per SD [+0.13, +0.40], P = 0.999, pooled across the four waves and adjusted for letter sounds, ability, hearing and age), modestly with within-period reading gains (+0.10 [+0.02, +0.19]), and strongly with nonword decoding (+2.3 nonwords across its range, P = 1.00); it does not predict the whole-study gain from baseline. **Hearing** is the surprise: the hearing-difficulty flag (impaired hearing or repeated ear infections at baseline) is associated with _larger_, not smaller, word-reading gains in every gain or change model that carries it (+2.4 words per SD of the flag over the whole study, P = 0.98; +0.23 log-odds per SD in the errors-in-variables gain model, P = 0.98; +0.07 per SD within period, P ≈ 0.94; +0.09 for flagged versus clear in the latent-change model, P = 0.90), leans the same way for the concurrent level at timepoint 3 (P = 0.91), and is unrelated to the reading level pooled across waves. Neither result is causal, and both rest on imperfect measures.

The direction is counter-intuitive and consistent, which is why it should be reported rather than hidden: children flagged for hearing difficulty at baseline gained slightly more words over the study than children not flagged, at similar reading levels. It should not be read as hearing difficulty helping reading. The flag is coarse and heterogeneous (it merges a hearing impairment with a history of ear infections), a fifth of the children have it imputed, and the most likely explanations are chance in a sample of about fifty, or the flag proxying something else — such as which children were being monitored more closely, or a lower starting point leaving more room to gain. Its association with reading _level_ is null, which fits the second explanation. Every one of these coefficients was included as an adjuster to clean the treatment or mechanism estimates, not to estimate hearing's own effect, and it is exactly the kind of coefficient the Table 2 fallacy warns about.

One encoding fault was found in checking this section (2026-08-19). The stored composite was derived upstream with a strict both-known OR, so one child who is recorded as hearing-impaired but has no ear-infection record was coded _unknown_ — and therefore imputed to _clear_ — in every fit above. Logically "impaired or unknown" is impaired, so the correct split is 25 flagged, 20 clear and 9 unknown. The loader now derives the composite from its two components with that rule (`notes/202608191030-hearing-composite-three-valued-or.md`); the stored fits and the figures quoted here still use the old coding, and the affected models will be refitted after the current review. The likely effect is small — one child in 54, in the immediate arm, who gained ten words from a low start, moving from the unknown group into the flagged group — and it cannot create the pattern, but it is a misclassification on every adjusted model and is flagged here so the numbers are read as what they are.

## 8. Further models and analyses worth considering

Grouped by the question they would sharpen. Several are cheap because the family already exists; the expensive ones are marked.

**On the intervention's effects (questions 1–2).**

- _Identify the taught-versus-not-taught contrasts properly._ The joint model currently sets residual correlation off, so its cross-outcome contrasts are dependence-limited. Refitting `itt-012/015/115/016` with the LKJ residual-correlation option on would give identified generalisation contrasts at little cost.
- _Pre-specify the practical-difference thresholds_ — **done (2026-08-19).** The half-natural-maturation rule (half the waiting-list arm's untreated first-period gain, rounded to whole items and floored at one) reproduces every adopted threshold and is now confirmed as the project's standing rule, to be applied to any new outcome before it is fitted (`notes/202608191130-practical-difference-rule-confirmed.md`). That settles future rounds; the thresholds behind this run's size statements were still derived after the results existed and are read with their sensitivity tables.
- _A prospectively powered follow-up._ With ~54 children the vocabulary contrasts have intervals seven to ten items wide. A Bayesian design analysis using these posteriors as priors would say what sample a follow-up trial needs to resolve a two-item vocabulary effect, and whether the expressive-grammar lean (+1.0 to +1.6 marks) is worth pursuing.
- _Baseline-balance-adjusted levels models._ The `level_factors` vocabulary contrasts inherit timepoint-1 arm offsets; a version that centres the group-by-time terms on the timepoint-1 gap (a genuine difference-in-differences parameterisation) would remove that artefact and make the family a fairer comparator.
- _Missing-outcome sensitivity for the other headline outcomes._ Only word reading has the full MAR / delta / extreme-case envelope; letter sounds and taught vocabulary should get the same treatment before they are quoted as firmly as word reading.

**On predictors of word-reading gains and levels (questions 3–4).**

- _Regenerate and reconcile the gradient-boosting layer._ The step-1 rankings for word-reading gain and level are not in the current output root; refitting `lrp-rli-gbg`/`gbl` for word reading and comparing the SHAP directions with the horseshoe and gain-factor coefficients would close the two-step loop the methodology promises.
- _A horseshoe ranking with a child random intercept._ The stacked-levels rankings (`hs-002/004`) treat a child's four waves as independent; adding the intercept (or ranking within `pooled_levels`) would make the levels ranking trustworthy.
- _Between/within splits for the other predictors._ `pooled_levels` currently splits only letter sounds; running it for expressive vocabulary, receptive vocabulary, word repetition and speech production against word reading would answer, for each, whether its level association is between-child or within-child — the single most informative distinction for the "does it contribute" questions.
- _Put the latent-change couplings on one scale._ The prior-level couplings for letter sounds and expressive vocabulary are per unit of different latent logits; the dominance analysis already standardises the blending–reading pair, and extending it to L versus E would say which prior level predicts later reading change more strongly.
- _Disentangle age from time in the study._ Age is the one consistently negative gain predictor; because everyone ages a year per wave, age and wave are collinear within child. A model with age at baseline and wave as separate terms (or age-at-onset in the aligned frame) would say whether the finding is about older children or about later periods.

**On letter sounds and decoding (question 5).**

- _A lagged, within-child mechanism model._ The transition models regress the period's end reading on the period's end letter sounds; a version with letter sounds at the _start_ of the period (the family's `mechanism_at_pre` option) plus a Mundlak split of the exposure would give the temporally ordered within-child association the current fits lack.
- _A two-dimensional letter-sound × decoding surface._ The moderation fits impose a linear interaction on the logit scale, which builds in substitution; a two-dimensional HSGP over letter sounds and nonword decoding (or blending) would show the joint surface without that constraint and settle whether the negative interactions are real or a scale artefact.
- _Sequential mediation with temporal ordering._ The L → N → W chain was fitted within the timepoint-2 window; the longitudinal-ordering form (letter sounds at t2, decoding at t3, reading at t4) is the design that could actually show decoding carrying a letter-sound effect forward. Expensive (g-formula), but the machinery exists (`med-076`).
- _A better decoding measure, or a better model of this one._ Nonword reading is six items with most children at zero. An item-response or graded model of the six items, or a longer decoding measure in future data collection, is the only way to test decoding's role without the floor dominating.
- _Dose-dependence of the letter-sound link._ A letter-sound × sessions interaction (or the ability-adjusted panel refitted with attendance as a moderator) would say whether the letter-sound → reading association is stronger for children who received more of the programme — the pattern a taught route would show.

**On vocabulary and word repetition (question 6).**

- _Adjust the taught-vocabulary mechanism fits for sessions._ `mech-088/089` flag the sessions backdoor but do not adjust for it; the adjusted version is a one-line change and would say how much of the taught-vocabulary → reading slope is exposure to the programme.
- _Vocabulary as a moderator of the treatment effect._ A gain-factor moderation variant with a treatment × baseline-vocabulary term would test whether the intervention's word-reading effect depends on the child's language level, which is a live practical question the current `gf-201` (ability, own baseline) does not answer.
- _Longitudinal-ordering mediation through expressive vocabulary_ (t2 vocabulary → t3 reading), mirroring `med-076`, since expressive vocabulary is the one language measure whose prior level predicts later reading change.

**On hearing and speech (question 7).**

- _Use the two hearing indicators separately._ The flag merges a hearing impairment with an ear-infection history (both recorded only as yes/no — there is no graded hearing measure in the file) and is imputed for a fifth of the children; a model entering the two indicators separately, and a sensitivity analysis dropping the imputed children (the loader's `require_observed` option exists), would say whether the counter-intuitive positive association survives and which component carries it.
- _Re-score speech production to the published standard._ The trial-era DEAP scoring runs about 20 points above the gold-standard percent-consonants-correct for the same cohort; re-scoring would put the speech results on an interpretable absolute scale.
- _Hearing and speech as treatment moderators._ Whether the intervention worked less well for children with hearing difficulty or less clear speech is a question the trial can address (a `gain_factors` moderation variant with treatment × hearing / treatment × speech terms), and it matters more for practice than either variable's own association with reading.
- _Speech production in the between/within split._ Its clear level association and small gain association is the same profile as letter sounds; a `pooled_levels` fit with speech production as the exposure would confirm that it, too, is a between-child correlate.
