# Gain-factor #575 remediation: the decisions behind the implementation

> [!NOTE]
> Drafted by a LLM-based AI tool (Claude Code/Fable 5).

Date: 2026-08-26. Scope: the implementation PR closing the code work of issue #575 (the 2026-08-23 gain-factor audit). The audit's findings were independently re-verified against `main` at `2ed27fcf` before anything changed: every remaining finding reproduced (finding 6 was closed by #618, and the `artifact_manifest.json` fail-open half of finding 7 by #578). Four items needed an implementer-level decision rather than a mechanical fix; this note records each choice and why, so a future reader can question them against the alternatives that were on the table.

## 1. Finding 2 (period-1 estimand): a mandatory period-1-only refit sensitivity, not a `beta_p1` redesign

The audit's recommended direction was a redesign — a causal `beta_p1` multiplying only `G × I(period = 1)`, with later arm/history effects modelled separately — with interim wording until that is demonstrated. The acceptance criterion accepts either the redesign or "a phase-1-only … sensitivity". The implementation takes the sensitivity: every model of record (the thirteen primaries and the `gf-306` link companion) now runs a **period-1-only refit** as a `run_subfit` sensitivity inside its own fit — the identical specification with every post-crossover row dropped from the likelihood — writing `period1_sensitivity.csv` (both fits' `beta_trt` and period-1 AME, with the refit's own convergence verdict) and a `subfit_provenance.csv` row, and every estimand statement now reads "randomisation-anchored, model-dependent period-stacked estimate" with the borrowing mechanism spelled out.

Why not the redesign now: it changes the family's likelihood and headline definition for all thirteen outcomes in one step, on top of an audit remediation — exactly the situation where the house pattern is _measure first_ (the MED-059 bounded-impact comparison before the #585 refits; the level family's decision 3, which kept the four-wave model of record and added the `lf-201–211` window comparators rather than restructuring). The sensitivity produces, for every primary at reporting tier, the number the redesign debate needs: how far the stacked likelihood actually moves the headline. A first dev-tier read on `gf-001` puts the shift at about +0.10 logits (0.40 → 0.50 — the stacked model, if anything, _shrinks_ the effect at that tier); if the reporting-tier sweep shows material, unfavourable borrowing anywhere, the `beta_p1` redesign should be opened as its own issue with that evidence attached. The refit keeps the child random intercept (each child then appears once, so it is identified only jointly with the overdispersion and leans on its prior); that is the price of a pure row restriction, and it is stated in the artefact and the report partial rather than silently absorbed.

## 2. Finding 3 (bounded-score marginals): the concurrent family's per-measure `+k` convention, not a corrected fixed `+5`

The arithmetic fix (Haldane-exact increments, `logit_safe(ȳ + k, N) − logit_safe(ȳ, N)`, with the increment capped at the items the scale has left and the row omitted at the ceiling) did not itself force a choice of `k`. The implementation also replaces the suite-wide `+5 items` with the concurrent family's per-measure `k = max(1, round(n/10))` (so E/R read `+17`, W `+8`, L `+3`, N and B `+1`), recorded per term in `config.json`. A fixed `+5` was a third of the 6-item nonword scale and half the 10-item blending one — the audit's extreme cases were exactly these — and keeping two conventions across families for the same quantity invites cross-family misreading. The published `association_marginals.csv` therefore changes label and value for every bounded-count row; the `+1 SD` rows are unchanged in definition (their items translation now uses the exact `N + 1` inverse). Binary hearing status switches from a `+1 SD` forward shift to the net-out-and-toggle 0 → 1 contrast the off-floor indicator already used.

## 3. Finding 10d (off-floor release evidence): aligned to the ITT floor rule's policy shape, on the family's own sweep

`release.py` documented the gap itself: gain off-floor fits were gated on `beta_trt` prior dependence alone, and a _clear_ one would have released with no grid at all, while the prior-data-conflict class released with a note and no evidence. The decision: gain off-floor fits now route through `_gain_offfloor_decision`, the ITT `_floor_decision` shape keyed on the family's own estimand-matched evidence — the gf treatment-prior sweep, per-fit trace-validated by `_standard_sweep_evidence` (schema, ≥ 2 prior cells, per-cell convergence, sha-256 binding to this fit's `config.json` and `trace.nc`, installed cell-trace digests, sign stability of the published risk-difference column). Any non-clean power-scaling verdict — conflict included — requires that evidence before the findings box may speak. The ITT six-cell grid itself and `evaluate_floor_sensitivity`'s content-addressed machinery stay ITT-bound: rebuilding them for a second family would duplicate provenance code the sweep already provides at the strength this family's evidence tier requires (the same one-rung-down argument as the #619 stored-artefact pairs against the ITT trace archive).

## 4. Finding 9 (association slopes): explicit descriptive labelling plus the `pooled_levels` cross-reference, not a gain-family Mundlak split

The audit offered "keep these quantities explicitly descriptive and model-dependent, or add a Mundlak/person-mean split". The implementation labels: every recipe now states that covariate slopes on time-varying baselines blend within- and between-child variation and points to the `pooled_levels` family, which already carries the Mundlak between/within decomposition of the levels question (r ≈ 0.81 between vs 0.45 within for letter sounds and word reading). A second Mundlak implementation inside the gain family would answer the levels question the pooled family already answers, on a gains model whose slopes the reports deliberately do not headline. `gf-012`/`gf-013` additionally declare `descriptive_skills=("R", "E")`, so the recipe and the `effective_adjustment` record in `config.json` now carry `descriptive_associate` for the downstream vocabulary terms instead of mislabelling them DAG-parent adjusters — the module docstrings said this all along; the machine-readable record now agrees.

## Consequences for stored artefacts

The settings and plan changes (`descriptive_skills`, `kappa_prior_family`, `gamma_own_prior_sigma`, `period1_sensitivity_required`, the reworded estimand/population fields) make **every stored gain-factor plan stale** under the #623 currency check, by design: the fits join the standing one-batch refit backlog rather than being refitted piecemeal. The shared-partial changes (`_setup.qmd` requiring a readable, structurally unblocked `release_decision.json`; `_key_findings.qmd` failing closed on a missing `key_findings.json` or unrecognised status and re-deriving the publication decision at render time) apply to **every family's** reports at their next render. The `association_marginals.csv` numbers for bounded-count rows are corrected, not merely relabelled — pre-#575 `+5 items` rows for short scales were arithmetically wrong (≈ 15 logits at the nonword ceiling) and must not be compared against the new per-measure rows.
