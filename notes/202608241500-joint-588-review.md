## New finding: the companion's stated purpose is not what these fits measure

Three report templates and three module docstrings said the companion exists to answer _"whether the parent's interval was too wide or too narrow"_, on the sign rule that `Var(A − B) = V_A + V_B − 2 Cov(A, B)`, so a positive within-child residual correlation leaves a factorised interval too wide. The audit corrected the _point-estimate_ invariance claim beside it (finding 1) and left this one standing.

The three stored pairs contradict it. Every companion estimates a **positive** residual correlation — `u_corr_pair` medians +0.036, +0.127, +0.086 — yet every companion's contrast interval is **wider** than its parent's, by 2.3%, 4.0% and 2.6%. Under the sign rule the widths should have moved the other way.

Computed from the stored traces, the reason is unambiguous. The posterior correlation between the two outcomes' average marginal effects is indistinguishable from zero in the companion as well as in the parent:

| pair      | AME corr, factorised | AME corr, correlated | contrast SD       | marginal SDs                  |
| --------- | -------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------- |
| 015 → 215 | −0.003               | +0.001               | 0.04798 → 0.04906 | 0.0293/0.0379 → 0.0305/0.0384 |
| 016 → 216 | −0.006               | +0.006               | 0.04242 → 0.04422 | 0.0291/0.0307 → 0.0308/0.0319 |
| 115 → 315 | −0.006               | +0.002               | 0.04716 → 0.04860 | 0.0306/0.0357 → 0.0313/0.0372 |

The factorised values are structurally zero, so they are this measurement's own noise floor — and the correlated values sit inside it. Decomposing each contrast's width change into what the companion's wider marginals alone would do and what the covariance change adds gives +0.00119/−0.00011, +0.00206/−0.00026 and +0.00164/−0.00020: the covariance channel is under 12% of the movement, in the narrowing direction, and inside Monte Carlo noise. **Every measurable part of the difference is the added logistic-normal layer's own parameter uncertainty.**

That is what the algebra predicts. A child-level offset shared by both outcomes reaches a _between-arm_ contrast only through `ρ σ₁ σ₂ (1/n₁ + 1/n₀)`; with arms of 28 and 26, residual SDs of 0.10–0.17 logit and a prior-dominated `ρ`, that term predicts induced τ correlations of +0.0015, +0.0102 and +0.0042 against measured +0.0007, +0.0053 and +0.0023 — the same order, two orders of magnitude below each contrast's own variance.

None of this makes the companions useless: they answer whether the contrast survives modelling within-child dependence, and it does. What was wrong was the stated purpose. A simulation (below) confirms the sign rule is right about the estimator and that the block does carry dependence when it is identified — so the near-nil covariance channel here is a statement about _these data_, not about the construction.

## Changes made

1. **Prose.** The purpose claim is replaced in `lrp_rli_itt_215/216/315.py` and their three report templates with the measured account: the sign rule stated as what it is (a property of the covariance term alone), then the measurement, then the corrected reading. The three parent reports gain the same result in one sentence. The two code comments that carried the old framing — `pipelines/joint.py` and `release._dependence_identification_note` — are corrected.
2. **Measurement, not assertion.** `release._joint_width_channels` splits the contrast's interval-width change into its marginal and covariance channels and records both, each fit's implied cross-outcome AME correlation, and which channel dominated, under `dependence_contrast` in `release_decision.json`. The factorised parent's implied correlation is recorded deliberately: it is structurally zero, so it calibrates the approximation. `_results_joint.qmd` renders the record, so the report's account of its companion is read from the decision rather than written into the template. The split is descriptive — a missing `tau_summary.csv` costs the split, not the release.
3. **Fail-closed.** `_joint_contrast_consequence` attached no qualifier when the comparison could not be taken at all — a missing or mismatched `tau_difference.csv` published as silently as a clean comparison. It now attaches the reason, matching the binding checks beside it: absence is not evidence that the dependence model left the contrast alone.
4. **Acceptance criterion — dependence simulation.** `tests/statistical_models/test_joint_dependence.py` covers zero, positive and negative outcome dependence at both levels the claim has. Exactly, on constructed posteriors: the per-draw contrast's width follows `√(1 − ρ)` while its median does not move, and shuffling one outcome's draws — what pairing two separate fits does — returns the zero-covariance width whatever the true dependence is. By simulation and sampling, for ρ ∈ {−0.7, 0, +0.7} with an identified block: `u_corr_pair` recovers −0.77, +0.05, +0.77; the AME correlation follows at −0.55, −0.01, +0.57; and the contrast's 89% interval orders 0.207, 0.167, 0.114 while the per-outcome marginal SDs stay within 8% of each other. That is the covariance channel in isolation — the exact complement of the registered pairs.
5. **Acceptance criterion — LOO holdout unit.** `test_diagnostics.py` gains a trace where each child supplies both outcomes across three transitions: the stored PSIS-LOO aggregates all six of a child's cells into one unit, while the outcome-specific LOO-PIT tree keeps six cell-level units. The two diagnostics answer different questions and the tests now say which is which.

## What this review does not close

1. **No refits.** Deferred by decision until the audit backlog is caught up. `dependence_contrast` and the channel split recompute over stored fits without one; the corrected prose reaches published reports only when they are regenerated.
2. `lrp-rlm-jc-102` at reporting tier, so `jc-002`'s prior-robustness claim stays preliminary — unchanged from #609.
3. A genuine new-child predictive calibration diagnostic for the joint families, and a target-specific out-of-sample assessment for historical joint. Both remain labelled rather than built.
4. The audit's finding 1 also asked for a new-child population-marginal standardisation reported _beside_ the latent-conditional one. It has been measured — integrating fresh residuals moves the medians by less than 0.00012, recorded in `_joint_ame_draws` — but is not published as its own quantity, which would need a refit. Given that magnitude, publishing it is a presentation decision rather than a correction.
