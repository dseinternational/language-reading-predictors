## Finding 1 (statistical/reporting, moderate): jm-001 is not "matched term-for-term" to ca-010 / ca-011 — it omits `hs_missing` and uses a different group-nuisance prior

The like-for-like replacement of the ca-011/ca-010 paired-draws ratio by the identified `share_retained` is one of the model's two core warrants, and the matching claim is asserted in the module docstring, the factory docstring, `_results_joint_mechanism.qmd` ("The adjustment set and slope prior are matched to `ca-010` / `ca-011`") and `docs/models/lrp-rli-jm-001/index.qmd`. Two terms do not match:

- **`hs_missing` is omitted.** `ca-010` / `ca-011` declare `covariates=("blocks", "hs", "hs_missing")`; `lrp_rli_jm_001.py` declares `covariates=("blocks", "hs")`. This is not vacuous: on the current CSV 9 of 54 children have unknown hearing status at t1 (`hs_missing` = 1), and `ca-010`'s fitted `-reporting` record confirms `hs_missing` is fitted (not dropped-constant) at every wave. In jm-001 those nine children enter with `hs` reference-filled to 0 — coded as hearing-clear with no offset — which is exactly the configuration the repo's missing-indicator policy exists to prevent ("Covariates whose filled value must be accompanied by an explicit missingness indicator whenever a model elects to keep the filled rows", `preprocessing.py::MISSINGNESS_INDICATOR_PAIRS`; the #244 team decision).
- **The group-nuisance prior differs.** `build_concurrent_model` gives `beta_group_nuisance` a deliberately wide Normal(0, 1) ("not the regularising association prior — it is not an association we report"); `build_joint_mechanism_model` builds the same-named term from `tau_prior()` = Normal(0, 0.5) in both designs. Half the width, and semantically the treatment-effect constructor for an avowed nuisance.

The practical effect on the published slopes is probably small (both terms carry weakly-regularising priors and the group term is a nuisance), but a claim of exact covariate matching that is checked nowhere and false in two particulars should not survive in five documents. **Recommendation:** either add `hs_missing` to jm-001's `covariates` (a refit; the loader broadcasts it from t1 exactly as ca-010 does) and align the nuisance prior with the concurrent factory's Normal(0, 1) — or amend every "matched term-for-term / like-for-like" sentence to name the two deviations. Given the model exists to _replace_ the ca-pair's ratio, matching the fit rather than the prose is the better repair. `jm-002`'s matching to `mech-096` / `mech-101` was checked term-by-term (own raw-logit baseline, free Normal(0, 0.5) phase intercepts, `tau_prior` `beta_G`, `gamma_cross` adjusters including both missingness indicators, `kappa` prior, the `attend`-to-pre-row split, proximal alpha tier) and is genuinely like-for-like.

## Finding 2 (statistical, moderate): PSIS-LOO in the levels design is saturated and unreliable, and nothing says so

The levels design carries one bivariate latent residual per child over exactly two likelihood cells. The family's own `_jm_marginal_ppc` docstring works through the consequence for the _posterior predictive_ — the conditional check is structurally 1.00 — and repairs it with the new-child marginal check, plus a full caveat paragraph in the results partial. PSIS-LOO has the same disease and got neither the repair nor the caveat: leave-one-child-out importance ratios for a child whose latent is informed almost solely by that child's own two cells are prior-versus-posterior mismatched, and the published jm-001 `-reporting` artefacts confirm it — `p_loo` = 66.1 on 53 observations (more effective parameters than data points), **48 of 53 Pareto-k above 0.7** (max 1.14), and an ArviZ warning in `loo.txt`. Yet the run plan sets `compute_loo=True` for both designs, `loo_elpd` is written into `config.json`'s extra block as if usable, and the machine-written recipe lists PSIS-LOO among the fit's required checks. The Pareto-k table and plot are published, so an attentive reader can see the failure — but the framing treats a diagnostic that cannot work in this design as a check that passed. **Recommendation:** either set `compute_loo=False` for the levels design (with a recipe sentence saying why, mirroring the mediation families), or keep it and add the explicit "conditional on saturated per-child latents; expect mass Pareto-k failure; elpd not comparable" caveat next to the marginal-PPC note in `_results_joint_mechanism.qmd` and drop `loo_elpd` from the metadata. A marginalised (integrated-residual) LOO is the principled third option but is real work for a diagnostic the family does not act on. The transition design is fine (2 of 153 rows flagged, max 1.04).

## Finding 3 (metadata, moderate): `loo_unit="child"` is wrong for the transition design — the unit is a child-by-transition row

`_joint_log_likelihood_by_child` aggregates the flattened cells by `y_post_cell_row`, and for `phase_mode="all"` a row is a child-by-transition, not a child: jm-002's published `pareto_k.csv` has **153 rows against 53 children**, and each "unit" leaves out one transition's two outcome cells while the child's other transitions keep informing the bivariate child intercept. But `resolve_joint_mechanism_run_plan` hard-codes `loo_unit="child"` for both designs, `JointMechanismPayload` types it as `Literal["child"]`, the aggregated DataArray's attrs say `loo_unit: "child"`, and the recipe prints "PSIS-LOO uses the `child` unit" — all four are wrong for jm-002. Row-level LOO is a defensible unit here; the label is not. **Recommendation:** either register a `loo_child_idx` constant (cells → child) in the transition factory so `_joint_log_likelihood_by_child` takes its first branch and the unit genuinely becomes leave-one-child-out (matching what the label promises and making the elpd comparable with per-child families), or make the plan resolve `loo_unit="child_transition_row"` for `design="transition"` and widen the payload literal. The levels design's label is correct (one row per child).

## Finding 4 (functional, moderate but latent): the key-findings builder and the results partial ignore the per-wave `converged` flag

`joint_mechanism_slopes.csv` carries a `converged` flag per wave precisely so a non-converged sub-fit wave is published flagged rather than dropped, and the partial's reading guide says "`converged` must be true before a row is interpreted". But neither consumer enforces it: `_kf_build_joint_mechanism` selects the headline wave by distance of P(>0) from 0.5 over **all** delta rows, quotes the share-retained range over all waves, and never reads the column — while the concurrent family's builder (the structural twin) explicitly filters `converged` before selecting. The partial's own inline lead-selection (`_delta.iloc[(_delta["prob_pos"] - 0.5).abs().argmax()]`) has the same gap, one paragraph after instructing the reader to check the flag. The fit-level release gate covers only the anchor wave, so a non-converged wave sub-fit could headline the gate-interlocked `key_findings.json`. Latent today — all four waves pass in the current reporting fit (max R-hat 1.001, zero divergences) — but this is the exact failure mode the flag exists for. **Recommendation:** filter both the builder's lead/range selections and the partial's inline selections to `converged == True` rows (footnoting any excluded wave), mirroring `_kf_build_concurrent`; regenerable without a refit via `scripts/regenerate_key_findings.py` (with the per-fit `_partials` sync).

## Finding 5 (functional, moderate): `priors_table.csv` mislabels three parameter groups — the family has no prior-table branch and no prior-inventory coverage

Confirmed in both published `-reporting` tables and reproduced from freshly built models:

- **jm-002's `beta_G` carries role `causal`** with the tau panel and the "Treatment effect tau ~ Normal(0, 0.5)" rationale, in a family whose every document insists nothing is causal and whose group term is declared a nuisance. The mechanism family demotes exactly this via its `_prior_table_overrides` branch; `joint_mechanism` has no branch, so `_RV_TO_CTOR["beta_G"] → "tau" → role "causal"` runs unopposed.
- **jm-001's `beta_group_nuisance` is reported as Normal(0, 1)** while the fitted prior is `tau_prior()` = Normal(0, 0.5). The `beta_group_nuisance` prefix route in `priors.prior_info_for_rv` returns the `_INLINE_PRIORS` record's hard-coded distribution without the read-the-built-RV preference every other route applies (and its rationale text describes the RLM cohort dummies, not an arm-composition nuisance). This is the one row in the table that misstates a fitted distribution outright — and it would also silently misreport any future prior-sensitivity variant.
- **`u_resid_chol` / `u_resid_z` / `u_child_chol` / `u_child_z` carry role `other`** with empty rationales — the sentinel `_classify_fallback` reserves for "genuinely unrecognised" RVs, meant to trip the completeness guard. It cannot trip: `test_prior_inventory.py::_representative_models` includes no `joint_mechanism` model, so `test_every_family_prior_is_documented` never sees these names (`u_resid_chol` misses the `u_chol` prefix branch; `u_resid_z` misses `u_z`).

**Recommendation:** add a `joint_mechanism` branch to `_prior_table_overrides` (demote `beta_G` to association with an explicit rationale, document the dependence-block RVs), make the `beta_group_nuisance` route prefer the built RV's distribution like its neighbours, and add one jm build to the prior-inventory fixture so the guard covers the family. All three are artefact-layer fixes; no refit needed (`priors_table.csv` regenerates from the stored model spec on the next backfill).

## Finding 6 (functional, minor): no `effective_adjustment` record

Every other converted family writes the standardised fitted-adjustment record (`adjustment.effective_adjustment` — per-term source column, wave, missing-indicator flag, dropped-constant list; the #258 P1 artefact, and the concurrent family even writes it per timepoint). The jm pipeline writes none: `config.json`'s top-level `adjustment` says `["G", "A"]` (jm-001) / `["G", "A", "W_pre", "N_pre"]` (jm-002) while the fits condition on `blocks` + `hs`, respectively `hs`/`hs_missing`/`attend`/`deapp_c`/`deapp_c_missing`. The resolved run plan does record `declared_adjustment` / `active_adjustment`, so the fit is auditable — but in a family-specific shape without wave or indicator metadata, and this is item 3 of the known new-family checklist gap (the same one pooled_levels shipped with). **Recommendation:** emit the standard record in both fit entry points' `write_run_metadata` extra (per wave for the levels design, matching concurrent).

## Observations (no action required)

- **The core algebra was verified.** `beta_mech_focal_given_held = beta_f − rho (sigma_f / sigma_h) beta_h` is the exact coefficient on the exposure after conditioning the focal latent logit on the held latent logit (the shared-covariate terms shift, the z-coefficient is exact), the contrast indexing (`hi`/`lo`, focal = `contrast[1]`) is correct against the declared ("N", "W") convention, and `delta_ls_decoding = beta_N − beta_W` matches every published label.
- **`_jm_marginal_ppc` is correct and careful**: the fitted residual is subtracted from the persisted `eta`, the redraw uses the closed-form 2×2 Cholesky of the estimated Σ, cells map through the persisted row/outcome constants with per-outcome denominators, the seed comes from the fit's sampling config, and doing it in NumPy rather than through `pm.sample_posterior_predictive` avoids the conditioning trap the docstring names. The published coverage (72% at the 50% level, 98% at 90%) behaves like the informative check it claims to be, against the conditional check's degenerate 1.00/1.00.
- **The dependence-block construction is right for this family**: `LKJCholeskyCov` with the scales inside `chol` (no double-scaling), which is the correct choice here — unlike the correlation-only families on bare `LKJCorr` — because the conditional slope needs the sds. Binomial-without-kappa in the levels design (one overdispersion mechanism) versus Beta-Binomial-plus-child-covariance in the transition design (two levels, separately identified) is coherent and well documented.
- **The per-wave lifecycle is sound**: the ten-row wave floor skips by name, the anchor rule (most rows, ties later) is documented as operational, wave sub-fits run through `run_subfit` with provenance, every published wave's gate quantities land in `joint_mechanism_fit_diagnostics.csv`, and `all_published_fits_converged` treats an uncheckable sub-fit (`converged=None`) as not-converged. The loader/factory row-selection conditions agree, and both subset helpers re-derive `child_idx` / `n_children` correctly (the levels one-row-per-child guard depends on it).
- **jm-002's loader requires `L_pre`** (the `pre_required` default over `outcomes=(W, N, L)`) although the model never uses the letter-sound baseline and the recipe's population sentence does not mention it. Empirically it binds on zero rows in the current CSV (all 154 otherwise-eligible transition rows have `L_pre`), so this is latent; passing `pre_required=("W", "N")` — or documenting the requirement — would make the fitted population match its description under future data.
- The levels exposure is standardised per wave on the fitted rows (matching ca-010/011's convention), so cross-wave comparisons of slope magnitudes fold in wave-to-wave changes in the letter-sound SD; the per-wave framing of the outputs keeps this honest.
- `share_retained` sits in the curated gate/diagnostic variable set; R-hat/ESS on a ratio whose denominator could approach zero is brittle (a spurious gate failure risk for any future contrast where the focal slope is weak — not for the current fits, where `beta_mech[W]` is far from zero). Relatedly, the slopes CSV and metadata carry a `mean` column for `share_retained` although the module and partial correctly insist on median-only for a ratio; the report and key findings quote medians, so this is a downstream-consumer trap rather than a live error.
- The release policy correctly leaves the family outside `GATED_KINDS` (no causal headline to gate), the run-plan validation (unknown keys, mixed declarations, cross-field contradictions, settings-before-I/O ordering) is comprehensive and well tested, `tier1_decoding_specificity` consumes jm-002's Δ correctly (added beside, not replacing, the product-of-marginals sensitivity row, with `identified` and `gate_ok` flags), and the boundary tests pin direct family imports and the no-`pm.sample`-outside-`run_subfit` rule.

## Resolution (fix batch, same day)

All seven findings were fixed in the accompanying change set; the note above records the pre-fix state.

- **Finding 1** — jm-001 now declares `covariates=("blocks", "hs", "hs_missing")` and the levels design's `beta_group_nuisance` is built on the concurrent factory's wide Normal(0, 1) instead of `tau_prior`; the matching claims in the module, factory, partial and report template were tightened to name both terms. **The published jm-001 numbers predate this: the model changed, so jm-001 needs a refit before its artefacts match the code.**
- **Finding 2** — the levels design resolves `compute_loo=False`: no PSIS-LOO, no `loo.txt`/`pareto_k` artefacts, no LOO-PIT (it shares the PSIS weights), with the log-density groups psense needs attached directly (the mediation families' route) and the recipe and results partial now saying why. The transition design keeps LOO.
- **Finding 3** — the transition factory registers a `loo_child_idx` cell-to-child map, so `_joint_log_likelihood_by_child` aggregates all of a child's cells across the three transitions and `loo_unit="child"` becomes true rather than relabelled; the helper's unit count for that branch now comes from the map itself rather than `G`'s per-row size (which would have appended zero-likelihood phantom units). jm-002's elpd/Pareto-k therefore change meaning at the next refit.
- **Finding 4** — `_kf_build_joint_mechanism` and the results partial's inline selections filter to `converged` rows (mirroring the concurrent builder), name any excluded wave explicitly, and fail unavailable when no converged delta row remains.
- **Finding 5** — `_prior_table_overrides` gained a `joint_mechanism` branch (`beta_G` demoted to association with its own rationale; family wording for `beta_group_nuisance`); the `beta_group_nuisance` route in `prior_info_for_rv` now reads the built RV's distribution; `_classify_fallback` / `_fallback_rationale` classify `u_resid_chol`/`u_resid_z`/`u_child_chol`/`u_child_z` as documented nuisances; and both jm designs joined the prior-inventory fixture so the no-`other` completeness guard covers them.
- **Finding 6** — both fit entry points write the standard `effective_adjustment` record (`adjustment.effective_adjustment`, extended with a plural `baseline_symbols` for the transition design's two autoregressive baselines).
- **Finding 7** — the README family row describes the LKJ dependence block; the key-findings direction sentence is design-aware; the slope/share sentences derive their outcome labels from the declared contrast; and the partial surfaces `prior_predictive_check_n.png` and (transition only) `loo_pit_n.png`.
- The latent `L_pre` observation was also closed: the transition plan passes `pre_required=("W", "N")`, so the fitted population matches its description (zero rows change on the current CSV).

Findings 1–3 change the fitted models or their LOO artefacts, so both models needed a refit before their artefacts matched the code. **The reporting refits were run the same day** — both pass the gate, both are publishable, and jm-001's published numbers moved by ≤ 0.04 on every median (jm-002's are identical; only the LOO unit changed meaning).
