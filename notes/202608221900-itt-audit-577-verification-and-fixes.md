## What was amended in the audit's recommendations

**Finding 3 (LKJ companions).** The diagnosis is right — `factories.py` sets `eta = eta_core + u` and `_joint_ame_draws` subtracts only `tau * G`, so the reported AME is conditional on the fitted children's posterior residuals, and the three module docstrings overstate point-estimate invariance. But the audit's proposed fix, integrating fresh residuals, is wrong for this companion: it exists to isolate the _interval_ effect of dependence while holding the point estimate comparable to a parent that has no random effect at all, and marginalising over `u_new` introduces attenuation the parent does not have — for a change the audit itself measures at under 0.00012. The proportionate fix is to document the estimand using the `intercept_basis` convention `predicted_scores.py` already establishes, and to soften the invariance prose.

The audit also understates the identification problem, which is the more consequential fact. LKJ(eta = 4) at d = 2 implies a marginal prior on the correlation with SD 0.3334 (equivalently `(rho + 1) / 2 ~ Beta(4, 4)`; verified numerically). The three posteriors report SD 0.334, 0.336 and 0.334, with means 0.032, 0.111 and 0.077 — all within a third of a prior SD of zero. **The correlation posterior is the prior.** At n = 53 the data supply essentially no information about rho, so the companions' "corrected" intervals are the LKJ prior's implied correction rather than a data-driven one, and they cannot answer the question their docstrings pose. This is specific to the correlation: `sigma_outcome` _is_ informed (posterior medians 0.11–0.18 against a prior median of 0.34).

**Finding 5 (kappa prior).** Every number checks out — HalfNormal(50) median 33.7; variance inflation `(n + kappa) / (1 + kappa)` of 2.12, 3.28 and 5.87 at n = 40, 80, 170; `kappa > 1689` needed to come within 10% of Binomial at n = 170. The `kappa_sigma` sweep really is confined to `STANDARD_SENSITIVITY_PROXIMAL_OUTCOMES = ("L", "W")` and capped at 200. The priority is nevertheless lower than the issue implies: R and E carry `n_trials = 170` against observed maxima of 82 and 77, so substantial genuine overdispersion is expected for those measures and the near-Binomial limit is close to a straw man there. Worth doing because `priors.inv_sqrt_kappa_prior` already exists and is already wired into the RLM historical factories, but it needs refits and is deferred.

**Finding 9 re-grades.** `lrp_rli_itt_027`'s "the fit stays on all 54 children" is plainly wrong (`config.json` records `n_children: 53`) and is fixed. `lrp_rli_itt_024`'s "Block design is complete for all 54 children, so no rows drop" is a covariate-completeness claim relative to LRPITT10 and is defensible as written — no change. `available_t1_n` is confirmed misleading (N's true t1 observed count is 50, W's is 53, R's and E's are 54), but `itt_audit.py` already documents the compromise and `reporting.py` validates stored `analysis_set.csv` files against the column, so a bare rename would break regeneration of 280 bundles; only the documentation and the misleading error message are changed here. The typed-`target_accept` limitation is already recorded verbatim in `itt.py` as "the 2026-08-20 ITT code review (finding 5)" and is a duplicate.

## Fixes applied

**Finding 1 — non-finite R-hat / ESS can pass the gate.** Reproduced: a trace with one healthy and one constant parameter returned `passed: true`, `max_rhat: 1.0003`, `rhat_failing: []`. The shared writer in `dse_research_utils` reduces with `np.nanmax` / `np.nanmin`, and NaN also compares False against the thresholds, so the parameter reaches neither the extrema nor the failing lists; its BFMI check was already hardened against exactly this. `sampling_quality` now reports an `unassessable` tuple naming the summarised variables whose R-hat or ESS is non-finite — extraction only, per that module's contract. `diagnostics.write_diagnostics_summary` consumes it in `_fail_on_unassessable`, recording a `diagnostics_assessable` check and rewriting `diagnostics_summary.json` with `passed: false`; `reporting.convergence_gate_failures` already fails closed on any unrecognised non-`True` check, so the release gate picks it up unchanged. `subfit_convergence` applies the same rule. The scan runs over the gated set (free RVs plus curated headline terms), so a mathematically constant _deterministic_ cannot trip it. Verified against every stored ITT trace: the only constant posterior variables anywhere are `u_corr` and `u_chol_corr` in the three LKJ companions, both Deterministics and both deliberately excluded from the curated list; no current fit newly fails.

**Finding 2 — the stored release evaluation accepts a missing manifest.** Reproduced: a copy of `lrp-rli-itt-001-reporting` stripped of its manifest, trace, `tau_summary.csv`, `rope_summary.csv`, `analysis_set.csv` and `ppc_summary.csv` still returned `publishable: True, status: ok`. This is live rather than hypothetical — `_key_findings.qmd` re-decides publication over the stored directory at _render_ time, as do `scripts/regenerate_key_findings.py` and the `reporting.py` regeneration path. `_recorded_required_artifacts` now splits the fit-time and stored paths explicitly: at fit time the live `ArtifactLog` is the authority and the manifest does not exist yet (finalisation writes it after the decision), while on the stored path a missing, unreadable or entry-less `artifact_manifest.json` is itself a missing artefact, and `_core_artifact_failures` applies a floor underneath it. The floor is deliberately narrow — `trace.nc`, `diagnostics.csv`, `priors_table.csv`, the three every registered family writes. Family result tables were considered and rejected: the key-findings layer already checks each family's outputs for presence _and_ internal consistency, and moving those verdicts to the artefacts stage would strip the explanation from them. All 15 stored directories without a manifest are `.pre-review-fix-*` backups, so the blast radius on live fits is nil; re-evaluating all 242 live bundles reproduces their stored decisions exactly.

**Finding 4 — the "paired" score effect uses independent noise.** Confirmed: `_scores()` is invoked once per arm and resamples both the Beta propensity and the Binomial count, so only the posterior draw, reference row, `kappa` and child intercept are shared — while the property was named `score_difference`, documented as "(CRN)", tabulated as `predicted_score_paired_difference` and annotated as "paired effect". The concrete harm is larger than the issue states: `arm_overlap_predictive.csv` wrote that difference under the column name **`average_marginal_effect`**, colliding with the genuine AME of the same name in `arm_overlap_mean.csv` and `predicted_scores.csv`. For `lrp-rli-itt-001` / TR that is 1.0 items [-5, 8] beside 1.368 items [0.19, 2.53], and `p_intervention_higher` 0.582 beside 0.968, with only the filename to tell a reader which is which — and the report cites both files by name. The property is now `score_difference_independent`, the row `predicted_score_difference_independent_children`, and `arm_overlap_summary` takes `effect_quantity` / `superiority_quantity` so the predictive table writes `p_intervention_child_higher` instead. The predictive figure now annotates the analytic AME, as the audit recommends. The quantity is retained, not deleted: a between-children superiority probability is legitimate and interpretable — it was only mislabelled as a within-child potential-outcome contrast. `predicted_scores.png` was already annotating `ame_items` and is unaffected.

**Finding 7 — plan-level validation.** Confirmed, with a consequence the issue omits: `pipelines/itt.py` orders `resolve_itt_run_plan` (line 176), then `make_context` (177, which resets the output directory), then `prepare_itt_data` (182). An unknown outcome symbol therefore resolved cleanly, **wiped the previous fit's directory**, and only then failed at `MEASURES[s]` — breaching the invariant `CLAUDE.md` states for resolution. `resolve_itt_run_plan` now rejects unknown symbols in `outcomes`, `cross_symbols`, `pre_required` and the baseline branch of `tau_moderator_symbol` (the covariate branch names a covariate column, not a measure, so it is excluded), and requires every baseline that enters the linear predictor to appear in `pre_required`. No floor-rule carve-out is needed: that rule already cannot set `use_own_baseline`. All 38 registered ITT and joint specs still resolve.

**Finding 9 (partial).** `_validate_binary_codes` checked `set(values.astype(int))` against the allowed codes, and `astype` truncates toward zero, so a corrupt 1.7 became a valid 1 and 0.4 a valid 0; `word_reading_t2` was bounds-checked only and then cast with `astype(np.int64)`, so 12.5 was modelled as 12. Both now reject non-integral values before the cast, matching the ordinary ITT loader. The ITT-010 provenance `coefficient_priors` omitted `tau` and `kappa` — describing a model with no treatment effect and no dispersion parameter, while the release check's own convergence scan covered both; all three shared-constructor entries are now rendered from the constructors so the record cannot drift from the model the factory builds. `lrp_rli_itt_027`'s docstring now states the correct 53-child analysis population, and `itt_audit.available_t1_n` carries an explicit deprecation note pointing at `analysed_archive_n`.

**Tests.** Regression tests were added for each fixed defect: `test_diagnostics.py` (the unassessable-parameter scan and the sub-fit gate), `test_release_decision.py` (missing / unreadable / entry-less manifest, the core-inventory floor, the intact bundle still publishing, and the fit-time path _not_ requiring the manifest), `test_arm_overlap.py` (the two figures cannot share contrast quantity names), `test_predicted_scores.py` (a zero treatment effect yields a non-zero simulated difference — pinning the arithmetic against the name), `test_itt_run_plan.py` (unknown symbols and unrestricted baselines rejected at resolution; the registered floor models still resolve) and `test_itt_missingness.py` (fractional codes and counts rejected; every sampled prior recorded). The `_fit_dir` / `_setup_dir` fixtures in `test_release_decision.py` and `test_key_findings.py` now build a _complete_ stored fit, generated from the release module's own contract so widening the floor cannot leave them behind. Full suite exit 0; `ruff check src/`, `npm run format:check` and `npm run spellcheck` all clean.

## Second batch: the regeneration and backfill fixes (findings 3, 8, 9 remainder)

No refit was needed for any of these. The batch also settled the refit question for the whole suite.

**All 38 stored ITT bundles are model-compatible with `HEAD`.** The first batch's note said `build_joint_model` had changed across the stored bundles' divergence from `4e924948` and that the seven joint fits needed re-examining. That was wrong, and came from reading hunk headers rather than the functions. Extracting `build_joint_model` from both revisions and diffing it shows **docstring prose only**; the 294-line `factories.py` diff is almost entirely `build_joint_mechanism_model`, a different family. With `build_itt_model` unchanged, `measures.py` / `itt.py` comment-only and `priors.py` defaults unchanged, the single remaining substantive difference is `preprocessing._subset_prepared` now filtering `pre_counts` — and nothing in the ITT path reads `pre_counts` (the readers are `build_gain_factors_model` and the aligned/block-exposure factories, `factories.py:4569,4608,5275,5303`). So no refit is warranted on provenance grounds, and of the deferred set **only finding 5 needs fresh sampling**.

**Finding 3 — the LKJ companions.** The fix is the one this note argued for rather than the audit's: document the estimand and correct the invariance prose, not marginalise over fresh residuals. Three things were added.

`reporting.dependence_identification_summary` writes `dependence_identification.csv`: one row per block parameter comparing the posterior SD against the prior SD **measured from the fit's own persisted prior group**. Measuring the prior empirically rather than from the LKJ closed form is deliberate and was forced by a discovery. The closed form for an off-diagonal of a `d x d` LKJ(`eta`) is `1 / sqrt(2a + 1)` with `a = eta + (d - 2) / 2` (now `priors.residual_correlation_prior_sd`, with `priors.JOINT_RESIDUAL_LKJ_ETA` naming the shape the factory fits under so the two cannot drift). At `d = 2` that gives exactly `1/3` and agrees with 200k draws from this environment's sampler to four decimals. At `d = 3` it does **not**: `pm.LKJCorr(n=3, eta=4)` yields off-diagonal SDs of 0.316, 0.302 and 0.301, where a true LKJ is exchangeable and all three must equal the closed form's 0.316. That is a real oddity in the environment's `LKJCorr` worth its own investigation, but it is also why the summary reads its yardstick off the fit's own prior draws and keeps the closed form only as a fallback, recorded in a `prior_source` column.

The measurement is unambiguous. Posterior-to-prior SD ratios are **1.001, 1.008 and 1.001** for the three companions' correlations: the posterior _is_ the prior, and the "dependence correction" each publishes is the LKJ prior's rather than the data's. The residual SDs are a different story — ratios 0.29 to 0.38 — so the block is not uniformly uninformative and the table reports each parameter separately for exactly that reason.

`release._dependence_identification_note` turns a prior-dominated correlation into a qualifier on the fit's own release decision, which flows through to the findings box as a `robustness` sentence. It is a note and never a withhold: the fit is valid, and what is qualified is the interpretation of the correlation. `_results_joint.qmd` renders the table with a `callout-warning` when the correlation is prior-dominated. The three module docstrings now state the ratio, drop the claim that "the point estimate is unaffected either way" (it held as an empirical observation about 0.0001–0.0011 shifts, not by construction — a logistic-normal offset re-estimates `alpha`, `tau`, the baseline slopes and `kappa` jointly under a nonlinear link), and record that the reported AME is conditional on the fitted children's residuals, with the reason that keeps it comparable to a parent that has no random effect at all. `_joint_ame_draws` carries the same statement.

Backfilled with `scripts/regenerate_dependence_identification.py` (new, following the existing `regenerate_*` pattern) for `lrp-rli-itt-215`, `-216` and `-315`, then `_partials` synced and the three reports re-rendered. Backups are `<dir>.pre-audit-577-20260822`. The script appends its entry to `artifact_manifest.json` in place rather than calling `artifacts.write_manifest`, which without a live `ArtifactLog` would reclassify every recorded artefact as `untracked` and destroy the provenance the manifest exists to hold.

**Finding 8 — the ITT-010 target design.** `itt_missingness.missingness_design_record` records the design's dimensions, the arm split of the 57 randomised targets, how many are observed and in the original analysis, and a sha256 over the four target arrays that define the completion problem (`target_G`, `target_outcome_observed`, `target_in_original_analysis`, `target_X`). Stored evaluation now checks both halves: `release._missingness_design_dimension_error` verifies the persisted trace actually covers 57 target profiles and 53 observed rows, and the recorded design block is checked against the registered trial contract. The dimension check is not hypothetical — the existing test fixture built a trace with **one** target and **one** observation, exactly the case the audit said would qualify, and it now fails. ITT-010's provenance was backfilled by rebuilding the loader from the pinned archive without sampling; the recorded design reproduces the contract exactly (57 = 29 + 28 randomised, 53 = 28 + 25 observed, 54 in the original analysis).

**Finding 9 remainder — `available_t1_n`.** Retired rather than renamed. It was a duplicate of `analysed_archive_n` under a name that claimed outcome-specific t1 availability, which is measure-specific (50 children for N against 53 for W). New fits stop writing it; `reporting._kf_analysis_set` no longer requires it and reads `analysed_archive_n` throughout, while still cross-checking the equality that made the alias redundant whenever a stored bundle carries it. No regeneration of the 242 stored `analysis_set.csv` files is needed.
