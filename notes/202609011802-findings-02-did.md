# Findings: the `did` family — arm-by-wave contrasts across the crossover

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 22 models in the `did` family, which re-estimate the intervention contrast from an arm-by-wave repeated-level model over three timepoints rather than a single post-treatment comparison. All 22 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and all are publishable (2026-09-01 rebuild). The four companions registered by the #576 review (`did-103`–`106`) are reported here for the first time at reporting tier, and the phoneme-blending pair is now complete.

## The data

**RLI trial only.** The arm-by-wave models use **timepoints 1, 2 and 3**, stacked (one row per child per timepoint): 53–54 children and 155–162 rows. The dose companions use fewer rows (105–107) because they need session-count records. Using three timepoints is the point of the design, but only one of the three comparisons contrasts treated children with untreated ones.

## What the model is for

The implemented headline is **not** a literal difference of differences. Timepoint 1 is modelled as an outcome level, and the immediate-minus-waiting arm gap is estimated separately at each wave, with a child random intercept that partially pools stable between-child differences. Three quantities must be read differently:

- **`arm_gap_t1`** — the gap _before_ anyone was treated: a balance check, never an effect.
- **`tau_t2`** — the gap at timepoint 2, when the immediate group has had about 20 weeks of intervention and the waiting group none. **This is the randomised contrast of assignment to immediate treatment versus no treatment yet**, read as the covariate-adjusted t2 arm-gap _level_; its baseline adjustment is soft and prior-weighted (`did-104` is the registered sensitivity over that allocation).
- **`arm_gap_t3`** — the gap at timepoint 3, when both groups have been taught for different lengths of time. Still a comparison of randomly assigned groups, but of a **different exposure**: early-start versus delayed-start treatment history. It is neither a treated-versus-untreated effect nor latent-ability-confounded; what it cannot supply is the mechanism. **`delta_crossover`** (`tau_t2` minus `arm_gap_t3`) is the change between two randomised regime contrasts and never an identified catch-up.

Heavily floored outcomes (P, N) use a Bernoulli on wave-specific off-floor status, so their contrasts concern off-floor **prevalence**.

Expressive information is on its doubled half-mark scale (of 80), so +1.9 half marks is about +0.9 whole marks.

**The randomised t2 contrasts broadly agree with the headline `itt` estimates**: letter sounds +3.5 against +3.5, word reading +2.2 against +2.4, taught expressive vocabulary +1.5 against +1.5, blending +0.9 against +1.0. These fits reuse the same children and window, so this is a specification check rather than independent evidence. The DiD intervals are wider — word reading spans −0.3 to +4.7 here against +0.7 to +4.1 in `itt` — because the model estimates more quantities and does not use the baseline as a precision covariate in the same way.

**Balance.** No pre-treatment gap is resolved. The immediate arm started a little lower on most language measures (P(gap > 0) between 0.18 and 0.46 for W, L, TE, TR, F, R, P, N) and a little higher on the two APT scales (0.65 and 0.76). For expressive information that matters: a t1 lean of +1.9 half marks in the immediate arm's favour sits behind a t2 gap of the same size, so its t2 contrast should not be read as movement.

**After crossover, the gaps mostly narrow, and that is not a finding about the intervention wearing off.** For letter sounds the t3 gap is +1.3 items against +3.5 at t2; for taught expressive vocabulary and both APT scales the t3 gap is slightly negative; word reading's gap is nearly unchanged. Each `delta_crossover` describes how the difference between two randomly assigned groups changed once the waiting group was taught. A shorter time in the intervention, ordinary development, ceilings and the different taught blocks cannot be separated within it.

## The companions

- **Independent-prior intercept (`did-101`)**: word reading +2.1 (−0.4 to +4.6) against +2.2 — the empirical-Bayes anchor does not drive the result.
- **Wide treatment prior (`did-102`)**: letter sounds +4.3 (+1.7 to +6.8) against +3.5. This is the largest disagreement in the family and exactly the attenuation the tier prior was expected to produce: read the reference estimate as conservative.
- **Baseline allocation (`did-104`, new)**: word reading +1.6 (−1.4 to +4.5, P = 0.80) against +2.2 when the t1 balance term and child-intercept priors are widened. The direction holds but the magnitude is sensitive to how a realised t1 imbalance is split between the balance term and the child intercepts, which is the #576 sign-off's stated caveat for this family.
- **Dispersion prior (`did-105`, `did-106`, new)**: basic concepts +0.63 against +0.62; receptive vocabulary −0.12 against −0.06. The concentration prior is not what these results rest on.
- **Guessing-floor blending (`did-103`, new)**: +0.6 items against +0.9 under the ordinary link, both at P = 0.96. Blending improved in this family too, with the size again reduced by the floor; the two are released as a pair.
- **Catch-up heterogeneity (`did-013`)**: the same t2 contrast as `did-001` (+2.2). Its t3 items-scale quantities are deliberately withheld because a child-specific catch-up deviation cannot be integrated by a scalar arm-gap toggle.

## Prior sensitivity and the qualified tier

Six focal terms lean substantially on the treatment prior and are released as **prior-informed and exploratory** rather than as ordinary releases: the word-reading t2 contrasts in `did-001`, `013`, `101` and `104`, the blending contrast in `did-003`, and the observational dose centre in `did-007`. The estimand-matched sweep for `did-001` shows what that qualification means:

| Prior width | Effect (items)   | P(>0) |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ----- |
| 0.25        | 1.3 [−0.7, +3.2] | 0.858 |
| 0.50        | 2.2 [−0.3, +4.7] | 0.920 |
| 0.75        | 2.5 [−0.2, +5.2] | 0.932 |

The direction is robust; the magnitude is not well determined by these data alone. That is a reason to treat the `itt` word-reading estimate, which has no such dependence, as the headline and this family as corroboration of direction.

**Within-child dependence.** The new within-child predictive check (`did_within_child_ppc.csv`) flags between one and four of its twelve statistics for word reading, receptive and expressive vocabulary, taught receptive vocabulary and phonetic spelling, and none for the others. For word reading the model over-predicts the spread of within-child t1→t2 change (observed SD 3.9 items against a replicated 6.1, 89% 4.7 to 7.7). The random-intercept covariance is an approximation, not a fixed-effect control, and this check now says where it is loose.

## The dose companions

Three models relate outcomes to how many intervention sessions a child actually received; **none reports a causal headline, by design.** The pooled word-reading companion `did-006` associates a one-standard-deviation step in sessions (about 17) with **+2.0 items** (89% +0.8 to +3.3, P = 0.997) among on-intervention rows. The period-resolved letter-sound companion `did-007` gives +0.6 items (−0.2 to +1.4, P = 0.89) and its pooled comparator `did-107` +0.6 (−0.2 to +1.3); their nested predictive comparison is invalid (Pareto-k 1.17 and 1.15), so the period-varying and pooled versions cannot be ranked. Attendance is not randomised, and under the saturated arm-by-period cell design `theta_treated` is a crossover cell contrast, not an isolated treatment-presence effect. Treat these as descriptive; the `dose_response` family reports the same question with a between/within split.

The t2 numbers reproduce August's to the first decimal. ## What these models cannot tell you

**Only `tau_t2` compares treated with untreated.** **`arm_gap_t3` and `delta_crossover` compare two treated groups** and answer the early-versus-late question only, without a mechanism. **The child random intercept is not a fixed-effect control.** **The model does not condition on the timepoint-2 score** when estimating later periods, deliberately — that score is treatment-affected.
