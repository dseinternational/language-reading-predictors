# Findings: the `joint` family — all outcomes in one model

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 7 models in the `joint` family: the ten-outcome factorised graph, the three taught-versus-not-taught and modality contrasts, and their three dependence-aware companions. All 7 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and all are publishable (2026-09-01 rebuild, refitted at the #626 branch commit `b18ea944` so that they carry the new-child predictive validation).

## The data

**RLI trial only**, the randomised **timepoint 1 to timepoint 2 window**, one row per child and outcome. `itt-012` uses one common 53-child analysis set across all 10 outcomes (530 cells); each two-outcome fit uses 54 children.

## What the model is for

Fitting several outcomes in one PyMC graph keeps definitions and posterior calculations aligned and lets the model generate a **difference between two outcome-specific effects** on the proportion-correct scale. The parents (`itt-012`, `015`, `016`, `115`) use a **factorised** likelihood — each outcome its own marginal model, no within-child residual covariance — so their contrasts pair factorised marginals. The companions `215`, `216`, `315` (#551) repeat the two-outcome contrasts with an LKJ residual correlation between the outcomes' child-level residuals. Since #626 every joint fit also declares an out-of-sample **prediction target** — a new child in a replicate cohort — and validates against it.

## What was found

**The joint estimates reproduce the single-outcome estimates almost exactly.** On the model's log-odds scale, letter sounds are 0.582 (89% 0.274 to 0.883) in `itt-012` against 0.582 (0.278 to 0.877) in `itt-007`; word reading 0.356 (0.101 to 0.610) against 0.354 (0.101 to 0.611). The two vocabulary contrasts differ by a little more (R 0.035 against 0.008; E −0.003 against 0.004) because the joint fit uses the 53 children with every outcome observed. Because the likelihood factorises, close agreement is expected; it is a numerical consistency check, not a test of dependence.

The expressive contrast is suggestive at best; the receptive contrast is inconclusive and, if anything, the not-taught receptive items gained as much as the taught ones (the UR marginal clears zero on its own). A positive taught-minus-not-taught contrast would not by itself show negligible generalisation; that needs the not-taught marginal read against a substantive threshold, and here neither not-taught marginal is small.

**Modelling the dependence costs almost nothing.** Point estimates move by at most 0.0014 on the proportion scale, intervals widen by 1–3%, and direction probabilities by less than 0.01. The estimated residual correlation did not move off its prior in any of the three companions, so their intervals are prior-informed sensitivities rather than measured within-child covariances — the expected picture when little residual dependence remains after each outcome's own baseline and age are in the model. The release gate's machine-readable comparison records each pair as "compared, not material, channel measured".

## The new-child prediction target (#626)

Every joint fit now scores how well it predicts a child it has never seen. The importance-sampling estimator is publishable for all six two-outcome fits (maximum Pareto-k 0.31 to 0.40; child-level ELPD −231 to −264 with standard errors 6–8), and the three LKJ-residual companions are the case where integrating the child latent out makes the estimator _easier_ (k 0.32–0.40 against 0.56–0.65 for the conditional version). For the ten-outcome `itt-012` one child sits at k = 0.70, exactly the threshold, so its ELPD of −1333 (SE 27) is flagged unreliable rather than published as a validated estimate. Held-out child-level probability-integral-transform values are close to uniform across the outcomes. These are predictive summaries; nothing about an estimand changes.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Only the outcome-specific assigned-arm contrasts have the randomisation basis**, as available-case modified ITT estimates. **The parents estimate no residual correlation between outcomes**, and the companions find it weakly identified; nothing here says whether one skill causes another. **A contrast between two effects is not a generalisation verdict** without a threshold for the not-taught effect.
