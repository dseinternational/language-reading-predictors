# Findings: the `growth` family — trajectories across the whole study

**Read `findings-00-overview` first.** This note covers the 4 models in the `growth` family. **Nothing here is causal.** All 4 pass the convergence gate with zero divergences and all are publishable (2026-09-01 rebuild).

## The data

**Both studies.** Three models use the RLI trial across all four timepoints (54 children, 216 rows). One uses the historical cohort across waves 1 to 3 (87 children, 261 possible child-wave cells with missing cells masked).

## What the model is for

A growth-curve model describes each child's trajectory as a starting level and a rate of change, linear in standardised age; in the trial models both vary between children. The question is whether **baseline non-verbal ability** (block design) predicts how _fast_ a child progresses (`gamma`) as distinct from how high they start (`delta`). Variants add a shared growth-tempo factor or an age-by-ability interaction. The historical model asks the same question with baseline verbal reasoning and reading-group nuisance trajectories.

## What was found

**Ability and rate, in the trial cohort.** The clearest relationship is between baseline non-verbal ability and growth in receptive grammar: **+0.11 logit units per SD** (89% +0.02 to +0.20, P = 0.98) in `gc-069`, +0.11 (+0.01 to +0.21) in the shared-tempo variant and +0.15 (+0.06 to +0.25) as the main effect in the interaction model. For the two reading measures the rate association is unresolved and, for letter sounds, leans negative (−0.15, 89% −0.33 to +0.04, P(negative) = 0.89, suggestive): more able children did not grow faster on letter sounds at the sample-mean entry age. Ability's association with the **starting level** is very strong for the three verbal measures (about +0.19 to +0.23 per SD) and suggestive for word reading and letter sounds.

**The age-by-ability interaction (`gc-085`).** Older-and-more-able children progress faster than the two main effects imply, most clearly for **letter sounds** (+0.27 logit units per SD-age × SD-ability, 89% +0.05 to +0.50, P = 0.97) and expressive vocabulary (+0.07, +0.00 to +0.14, P = 0.95); directional but unresolved for word reading (+0.10, P = 0.83) and receptive vocabulary (+0.05, P = 0.86); absent for grammar (+0.01). So the ability–growth association for letter sounds is concentrated in the older entrants, exactly where its main effect at the mean entry age is unresolved. The age main effects on the rate are negative for every measure and very strong for word reading (−0.40 per SD of entry age): older entrants grew more slowly across the study on the logit scale, the same age pattern the `gain_factors`, `adjusted` and `lcsm` families show on other scales.

**The shared-tempo factor (`gc-070`)** loads positively on all five measures (word reading 0.38 and letter sounds 0.35 the largest, the three verbal measures 0.11–0.17), so the measures' rates do move together; ability's correlation with the common tempo itself is −0.02 (89% −0.24 to +0.21), so its growth associations are carried by the per-measure terms, chiefly grammar.

**The historical cohort (`rlm-gc-001`).** Baseline verbal reasoning is associated with BAS word-reading growth at **−0.13 logit units per SD** (89% −0.25 to −0.01, P(negative) = 0.95, moderate): children with higher baseline verbal reasoning grew _more slowly_ over waves 1 to 3, while starting higher (+0.30, P = 0.97). The observation-influence refit masked five high-Pareto child-wave cells across four children, fully excluding one: the rate coefficient moved to −0.10 (89% −0.22 to +0.03), P(negative) = 0.90, so the direction survives and the evidence weakens from moderate to suggestive with an interval that now includes zero. The release gate's coefficient-stability criteria (median direction and interval overlap) are met.

## The negative result needs care, not a story

This is not the ordinary setup in which a noisy outcome is subtracted from itself: the predictor is wave-1 verbal reasoning and the repeated outcome is word reading, estimated jointly, so regression to the mean does not by itself force the coefficient negative. But the reading-matched group was selected on baseline reading, verbal reasoning is a noisy proxy correlated with initial reading level, and a bounded test leaves less headroom for children nearer its top. Selection, measurement, ceiling and a genuine association are possible explanations rather than findings from this model; it is not evidence that verbal reasoning impedes reading development. The same caution applies, with the opposite sign, to the trial cohort's small positive grammar result.

## What these models cannot tell you

**Baseline ability is not a lever.** **A linear trajectory is an assumption.** **Growth rates are hard to estimate from four waves.** **The trial models pool across arms**, so they describe development in the study as a whole, not treatment effects.
